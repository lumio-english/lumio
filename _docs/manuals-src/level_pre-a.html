Pre-A · First Words

LUMIO ENGLISH

The very beginning of the journey: first greetings, the alphabet, numbers, colors, family, and everyday things -- all through play, songs, and repetition, no reading required.

By the end of this level, your child can:

  • Say hello and goodbye, and their own name
  • Recognize and say all 26 letters of the alphabet
  • Count from zero to ten
  • Name basic colors
  • Name family members and parts of the body
  • Name common animals, foods, toys, and school things
  • Say simple action words and how they feel

Vocabulary and functional language only -- formal grammar begins in Level 3. At this age, the goal is confident first words and sounds, not sentence structure.

A printable page for every single lesson

Lesson 1 homework sheet
Lesson 1 flashcards (print double-sided)

Who they're for, and why

Writing practice: At this age, before reading is even required, the task is simple and physical -- tracing and copying each word by hand, then drawing a picture for it. This builds letter formation and muscle memory long before your child is asked to write a sentence, and gives them a non-verbal way to show they understood a word.

Flashcards: Every card shows the picture and English word on one side and the Arabic translation on the other. That means either parent can quiz their child at home, even without speaking English themselves -- a two-minute flip-through before bed is real, screen-free repetition that helps new words genuinely stick.

Lumi's Pocket — How It Works

LUMIO ENGLISH

Lumi's Pocket, this level's bonus game

A letter-and-listening game built around the alphabet your child is currently learning: Lumi names a specific word, and your child finds it among two or three other words that start with the very same letter -- a real test of listening past the first sound, not just picture-matching.

How to play

  1. Lumi shows a letter your child has already learned, and asks for one specific word that starts with it (for example, "Find 'nine'!"). Every picture shown genuinely starts with that same letter, so your child has to listen for the whole word, not just the first sound.
  2. The letters used only ever come from lessons your child has already completed -- nothing shows up before it's been taught.
  3. A few rounds also ask about a word or short sentence from the current lesson itself, mixing in vocabulary alongside the alphabet practice.
  4. Each correct answer earns a star; by the final round, all six stars are filled in.
A new round of the game unlocks after every lesson your child finishes, using that lesson's own words — so it is always practice, never random.
