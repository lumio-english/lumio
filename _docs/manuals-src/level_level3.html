Level 3 · Everyday Life

Real grammar begins here, and the full character cast joins in: Omar, Sara, Noor, Ziad, and Hamad. Conversations get longer, opinions get shared, and your child starts using English the way friends actually talk to each other.

By the end of this level, your child can:

  • Talk about what friends do together (Present Simple)
  • Describe their room with "there is / there are"
  • Say what they can and can't do
  • Ask and answer Wh- questions about routines and skills
  • Compare things ("faster", "bigger")
  • Talk about what is happening right now
  • Read a short text and answer questions about it

Level 3 · Bonus Classes

Four Bonus Classes: how English works

After every four lessons comes a 60-minute Bonus Class. It explains how English really works — sounds, spelling, grammar or where words come from — compares it with Arabic, and practises it with games and creative tasks. At home, your child completes a bonus mission board, and each Bonus Class has its own printable worksheet and cards.

B1

Every Word Has a Job: Parts of Speechلكل كلمة وظيفة: أقسام الكلام

After Lesson 4
Every word in a sentence has a job (noun, verb, adjective, pronoun, preposition), and every English sentence needs a verb, even when Arabic does not: He is a teacher, not He teacher.
B2

Word Order: Who Does Whatترتيب الكلمات: مَن يفعل ماذا

After Lesson 8
In English the position of a word tells you its job: the doer comes first, then the verb, then the thing (S-V-O), and adjectives come before the noun and never change for plural or gender.
B3

The Question Machineآلة الأسئلة

After Lesson 12
English builds a question by moving a helper verb (is, are, can) in front of the subject; if there is no helper, it calls do or does. Arabic just adds هل or changes the tone.
B4

Time Inside Verbs: Simple vs Continuousالزمن داخل الأفعال: البسيط والمستمر

After Lesson 16
English uses two presents: the simple for habits and facts (I study every day) and the continuous for things in progress now (I am studying right now). Arabic يدرس can mean both, and state verbs like know and want stay simple.

Crew Chat — How It Works

Crew Chat, this level's game

A group-chat simulation with the full character cast, mixing four kinds of rounds: translating a word, describing a shared photo, spelling a word letter by letter, and understanding a full sentence.

How to play

  1. Translate rounds: a crew member asks how to say an Arabic word in English -- tap the right reply, or spell it back letter by letter using scrambled tiles.
  2. Caption rounds: a crew member shares a photo and asks what's happening -- your child picks which of the lesson's own real sentences actually describes it.
  3. Meaning rounds: a crew member sends a full English sentence and asks what it means -- your child picks the matching Arabic translation, practicing whole-sentence understanding.
  4. Every playthrough mixes all four round types, and a short answer streak gets a hype message from the crew. Correct answers appear as real sent messages, building an actual conversation as the game goes on.
A new round of the game unlocks after every lesson your child finishes, using that lesson's own words — so it is always practice, never random. The Hub adds Memory Match, Word Pop, Word Builder and Balloon Pop for extra word practice.
