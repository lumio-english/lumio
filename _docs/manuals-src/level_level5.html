Level 5 · Telling My Story

The past tense arrives here: what happened, when, and why. Your child moves from describing the present to genuinely telling stories -- their weekend, a trip, a party, a mistake -- in connected, real narrative.

By the end of this level, your child can:

  • Talk about what happened yesterday or last weekend
  • Ask and answer questions in the past ("Did you...?")
  • Describe how something was and why it happened
  • Tell a story in order (first, then, finally)
  • Describe what was happening at a specific moment
  • Talk about a trip, a party, or a memorable day
  • Read a short story and answer questions about it

Grammar

Past simple (regular and irregular), past questions and negatives, was/were, there was/were, time words, sequencing words, because, and the past continuous -- one grammar point per lesson with practice, the common mistakes Arabic speakers make, and a Grammar Hub rule page.

Level 5 · Bonus Classes

Four Bonus Classes: how English works

After every four lessons comes a 60-minute Bonus Class. It explains how English really works — sounds, spelling, grammar or where words come from — compares it with Arabic, and practises it with games and creative tasks. At home, your child completes a bonus mission board, and each Bonus Class has its own printable worksheet and cards.

B1

The Three Sounds of -ed and the Old Verbsأصوات -ed الثلاثة والأفعال القديمة

After Lesson 4
The past ending -ed is one spelling with three sounds, /t/, /d/ and /ɪd/, and the last sound of the verb chooses which one. Many irregular verbs are old 'fossils' that show the past by changing the vowel inside the word, a little like Arabic does.
B2

How Stories Are Built: The Story Mountainكيف تُبنى الحكايات: جبل القصة

After Lesson 8
Most stories, in English or in Arabic, climb a similar mountain: a setting, a problem that grows, a climax, and an ending. Linking words are the ropes that hold the parts together.
B3

Background and Action: Was Doing vs Didالخلفية والحدث: was doing مقابل did

After Lesson 12
A story has two layers: the background, filmed like a video (was/were + -ing: It was raining, people were waiting), and the events, taken like snapshots (past simple: the bus arrived). English chooses the form by how we look at the action, not only by when it happened.
B4

Speak Like a Storyteller: The Music of Englishتكلّم كراوٍ محترف: موسيقى اللغة الإنجليزية

After Lesson 16
Spoken English has a beat: the important words (content words) are strong and the small words (was, were, to, and, of, a, the) become short and weak, often with the sound /ə/. Words link together and good storytellers pause in the right places.

Story Detective — How It Works

Story Detective, this level's game

A detective mystery theme covering three kinds of clues: finding the right meaning of a word, judging whether a witness got a translation right, and figuring out what was really said next in an overheard conversation.

How to play

  1. Find the evidence: an Arabic word or phrase is shown with three English options -- tap the one that matches its meaning.
  2. Verify the clue: a witness claims an Arabic phrase means a certain English word -- your child judges True or False.
  3. Witness testimony: two lines of a real overheard conversation are shown, and your child figures out what was really said next from three options. Every confirmed answer gets pinned to the case file as evidence; by the end, the full case file is revealed -- "mystery solved!"
A new round of the game unlocks after every lesson your child finishes, using that lesson's own words — so it is always practice, never random. The Hub adds Memory Match, Word Pop, Word Builder and Balloon Pop for extra word practice.
