Level 5 · Telling My Story

The past tense arrives here: what happened, when, and why. Your child moves from describing the present to genuinely telling stories -- their weekend, a trip, a party, a mistake -- in connected, real narrative.

By the end of this level, your child can:

  • Talk about what happened yesterday or last weekend
  • Ask and answer questions in the past ("Did you...?")
  • Describe how something was and why it happened
  • Tell a story with sequence ("and then...")
  • Describe what was happening at a specific moment
  • Talk about a trip, a party, or a memorable day
  • Interview someone and recap a story

The Telling My Story Hub

Open any time, not just during lessons: Vocabulary, Idioms, Writing & Speaking, Grammar (page 9). And after every finished lesson, a new round of this level's bonus game, Story Detective, unlocks (page 11).

Every lesson has the same shape: new words with pictures, Arabic and audio → listening and matching practice → a grammar point → a short quiz and spelling → homework, a printable worksheet and flashcards. Lesson 20 is the level's Big Review.
Live classes run 45–90 minutes depending on the lesson: a little shorter at the start of the level, and the full 90 minutes for the Big Review in Lesson 20.

Who they're for, and why

Writing practice: Your child writes three original sentences using that day's real grammar pattern -- for example, Past Simple regular verbs in Lesson 1. Since this level's whole focus is telling real stories about the past, this is where your child actually practices narrating their own weekend, not just recognizing past-tense forms on screen.

Flashcards: Every card shows the picture and English word on one side and the Arabic translation on the other. That means either parent can quiz their teen at home, even without speaking English themselves -- a quick flip-through is real, screen-free repetition that keeps vocabulary fresh alongside the grammar work.

Story Detective — How It Works

Story Detective, this level's bonus game

A detective mystery theme covering three kinds of clues: finding the right meaning of a word, judging whether a witness got a translation right, and figuring out what was really said next in an overheard conversation.

How to play

  1. Find the evidence: an Arabic word or phrase is shown with three English options -- tap the one that matches its meaning.
  2. Verify the clue: a witness claims an Arabic phrase means a certain English word -- your child judges True or False.
  3. Witness testimony: two lines of a real overheard conversation are shown, and your child figures out what was really said next from three options. Every confirmed answer gets pinned to the case file as evidence; by the end, the full case file is revealed -- "mystery solved!"
A new round of the game unlocks after every lesson your child finishes, using that lesson's own words — so it is always practice, never random.
