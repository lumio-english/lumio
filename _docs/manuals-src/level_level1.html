Level 1 · About Me

LUMIO ENGLISH

Real sentences begin here: describing things, saying what your child likes and doesn't like, and talking about their home, clothes, and abilities.

By the end of this level, your child can:

  • Form simple sentences with "this is..." / "I am..."
  • Describe size ("big", "small") and count to 20
  • Say what they like and don't like
  • Name rooms, furniture, and clothes
  • Talk about the weather and days of the week
  • Use prepositions like "in", "on", and "under"
  • Describe shapes and simple opposites
  • Say what they can and can't do

Grammar

A new sentence pattern in nearly every lesson -- "this is", "I like", "I can", "where is" -- used in real sentences from day one. Formal grammar rules and the Grammar Hub begin in Level 3.

Lesson 1 homework sheet

Who they're for, and why

Writing practice: At this level, the task is still tracing and copying each word by hand, then drawing a picture for it. This continues building letter formation and muscle memory before sentence-writing begins at Level 3, and gives your child a non-verbal way to show they understood a word.

Flashcards: Every card shows the picture and English word on one side and the Arabic translation on the other. That means either parent can quiz their child at home, even without speaking English themselves -- a two-minute flip-through before bed is real, screen-free repetition that helps new words genuinely stick.

Treehouse Builder — How It Works

LUMIO ENGLISH

Treehouse Builder, this level's bonus game

A phonics-focused listening game: Lumi says a specific word built from letter sounds your child has already learned, and they find it among other words from that same phonics group -- with a treehouse piece added for every correct answer.

How to play

  1. Lumi says a specific word (for example, "egg") built from phonics sounds your child has already covered in this level, and shows a few labeled pictures.
  2. Your child taps the picture whose word matches what they heard -- since the sounds are still fresh, this trains real letter-sound recognition, not just picture memory.
  3. A few rounds also use this lesson's own vocabulary and example sentences, so words and simple sentence patterns get practiced too.
  4. Each correct answer adds a new piece to the treehouse -- a wall, a roof, a ladder -- until it's fully built and ready to celebrate.
A new round of the game unlocks after every lesson your child finishes, using that lesson's own words — so it is always practice, never random.
