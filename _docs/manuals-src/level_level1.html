Level 1 · About Me

Real sentences begin here: describing things, saying what your child likes and doesn't like, and talking about their home, clothes, and abilities. Reading begins too: a step-by-step phonics course teaches the sound of every letter, so your child can blend sounds and read real words and short stories.

By the end of this level, your child can:

  • Form simple sentences with "this is..." / "I am..."
  • Describe size ("big", "small") and count to 20
  • Say what they like and don't like
  • Name rooms, furniture, clothes, and pets
  • Talk about the weather and days of the week
  • Use prepositions like "in", "on", and "under"
  • Say what they can and can't do
  • Read every single letter sound and blend short words (sat, dog, chick)
  • Read a short story using the sounds they know

Sentences, sounds and grammar

A new sentence pattern in nearly every lesson -- "this is", "I like", "I can", "where is" -- used in real sentences from day one, with Grammar Time and Common Mistakes slides where a pattern is new. Phonics runs alongside: 8 sound groups, one every other lesson. Formal grammar rules and the Grammar Hub begin in Level 3.

Level 1 · Phonics

Reading English, sound by sound

Level 1 teaches every single letter sound, in a careful order: s, a, t, p first, because they already make real words (sat, tap, pat). Your child blends sounds into words from the very first lesson. Every practice word and every short story uses only the sounds taught so far, so your child can always read it on their own. A few very common words that don't follow the rules ("tricky words") come one to three at a time.

Level 1 · Bonus Classes

Four Bonus Classes: how English works

After every four lessons comes a 60-minute Bonus Class. It explains how English really works — sounds, spelling, grammar or where words come from — compares it with Arabic, and practises it with games and creative tasks. At home, your child completes a bonus mission board, and each Bonus Class has its own printable worksheet and cards.

B1

How English Sounds Work: Lazy A, Quick E and Smiley Iكيف تعمل أصوات الإنجليزية: A الكسولة و E السريع و I المبتسمة

After Lesson 4
Letters are what we see; sounds are what we hear. English has only 5 vowel letters but many more vowel sounds, so we must train our mouth and our ears.
B2

Sounds Arabic Doesn't Have: P, V and the Buzzأصوات ليست في العربية: P و V والطنين

After Lesson 8
Many English sounds come in twins: same mouth, but one buzzes in the throat and one only whispers. Arabic has b and f, but not their twins p and v, so we train them on purpose.
B3

Blending Builders: Sounds Into Wordsبنّاؤو الكلمات: من الأصوات إلى الكلمات

After Lesson 12
Reading is pushing sounds together from left to right (blending); spelling is pulling a word apart into its sounds (segmenting). English writes its vowels as letters, so we read every letter and never skip the middle.
B4

Tricky Words and Sentence Rulesالكلمات الخادعة وقواعد الجملة

After Lesson 16
Most English words can be sounded out, but some very common words keep old spellings and must be learned by heart. A sentence also has rules: a capital letter at the start, spaces between words and a period or question mark at the end.

Printable Practice & Flashcards

A printable page for every lesson

Beyond the screen, every one of Level 1's 20 lessons comes with its own homework sheet and flashcard set, plus a writing sheet, ready to print. Each Bonus Class has a worksheet and cards too.

Lesson 1 homework sheet
Lesson 1 flashcards (print double-sided, flip on the long edge)

Made for home

Homework and writing sheets: Done at home, partly alone and partly with a parent (each exercise is labelled). Your child writes each word in letter boxes (the first letter is given), reads the lesson's phonics words and its short story aloud to you, circles the correct sentence and completes sentences from a word box. The writing sheet has tracing on school lines, copying sentences, putting words in order and a first sentence about themselves. Answers are printed upside down for you to check together.

Flashcards: Six large cards per sheet: the picture and the English word on the front; the Arabic (and, from Level 1, the lesson's example sentence) on the back. Print double-sided, flipping on the long edge, and every back lands behind its own front. Either parent can quiz their child, even without speaking English: a two-minute flip-through before bed is real, screen-free practice.

Treehouse Builder — How It Works

Treehouse Builder, this level's game

A phonics-focused listening game: Lumi says a specific word built from letter sounds your child has already learned, and they find it among other words from that same phonics group -- with a treehouse piece added for every correct answer.

How to play

  1. Lumi says a specific word (for example, "egg") built from phonics sounds your child has already covered in this level, and shows a few labeled pictures.
  2. Your child taps the picture whose word matches what they heard -- since the sounds are still fresh, this trains real letter-sound recognition, not just picture memory.
  3. A few rounds also use this lesson's own vocabulary and example sentences, so words and simple sentence patterns get practiced too.
  4. Each correct answer adds a new piece to the treehouse -- a wall, a roof, a ladder -- until it's fully built and ready to celebrate.
A new round of the game unlocks after every lesson your child finishes, using that lesson's own words — so it is always practice, never random. The Hub adds Memory Match, Word Pop, Word Builder and Balloon Pop for extra word practice.
