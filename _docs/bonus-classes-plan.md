# Bonus Classes — design (12 Oct 2026)

Status: DESIGN, not live. Content is written and reviewable; the website still runs the old 20-lesson model until the owner says "make it live".

## 1. What changes

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Classes per level | 20 lessons | **24 classes** = 20 lessons + **4 Bonus Classes** |
| Where bonus classes sit | — | one after every 4 lessons: after Lessons 4, 8, 12 and 16 |
| Level length | 2 months, 10 classes a month | **about 3 months**; no fixed number per month |
| Pace rule | 10 a month | **minimum 2, maximum 3 classes a week** |
| Class length | 45-90 min | every Bonus Class is **60 minutes** |

### The 24-class sequence (same for every level)

| Class | 1-4 | **5** | 6-9 | **10** | 11-14 | **15** | 16-19 | **20** | 21-24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Lessons 1-4 | **Bonus Class 1** | Lessons 5-8 | **Bonus Class 2** | Lessons 9-12 | **Bonus Class 3** | Lessons 13-16 | **Bonus Class 4** | Lessons 17-20 (20 = Big Review) |

Lessons keep their numbers (Lesson 1-20) so nothing already built (decks, homework, worksheets, the Hub, the story unlocks, voice files) has to be renumbered. Bonus classes have their own label: "Bonus Class 1-4". A class's place in the level (1-24) is its `classPosition`.

### Why 3 months works with 2-3 classes a week
- 24 classes at 2 a week = 12 weeks; at 3 a week = 8 weeks.
- "About 3 months" (13 weeks) is the slowest normal pace plus one spare week for a holiday, an absence or the level test. Faster students finish in 2-2.5 months; nobody is forced into 10 a month.

## 2. What a Bonus Class is

A 60-minute class with its own materials whose job is to explain **how English works** — the level's core skill — and to widen general knowledge, then practise it hard. It is not a review lesson and does not add new topic vocabulary.

Every Bonus Class has the same fixed parts (the data files follow this exactly):

1. **Big idea** — one sentence the student should be able to explain at home.
2. **Objectives** — 3-4 "I can..." lines (English + Arabic).
3. **Linguistics Corner** — real linguistics made child-sized (how sounds are made, why spelling is odd, how sentences are built, where words come from).
4. **Arabic Bridge** — what is the same and what is different in Arabic, so the student uses Arabic as a tool, not an obstacle.
5. **Common errors** — the typical mistakes of Arabic speakers on this point, with the reason.
6. **Stages** — 6-8 timed stages that always add up to 60 minutes: warm-up hook → discovery → Arabic bridge → guided practice → game/creative practice → production → exit ticket + "Did you know?". Each stage has: method, purpose, exactly what the teacher says (English + Arabic support), what students do, an exercise with answers, and tips.
7. **Exit ticket** (5 quick items with answers), **homework**, **differentiation** (support + challenge), **assessment** (success criteria + what to watch for), **teacher notes**.

Teaching methods used across the set (all research-backed): explicit systematic phonics, minimal pairs, multisensory work (mirror, hand on throat, tissue test), TPR gestures, character mnemonics, guided discovery of grammar rules, contrastive analysis with Arabic, retrieval practice, task-based speaking, games and gamification, peer teaching, formative exit tickets.

Everything works online on Teams with up to 4 students: the only things a student needs at home are a mirror (or camera self-view), paper, a pencil and sometimes a tissue or letter cards they make in class.

## 3. The core skill of each level and the 28 classes

| Level | Ages | Strand (core skill) |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-A | 4-6 | **Sound Explorers** — listening and sound awareness (letter names vs sounds, first sounds, rhyme, syllables, letter shapes) |
| Level 1 | 6-8 | **Sound Lab** — phonics and pronunciation (vowels, sounds Arabic does not have, blending, tricky words) |
| Level 2 | 8-10 | **Spelling Detectives** — digraphs, consonant clusters, long vowels, syllables and stress |
| Level 3 | 10-12 | **Grammar Lab** — how English sentences work (word classes, word order, questions, verb time) |
| Level 4 | 11-13 | **Word Power** — grammar meaning and word building (the -s mystery, countability, prefixes/suffixes, politeness) |
| Level 5 | 12-14 | **Story Science** — the past tense, narrative and the music of spoken English |
| Level 6 | 13-15 | **English in the World** — future meaning, modality, word origins and persuasion |

Each class only uses what the students have met in the lessons before it.

### Pre-A — Sound Explorers
1. (after L4: hello, name, ABC A-R) **My Mouth Makes Music** — noises vs speech sounds; every letter has a name and a sound (B is "bee", says /b/), exactly like Arabic (باء says /b/); lips, teeth, tongue.
2. (after L8: ABC S-Z, numbers, colours) **First Sound Detectives** — hearing the first sound of a word, I-spy, sorting pictures by first sound, alliteration chants.
3. (after L12: family, body, animals) **Rhyme Time and Clap the Beats** — rhyme (cat/hat; Arabic poems rhyme too), syllable clapping (el-e-phant).
4. (after L16: food, school) **Big Letters, Small Letters, Left to Right** — capital/small letter families, English is written left to right and letters do not change shape by position like Arabic (ب ـبـ ـب).

### Level 1 — Sound Lab
1. (after L4; phonics s a t p i n m d) **How English Sounds Work: Lazy A, Quick E and Smiley I** — letters vs sounds, consonants vs vowels, harakat vs English vowels, the Vowel Elevator, pan/pen/pin. *(model class, written first)*
2. (after L8; g o c k, ck e u r) **Sounds Arabic Doesn't Have: P, V and the Buzz** — /p/ vs /b/ (tissue test), /v/ vs /f/, voiced vs voiceless (hand on throat), soft English r, short o vs u (hot/hut).
3. (after L12; h b f l, blending) **Blending Builders: Sounds Into Words** — blending and segmenting, sound boxes, phoneme counting, sound chains (cat→cap→map); Arabic often leaves short vowels unwritten, English never does.
4. (after L16; sight words) **Tricky Words and Sentence Rules** — why some words can't be sounded out (the, was, said — English spelling is old), capital letters, full stops, question marks, spaces between words.

### Level 2 — Spelling Detectives
1. (after L4; j v w x y z, sh ch) **Two Letters, One Sound: Digraphs** — sh, ch, th, ck, ph, wh; Arabic gives these one letter each (ش، ث، ذ); th has two sounds, like ث and ذ.
2. (after L8; th ng, L-blends) **Consonant Clusters: Why We Say "Isport"** — Arabic words never start with two consonants, so speakers add a vowel (ispoon, bilack); cluster practice without the extra vowel.
3. (after L12; R- and S-blends) **Long and Short Vowels: Magic E and Vowel Teams** — short vs long = harakat vs alif/waw/ya; magic e (cap→cape, kit→kite); ai, ee, oa.
4. (after L16; vowel teams, sight words) **Syllables, Stress and the Three Sounds of -s** — counting syllables, stressed syllables (BIRTH-day), plural -s said /s/, /z/, /iz/ (cats, dogs, buses).

### Level 3 — Grammar Lab
1. (after L4: present simple, negatives, there is/are) **Every Word Has a Job: Parts of Speech** — noun, verb, adjective, pronoun, preposition vs Arabic اسم/فعل/حرف; every English sentence needs a verb ("He is a teacher", not "He teacher" like هو معلم).
2. (after L8: this/that, plurals, possessives, prepositions) **Word Order: Who Does What** — English fixed S-V-O vs Arabic's free order; adjective before noun (a red car / سيارة حمراء); adjectives never change for plural or gender.
3. (after L12: can, wh-questions, comparatives, articles) **The Question Machine** — how English builds questions (move the helper, add do/does) vs Arabic هل + tone; rising and falling intonation.
4. (after L16: imperatives L13, present continuous L14-15, comparatives review L16) **Time Inside Verbs: Simple vs Continuous** — habit vs now; Arabic يدرس covers both; state verbs (I know, not I am knowing).

### Level 4 — Word Power
1. (after L4: do/does, routines, frequency, in/on/at) **The -s Mystery and the Time Triangle** — why only he/she/it gets -s (old English "he doeth" → does), the three sounds of -s, in/on/at as a triangle (at = point, on = day, in = longer).
2. (after L8: pronouns, some/any, countable) **How English Counts the World** — countable vs uncountable (information, advice, homework, furniture are uncountable in English but countable in Arabic: معلومات); English "you" vs Arabic أنتَ/أنتِ/أنتما/أنتم/أنتنّ.
3. (after L12: superlatives, would like, should) **Word Builders: Roots, Prefixes and Suffixes** — English adds pieces (happy, unhappy, happiness); Arabic builds from three-letter roots (ك ت ب → كاتب، مكتب، مكتبة); -er/-est, un-, re-, -ful, -less.
4. (after L16: group chat, teamwork, decisions) **Polite English: Sounding Kind and Clear** — register (chat vs email vs teacher), softeners (Could you..., I'd like...), why "Give me" sounds rude in English; Arabic politeness formulas compared.

### Level 5 — Story Science
1. (after L4: regular/irregular past, did, was/were) **The Three Sounds of -ed and the Old Verbs** — walked /t/, played /d/, wanted /id/; irregular verbs as fossils (sing-sang-sung change the vowel inside, like Arabic كَتَبَ/كُتُب).
2. (after L8: there was, time expressions, sequencing, because) **How Stories Are Built** — the story mountain (setting, problem, climax, ending), linking words, "Once upon a time" vs "كان يا ما كان".
3. (after L12: feelings, past continuous, trip) **Background and Action: Was Doing vs Did** — scene-setting vs events on a timeline, when/while; Arabic كان يلعب = was playing.
4. (after L16: party, a day to remember, while) **Speak Like a Storyteller: The Music of English** — sentence stress, content vs function words, weak forms (was = /wəz/), linking, pausing; English is stress-timed and shrinks its small words, while Arabic speakers tend to give every small word a full vowel; dramatic reading.

### Level 6 — English in the World
1. (after L4; future forms taught in L1-3, past continuous L4) **Does English Have a Future Tense?** — English has no future ending (Arabic has سـ / سوف); choosing will / going to / present continuous by meaning; 'll and gonna in speech.
2. (after L8: have to, adverbs of manner, long comparatives) **The Language of Rules** — introduces must alongside the taught have to; must / have to / don't have to / mustn't (the "don't have to" trap vs لا يجب), adverb spelling rules (-ly, -ily, -ally).
3. (after L12: rules at home, the way we talk, which is better) **Where English Words Come From** — language families, Old English / French / Latin layers, English words from Arabic (sugar, coffee, cotton, algebra, zero, giraffe, sofa, magazine), synonyms by origin (buy/purchase).
4. (after L16: debate, next year, getting ready) **The Art of Persuasion** — ethos, pathos, logos; discourse markers (firstly, however, on the other hand), hedging, a timed mini-debate.
