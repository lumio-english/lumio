# Level 5 — Image Prompts (complete set: every word, NEW + REPLACE)

Standard production format, same as the Level 0–4 batches. This is the **whole of Level 5**: every vocabulary image file the 19 teaching lessons use (Lesson 20 is review and reuses earlier files), checked against `assets/vocab/` on 6 Oct, after the Academic review that re-categorised every word (verb / word / grammar word) and re-ordered the lessons verbs → words → grammar words.

- **NEW** (44 Level 5 words + 3 overrides = 47) — no image has ever been generated for the word. These are the only files the decks are currently missing; until they arrive the vocab slide, recap chip and flashcard show an empty frame.
- **REPLACE** (140, including the `first.png` meaning fix) — an image exists; regenerate at the new quality bar. Every one of these was on your request list, so all of them are written out in full. Where the existing picture is still right you can skip it — but the prompt is here so nothing has to be guessed later.
- **Context overrides** (4, at the end) — words that are also taught in another level with a *different meaning*; the duplicate-meaning scan across all seven levels found exactly these. Each gets its own file name and the lesson JSON points at it, so the other level keeps its picture. (Same word, same meaning — `nervous`, `proud`, `relieved`, `hobby`, `umbrella`, `ticket`, `traffic`, `meanwhile`, `lost`, `found`, `was cooking`, `was texting` … — is **not** regenerated; one file serves every lesson.)

Each prompt is written to the meaning the Level 5 lesson teaches (lesson + example sentence shown). Level 5 is the **past tense** level: every picture that shows an action should read as *finished* or *in progress at a past moment* — a small visual cue (a clock on the wall, a calendar page, a "done" tick, a thought-bubble frame) is used only where it helps and never with text.

**Character rule:** a character brief is included only where the picture genuinely needs a person (marked `CHAR:`); everything else is an object or scene with no character. `CHAR:` items only ever use the core **Teen Track** cast. No physical contact between boys and girls — mixed scenes keep a clear gap; high-fives, fist-bumps and shoulder-arms are boys-with-boys or girls-with-girls only. Everything modest and Gulf-appropriate: no dating cues, no music-video/party imagery (a *family* party is fine: balloons, cake, lanterns, no dancing), sports kit with long shorts, no revealing clothing. Family members (mom, dad, grandparents, little sister) are drawn modestly in the same style; women and girls in hijab.

**Master style** (append to every prompt): Simple flat vector illustration for a teen English-learning app. Bold clean dark outlines, smooth soft shading, rich but not childish colours (orange, teal, yellow accents), one centred subject, ISOLATED ON A FULLY TRANSPARENT BACKGROUND (PNG cutout, no background, no shadow plane, no scenery), no text, letters or numerals, no watermark, square 1:1. Characters are aged 13–16 with realistic teen proportions and use the established Teen Track designs exactly — **Omar:** short dark hair, warm brown skin, white thobe with orange trim, white-and-orange sneakers. **Sara:** orange hijab, glasses, orange cardigan over a white top, long orange skirt, orange-and-white sneakers. **Noor:** orange-and-teal patterned hijab, teal tunic dress with patterned trim over white sleeves, orange-and-white sneakers. **Ziad:** short tousled brown hair, blue-and-yellow zip hoodie over a white t-shirt, dark grey cargo pants, blue-and-white sneakers, on-ear headphones around his neck. **Hamad:** white ghutra headscarf with black agal, white thobe, plain dark sneakers.
