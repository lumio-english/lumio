Tricky Words and Sentence Rules

الكلمات الخادعة وقواعد الجملة
Big idea: Most English words can be sounded out, but some very common words keep old spellings and must be learned by heart. A sentence also has rules: a capital letter at the start, spaces between words and a period or question mark at the end.
معظم كلمات الإنجليزية يمكن قراءتها بالأصوات، لكن بعض الكلمات الشائعة جداً احتفظت بتهجئة قديمة ويجب حفظها عن ظهر قلب. وللجملة أيضاً قواعد: حرف كبير في البداية، ومسافات بين الكلمات، ونقطة أو علامة استفهام في النهاية.

Linguistics Corner — English spelling is an old photo · تهجئة الإنجليزية صورة قديمة

English spelling is like an old photo of the language. Long ago, people said almost every letter they wrote. Then the way people spoke changed, but many spellings stayed the same.تهجئة الإنجليزية مثل صورة قديمة للغة. قديماً كان الناس ينطقون كل حرف تقريباً، ثم تغيّرت طريقة الكلام لكن كثيراً من الكلمات بقيت تُكتب كما كانت.
Look at knife (Lesson 10). Hundreds of years ago people really said the k: 'k-neef'. The k sound slowly disappeared (it was gone by about 300 years ago), but the letter stayed.انظر إلى كلمة knife (الدرس 10). قبل مئات السنين كان الناس ينطقون حرف k فعلاً: 'k-neef'. ثم اختفى الصوت شيئاً فشيئاً (قبل نحو 300 سنة)، لكن الحرف بقي.
When printing came to England (William Caxton, 1476), spellings slowly became fixed in books, while the vowel sounds of English kept changing for about 200 years. That is why many vowels look one way and sound another.عندما وصلت الطباعة إلى إنجلترا (ويليام كاكستون عام 1476) بدأت التهجئة تثبت في الكتب شيئاً فشيئاً، بينما استمرّت أصوات الحركات في التغيّر نحو 200 سنة. لهذا تبدو حركات كثيرة بشكل وتُنطق بشكل آخر.
The word 'the' was once written with an old letter called thorn (þ). Early printers had no þ in their type, so they used y, which looked a bit like it. That is why 'Ye Olde Shop' on old-style signs is really read 'The Old Shop'.كانت كلمة the تُكتب قديماً بحرف قديم اسمه «ثورن» (þ). ولم يكن هذا الحرف عند الطابعين الأوائل، فاستعملوا مكانه حرف y لأنه يشبهه قليلاً. لذلك فعبارة Ye Olde Shop على اللافتات ذات الطراز القديم تُقرأ في الحقيقة The Old Shop.
About 100 very common words make up roughly half of all the words we read in English, and many of them are tricky (the, was, said, you). Learn them by heart and you can read a lot!نحو 100 كلمة شائعة جداً تشكّل تقريباً نصف الكلمات التي نقرؤها بالإنجليزية، وكثير منها خادع (the و was و said و you). احفظها وستستطيع أن تقرأ الكثير!

1. Warm-up: Blend It or Not?

0–5 min
إحماء: هل تُدمج أم لا؟
Method: Retrieval of blending + discovery hook (a word that breaks the rules)
Purpose: Warm up blending from Bonus Class 3, then discover that some words do not work with blending.
Teacher says

“Blending Builders, welcome back! Arm out. Let's blend: h-o-t, hot! c-a-t, cat! r-u-n, run!”

“Now this one: s-a-i-d. Blend it: s-a-i-d... 'sa-id'? Hmm. Is that a word?”

“No! This word is said. It is a tricky word. It breaks the rules! Today we find out why, and how to beat tricky words.”

“And then we will learn the rules of a sentence, like a real writer.”

  • لنَدمج: hot و cat و run. والآن s-a-i-d... هل هذه كلمة؟
  • هذه كلمة said، وهي كلمة خادعة لا تتبع القواعد! اليوم نعرف السبب.
Students do
  • Arm-tap and blend 3 decodable words.
  • Try to blend said and notice that it doesn't work.
  • Guess which other words might be tricky.
Tips:
  • Make the failed blend funny ('sa-id? Is that a word? No!'), so children feel curious, not stuck. Some children may read said like the Arabic name سعيد: praise the logic, then show the heart part. Do not joke about anyone's name.

2. Linguistics Corner: Why Are Some Words Tricky?

5–14 min
ركن اللغويات: لماذا بعض الكلمات خادعة؟
Method: Storytelling with real language history + sorting (decodable vs tricky)
Purpose: Understand that tricky spellings are fossils of old English, and tell decodable words from tricky ones.
Teacher says

“Long, long ago, English people said almost every letter. Look at knife from Lesson 10. They said 'k-neef'! The k sound went away, but the letter stayed.”

“English spelling is like an old photo. Grandpa's photo shows him young, but today he looks different. The spelling shows how words sounded long ago!”

“When books were printed, spellings got stuck. But people's mouths kept changing.”

“Some old-style shop signs say 'Ye Olde Shop'. Ye? That is really the! The old letter þ (thorn) was printed as y.”

“Now be detectives: can we blend it, or is it tricky?”

  • قديماً كان الناس ينطقون كل حرف تقريباً. في كلمة knife كانوا ينطقون k! اختفى الصوت وبقي الحرف.
  • التهجئة مثل صورة قديمة لجدّك: تُظهر كيف كانت الكلمات تُنطق قديماً.
Students do
  • Say knife the old way and the new way.
  • Sort words on the slide: Blend it (green) or Tricky (red).
  • Show a thumbs-up (blend it) or a heart shape with hands (tricky).
Exercise · sort — Can you blend it? Thumbs-up for blend it, a hand heart for tricky.
هل تستطيع دمج أصواتها؟ ارفع إبهامك إذا استطعت، واصنع قلباً بيديك إذا كانت خادعة.
  1. cat → blend it
  2. said → tricky
  3. dog → blend it
  4. was → tricky
  5. the → tricky
  6. run → blend it
  7. you → tricky
  8. bed → blend it
Tips:
  • Keep the history to a story; the dates are for the teacher (Caxton 1476; the Great Vowel Shift, about 1400 to 1700).
  • Stress the good news: most English words can be blended. Only some are tricky, and they are tricky only in one small part.

3. Heart Words: Find the Tricky Part

14–24 min
كلمات القلب: اعثر على الجزء الخادع
Method: Heart Word method (mark regular parts, put a heart on the irregular part) + sound links to earlier Bonus Classes
Purpose: Learn the tricky words by seeing that most letters are regular and only one part must be learned by heart.
Teacher says

“Tricky words are not all tricky! Look at said. s: normal. d: normal. Only ai is tricky: it says Quick E! s-e-d. We put a heart over ai, because we learn it by heart.”

“was: w is normal. a is tricky: it says Round O, not Lazy A. And s says z, with a buzz! woz.”

“the: th is the sound ذ. Tongue between your teeth and buzz. e says a short, relaxed 'uh', like Grumpy U: 'thuh'.”

“to: t is normal. o says oo. he, she, we, me: e says its name, ee! go and no: o says its name.”

“my: y says 'eye'. I: always a capital, says its name. have: h-a-v normal, the e is silent. Fun fact: English words almost never end in v, so we add an e!”

“you: y is normal, like in yellow. ou is tricky: it says oo. Put the heart over ou.”

  • الكلمة الخادعة ليست كلها خادعة! في said: الحرفان s و d عاديان، والجزء الخادع فقط ai الذي يُنطق مثل E السريع. نضع قلباً فوقه لأننا نحفظه عن ظهر قلب.
  • th في the هي صوت الذال: اللسان بين الأسنان مع طنين.
Students do
  • Write each heart word on a card and draw a heart over the tricky part (in red if possible).
  • Read each word three times: point, say, write it in the air (sky-writing).
  • Say the with the tongue between the teeth in the mirror (ذ, not z or d).
Exercise · find-the-tricky-part — Which part is tricky? Draw a heart over it and say how it sounds.
أيّ جزء خادع؟ ارسم قلباً فوقه وقل كيف يُنطق.
  1. said → ai (says e: sed)
  2. was → a (says o) and s (says z): woz
  3. the → th (ذ sound) and e (says 'uh')
  4. to → o (says oo)
  5. he → e (says its name, ee)
  6. go → o (says its name, oh)
  7. my → y (says 'eye')
  8. have → e (silent)
  9. you → ou (says oo)
Tips:
  • 'was' sounds like 'woz' in British English and like 'wuz' (Grumpy U) in American English. Accept both; just not Lazy A.
  • Link to earlier Bonus Classes: s in was buzzes like z (Bonus 2 twins), ai in said is Quick E (Bonus 1).
  • Children who say 'ze' or 'de' for the: most Gulf dialects keep ذ in everyday speech; Egyptian children know it from Standard Arabic (fusha) at school and, for many, from Quran recitation. Point to that sound.

4. Tricky Word Games: Flash, Fill the Gap and Look-Say-Cover-Write

24–32 min
ألعاب الكلمات الخادعة: الوميض وأكمل الفراغ وانظر-قل-غطِّ-اكتب
Method: Retrieval practice games + Look-Say-Cover-Write-Check (spelling memory)
Purpose: Make tricky words automatic for reading and spelling through fast, repeated retrieval.
Teacher says

“Flash game! A word flashes on the screen for one second. Shout it out!”

“Now Look, Say, Cover, Write, Check. Look at said. Say it. Cover it with your hand. Write it. Check: did you get the heart part?”

“Fill the gap! Read the sentence and choose the tricky word.”

  • لعبة الوميض: تظهر الكلمة ثانية واحدة، قلها بسرعة!
  • انظر، قل، غطِّ، اكتب، ثم تحقّق: هل كتبت الجزء الخادع صحيحاً؟
Students do
  • Read flashed words as fast as possible.
  • Do Look-Say-Cover-Write-Check for said, was, the and you.
  • Complete gap sentences on the slide in the chat or on paper.
Exercise · fill-the-gap — Choose from the word bank and write the missing tricky word: the, was, said, you, to, he, my, have.
اختر من بنك الكلمات واكتب الكلمة الخادعة المفقودة: the أو was أو said أو you أو to أو he أو my أو have.
  1. I ___ a pet dog. → have
  2. Can ___ swim? Yes, I can. → you
  3. Yesterday the cat ___ in the box. → was
  4. Mom ___, "Sit down." → said
  5. ___ dog is big. (It is my dog.) → My
  6. I go ___ bed. → to
  7. Sam is my friend. ___ can run. → He
  8. ___ sun is hot. → The
Tips:
  • Note that three answers start a sentence (My, He, The): a perfect bridge to the capital-letter rule in the next stages.
  • Keep flash speed high but repeat the hardest words (said, was, you) more often.

5. Arabic Bridge: Capitals, ? and ؟

32–39 min
جسر العربية: الحروف الكبيرة و ? و ؟
Method: Contrastive analysis of writing systems (Arabic vs English text side by side)
Purpose: Show what English sentences need that Arabic does not (capitals) and what faces the other way (? and ,).
Teacher says

“Look at this Arabic sentence and this English sentence. What is the same? Both have spaces between words. Both have a dot at the end!”

“What is different? English has big letters and small letters. Arabic has no capital letters at all. But Arabic letters change shape by their place in the word: بـ ـبـ ـب.”

“Look at the question marks: ؟ and ?. They are mirror twins! Arabic goes right to left, so its question mark is flipped, like in a mirror. English goes left to right, so ? faces the other way.”

“Arabic has tricky words too! هذا: we say haadhaa, but the first long a is not written. الشمس: we write ل but we don't say it!”

  • ما المتشابه؟ المسافات بين الكلمات والنقطة في النهاية. ما المختلف؟ لا توجد حروف كبيرة في العربية.
  • علامتا الاستفهام ؟ و? توأمان في المرآة! وفي العربية أيضاً كلمات خادعة: هذا والشمس.
Students do
  • Find what is the same and what is different in the two sentences.
  • Draw ؟ and ? side by side on paper and show the camera.
  • Find the Arabic tricky part in هذا and الشمس.
Tips:
  • Many children write ؟ in English because their hand is used to it; the mirror-twins picture helps them remember.
  • Celebrate the shared rules (spaces, period): the child already knows half the sentence rules from Arabic.

6. Sentence Doctor: Capital, Space, Stop!

39–48 min
طبيب الجمل: حرف كبير، مسافة، توقّف!
Method: Kinesthetic punctuation (actions for each rule) + error correction (Sentence Doctor)
Purpose: Learn the four sentence rules with actions, then find and fix mistakes in sentences.
Teacher says

“Every sentence has rules. Let's do them with our bodies!”

“Capital letter: stand tall, arms up! Finger space: two fingers up between words. Period: stamp your foot or punch the air: stop! Question mark: shrug your shoulders and draw a hook in the air.”

“Let's read with actions: (stand tall) I have a cat (stamp)!”

“Now you are Sentence Doctors. These sentences are sick! Find what is wrong and make them better.”

“Remember: I is always a capital, even in the middle. Days and names start with a capital too: Monday, Sam.”

  • حرف كبير: قف منتصباً وارفع ذراعيك! مسافة: إصبعان بين الكلمات. نقطة: اضرب الأرض بقدمك. علامة استفهام: هزّ كتفيك وارسم خطافاً في الهواء.
  • أنتم أطباء الجمل: هذه الجمل مريضة، اعثروا على الخطأ وأصلحوه!
Students do
  • Do the four punctuation actions while the teacher reads sentences.
  • Find the mistake in each sick sentence and say what the cure is.
  • Write the corrected sentence and hold it up to the camera.
Exercise · sentence-doctor — This sentence is sick. Find the mistake and write it correctly.
هذه الجملة مريضة. اعثر على الخطأ واكتبها صحيحة.
  1. i have a cat. → I have a cat.
  2. Today is monday → Today is Monday.
  3. Can you swim؟ → Can you swim?
  4. the dog is big. → The dog is big.
  5. Mydogisinthebox. → My dog is in the box.
  6. Where is the ball. → Where is the ball?
  7. sam and i can run → Sam and I can run.
  8. I have a red hat? → I have a red hat.
Tips:
  • Ask 'What is the cure?' so children name the rule (capital, space, period, question mark), not just copy the right answer.
  • Level 1 questions usually start with Can, Where, What or How. Ask 'Is this sentence asking something?' If yes, it needs a question mark.

7. Writer's Challenge: My Three Sentences

48–55 min
تحدّي الكاتب: جملي الثلاث
Method: Guided writing (production) + peer checking with a rules checklist
Purpose: Write real sentences using tricky words and all four sentence rules, then check a friend's work.
Teacher says

“Now you are writers! Write three sentences about you.”

“Sentence 1 is about a pet: I have a... Sentence 2 is about the day: Today is... Sentence 3 is a question for a friend: Can you...?”

“Use the checklist: capital? spaces? period or question mark? tricky words spelled right?”

“Read your sentences to the group. Your friends give you a star for each rule you got right!”

  • أنتم الآن كتّاب! اكتبوا ثلاث جمل عن أنفسكم.
  • استعملوا قائمة التحقّق: حرف كبير؟ مسافات؟ نقطة أو علامة استفهام؟ الكلمات الخادعة صحيحة؟
Students do
  • Write three sentences using the frames.
  • Tick the checklist for each sentence.
  • Read the sentences aloud; friends answer the question and give stars.
Exercise · guided-writing — Finish each sentence with your own idea. Check the four rules.
أكمل كل جملة بفكرتك الخاصة، وتحقّق من القواعد الأربع.
  1. I have a ___ → e.g. I have a cat. (capital I, period)
  2. Today is ___ → e.g. Today is Monday. (capital for the day, period)
  3. Can you ___ → e.g. Can you swim? (question mark)
  4. My ___ is ___ → e.g. My dog is big. (capital M, period)
  5. Where is the ___ → e.g. Where is the ball? (question mark)
  6. He said, "___" → e.g. He said, "I can run." (tricky words he, said)
Tips:
  • Three sentences are enough for most children; items 4-6 are for fast finishers.
  • Peer stars should name the rule ('a star for your question mark'), so feedback teaches.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

55–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check tricky-word spelling and the sentence rules alone, and finish with a fact to share at home.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket! Write the words and the sentence I say.”

“Did you know? Some old-style signs in England say 'Ye Olde Shop', but ye is really the! The old letter thorn (þ) looked a bit like y, so early printers used y. English spelling is full of secrets from the past.”

“Your mission at home: be a Sentence Doctor. Find a sentence in a book and show your family the capital letter and the period.”

  • هل تعلم؟ بعض اللافتات ذات الطراز القديم في إنجلترا مكتوب عليها Ye Olde Shop، لكن ye هي في الحقيقة the! الحرف القديم þ كان يشبه y قليلاً.
  • مهمتك في البيت: كن طبيب جمل! ابحث عن جملة في كتاب وأرِ عائلتك الحرف الكبير والنقطة.
Students do
  • Write 3 tricky words and 2 sentences.
  • Say goodbye with the actions: stand tall, 'Bye!', stamp.
Tips:
  • Note who misses capitals or uses ؟; remind them in Lessons 17-19 writing tasks with the action gestures.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Items 1-3: listen and write the tricky word. Items 4-5: listen and write the sentence with a capital letter and the right end mark.
1-3: استمع واكتب الكلمة الخادعة. 4-5: استمع واكتب الجملة بحرف كبير وعلامة النهاية الصحيحة.
  1. said → said
  2. was → was
  3. you → you
  4. I have a dog. → I have a dog.
  5. Can you run? → Can you run?

Homework

Heart Word Hunt and Sentence Doctor: find the, was, said and you in a book or on a box at home and count how many times you see them. Then write three sentences about your family, with capitals, spaces and periods or question marks (one must be a question).

صيد كلمات القلب وطبيب الجمل: ابحث عن the و was و said و you في كتاب أو على علبة في البيت، وعُدّ كم مرة تراها. ثم اكتب ثلاث جمل عن عائلتك بحروف كبيرة ومسافات ونقاط أو علامات استفهام (واحدة منها على الأقل سؤال).

  • Make heart word cards for the, was, said, you, to, he, my, have and read them every day.
  • Do Look-Say-Cover-Write-Check for said and was.
  • Write three sentences about your family (one must be a question).

Support

  • Learn four heart words only (the, to, I, you) and add the rest in later warm-ups.
  • Give sentence frames with the capital letter and end mark already printed; the child fills the gap.
  • In Sentence Doctor, tell the child which rule is broken and ask them to fix it.

Challenge

  • Find the tricky part in more words: of (f says v!), are, do, come.
  • Write a sentence with a name, a day and the word I: 'On Monday, Sam and I can swim.'
  • Find a silent letter in a word from the lessons (knife: k) and explain why it is there.

Success criteria

  • Sorts at least 6 of 8 words correctly as blend it or tricky.
  • Marks the tricky part in at least 6 of 9 heart words.
  • Spells said, was and you correctly in the exit ticket.
  • Writes three sentences with capital letters, finger spaces and the correct end mark, and I always as a capital.

Watch for

  • tricky words spelled by sound (sed, woz, yu)
  • tricky words sounded out when reading (sa-id)
  • lowercase i
  • days without a capital
  • Arabic ؟ in English
  • no spaces between words
  • the said as ze or de

Teacher notes

  • This class closes the Sound Lab strand: Bonus Classes 1-3 showed how regular English is; this one shows where it is not regular and why.
  • Use the heart word idea for every new tricky word in Lessons 17-20: mark the regular parts, put a heart on the tricky part.
  • History facts are simplified for children but accurate: knife (late Old English cnīf, from Old Norse) kept a pronounced k until about the 1600s; William Caxton set up the first printing press in England in 1476; the Great Vowel Shift ran from about 1400 to 1700; early printers' type had no þ (thorn), so they used y, which looked like it in late handwriting. 'Ye' was always read 'the', and most 'Ye Olde' signs are modern imitations of an old style.
  • Keep the Arabic comparison respectful and balanced: Arabic has fewer tricky spellings than English, but it does have some (هذا، لكن، الشمس، عمرو).
  • Level 2 (Spelling Detectives) continues with digraphs such as sh, ch and th, including the two sounds of th (ث and ذ).
