Syllables, Stress and the Three Sounds of -s

المقاطع والنبر وأصوات -s الثلاثة
Big idea: English words are made of beats called syllables, and one beat is the boss: louder, longer and higher (BREAK-fast, ba-LOON). The plural -s also has rules: it hisses /s/ (books), buzzes /z/ (pens) or adds a whole beat /ɪz/ (boxes).
تتكوّن الكلمات الإنجليزية من نبضات تسمى المقاطع، وأحدها هو «الرئيس»: أعلى صوتاً وأطول وأحدّ نغمة (BREAK-fast و ba-LOON). وحرف -s في الجمع له قواعد أيضاً: إما يصفر /s/ (books)، أو يطنّ /z/ (pens)، أو يضيف مقطعاً كاملاً /ɪz/ (boxes).

Linguistics Corner — English has a heartbeat · للإنجليزية نبض قلب

In English, syllables are not equal. One syllable is stressed: it is louder, longer and higher. The others become small and quick.مقاطع الكلمة الإنجليزية ليست متساوية؛ واحد منها منبور: أعلى صوتاً وأطول وأحدّ نغمة، والباقي صغير وسريع.
Stress can change the meaning! A PREsent is a gift; to preSENT is to show something to people. Same letters, different boss.النبر قد يغيّر المعنى! PREsent تعني هدية، و preSENT تعني أن تعرض شيئاً على الناس. الحروف نفسها والرئيس مختلف.
Weak syllables often use the most common vowel sound in English: schwa, a lazy 'uh'. Listen to banana: buh-NA-nuh. Only the middle a is a real 'a'!المقاطع الضعيفة تستخدم غالباً أكثر صوت متحرك شيوعاً في الإنجليزية: «الشوا»، وهو «أَ» كسولة. استمع إلى banana: بَ-نا-نَه. الحرف a في الوسط فقط يُنطق كاملاً!
Arabic has stress too, but Arabic does not squash its weak syllables into a lazy 'uh' the way English does. Even Arabic dialects differ: many Egyptians (especially in Cairo) say mad-RA-sa and many Gulf speakers say MAD-ra-sa.في العربية نبر أيضاً، لكن العربية لا تضغط مقاطعها الضعيفة إلى «أَ» كسولة كما تفعل الإنجليزية. وحتى اللهجات تختلف: كثير من المصريين (خاصة في القاهرة) يقولون مَدْ-رَ-سة بالنبر على «رَ»، وكثير من أهل الخليج ينبرون «مَدْ».
The plural -s changes its sound by itself to match the last sound of the word: a hiss after quiet sounds (books), a buzz after buzzy sounds (pens), and an extra beat after hissing and hushing sounds (boxes, dishes, watches). Your mouth chooses the easiest way.يغيّر -s الجمع صوته تلقائياً ليناسب آخر صوت في الكلمة: صفير بعد الأصوات المهموسة (books)، وطنين بعد الأصوات المجهورة (pens)، ومقطع إضافي بعد أصوات الصفير (boxes). فمك يختار الطريقة الأسهل.

1. Warm-up: Robot or Human?

0–5 min
إحماء: روبوت أم إنسان؟
Method: Listening hook with role-play voices
Purpose: Make children notice that natural English has strong and weak beats.
Teacher says

“Detectives, listen to two speakers. Speaker 1: 'I - HAVE - BREAK - FAST - AT - SE - VEN.' Speaker 2: 'I have BREAKfast at SEVen.'”

“Which one is a robot? Which one is a human? Why?”

“The human has a heartbeat: some beats are big, some are small. Today we find the heartbeat of English!”

  • أيهما روبوت وأيهما إنسان؟ ولماذا؟
  • الإنسان له نبض: بعض المقاطع كبيرة وبعضها صغير. اليوم نكتشف نبض الإنجليزية!
Students do
  • Vote robot or human for 3 pairs of sentences.
  • Copy the robot voice, then the human voice, for 'I have breakfast at seven.'
Tips:
  • Make the robot voice very flat and funny; children love imitating it.
  • Use sentences from Lessons 13-16 so the words are familiar.

2. Syllable Beats: Clap, Chin, Count

5–13 min
نبضات المقاطع: صفّق والمس ذقنك وعُدّ
Method: Multisensory syllable counting (clapping + hand under the chin)
Purpose: Count syllables reliably: every syllable has one vowel sound, and the jaw drops once per syllable.
Teacher says

“Remember the Clap Test from Bonus Class 2? Now we use it to count beats.”

“Second trick: put your hand under your chin. Say break-fast. Your chin drops two times: two syllables!”

“Careful, detectives: cake has a silent e. Does the silent e make a beat? No! cake = 1.”

“Every syllable has one vowel SOUND - not one vowel letter. tree has two e letters, but one sound: one beat.”

  • ضع يدك تحت ذقنك: كلما نزل الذقن فهذا مقطع.
  • e الصامتة لا تصنع مقطعاً: cake مقطع واحد.
Students do
  • Clap and feel the chin for each word.
  • Show the number of syllables with fingers.
  • Write the word split into beats (break / fast).
Exercise · count-syllables — Clap the word and feel your chin. How many syllables?
صفّق للكلمة والمس ذقنك. كم مقطعاً فيها؟
  1. cake → 1
  2. lunch → 1
  3. dinner → 2 (din-ner)
  4. breakfast → 2 (break-fast)
  5. balloon → 2 (ba-loon)
  6. homework → 2 (home-work)
  7. toothbrush → 2 (tooth-brush)
  8. pajamas → 3 (pa-ja-mas)
  9. afternoon → 3 (af-ter-noon)
  10. November → 3 (No-vem-ber)
Tips:
  • Children often count letters or vowel letters; keep asking 'how many times did your chin drop?'
  • Breakfast is said 'brek-fust': the spelling 'break' shows its origin (breaking the night's fast) - a nice fact for curious children.

3. Linguistics Corner: The Boss Syllable

13–21 min
ركن اللغويات: المقطع الرئيس
Method: Guided discovery with exaggeration (louder, longer, higher) + meaning contrast
Purpose: Understand what stress is, that it can change meaning, and that weak syllables become a lazy 'uh'.
Teacher says

“In every English word with 2 or more syllables, one syllable is the BOSS. The boss is louder, longer and higher: BIRTH-day.”

“Watch my hand: it goes up on the boss. BIRTH-day. ba-LOON. Can you hear it?”

“Big secret: the boss can change the meaning! For your birthday you get a PREsent. In class, you preSENT your poster.”

“And the small syllables get lazy. Listen: banana. buh-NA-nuh. The lazy sound 'uh' is the most common vowel in English! It is called schwa.”

“Arabic has stress too. Many Egyptians say mad-RA-sa; many people in the Gulf say MAD-ra-sa. Same word, different boss!”

  • في كل كلمة مقطع رئيس: أعلى صوتاً وأطول وأحدّ نغمة.
  • الرئيس قد يغيّر المعنى: PREsent هدية، و preSENT أي يعرض.
  • حتى في العربية: كثير من المصريين يقولون «مَدْرَسة» بالنبر على «رَ»، وكثير من أهل الخليج ينبرون «مَدْ».
Students do
  • Say BIRTH-day three ways: loud boss, long boss, high boss.
  • Raise a hand on the boss syllable while saying balloon, candle, present.
  • Say banana with a lazy 'uh' on the first and last syllables.
  • Say madrasa the Egyptian way and the Gulf way and decide which one their family uses.
Tips:
  • Exaggerate everything: big body movements help children feel stress.
  • Do not insist on the word 'schwa'; 'lazy uh' is enough. Curious children can learn the name.

4. Stress Detective: Find the Boss

21–31 min
محقق النبر: جِد الرئيس
Method: Stress-pattern matching with big and small circles (O o) + minimal stress pairs
Purpose: Identify the stressed syllable in familiar words and hear thirteen vs thirty.
Teacher says

“Each word has a stress pattern. A big O is the boss, a small o is a weak beat. BREAKfast is big-small: Oo. baLOON is small-big: oO.”

“Show me your big O card when you hear the boss!”

“Now the most important pair in money and time: thirTEEN and THIRty. Thirteen: small-big. Thirty: big-small. If you say them the same, you might pay 30 instead of 13!”

  • الدائرة الكبيرة O هي الرئيس، والصغيرة o مقطع ضعيف.
  • انتبه: thirTEEN صغير ثم كبير، و THIRty كبير ثم صغير. لو نطقتهما بالطريقة نفسها قد تدفع 30 بدلاً من 13!
Students do
  • Say each word and choose its pattern: Oo, oO, Ooo, oOo or ooO.
  • Hold up the big O card on the boss syllable.
  • Thirteen or thirty? Write the number the teacher says (13-19 or 30-90).
Exercise · stress-pattern — Say the word. Where is the boss? Choose the pattern.
انطق الكلمة: أين المقطع الرئيس؟ اختر النمط.
  1. breakfast → Oo (BREAK-fast)
  2. balloon → oO (ba-LOON)
  3. homework → Oo (HOME-work)
  4. o'clock → oO (o-CLOCK)
  5. candle → Oo (CAN-dle)
  6. pajamas → oOo (pa-JA-mas)
  7. afternoon → ooO (af-ter-NOON)
  8. midnight → Oo (MID-night)
  9. September → oOo (sep-TEM-ber)
  10. thirteen → oO (thir-TEEN)
  11. thirty → Oo (THIR-ty)
  12. Listen and write: 'fifteen' / 'fifty' → 15 / 50
Tips:
  • Most two-syllable English nouns are Oo; balloon is a good exception to notice, and o'clock (not a noun) is oO too.
  • In fast speech, thirteen can move its stress (THIRteen MINutes); teach the clear dictionary stress here.
  • Pair this with Lesson 13 times: 'It's thirteen minutes past three' vs 'It's three thirty'.

5. Arabic Bridge: How Arabic and English Make Plurals

31–37 min
جسر العربية: كيف تصنع العربية والإنجليزية الجمع
Method: Contrastive analysis with Arabic plurals (sound, broken, dual) + number check
Purpose: Prepare the plural -s by comparing it with Arabic plurals the children know well.
Teacher says

“In Arabic, how do we make plurals? سيارة -> سيارات: we add an ending. كتاب -> كُتُب: we change the inside!”

“English mostly adds an ending: one book, two books. Like سيارات.”

“But a few English words change inside, like Arabic broken plurals: man -> men, foot -> feet, child -> children.”

“And Arabic has كتابان for two. English has no dual: two books. And after twenty? Arabic says عشرون كتاباً - singular! English says twenty books - plural. With every number from 2 up, use the plural!”

  • الإنجليزية تضيف نهاية غالباً مثل جمع المؤنث السالم، وقليل من كلماتها يتغيّر من الداخل مثل جمع التكسير.
  • لا يوجد مثنّى في الإنجليزية، ومع كل عدد من 2 فأكثر نستخدم الجمع: twenty books.
Students do
  • Give one Arabic sound plural and one broken plural.
  • Match man/men, foot/feet, child/children to the idea of broken plurals.
  • Correct three sentences: 'two pen', 'twenty book', 'three clock'.
Tips:
  • Children enjoy discovering that English also has 'broken plurals'; Lesson 19 teaches them properly.
  • The number rule is one of the most frequent errors of Arabic speakers; spend time on it.

6. The Three Sounds of -s: Snake, Bee and Bus

37–49 min
أصوات -s الثلاثة: الثعبان والنحلة والحافلة
Method: Guided discovery with hand on throat + character mnemonics + sound sort
Purpose: Discover that plural -s is said /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/ depending on the last sound of the word.
Teacher says

“Hand on your throat. Say book. Last sound: k. Buzz? No! So -s is Sammy the Snake: books - sss.”

“Say pen. Last sound: n. Buzz? Yes! So -s is Buzzy the Bee: pens - zzz.”

“Now say box. It ends with a hiss: k-s. Can you say box-s? Too hard! So English adds a beat: box-es. That is the Big Bus: buses, boxes, watches. Clap: box (1), box-es (2)!”

“Detective rule: words ending in s, x, z, sh, ch get -es and one extra beat. That's why we write the e!”

“Bonus Class 3 trap: cakes. Does cake end in e? Look at the SOUND: k. No buzz. So cakes = sss, one beat!”

  • المس حلقك: إذا كان آخر صوت بلا اهتزاز فـ -s ثعبان /s/، وإذا كان مع اهتزاز فهي نحلة /z/.
  • الكلمات التي تنتهي بـ s أو x أو z أو sh أو ch نضيف لها -es ومقطعاً كاملاً: هذه هي الحافلة /ɪz/.
  • انتبه: نحكم على آخر صوت وليس آخر حرف، فـ cakes تُنطق /s/.
Students do
  • Say each singular word with a hand on the throat and decide buzz or no buzz.
  • Show the S, Z or IZ card for each plural.
  • Clap the singular and the plural: does the plural have an extra beat?
Exercise · sort — Say the plural. Snake /s/, Bee /z/ or Bus /ɪz/? Show your card.
انطق الجمع: ثعبان /s/ أم نحلة /z/ أم حافلة /ɪz/؟ ارفع البطاقة.
  1. books → /s/ (k = no buzz)
  2. dogs → /z/ (g = buzz)
  3. boxes → /ɪz/ (ends in x)
  4. clocks → /s/
  5. pens → /z/
  6. watches → /ɪz/ (ends in ch)
  7. cakes → /s/ (last sound is k; the e is silent)
  8. balloons → /z/
  9. buses → /ɪz/ (ends in s)
  10. minutes → /s/ (t = no buzz)
  11. candles → /z/ (l = buzz)
  12. oranges → /ɪz/ (ends in the j sound; spelled -s because there is already an e)
Tips:
  • Words ending in a vowel sound always take /z/: days, keys, trees, because vowels buzz.
  • The /s/ vs /z/ difference is small and native listeners usually forgive it; the /ɪz/ beat matters most for being understood. Prioritize it.
  • This is the same rule children will meet again for verbs (he watches, she plays) in later levels.

7. Plural Machine and My Desk Count

49–55 min
آلة الجمع وعدّ أشياء مكتبي
Method: Game (transformation race) + task-based speaking with real objects
Purpose: Produce plurals with the right sound and beat in real sentences, with peer checking.
Teacher says

“The Plural Machine! I put in one word; you make it plural, say the sound - snake, bee or bus - and clap the beats.”

“Now look at your desk. Count your things: 'I have got two pens, three books and two boxes.'”

“Partners: listen for the -s. Did you hear a snake, a bee or a bus?”

  • حوّل الكلمة إلى جمع، وقل صوتها (ثعبان أو نحلة أو حافلة)، وصفّق لمقاطعها.
  • عُدّ الأشياء على مكتبك: I have got two pens...
Students do
  • Race to transform each word (one child per word, rotating).
  • Say 2 sentences about real objects on their desk with numbers and plurals.
  • Partners name the -s sound they heard.
Exercise · word-transformation — Make it plural. Which sound? How many beats?
حوّل الكلمة إلى جمع: أيّ صوت؟ وكم مقطعاً؟
  1. one box -> two ... → boxes, /ɪz/, 2 beats
  2. one cat -> two ... → cats, /s/, 1 beat
  3. one key -> two ... → keys, /z/, 1 beat
  4. one dish -> two ... → dishes, /ɪz/, 2 beats
  5. one snack -> two ... → snacks, /s/, 1 beat
  6. one lunch -> two ... → lunches, /ɪz/, 2 beats
  7. one bag -> two ... → bags, /z/, 1 beat
  8. one glass -> two ... → glasses, /ɪz/, 2 beats
Tips:
  • Keep the race light: accuracy of the beat counts more than speed.
  • If children have few objects, use the slide picture of a school bag instead.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

55–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check (stress + plural sound) + curiosity close + level reflection
Purpose: Check stress and plural sounds, and celebrate the four Spelling Detective cases of Level 2.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five quick questions about the boss syllable and the -s sound.”

“Did you know? The lazy 'uh' sound, schwa, is the most common vowel sound in English - and it has no letter of its own!”

“Detectives, you solved four cases this level: digraphs, clusters, long vowels and stress. Case closed - well done!”

  • هل تعلم؟ صوت «أَ» الكسول (الشوا) هو أكثر صوت متحرك في الإنجليزية، وليس له حرف خاص به!
  • حللتم أربع قضايا في هذا المستوى: الحروف المزدوجة والتجمّعات والأصوات الطويلة والنبر. أُغلقت القضية، أحسنتم!
Students do
  • Answer 5 exit-ticket items on paper.
  • Name their favorite 'case' of the four Bonus Classes.
Tips:
  • Note who confuses thirteen/thirty and who never adds the /ɪz/ beat; revisit in Lesson 19 (plurals).

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer quickly: find the boss syllable or the -s sound.
أجب بسرعة: جِد المقطع الرئيس أو صوت -s.
  1. Which syllable is the boss in 'balloon'? → the 2nd (ba-LOON)
  2. How many syllables in 'afternoon'? → 3
  3. Listen: thirTEEN or THIRty? (teacher says 'thirty') → thirty (30)
  4. The -s in 'pens': snake, bee or bus? → bee /z/
  5. The -es in 'boxes': snake, bee or bus? → bus /ɪz/

Homework

Stress and Plural Detective: choose 6 words from your daily routine (breakfast, homework, toothbrush...) and draw their stress pattern with big and small circles. Then count 6 kinds of things at home and write sentences with plurals (I have got two boxes). Mark each -s as S, Z or IZ.

محقق النبر والجمع: اختر 6 كلمات من روتينك اليومي (breakfast و homework و toothbrush...) وارسم نمط النبر بدوائر كبيرة وصغيرة. ثم عُدّ 6 أنواع من الأشياء في البيت واكتب جملاً بالجمع (I have got two boxes)، وضع على كل -s علامة S أو Z أو IZ.

  • Draw stress patterns for 6 routine words.
  • Write 6 plural sentences and mark the -s sound.
  • Say 'thirteen' and 'thirty' to a family member and see if they can hear the difference.

Support

  • Count syllables only with 1- and 2-syllable words first.
  • For plurals, start with just two groups: 'extra beat' (boxes) and 'no extra beat' (books, pens).
  • Give the stress pattern and let the child match the word to it.

Challenge

  • Find more stress pairs that change meaning: REcord / reCORD, DEsert / deSERT.
  • Find words with 4 syllables and their boss: watermelon (WA-ter-mel-on), television (TEL-e-vi-sion).
  • Explain to the group why we write -es in boxes but only -s in books.

Success criteria

  • Counts syllables correctly in at least 8 of 10 words.
  • Identifies the stress pattern in at least 8 of 12 words.
  • Says thirteen and thirty with clearly different stress.
  • Sorts at least 9 of 12 plurals into /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/ and always adds the /ɪz/ beat after s, x, sh, ch.

Watch for

  • equal stress on every syllable (robot speech)
  • thirteen/thirty confusion
  • the silent e read as an extra beat (cak-es)
  • missing plural -s after numbers (two pen)
  • no extra beat in boxes, watches, buses

Teacher notes

  • Technical rule for the teacher: plural -s is /ɪz/ after sibilants (s, z, sh, ch, j sound, zh), /s/ after other voiceless sounds (p, t, k, f, th), and /z/ after other voiced sounds and all vowels.
  • Stress = more loudness and length, a pitch change (usually higher in a word said alone), plus full vowel quality. Unstressed vowels often reduce to schwa /ə/.
  • Plurals are taught as grammar in Lesson 19; this class gives them the sound system first. In Lesson 19 recall Sammy the Snake, Buzzy the Bee and the Big Bus.
  • The same three-sound rule returns in Level 4 Bonus Class 1 (he/she/it -s) and the -ed version in Level 5 Bonus Class 1.
  • This is the last Bonus Class of Level 2: end with a short celebration of the four 'cases' before Lessons 17-20.
  • Syllabic consonants: in candle (CAN-dle) the second beat has no clear vowel; the l itself makes the beat. If a child asks, say the l 'acts like a vowel' here.
