Word Builders: Roots, Prefixes and Suffixes

بُناة الكلمات: الجذور والسوابق واللواحق
Big idea: English builds most new words like Lego, by adding pieces to the front and back of a word (happy → unhappy → unhappiness); Arabic weaves vowels and extra letters around a three-letter root (ك ت ب → كاتب، مكتب، مكتبة). Know the pieces and you can understand words you have never seen.
تبني الإنجليزية معظم كلماتها الجديدة مثل الليغو، بإضافة قطع إلى أول الكلمة وآخرها (happy ← unhappy ← unhappiness)، أما العربية فتنسج الحركات وحروفاً زائدة حول جذر ثلاثي (ك ت ب ← كاتب، مكتب، مكتبة). اعرف القطع تفهم كلمات لم ترها من قبل.

Linguistics Corner — Lego languages and weaving languages · لغات الليغو ولغات النسيج

A morpheme is the smallest piece of meaning. 'Unhelpful' has three: un (not) + help + ful (full of). 'Cat' has one. 'Cats' has two (cat + plural -s).المورفيم أصغر قطعة لها معنى. كلمة unhelpful فيها ثلاث: un (ليس) + help + ful (مليء بـ). و cat فيها واحدة، و cats فيها اثنتان (cat + -s الجمع).
English is mostly concatenative: it glues pieces in a line, like Lego bricks. The base usually stays visible: teach → teacher, kind → kindness, happy → unhappiness.الإنجليزية في الغالب «إلصاقية»: تلصق القطع في خط واحد كقطع الليغو، ويبقى الأصل ظاهراً عادة: teach ← teacher و kind ← kindness.
Arabic is famous for non-concatenative morphology: a root of (usually) three consonants carries the core idea, and patterns weave vowels and extra letters through it. ك-ت-ب (writing): كَتَبَ wrote, كاتِب writer, مَكْتوب written, مَكْتَب desk/office, مَكْتَبة library, كِتاب book.تشتهر العربية بالصرف «غير الخطّي»: جذر من ثلاثة حروف غالباً يحمل الفكرة الأساسية، والأوزان تنسج الحركات وحروفاً زائدة خلاله. ك-ت-ب: كَتَبَ، كاتِب، مَكْتوب، مَكْتَب، مَكْتَبة، كِتاب.
Arabic patterns carry meaning like English suffixes do: فاعِل = the doer (كاتب، لاعب) ≈ English -er (writer, player); مَفْعول = the thing done (مكتوب) ≈ -ed/-en (written); مَفْعَل / مَفْعَلة = a place (ملعب، مدرسة, مكتبة).الأوزان العربية تحمل معنى كما تحمله اللواحق الإنجليزية: فاعِل للفاعل ≈ -er، ومَفْعول لما وقع عليه الفعل ≈ -ed/-en، ومَفْعَل ومَفْعَلة للمكان (ملعب، مدرسة، مكتبة).
Neither language is 100% one type. Arabic also adds pieces (ال، ـون، ـة، سـ), and English also changes vowels inside words (sing → sang, man → men, foot → feet), a small echo of the Arabic way.لا توجد لغة من نوع واحد تماماً: العربية تضيف قطعاً أيضاً (ال، ـون، ـة، سـ)، والإنجليزية تغيّر الحركات داخل بعض الكلمات (sing ← sang و man ← men)، وهذا صدى صغير للطريقة العربية.
One suffix can have two jobs: -er makes comparatives (bigger) and also people who do things (teacher, player). Same spelling, different morphemes. And -s has three jobs: plural, he/she/it, and possessive 's.قد تكون للاحقة الواحدة وظيفتان: -er للمقارنة (bigger) ولمن يقوم بالفعل (teacher). الكتابة واحدة والمورفيم مختلف. و-s لها ثلاث وظائف: الجمع، و he/she/it، والملكية 's.

1. Warm-up: Word Explosion

0–5 min
إحماء: انفجار الكلمات
Method: Brainstorm race (activating word-family knowledge)
Purpose: Show that one small word can grow into a big family, and get teens competing from minute one.
Teacher says

“One word on the screen: HELP. In 60 seconds, how many words can you make from it? Go!”

“Let's see... helpful, helpless, helper, helping, helped, unhelpful! Six words from one!”

“How did you do it? You added pieces. Today you become Word Builders: you'll learn the pieces and how to use them.”

  • كم كلمة تستطيع أن تصنع من help في 60 ثانية؟
  • صنعتم كلمات جديدة بإضافة قطع. اليوم ستصبحون بُناة كلمات!
Students do
  • Type all the words they can build from help in the chat in 60 seconds.
  • Count: the student with the most correct words gets the title 'Master Builder'.
Tips:
  • Accept inflections (helps, helped, helping) but point out they are 'grammar pieces' while helpful/helper are 'new-word pieces'.
  • If the group is slow, give the first two pieces as hints: -ful, -er.

2. Discovery: Morpheme Surgery

5–15 min
اكتشاف: جراحة الكلمات
Method: Guided discovery (segmenting words into morphemes and inferring meanings)
Purpose: Students learn the term morpheme, split real words into pieces and work out what each prefix and suffix means.
Teacher says

“Put on your surgeon's gloves! We're going to cut words into their smallest meaningful pieces. Linguists call them morphemes.”

“Example: unhelpful. un + help + ful. Three morphemes. What does un mean? Not. What does ful mean? Full of. So unhelpful = not full of help!”

“Careful: you can't cut 'under' into un + der. Der means nothing. A morpheme must have a meaning.”

“Now you operate. Cut each word and tell me the meaning of each piece.”

  • المورفيم أصغر قطعة في الكلمة لها معنى.
  • unhelpful = un (ليس) + help (مساعدة) + ful (مليء بـ).
  • لا نقطع إلا إذا كان لكل قطعة معنى: under ليست un + der.
Students do
  • Draw slashes between morphemes on paper (un/help/ful).
  • Write the meaning of each prefix and suffix in a table: un- = not, re- = again, -ful = full of, -less = without, -ness = state of, -er = person who / more.
  • Check answers with a partner and explain one in Arabic.
Exercise · segment — Cut the word into morphemes. How many pieces? What does each piece mean?
قسّم الكلمة إلى مورفيمات. كم قطعة؟ وما معنى كل قطعة؟
  1. unhappiness → un + happi + ness (3): not + happy + state of
  2. teachers → teach + er + s (3): teach + person who + plural
  3. careless → care + less (2): care + without
  4. rewrite → re + write (2): again + write
  5. unpopular → un + popular (2): not + popular
  6. suggestion → suggest + ion (2): suggest + noun (the thing suggested)
  7. improvement → improve + ment (2): improve + noun (the result)
  8. biggest → big + est (2, with double g): big + the most
  9. players → play + er + s (3): play + person who + plural
  10. kindness → kind + ness (2): kind + state of
Tips:
  • Accept 'happi' as happy with y → i: spelling changes don't change the morpheme.
  • Ask 'Is it a new word or a grammar form?' to separate -er (teacher) from -s (plural).

3. Linguistics Corner + Arabic Bridge: Lego vs Weaving

15–25 min
ركن اللغويات وجسر العربية: الليغو مقابل النسيج
Method: Contrastive analysis using students' Arabic expertise (students teach the teacher)
Purpose: Compare English affixation with Arabic root-and-pattern morphology and show that students already have strong morphological skills from Arabic.
Teacher says

“English builds words like Lego: you see the base, and you glue bricks to the front and back. happy, unhappy, unhappiness.”

“Arabic is a weaving language. You are the experts here! What do these words share: كاتب، مكتوب، مكتب، مكتبة، كتاب، كَتَبَ?”

“Yes, ك ت ب, the idea of writing. The root letters stay in order, and the pattern weaves vowels and letters between and around them.”

“And patterns have meanings, like suffixes! فاعِل: the person who does it. كاتب = writer, لاعب = player. Look: Arabic فاعِل is like English -er!”

“مَفْعَل and مَفْعَلة: a place. ملعب, مدرسة, مكتبة. English doesn't have a place suffix: it says playground, school, library.”

“So Arabic speakers are already great at finding roots. Use that skill in English: find the base inside the long word!”

  • أنتم الخبراء هنا! ما المشترك بين كاتب ومكتوب ومكتب ومكتبة وكتاب؟ الجذر ك ت ب.
  • وزن فاعِل يشبه اللاحقة -er، ووزن مَفْعَل يدل على المكان.
  • مهارتكم في استخراج الجذر في العربية تساعدكم على إيجاد الأصل في الكلمات الإنجليزية الطويلة.
Students do
  • Find the root of each Arabic word and give the English equivalent.
  • Say which English piece (if any) does the same job as the Arabic pattern.
  • Build the ل-ع-ب family together on the whiteboard (لعب، لاعب، ملعب، لعبة، ألعاب).
Exercise · root-finder — Find the Arabic root. Then give the English word and say how English builds it.
استخرج الجذر العربي، ثم اذكر الكلمة الإنجليزية وكيف تبنيها الإنجليزية.
  1. كاتِب → ك ت ب → writer (write + -er)
  2. لاعِب → ل ع ب → player (play + -er)
  3. مَلْعَب → ل ع ب → playground (compound: play + ground)
  4. مُدَرِّس → د ر س → teacher (teach + -er)
  5. مَكْتوب → ك ت ب → written (write + -en, with a vowel change)
  6. مَكْتَبة → ك ت ب → library (a different word, not built from write)
  7. نَصيحة → ن ص ح → advice (a different word; the verb is advise)
  8. حَكَم → ح ك م → judge (Lesson 10; the same form is a verb and a noun in English: to judge / a judge)
Tips:
  • Let students lead the Arabic part; teens love being the experts.
  • Be honest: Arabic also has prefixes and suffixes (ال، ـون، ـات), and English has a few inside changes (sing / sang, man / men). The difference is in what each language does most.
  • Some students may know مُدَرِّس has an extra shadda pattern (فعّل): accept and praise.

4. Affix Lab: Meaning and Word Class

25–33 min
مختبر السوابق واللواحق: المعنى ونوع الكلمة
Method: Concept table + gap-fill with word families
Purpose: Understand that prefixes change meaning while most suffixes also change the word class (verb → noun, noun → adjective), then use the right form in a sentence.
Teacher says

“Prefixes change the meaning: happy → unhappy, write → rewrite. The word class stays the same.”

“Suffixes often change the job of the word. -ful and -less make adjectives: helpful, careless. -ness, -ion and -ment make nouns: kindness, suggestion, improvement. -er makes a person: teacher.”

“So when you choose a form, ask: what job does the sentence need here? A noun? An adjective? A verb?”

“Let's try with Lesson 12 words: improve, suggest, patience.”

  • السوابق تغيّر المعنى، أما كثير من اللواحق فتغيّر نوع الكلمة: فعل ← اسم، اسم ← صفة.
  • اسأل نفسك: ما الوظيفة المطلوبة هنا؟ اسم أم صفة أم فعل؟
Students do
  • Complete a table: verb / noun / adjective for help, care, use, suggest, improve.
  • Fill each gap with the right form of the word in brackets.
  • Say why: 'We need an adjective before the noun.'
Exercise · word-form-gap-fill — Use the word in brackets in the correct form. Add a prefix or suffix.
استخدم الكلمة بين القوسين بالشكل الصحيح، وأضف سابقة أو لاحقة.
  1. Thanks for the tip. It's really (use). → useful
  2. Ziad made a (care) mistake on his test. → careless
  3. The waiter made a great (suggest): try the dessert! → suggestion
  4. I can see a big (improve) in your writing. → improvement
  5. Your essay has mistakes. Please (write) it. → rewrite
  6. Noor is never angry. Her (kind) is famous. → kindness
  7. Hamad hates waiting. He is very (patient). → impatient
  8. The judge said the song was (popular) with young people. Nobody liked it. → unpopular
  9. Omar is a fantastic football (play). → player
  10. Without a map we felt totally (help). → helpless
Tips:
  • impatient uses im- because patient starts with p (im- before p, b, m: impossible, imbalance). Mention briefly; don't overload.
  • Highlight that -ful has one l (useful), even though full has two: a classic spelling trap.

5. Word-Building Race

33–42 min
سباق بناء الكلمات
Method: Team race with morpheme cards (gamified production)
Purpose: Build words fast from meanings, combining bases and affixes, under friendly time pressure.
Teacher says

“Race time! Team Sara versus Team Omar. I give a meaning; you build the word from the cards and type it.”

“Example: 'a person who judges a talent show'... judge! Careful, some words don't need a suffix!”

“First correct spelling wins 1 point. Wrong spelling? The other team can steal.”

  • أعطيكم المعنى وأنتم تبنون الكلمة من البطاقات وتكتبونها.
  • الإملاء الصحيح الأسرع يربح النقطة.
Students do
  • Combine cards on the whiteboard or on paper and hold them up to the camera.
  • Type the full word in the chat.
  • Use 3 of the new words in one silly sentence for a bonus point.
Exercise · build-the-word — Read the meaning. Build the word with a base + prefix/suffix.
اقرأ المعنى وابنِ الكلمة من الأصل مع سابقة أو لاحقة.
  1. not kind → unkind
  2. read again → reread
  3. a person who teaches → teacher
  4. full of care → careful
  5. without hope → hopeless
  6. the state of being sad → sadness
  7. the thing you recommend → recommendation
  8. a person who sings → singer
  9. not happy, and the noun of it → unhappiness
  10. having talent (adjective from Lesson 10) → talented
  11. the result of improving → improvement
  12. do it again: (try → ...) → retry
Tips:
  • talented uses -ed, not -ful: a nice surprise that shows English is not 100% regular.
  • recommendation: the stress moves (reCOMmend → recommenDAtion). Say it with the class; suffixes can move stress.

6. Spelling Surgeon: -er and -est

42–50 min
جرّاح الإملاء: -er و-est
Method: Rule discovery from examples + sorting + error check
Purpose: Apply the three spelling rules when adding -er/-est (doubling, y → i, drop e) and know the irregular 'good, better, best'.
Teacher says

“When we glue -er and -est, sometimes the word changes shape. Look: big → biggest, happy → happiest, nice → nicest. What happened?”

“Rule 1: one short vowel + one consonant at the end → double it: big, biggest; hot, hottest.”

“Rule 2: consonant + y → change y to i: happy, happiest; funny, funniest.”

“Rule 3: silent e at the end → just add -r or -st: nice, nicest; large, largest.”

“And the rebels: good, better, best; bad, worse, worst. They come from completely different old words. Linguists call this suppletion. Arabic has a rebel too: خير/أفضل!”

  • قاعدة 1: حرف علة قصير + ساكن واحد ← نضاعف الساكن.
  • قاعدة 2: ساكن + y ← نحوّل y إلى i.
  • قاعدة 3: e صامتة في الآخر ← نضيف -r أو -st فقط.
  • good و bad شاذتان لأن أشكالهما جاءت من كلمات قديمة مختلفة.
Students do
  • Write the -er and -est forms and name the rule (1, 2, 3 or rebel).
  • Spot the misspelled superlatives on the 'talent show results' slide.
Exercise · spell-and-rule — Write the superlative (the ___est). Which rule did you use?
اكتب صيغة التفضيل العليا. أي قاعدة استخدمت؟
  1. big → the biggest (rule 1: double)
  2. happy → the happiest (rule 2: y → i)
  3. nice → the nicest (rule 3: drop e)
  4. hot → the hottest (rule 1)
  5. funny → the funniest (rule 2)
  6. large → the largest (rule 3)
  7. good → the best (rebel)
  8. cheap (Lesson 9) → the cheapest (no change: two vowels before p)
  9. popular → the most popular (long adjective: most, not -est)
Tips:
  • cheap does not double the p because it has two vowel letters (ea) before it: a good test of rule 1.
  • Remind students that long adjectives (popular, talented, impressive) use most: the -est suffix is only for short words.

7. Production: Talent Show Judges

50–56 min
إنتاج: لجنة حكّام برنامج المواهب
Method: Creative role-play (task with a word-building checklist)
Purpose: Use built words naturally in speech: judges give comments and advice using affixed words and superlatives.
Teacher says

“You are the judges of the Lumio Talent Show! Each of you writes a 4-sentence comment for an act: Lumi's dance, Ziad's magic trick or Sara's song.”

“Your comment must use: one un- word, one -ful or -less word, one -ness or -ment word, and one superlative. And some advice with should!”

“Example: 'Lumi, that was the funniest dance tonight! You were fearless. You should work on your timing. I can see a big improvement since last week.'”

  • أنتم حكّام برنامج المواهب! اكتبوا تعليقاً من 4 جمل فيه: كلمة بـ un-، وكلمة بـ -ful أو -less، وكلمة بـ -ness أو -ment، وصيغة تفضيل، ونصيحة بـ should.
Students do
  • Write a 4-sentence judge's comment with the checklist.
  • Read it aloud dramatically; the others tick the checklist and award applause points.
  • Vote for the most creative comment.
Tips:
  • Write the checklist on the slide and let students tick their own words before speaking.
  • Encourage kind feedback: the judges should be helpful, not hurtful (a nice -ful pair!).

8. Exit Ticket and Did You Know?

56–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check segmenting, affix meaning, spelling and the English/Arabic contrast individually.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five items, privately to me in the chat.”

“Did you know? One of the longest words in English dictionaries, 'antidisestablishmentarianism', is just Lego: anti + dis + establish + ment + arian + ism. And Arabic can pack a whole sentence into one word: وسيكتبونها (and they will write it) = و + سـ + يكتب + ون + ها, built on our old friend ك ت ب!”

“Mission: find a long English word this week and cut it into morphemes.”

  • هل تعلم؟ من أطول الكلمات الإنجليزية antidisestablishmentarianism، وهي مجرد قطع ليغو! والعربية تستطيع أن تجمع جملة كاملة في كلمة واحدة: «وسيكتبونها» = و + سـ + يكتب + ون + ها، من صديقنا القديم ك ت ب!
  • مهمتك: ابحث عن كلمة إنجليزية طويلة هذا الأسبوع وقسّمها إلى مورفيمات.
Students do
  • Answer 5 items privately.
  • Share one word they built today that they really like.
Tips:
  • Note who confuses word classes (use vs useful); revisit with quick 'word family' warm-ups in Lessons 13-16.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer each item.
أجب عن كل سؤال.
  1. How many morphemes are in 'careless'? Name them. → 2: care + less
  2. What does re- mean in 'rewrite'? → again
  3. Complete: Thank you! Your advice is very ___ (use). → useful
  4. Write the superlative of 'happy'. → the happiest
  5. Which Arabic pattern means 'the person who does it', like English -er? → فاعِل (كاتب، لاعب)

Homework

Word Family Poster: choose 2 bases (for example help and kind, or care and use) and build a family tree with at least 5 words for each, labelled noun / verb / adjective. Then build one Arabic root family (for example ل ع ب or د ر س) with 5 words and write the English meaning of each. Finish with 5 sentences that use one word from each family.

ملصق عائلة الكلمات: اختر أصلين (مثل help و kind أو care و use) وابنِ لكل منهما شجرة عائلة من 5 كلمات على الأقل، واكتب نوع كل كلمة (اسم / فعل / صفة). ثم ابنِ عائلة جذر عربي (مثل ل ع ب أو د ر س) من 5 كلمات واكتب معناها بالإنجليزية. واختم بـ 5 جمل.

  • Two English word-family trees (5+ words each) with word classes.
  • One Arabic root family (5 words) with English meanings.
  • Five sentences using the new words.
  • Bonus: find one English word with three or more morphemes in a book, a game or an app.

Support

  • Give a reference card with the meaning of each affix and an example.
  • In the race, show the base word and ask only for the affix.
  • For spelling, focus on rule 2 (y → i) and the rebel good/best first.

Challenge

  • Find words with two prefixes or two suffixes (re + un + ...; care + less + ness = carelessness).
  • Investigate -er with two jobs: list 5 comparatives and 5 'person' words and explain how you can tell them apart.
  • Compare: English 'library' and Arabic 'مكتبة' vs 'bookshop'. Why do some languages use one word for both?

Success criteria

  • Segments at least 8 of 10 words into morphemes correctly.
  • Chooses the right word form in at least 8 of 10 gap-fills.
  • Spells at least 7 of 9 superlatives correctly and names the rule.
  • Explains the difference between English affixation and Arabic root-and-pattern morphology in 1-2 sentences.
  • Uses at least four built words correctly in the judge's comment.

Watch for

  • Wrong word class (very use, a care driver, I want to improvement)
  • the most big / more good / the goodest
  • bigest, happyest, usefull
  • Separate 'not' instead of a prefix (very not patient)
  • Cutting words into pieces with no meaning (un + der)

Teacher notes

  • The class uses Lesson 9-12 vocabulary (talented, popular, judge, suggest, recommend, improve, advice, patience, mistake, cheap) as raw material for word building.
  • Celebrate Arabic morphology: Arabic-speaking students are often excellent at finding roots, which transfers well to English word families.
  • Keep the 'concatenative / non-concatenative' terms for the teacher and strong students; for most, 'Lego vs weaving' is enough.
  • The word وسيكتبونها shows that Arabic also glues pieces (و، سـ، ـون، ـها) around a pattern-built stem: a good reminder that both languages mix the two methods.
  • Bonus Class 4 moves from words to how we use them with people: politeness and register.
