Blending Builders: Sounds Into Words

بنّاؤو الكلمات: من الأصوات إلى الكلمات
Big idea: Reading is pushing sounds together from left to right (blending); spelling is pulling a word apart into its sounds (segmenting). English writes every vowel, so we read every letter and never skip the middle.
القراءة هي ضمّ الأصوات معاً من اليسار إلى اليمين (الدمج)، والكتابة هي تفكيك الكلمة إلى أصواتها (التقطيع). الإنجليزية تكتب كل حركة، لذلك نقرأ كل حرف ولا نتخطّى الوسط أبداً.

Linguistics Corner — Phonemes: the bricks of words · الفونيمات: طوب الكلمات

Words are built from small sound bricks called phonemes. Change one brick and you get a new word: cat, hat, hot, hop. Linguists find the bricks of a language exactly like this.الكلمات مبنيّة من طوب صوتي صغير اسمه الفونيمات. غيّر طوبة واحدة تحصل على كلمة جديدة: cat و hat و hot و hop. وعلماء اللغة يكتشفون طوب أي لغة بهذه الطريقة تماماً.
Sounds and letters are not always the same number. duck has 4 letters but only 3 sounds, because ck is one sound. bell has 4 letters and 3 sounds.عدد الأصوات لا يساوي دائماً عدد الحروف. كلمة duck فيها 4 حروف و3 أصوات فقط، لأن ck صوت واحد. وكذلك bell: 4 حروف و3 أصوات.
Some sounds can be stretched (stretchy sounds: mmm, sss, fff, lll, nnn, rrr and all the vowels). Others are quick pops that cannot be stretched (bouncy sounds: p, b, t, d, k, g). Stretchy sounds are the easiest to blend: mmm-aaa-p, map.بعض الأصوات يمكن مدّها (أصوات مطّاطة: mmm و sss و fff و lll و nnn و rrr وكل الحركات)، وبعضها فرقعة سريعة لا تُمدّ (أصوات قافزة: p و b و t و d و k و g). الأصوات المطّاطة أسهل في الدمج: mmm-aaa-p ← map.
English has about 44 phonemes but only 26 letters. Arabic has about 34 phonemes (28 consonants and 6 vowels: 3 short and 3 long) and 28 letters plus the harakat.في الإنجليزية نحو 44 فونيماً و26 حرفاً فقط. وفي العربية نحو 34 فونيماً (28 صامتاً و6 حركات: 3 قصيرة و3 طويلة) و28 حرفاً إضافة إلى الحركات.
Arabic writing is an abjad: it mostly writes consonants and long vowels, and readers fill in the short vowels. The Greeks borrowed the old Phoenician letters (the same family as Arabic أ ب) and added letters for vowels. That is where the word 'alphabet' comes from: alpha + beta, like ألف + باء.الكتابة العربية «أبجدية»: تكتب غالباً الحروف الساكنة وحروف المدّ، والقارئ يضيف الحركات القصيرة بنفسه. أخذ الإغريق الحروف الفينيقية القديمة (من عائلة أ ب العربية نفسها) وأضافوا حروفاً للحركات. ومن هنا جاءت كلمة alphabet: ألفا + بيتا، مثل ألف + باء.

1. Warm-up: Robot Talk

0–5 min
إحماء: كلام الروبوت
Method: Oral blending game (phonemic awareness, no letters)
Purpose: Practise blending by ear before seeing letters, and make it fun.
Teacher says

“Beep beep! I am a robot. I can only talk in sounds. Can you understand me? h-a-t!”

“Yes! hat! Robots, show me the hat on your head!”

“Next: c-u-p. s-u-n. d-o-g. b-e-d. Say the word and point to it or act it out!”

“Now you are the robot. Say a word in robot sounds for your friends.”

  • أنا روبوت ولا أتكلّم إلا بالأصوات. هل تفهمونني؟ h-a-t!
  • الآن أنت الروبوت: قل كلمة بأصوات الروبوت لأصدقائك.
Students do
  • Listen to robot sounds and say the whole word.
  • Act or point to the word (hat on head, drink from a cup).
  • One child is the robot for the group.
Exercise · oral-blending — Listen to the robot. Say the word.
استمع للروبوت وقل الكلمة.
  1. h-a-t → hat
  2. c-u-p → cup
  3. s-u-n → sun
  4. d-o-g → dog
  5. b-e-d → bed
  6. h-o-t → hot
Tips:
  • Use robot arms and a flat robot voice; children love it and it keeps sounds separate.
  • Leave one second between sounds at first, then make the gaps shorter.

2. Linguistics Corner: Sound Bricks and Counting Sounds

5–14 min
ركن اللغويات: طوب الأصوات وعدّ الأصوات
Method: Guided discovery: phoneme counting with fingers + letters-vs-sounds contrast
Purpose: Understand that words are built from phonemes, and that the number of sounds is not always the number of letters.
Teacher says

“Words are like Lego houses. Each sound is one brick. These bricks have a science name: phonemes.”

“Let's count bricks with our fingers. hat: h (one finger), a (two), t (three). hat has 3 sounds!”

“Now duck. d... u... ck. How many sounds? 3! How many letters? d-u-c-k: 4! Why? Because c and k together make one sound, so they live in one brick.”

“Look: bell. b... e... ll. 3 sounds, 4 letters. Two l's, one sound.”

“And some words have only 2 bricks: up, it, on, egg.”

  • الكلمات مثل بيوت الليغو، وكل صوت طوبة. اسم هذه الطوبات العلمي: الفونيمات.
  • duck فيها 4 حروف لكن 3 أصوات فقط، لأن ck صوت واحد.
Students do
  • Count sounds on fingers for each word, then say the number.
  • Count letters too, and spot the words where sounds and letters are different.
  • Show the number with fingers on camera: 2 or 3.
Exercise · count-the-sounds — Say the word slowly. Count the sounds on your fingers. Show the number.
قل الكلمة ببطء، وعدّ الأصوات على أصابعك، ثم أرِنا العدد.
  1. cat → 3
  2. up → 2
  3. duck → 3 (d-u-ck; 4 letters)
  4. sun → 3
  5. egg → 2 (e-gg; 3 letters)
  6. bell → 3 (b-e-ll; 4 letters)
  7. on → 2
  8. sock → 3 (s-o-ck; 4 letters)
Tips:
  • Count sounds by ear first, letters second. Saying 'sounds are for ears, letters are for eyes' links back to Bonus Class 1.
  • Double letters (ll, gg, ss) and ck are one sound each: one finger, one box.

3. Arabic Bridge: Where Did the Vowel Go?

14–21 min
جسر العربية: أين ذهبت الحركة؟
Method: Contrastive analysis: 'Arabic-style English' puzzle (words written without vowels)
Purpose: Show why English always writes the vowel, and why children must never skip it when reading or spelling.
Teacher says

“Read this Arabic word: كتب. Is it كَتَبَ, كُتُب or كُتِبَ? We don't know until we see the sentence! In Arabic we often don't write the short vowels.”

“Now look: what if English did the same? h_t. Is it hat? hit? hot? hut? Four words!”

“That is why English never hides the vowel. It is always there, in the middle of the word, as a full letter.”

“So, Blending Builders, rule number one: never skip the middle sound!”

“Did you know? Arabic writing is called an abjad. The Greeks took the old letters from the same family as Arabic and added vowel letters. That is why we say 'alphabet': alpha, beta, like ألف, باء!”

  • كلمة كتب: هل هي كَتَبَ أم كُتُب أم كُتِبَ؟ لا نعرف إلا من الجملة. أما الإنجليزية فتكتب الحركة دائماً.
  • القاعدة الأولى لبنّائي الكلمات: لا تتخطَّ الصوت الأوسط أبداً!
Students do
  • Read كتب in three ways with the harakat on the slide.
  • Solve the vowel-less English words: say every real word you can make.
  • Write the missing vowel letters on paper or in the chat.
Exercise · missing-vowel — The vowel is missing! How many real words can you make? Write them.
الحركة مفقودة! كم كلمة حقيقية تستطيع أن تصنع؟ اكتبها.
  1. h_t → hat, hit, hot, hut
  2. b_g → bag, beg, big, bog, bug
  3. p_t → pat, pet, pit, pot
  4. c_p → cap, cup
  5. b_d → bad, bed, bid, bud
  6. s_t → sat, set, sit
Tips:
  • Accept any real word; bog, bid and bud are bonus words, just explain them quickly.
  • Keep the message proud, not negative: Arabic readers are clever at guessing vowels; English just shows them.

4. Blending Builders: Arm-Tap and Stretchy Sounds

21–31 min
بنّاؤو الكلمات: النقر على الذراع والأصوات المطّاطة
Method: Explicit modelled blending (I do, we do, you do) + kinesthetic arm-tap + continuous blending
Purpose: Read CVC words by blending letter sounds from left to right, smoothly and without extra 'uh' sounds.
Teacher says

“Watch me read. Arm out! Touch your shoulder: s. Touch your elbow: u. Touch your wrist: n. Now slide your hand down your arm: sun!”

“Stretchy sounds help us. Listen: mmmaaap. I don't stop between sounds. mmm-aaa-p: map!”

“Bouncy sounds are quick: b, d, t, p. Say them short: b, not 'buh'. Then jump straight to the vowel: b-e-d... bbed... bed!”

“Your turn. Follow the arrow on the slide from left to right. Read every sound, then slide!”

“Read the silly sentence: 'A red hen got a big hat.'”

  • المس كتفك للصوت الأول، ومرفقك للثاني، ورسغك للثالث، ثم مرّر يدك على ذراعك وقل الكلمة.
  • الأصوات المطّاطة نمدّها بلا توقّف، والأصوات القافزة نقولها قصيرة بدون 'أَه'.
Students do
  • Arm-tap each word: shoulder, elbow, wrist, then slide.
  • Read the words on the slide one by one, following the arrow.
  • Read the silly sentences: 'A red hen got a big hat.' / 'The pup can run in the sun.' / 'Dad has a hot cup.'
Exercise · read-and-blend — Arm-tap the sounds, then slide and say the word.
انقر الأصوات على ذراعك ثم مرّر يدك وقل الكلمة.
  1. m-a-p → map
  2. h-e-n → hen
  3. f-i-t → fit
  4. l-o-g → log
  5. r-u-g → rug
  6. b-e-d → bed
  7. d-u-ck → duck
  8. l-e-g → leg
  9. p-i-g → pig
  10. h-o-p → hop
Tips:
  • Start with words that begin with a stretchy sound (map, sun, log, fit, rug); they are easiest to blend smoothly.
  • If a child says every sound correctly but cannot put them together, blend the first two sounds only ('hhheee... hen'), then add the last.
  • On camera, ask children to watch the slide arrow rather than your arm; their self-view may be mirrored.
  • 'The' in the silly sentences is a tricky word; just tell children the word. It is the topic of Bonus Class 4.

5. Sound Boxes: Builders Become Spellers

31–41 min
صناديق الأصوات: البنّاؤون يصبحون كتّاباً
Method: Elkonin sound boxes with counters (segmenting), then writing letters in the boxes
Purpose: Spell CVC words by pulling them apart into sounds, one sound per box, never forgetting the vowel box.
Teacher says

“Now we do the opposite: we take a word apart. Draw three boxes on your paper. Put three coins under the boxes.”

“Listen: cup. Say it slowly: c... u... p. Push one coin into a box for each sound. c - push, u - push, p - push!”

“Now write the letter for each sound in its box. c | u | p. The middle box is the vowel box. It is never empty!”

“Tricky one: duck. d | u | ck. Where does ck go? In the last box, together, because it is one sound.”

“Hold your paper up to the camera. Let's check!”

  • الآن نفعل العكس: نفكّك الكلمة. ارسم ثلاثة صناديق، وادفع قطعة نقود إلى صندوق مع كل صوت.
  • الصندوق الأوسط هو صندوق الحركة، ولا يكون فارغاً أبداً!
Students do
  • Push a counter into a box for each sound, saying the sound.
  • Write the letters in the boxes.
  • Hold the paper up to the camera to check with the teacher.
  • Check a friend: is the vowel box full?
Exercise · sound-boxes — Push a coin for each sound. Then write one sound in each box.
ادفع قطعة نقود مع كل صوت، ثم اكتب صوتاً واحداً في كل صندوق.
  1. cup → c | u | p
  2. hat → h | a | t
  3. bed → b | e | d
  4. sun → s | u | n
  5. dog → d | o | g
  6. fan → f | a | n
  7. duck → d | u | ck
  8. red → r | e | d
Tips:
  • Do the coins first (sounds only), then the letters. Coins show the child is hearing the sounds before worrying about spelling.
  • If the vowel box is empty or wrong, go back to the vowel friends: 'Which friend is in the middle? Grumpy U or Round O?'
  • Accept 'k' for the cup sound only if the child hears it right; then show that cup starts with c (Lesson 5: c and k say the same sound).

6. Sound Chains: One Brick at a Time

41–50 min
سلاسل الأصوات: طوبة واحدة في كل مرة
Method: Phoneme substitution chain (word ladder) as a team game
Purpose: Strengthen blending and segmenting together by changing one sound at a time: first, last or middle.
Teacher says

“Team game! We start with cat. Change ONE brick to make a new word. Change t to p... cap! Change c to m... map!”

“Each child adds one link to the chain. Say the new word, and tell us which brick changed: first, middle or last?”

“Our chain: cat, cap, map, mop, hop, hot, hut, hug, bug, bun, sun. Can we make it longer?”

“Build each word with your letter cards or write it, then read it with the arm-tap.”

  • نبدأ بكلمة cat. غيّر طوبة واحدة فقط لتصنع كلمة جديدة.
  • قل الكلمة الجديدة، وأخبرنا أيّ طوبة تغيّرت: الأولى أم الوسطى أم الأخيرة؟
Students do
  • Change one sound to make the next word in the chain.
  • Write each new word and read it aloud.
  • Say which part changed: first, middle or last sound.
  • Invent the next link for a friend.
Exercise · sound-chain — Change one sound. Write the new word and read it.
غيّر صوتاً واحداً، واكتب الكلمة الجديدة واقرأها.
  1. cat: change t to p → cap
  2. cap: change c to m → map
  3. map: change a to o → mop
  4. mop: change m to h → hop
  5. hop: change p to t → hot
  6. hot: change o to u → hut
  7. hut: change t to g → hug
  8. hug: change h to b → bug
  9. bug: change g to n → bun
  10. bun: change b to s → sun
Tips:
  • Middle-sound changes (map to mop, hot to hut) are the hardest; they are exactly where Arabic speakers need practice.
  • Give points to the team for each correct link, not to individuals, so slower children are not exposed.
  • Stronger children can make the chain without a prompt: 'Make a new word from sun!'

7. Builder's Dictation

50–55 min
إملاء البنّائين
Method: Peer dictation (production: segment and spell independently)
Purpose: Apply segmenting in independent spelling, with a partner as the teacher.
Teacher says

“Partner dictation! One child is the teacher and says a word from the card I send. The others write it.”

“Teacher, say it twice: once normal, once slowly. Writers, use your boxes in your head!”

“Show your paper. Teacher, is the vowel box right?”

  • إملاء بين الأصدقاء: أحدكم المعلّم يقول الكلمة، والباقون يكتبونها.
  • قلها مرّتين: مرة عادية ومرة ببطء.
Students do
  • Take turns saying a word for the group.
  • Write each word and hold it up.
  • The child-teacher checks the vowel.
Exercise · dictation — Listen to your friend. Write the word.
استمع لصديقك واكتب الكلمة.
  1. pig → pig
  2. hen → hen
  3. rock → rock
  4. bus → bus
  5. lip → lip
  6. fog → fog
Tips:
  • Send each child-teacher their words in the private chat.
  • If the child-teacher's vowel is unclear, that is good practice too: ask the group to listen again.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

55–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check blending and segmenting alone, and finish with a fact to share at home.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket! Two jobs. Read the words I show. Then write the words I say.”

“Did you know? The word 'alphabet' comes from alpha and beta, the first two Greek letters, and they come from the same old letters as ألف and باء! English and Arabic letters are distant cousins.”

“Your mission at home: play Robot Talk with your family. Can they understand your robot words?”

  • هل تعلم؟ كلمة alphabet تأتي من ألفا وبيتا، أول حرفين في اليونانية، وهما من الحروف القديمة نفسها التي جاءت منها ألف وباء! حروف الإنجليزية والعربية أبناء عمومة بعيدون.
  • مهمتك في البيت: العب لعبة كلام الروبوت مع عائلتك.
Students do
  • Read 3 words aloud and write 2 words.
  • Say goodbye in robot talk: 'b-y-e!'
Tips:
  • Note who reads with guessing (wrong vowel) or writes without the vowel; give them sound-box practice in Lessons 13-15 warm-ups.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Items 1-3: read the word aloud. Items 4-5: listen and write the word.
1-3: اقرأ الكلمة بصوت عالٍ. 4-5: استمع واكتب الكلمة.
  1. Read: hut → hut
  2. Read: leg → leg
  3. Read: sock → sock
  4. Write: (teacher says) bag → bag

Homework

Robot Talk and Sound Boxes: say 5 things at home in robot talk (c-u-p, b-e-d, m-a-p...) for your family to guess. Then draw 3 boxes and spell 4 of them, one sound in each box.

كلام الروبوت وصناديق الأصوات: قل 5 أشياء في البيت بكلام الروبوت (c-u-p و b-e-d و m-a-p...) لتخمّنها عائلتك. ثم ارسم 3 صناديق واكتب 4 منها، صوتاً واحداً في كل صندوق.

  • Play Robot Talk with a family member (5 words).
  • Spell 4 words in sound boxes: cup, bed, hat, sun.
  • Make a sound chain of 5 words starting from cat.

Support

  • Blend only words that start with a stretchy sound (map, sun, man, fan, log).
  • Use counters only (no writing) for sound boxes.
  • Give the vowel letter in the middle box and let the child add the first and last letters.

Challenge

  • Try 4-sound words with 4 boxes: frog (f-r-o-g), lamp (l-a-m-p), stop (s-t-o-p).
  • Puzzle: ox has 2 letters but 3 sounds (o-k-s). How? (x says two sounds: k + s.)
  • Make the longest sound chain you can in two minutes.

Success criteria

  • Blends at least 8 of 10 CVC words correctly without adding 'uh'.
  • Counts the sounds in at least 6 of 8 words, including duck and bell.
  • Spells at least 6 of 8 sound-box words with the correct vowel.
  • Explains in English or Arabic why English writes every vowel (h_t could be four words).

Watch for

  • vowel missing in writing (ct for cat)
  • extra 'uh' after consonants (cuh-a-tuh)
  • guessing from the first letter or picture
  • reading right to left (pit for tip)
  • counting letters instead of sounds (duck = 4)
  • ck at the start of a word

Teacher notes

  • All words are decodable with the Level 1 letters taught by Lesson 11 (s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r h b f l), plus the tricky words a and the, which the teacher simply reads aloud.
  • Blending (reading) and segmenting (spelling) are two sides of one skill; always do both in the same class.
  • The Arabic Bridge is about writing, not speaking: Arabic readers are experts at filling in vowels from context, which is useful later but makes guessing a habit. Praise every child who reads the middle sound.
  • Bonus Class 4 builds on this: the tricky words that cannot be blended (the, was, said) and the rules for writing sentences.
