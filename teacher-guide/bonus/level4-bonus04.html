Polite English: Sounding Kind and Clear

الإنجليزية المهذّبة: كيف تبدو لطيفاً وواضحاً
Big idea: Politeness is built differently in each language: Arabic often makes a direct request polite with formulas (لو سمحت، من فضلك, and in spoken Arabic الله يخليك), while English makes it polite with indirect questions and modals (Could you..., Would you mind..., I'd like...). So a word-for-word translation like 'Give me water' can sound rude in English, even with please.
التهذيب يُبنى بطرق مختلفة في كل لغة: العربية كثيراً ما تجعل الطلب المباشر مهذباً بعبارات مثل «لو سمحت» و«من فضلك» و«الله يخليك» في العامية، أما الإنجليزية فتجعله مهذباً بالسؤال غير المباشر والأفعال المساعدة (Could you... و Would you mind... و I'd like...). لذلك قد تبدو الترجمة الحرفية مثل Give me water وقحة في الإنجليزية حتى مع please.

Linguistics Corner — Pragmatics: words do things · التداولية: الكلمات تفعل أشياء

Linguists say words don't just mean things, they do things: they ask, promise, warn, thank. These are called speech acts.يقول اللغويون إن الكلمات لا تعني أشياء فقط، بل تفعل أشياء: تطلب وتَعِد وتحذّر وتشكر. وتُسمّى «أفعال الكلام».
Many English requests are disguised as questions: 'Can you pass the salt?' is not about your ability. Nobody answers 'Yes, I can' and does nothing! This is an indirect speech act.كثير من الطلبات الإنجليزية متنكّرة في صورة أسئلة: Can you pass the salt? ليست سؤالاً عن قدرتك. لا أحد يجيب Yes, I can ثم لا يفعل شيئاً! هذا «فعل كلامي غير مباشر».
Even a statement can be a request: 'It's a bit cold in here' can mean 'Please close the window.' The listener has to work out the hidden meaning.حتى الجملة الخبرية قد تكون طلباً: It's a bit cold in here قد تعني «أغلق النافذة من فضلك». وعلى السامع أن يستنتج المعنى الخفي.
In English, could and would (old past forms of can and will) sound softer than the present forms: Could you... is softer than Can you..., Would you... is softer than Will you.... Many linguists think distance in time feels like polite distance.في الإنجليزية تبدو صيغ الماضي ألطف من المضارع: Could you ألطف من Can you، و Would you ألطف من Will you. ويرى كثير من اللغويين أن البعد في الزمن يوحي بمسافة مهذّبة.
Every culture is polite, but in different ways. Arabic often uses rich formulas and blessings (لو سمحت, and in spoken Arabic الله يخليك، يعطيك العافية) and is comfortable with direct imperatives plus a formula. English relies more on indirectness, modals and softeners like just and a bit.كل الثقافات مهذبة، لكن بطرق مختلفة. العربية كثيراً ما تستخدم عبارات ودعوات غنية (لو سمحت، و«الله يخليك» و«يعطيك العافية» في العامية) وتقبل صيغة الأمر المباشر مع عبارة مجاملة. أما الإنجليزية فتعتمد أكثر على عدم المباشرة والأفعال المساعدة والملطِّفات مثل just و a bit.
Register is choosing the right 'outfit' for your words: you wear different clothes to a wedding, a football match and bed. Group chat English (thx, pls, u) is fine with friends, but not in an email to a teacher.مستوى اللغة هو اختيار «الملابس» المناسبة لكلماتك: لا تلبس الثياب نفسها في العرس ومباراة كرة القدم والنوم. لغة المحادثة الجماعية (thx، pls، u) مناسبة مع الأصدقاء، لكن ليس في بريد إلكتروني إلى المعلم.

1. Warm-up: Which One Would You Say?

0–5 min
إحماء: أيّها ستقول؟
Method: Reaction poll (affective hook: how does it feel?)
Purpose: Get teens to feel the difference in politeness before analyzing it.
Teacher says

“Imagine Ziad in a café. He says to the waiter: 'Water.' How does the waiter feel?”

“Now: 'Give me water.' Then: 'Give me water, please.' Then: 'Could I have some water, please?'”

“Show your card: 1 rude, 2 OK, 3 polite. Interesting! 'Please' helps, but is it enough?”

“Today you learn the secret machine of English politeness. It's not just please!”

  • كيف يشعر النادل في كل مرة؟ ارفعوا البطاقة: 1 وقح، 2 مقبول، 3 مهذب.
  • please تساعد، لكن هل تكفي؟ اليوم نكتشف آلة التهذيب الإنجليزية.
Students do
  • Make the 1, 2, 3 cards and react to four versions of the same request.
  • Say in one sentence (English or Arabic) why the last one feels better.
Tips:
  • Act the waiter's face for each version: teens love the drama.
  • Expect disagreement about 'Give me water, please': perfect bridge to the Arabic comparison later.

2. Discovery: The Politeness Ladder

5–14 min
اكتشاف: سُلَّم التهذيب
Method: Guided discovery (ranking + noticing the tools of politeness)
Purpose: Students rank requests and identify the 'tools' that move a request up the ladder: question form, modals (can → could → would), I'd like, please, softeners (just, a bit, maybe).
Teacher says

“Here's the ladder. Bottom step: 'Water!' Top step: 'Would you mind getting me some water?' Where do the others go?”

“What changes when we climb? 1) An order becomes a question. 2) Can becomes could, will becomes would. 3) We add please, just, a bit, maybe.”

“Interesting fact: could and would began as the past forms of can and will, and English uses them to sound softer and less pushy.”

“But careful: the top of the ladder isn't always best. With your best friend, 'Would you mind passing me the chips?' sounds funny! Choose the step for the person.”

  • ما الذي يتغيّر كلما صعدنا السُّلَّم؟ الأمر يصبح سؤالاً، و can تصبح could، ونضيف please و just و a bit.
  • أعلى درجة ليست الأفضل دائماً؛ مع صديقك المقرّب قد تبدو مضحكة. اختر الدرجة المناسبة للشخص.
Students do
  • Rank 8 requests from 1 (most direct) to 5 (most polite).
  • Circle the politeness tools in each one.
  • Decide who you would say the top and bottom requests to.
Exercise · rank-and-label — Give each request a step on the ladder (1 = very direct, 5 = very polite). Name the tool that makes it polite.
ضع كل طلب على درجة من السُّلَّم (1 = مباشر جداً، 5 = مهذب جداً) واذكر الأداة التي جعلته مهذباً.
  1. Pass the ball! → 1: bare imperative (fine in a fast game)
  2. Pass the ball, please. → 2: imperative + please
  3. Can you pass the ball? → 3: question with can
  4. Could you pass me the ball, please? → 4: could + please
  5. Would you mind passing me the ball? → 5: would you mind + -ing
  6. I'd like the chicken, please. → 4: I'd like + please
  7. I want the chicken. → 1-2: I want (direct)
  8. Could you just check my caption? It's a bit long. → 4-5: could + softeners just and a bit
Tips:
  • Accept slightly different ranks if the student gives a good reason: politeness is a scale, not a math answer.
  • Point out 'Would you mind + -ing' (passing, checking): a common grammar slip is 'Would you mind to pass'.

3. Linguistics Corner: Words Do Things

14–22 min
ركن اللغويات: الكلمات تفعل أشياء
Method: Concept explanation + 'hidden meaning' decoding (indirect speech acts)
Purpose: Introduce pragmatics: understand that the real meaning of a sentence can be different from its form.
Teacher says

“Linguists have a special field called pragmatics: what people really mean when they talk.”

“'Can you pass the salt?' Is it a question about your arm muscles? No! It's a request. If someone answers 'Yes, I can' and does nothing, it's a joke.”

“Even a statement can be a request: 'It's a bit cold in here.' What does Mom really want? Close the window!”

“English speakers often hide requests like this to be polite. So be a meaning detective: what do they want?”

  • التداولية تدرس ما يقصده الناس حقاً حين يتكلمون.
  • Can you pass the salt? طلب وليس سؤالاً عن القدرة، و It's cold in here قد تعني «أغلق النافذة».
Students do
  • Decode the real meaning of each sentence.
  • Answer as a polite English speaker would (do it or offer to do it).
Exercise · decode-the-meaning — What does the speaker really want? Say what you would do or answer.
ماذا يريد المتكلم حقاً؟ قل ماذا ستفعل أو بماذا ستجيب.
  1. Teacher: 'It's a bit noisy in here.' → Please be quiet. (Students lower their voices.)
  2. Captain: 'Can you take the corner kick?' → Please take the corner kick. (Sure!)
  3. Friend in the group chat: 'Does anyone have the homework?' → Please send me the homework. (Here it is!)
  4. Mom: 'Your room is a bit messy.' → Please tidy your room. (OK, I'll do it now.)
  5. Waiter: 'Are you ready to order?' → Tell me your order, please. (Yes, I'd like...)
  6. Sara: 'I don't have a pen...' → Can I borrow a pen? (Here, you can use mine.)
  7. Noor: 'Would you like to come to my party?' → She is inviting you; this one is not hidden. (I'd love to, thank you! / I'm sorry, I can't.)
Tips:
  • Arabic has indirect requests too (spoken Arabic: الجو حار شوي) so this is a familiar idea; the difference is how often English uses them.
  • Keep 'speech act' as a bonus term; 'hidden meaning' is enough for most students.

4. Arabic Bridge: Formula vs Frame

22–30 min
جسر العربية: العبارة الجاهزة مقابل الإطار
Method: Contrastive pragmatics + translation for meaning (not word for word)
Purpose: Show that Arabic politeness is often carried by formulas around a direct request, while English puts politeness in the frame of the sentence; practice translating requests for function, not form.
Teacher says

“In Gulf Arabic we say: عطني ماي لو سمحت. It's an imperative, 'give me', and it's polite because of لو سمحت. Totally normal!”

“In English, the same shape, 'Give me water, please', is still an order. English puts the politeness in the frame: Could I have some water, please?”

“Spoken Arabic has beautiful formulas: الله يخليك، يعطيك العافية، تسلم إيدك. English doesn't have the same blessings, so we don't translate them word for word. We translate the job they do.”

“So the rule is: translate the meaning, not the words. Ready? Translate like a diplomat!”

  • في العربية: الأمر + لو سمحت = طلب مهذب. في الإنجليزية: الأمر + please = ما زال أمراً.
  • لا نترجم الكلمات بل نترجم وظيفتها: ترجم كالدبلوماسي!
Students do
  • Translate each Arabic request into natural, polite English.
  • Compare answers: is it a frame (Could you / Can I / I'd like) or a direct imperative?
Exercise · translate-for-function — Translate into polite, natural English. Don't translate word for word. (Most prompts are in spoken Gulf Arabic.)
ترجم إلى إنجليزية مهذبة وطبيعية، ولا تترجم كلمة بكلمة. (أغلب الجمل باللهجة الخليجية المحكية.)
  1. لو سمحت، عطني المنيو. → Excuse me, could I see the menu, please?
  2. أبغى عصير برتقال. → I'd like an orange juice, please. / Can I get an orange juice, please?
  3. الله يخليك، ساعدني بالواجب. → Could you help me with my homework, please?
  4. من فضلك، افتح النافذة. → Could you open the window, please? / Would you mind opening the window?
  5. يا أستاذ، أرسل لي الملف. → Excuse me, Mr. Adams, could you send me the file, please?
  6. عطنا الفاتورة لو سمحت. → Could we have the bill, please?
  7. ممكن أستلف قلمك؟ → Can I borrow your pen? / Could I borrow your pen, please?
  8. يعطيك العافية! (to a teammate after the match) → Thanks a lot! / Great job today! / Well played!
Tips:
  • Stress that Arabic politeness is not 'less polite': it uses different tools. Avoid any ranking of cultures.
  • Notice ممكن أستلف قلمك؟ is already a question in Arabic: Arabic also has indirect requests, so students already have the idea.

5. Softener Workshop: Make It Kinder

30–39 min
ورشة الملطِّفات: اجعلها ألطف
Method: Transformation drill (rewrite with a given frame) + peer check
Purpose: Practice turning direct messages into polite requests and suggestions with Could you, Can I, Would you mind + -ing, I'd like, Maybe we should.
Teacher says

“Your tools: Could you...? Can I...? Would you mind + -ing? I'd like... Maybe we should... And the softeners: just, a bit, maybe, I think.”

“These messages are from Hamad. Hamad is a nice guy, but his messages sound bossy! Let's help him.”

“Example: 'Send me the photo.' → 'Could you send me the photo, please?'”

“For disagreeing in a team, use a suggestion: 'Your idea is bad.' → 'I think maybe we should consider another option.'”

  • رسائل حمد تبدو متسلّطة رغم أنه لطيف! ساعدوه بأدوات التهذيب.
  • للاعتراض في الفريق نستخدم الاقتراح: I think maybe we should...
Students do
  • Rewrite each message using the frame in brackets.
  • Swap with a partner and give a Politeness Meter score (1-5 stars).
  • Read the best version aloud with a friendly tone (tone matters too!).
Exercise · rewrite — Make Hamad's message polite. Use the frame in brackets.
اجعل رسالة حمد مهذبة باستخدام الإطار بين القوسين.
  1. Send me the photo. (Could you...?) → Could you send me the photo, please?
  2. Give me your notebook. (Can I borrow...?) → Can I borrow your notebook, please?
  3. Turn off the music. (Would you mind + -ing?) → Would you mind turning off the music?
  4. I want a pizza. (I'd like...) → I'd like a pizza, please.
  5. Your idea is bad. (I think maybe we should...) → I think maybe we should consider another option.
  6. Delete that comment! (Could you just...?) → Could you just delete that comment, please?
  7. Help me with the poster. (Would you mind + -ing?) → Would you mind helping me with the poster?
  8. Be quiet, I'm studying. (Could you... a bit...?) → Could you be a bit quieter, please? I'm studying.
  9. Change the caption. (Maybe you could...?) → Maybe you could change the caption?
Tips:
  • Model tone: a polite sentence in a cold voice is still cold. Let students hear the difference.
  • Remind: Would you mind + -ING (turning, helping). Correct 'Would you mind to help' gently.

6. Register-Switch Challenge

39–48 min
تحدّي تبديل المستوى اللغوي
Method: Register sorting + rewriting the same message for three audiences
Purpose: Recognize informal, neutral and formal language and switch between group chat, team message and teacher email.
Teacher says

“Register is your language outfit. Pajamas for a group chat, a school uniform for a team message, your best clothes for an email to a teacher.”

“Look at these 9 lines. Who is each one for: friends (informal), team (neutral) or teacher (formal)?”

“Now the challenge: the same news, three outfits. 'I can't come to practice tomorrow.' Write it for the group chat, for the captain and for the coach by email.”

  • مستوى اللغة هو «ملابس» كلماتك: بيجامة للمحادثة الجماعية، وزيّ مدرسي لرسالة الفريق، وأفضل ملابسك لبريد المعلم.
  • اكتب الخبر نفسه بثلاثة مستويات.
Students do
  • Label each line informal, neutral or formal.
  • Write the same message in three registers.
  • Read them aloud; the group guesses who each one is for.
Exercise · label-the-register — Informal (friends), neutral (team / classmates) or formal (teacher / coach email)?
غير رسمي (أصدقاء)، محايد (الفريق / الزملاء)، أم رسمي (بريد للمعلم / المدرّب)؟
  1. omg that's sooo funny 😂 → informal
  2. Dear Ms. Lina, → formal
  3. Hi everyone, practice is at 5 tomorrow. → neutral
  4. thx bro, cu later → informal
  5. I look forward to hearing from you. → formal
  6. Best wishes, Omar → formal
  7. Can someone share the notes? Thanks! → neutral
  8. I would like to ask about my grade. → formal
  9. Sorry I'm late, guys! → informal
Tips:
  • Model answer for the challenge: Chat: 'can't come tmrw, sorry guys 😢' / Captain: 'Hi Ziad, sorry, I can't come to practice tomorrow. I have a dentist appointment.' / Coach email: 'Dear Coach Adams, I am sorry, but I will not be able to attend practice tomorrow because I have a dentist appointment. Best wishes, Sara'.
  • Point out that informal is not wrong: it's wrong only in the wrong place.

7. Role-Play: Politeness Under Pressure

48–56 min
تمثيل أدوار: التهذيب تحت الضغط
Method: Task-based role-play with peer feedback (Politeness Meter)
Purpose: Use polite requests, suggestions and refusals spontaneously in realistic, slightly stressful situations (linking to Lesson 16).
Teacher says

“Each of you gets a situation card. You have 30 seconds to think, then we act it out. Stay calm and polite, even under pressure!”

“Card 1: Café. The waiter brought the wrong drink. Ask politely for the right one.”

“Card 2: Team Captain. A teammate is always late. Talk to them kindly but clearly.”

“Card 3: Group Project. You don't like the group's idea. Suggest a compromise.”

“Card 4: Borrowing. You forgot your notebook and need to borrow one for class.”

“The audience gives Politeness Meter stars and one tip.”

  • لكل واحد بطاقة موقف. فكّر 30 ثانية ثم مثّل الموقف، وابقَ هادئاً ومهذباً حتى تحت الضغط!
  • الجمهور يعطي نجوماً على مقياس التهذيب ونصيحة واحدة.
Students do
  • Act out one situation with the teacher or a classmate (about 1 minute each).
  • Use at least two politeness tools and one softener.
  • Give a classmate stars and one specific tip ('Try could instead of can').
Tips:
  • Model one role-play first with a strong student, deliberately starting rude and then 'rewinding' to the polite version: teens find the rewind funny and memorable.
  • Useful frames on screen: Sorry, but I think this isn't what I ordered. / Could you try to come on time? It really helps the team. / What if we combine both ideas? / Would you mind lending me your notebook?

8. Exit Ticket and Did You Know?

56–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check each student's ability to make requests polite and choose the right register; end the Level 4 bonus series with a reflection.
Teacher says

“Last exit ticket of our Word Power series: five items, privately in the chat.”

“Did you know? In Britain, many people say 'sorry' so often that some even say it when someone else bumps into them! Politeness is part of culture, not just grammar.”

“Mission: this week, notice how people ask for things in English videos or games. Find one Could you... or Would you mind... and share it next class.”

  • هل تعلم؟ في بريطانيا يقول كثير من الناس sorry كثيراً جداً، حتى إن بعضهم يقولها عندما يصطدم بهم شخص آخر! التهذيب جزء من الثقافة وليس قواعد فقط.
  • مهمتك: لاحظ كيف يطلب الناس الأشياء في الفيديوهات أو الألعاب الإنجليزية، وشاركنا مثالاً.
Students do
  • Answer 5 items privately.
  • Say which of the four Word Power classes was their favorite and why.
Tips:
  • This is the last Bonus Class of Level 4: take 30 seconds to celebrate the series and link back to the -s mystery, counting and word building.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer each item.
أجب عن كل سؤال.
  1. Make it polite: 'Give me the menu.' → Could I see / have the menu, please?
  2. Complete: Would you mind ___ (open) the door? → opening
  3. Someone asks 'Would you mind helping me?' You want to help. What do you say? → No, not at all. / Sure!
  4. Formal or informal? 'Dear Mr. Adams, I would like to ask about the test.' → formal
  5. Why can 'Give me water, please' sound rude in English but عطني ماي لو سمحت is polite in Arabic? → Arabic makes a direct imperative polite with formulas like لو سمحت; English needs an indirect question or modal (Could I have...?), so an imperative + please still sounds like an order.

Homework

Politeness Makeover: write three short messages about the same problem (for example, you need help with a school project): one for a friend in a group chat, one for a team captain or classmate, and one email to a teacher. Then find 3 direct requests you hear at home or on TV in Arabic, and write how you would say them politely in English.

تحويل التهذيب: اكتب ثلاث رسائل قصيرة عن المشكلة نفسها (مثلاً تحتاج مساعدة في مشروع مدرسي): واحدة لصديق في محادثة جماعية، وواحدة لقائد الفريق أو زميل، وبريداً إلكترونياً للمعلّم. ثم اختر 3 طلبات مباشرة تسمعها بالعربية في البيت أو على التلفاز، واكتب كيف تقولها بأدب في الإنجليزية.

  • Write one message in each register (informal, neutral, formal).
  • Translate 3 Arabic requests into polite English for function, not word for word.
  • Use at least three different politeness tools (Could you, Would you mind, I'd like, Can I).
  • Bonus: record yourself ordering a meal politely in English.

Support

  • Give a 'politeness toolbox' card with 5 frames and example sentences.
  • In role-plays, allow students to read from the frames on screen.
  • Focus on two tools only: Could you...? and I'd like...

Challenge

  • Add very formal frames: I was wondering if you could... / Would it be possible to...?
  • Write a polite refusal and a polite complaint (Sorry, but... / I'm afraid...).
  • Research: how do people make polite requests in another language you know (French, Urdu, Turkish)? Compare with Arabic and English.

Success criteria

  • Turns at least 7 of 9 direct messages into polite requests with a correct frame.
  • Translates Arabic requests for function (no word-for-word 'Give me...').
  • Labels the register of at least 7 of 9 lines and writes the same message in three registers.
  • Uses Would you mind + -ing correctly and answers it correctly (No, not at all).
  • Explains the Arabic/English politeness difference in one or two sentences without judging either culture.

Watch for

  • Bare imperatives to adults and strangers (Give me..., Send me...)
  • I want... when ordering
  • Would you mind to + verb
  • Answering Yes to Would you mind...? when they mean OK
  • Chat style (u, thx, no capitals) in formal messages
  • Literal translation of blessings (God keep you)

Teacher notes

  • This class uses Lessons 11-16 language (I'd like, should, group chat, teamwork, decisions, feelings) and adds no new topic vocabulary.
  • Pragmatic errors can be more damaging than grammar errors: a listener forgives a missing -s but may think a direct request is rude. Explain this to students as the reason this class matters.
  • Treat both cultures with respect: Arabic politeness is rich and warm; it simply uses different tools. Never present English as 'more polite'.
  • Teacher names in examples (Ms. Lina, Mr. Adams, Coach Adams) are fictional; replace them with real names from the student's school if helpful.
  • This is the last Bonus Class of Level 4; Level 5's bonus series (Story Science) builds on these speaking skills with storytelling and sentence stress.
