First Sound Detectives

محققو الصوت الأول
Big idea: Every word starts with a sound. Good listeners can catch that first sound, find other words that start the same way, and even spot letters that play tricks.
كل كلمة تبدأ بصوت. المستمع الذكي يلتقط هذا الصوت الأول، ويجد كلمات أخرى تبدأ بالصوت نفسه، بل ويكتشف الحروف التي تخدعنا.

Objectives

I can hear and say the first sound of a word.أستطيع أن أسمع أول صوت في الكلمة وأنطقه.
I can find things that start with the same sound (I spy).أستطيع أن أجد أشياء تبدأ بالصوت نفسه (لعبة أرى بعيني).
I can say a silly sentence where the words start the same (Six silly suns).أستطيع أن أقول جملة مضحكة كلماتها تبدأ بالصوت نفسه.

Builds on

  • Bonus Class 1: letter names vs letter sounds, lips / teeth / tongue

  • Lessons 6-8: numbers zero-ten, clock; red, blue, yellow, green

listening, phonological awareness, first sounds, alliteration, linguistics

Linguistics Corner — Every word has a front door · لكل كلمة باب أمامي

A word is a little train of sounds. Sun is /s/ + /ʌ/ + /n/. The first sound is the engine at the front, and it is the easiest one to hear.الكلمة قطار صغير من الأصوات: sun هي /s/ + /ʌ/ + /n/. الصوت الأول هو القاطرة في المقدمة، وهو أسهل صوت نسمعه.
Hearing the first sound of a word is an important early step towards reading in languages written with letters, English and Arabic too.سماع الصوت الأول في الكلمة خطوة مبكرة مهمة نحو القراءة في اللغات التي تُكتب بالحروف، ومنها الإنجليزية والعربية.
Detectives listen, they do not just look! Cat, kite and clock all start with the same sound /k/, but with two different letters: C and K.المحقق يستمع ولا يكتفي بالنظر! cat و kite و clock تبدأ كلها بالصوت نفسه /k/ لكن بحرفين مختلفين: C و K.
Some letters play tricks at the start of a word: xylophone starts with the sound /z/ (like zebra), and one starts with the sound /w/ (like water), even though it is spelled with O.بعض الحروف تخدعنا في أول الكلمة: xylophone تبدأ بالصوت /z/ مثل zebra، و one تبدأ بالصوت /w/ مثل water مع أنها تُكتب بحرف O.
Repeating the first sound is called alliteration. It makes names easy to remember, which is why so many cartoon characters have it: Mickey Mouse, Donald Duck, Peppa Pig.تكرار الصوت الأول يسمى الجناس الاستهلالي، وهو يجعل الأسماء سهلة الحفظ، ولهذا تحمله أسماء كثير من الشخصيات الكرتونية: Mickey Mouse و Donald Duck و Peppa Pig.

Arabic Bridge

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
sun, six, seven: /s/س: سمكة، سيارةMost English first sounds have an Arabic friend: s = س, m = م, n = ن, l = ل, d = د, f = ف, k = ك, z = ز, r = ر, y = ي, w = و.
معظم الأصوات الأولى في الإنجليزية لها صديق في العربية: s = س، m = م، n = ن، l = ل، d = د، f = ف، k = ك، z = ز، r = ر، y = ي، w = و.
three /θ/ vs tree /t/ثلاثة (ث) و تفاحة (ت)Arabic has both sounds: three starts like ث in ثلاثة, tree starts like ت. In everyday Egyptian Arabic ث often becomes ت or س (most Gulf dialects keep ث), so three can turn into 'tree' or 'sree'. Tongue out a little for ث!
العربية فيها الصوتان: three تبدأ مثل الثاء في ثلاثة، و tree تبدأ مثل التاء. في العامية المصرية تتحول الثاء كثيراً إلى تاء أو سين (ومعظم لهجات الخليج تحافظ على الثاء)، فتصبح three 'tree' أو 'sree'. أخرِج طرف اللسان قليلاً مع الثاء!
van /v/ and pen /p/لا يوجد ڤ ولا پ في الأبجدية العربيةArabic has no /v/ and no /p/, so van becomes 'fan' and pen becomes 'ben'. For /v/: teeth on the lip like ف, but switch the buzz on. (Arabic writers sometimes borrow the letter ڤ for v in foreign words.)
لا يوجد في العربية صوت /v/ ولا /p/، فتصبح van 'fan' و pen 'ben'. لنطق /v/: ضع أسنانك على شفتك كما في الفاء، لكن شغّل اهتزاز الحلق. (أحياناً يُستعار الحرف ڤ لكتابة v في الكلمات الأجنبية.)
green, goat: /g/ج في العامية المصرية، ق في كثير من لهجات الخليجStandard Arabic has no letter for /g/, but children already say it: Egyptians say ج as /g/ (جمل = gamal) and many Gulf speakers say ق as /g/ (قهوة = gahwa).
لا يوجد حرف لصوت /g/ في الفصحى، لكن الأطفال ينطقونه أصلاً: المصريون ينطقون الجيم /g/ (جمل)، وكثير من أهل الخليج ينطقون القاف /g/ (قهوة).
apple, egg, orange, umbrellaأرنب تبدأ بالهمزة ءIn Arabic every word starts with a consonant; even أرنب starts with hamza, a little catch in the throat. Many English words start straight away with a vowel sound: apple, egg, ice cream, orange, umbrella.
في العربية تبدأ كل كلمة بصوت صامت (حرف)، حتى أرنب تبدأ بالهمزة، وهي وقفة صغيرة في الحلق. أما الإنجليزية ففيها كلمات كثيرة تبدأ مباشرة بصوت علّة (حركة): apple و egg و orange و umbrella.
Big Blue Ball (alliteration)خيط حرير على حيط خليل (بالعامية)Arabic tongue twisters (like this well-known colloquial one) also play with similar sounds at the start of words. Both languages enjoy this game!
الجمل العربية الصعبة النطق (مثل هذه الجملة العامية المشهورة) تلعب أيضاً بأصوات متشابهة في أوائل الكلمات. اللغتان تحبان هذه اللعبة!

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
fan (for van)vanArabic has no /v/. The mouth is the same as /f/ (teeth on the lip) but /v/ buzzes: hand on throat.
ben (for pen)penArabic has no /p/. Use the puff test from Bonus Class 1.
tree or sree (for three)threeIn Egyptian colloquial Arabic (and some other dialects) ث is often said as ت or س; most Gulf dialects keep ث. The child knows ث from Standard Arabic: tongue tip between the teeth.
'C' or 'see' given as the first sound of cat/k/The child answers with the letter name or the letter they have seen, not the sound they hear. Detectives listen first.
the whole word or the first syllable ('sev' for seven)/s/Young children often hear the first beat, not the first single sound. Stretch the word (sssseven) and stop after the first sound.

Students need at home

  • Something red, blue, yellow or green

1. Warm-up: Detective Badges and the Mystery Bag

0–5 min
إحماء: شارات المحققين والكيس الغامض
Method: Role-play hook + listening guess (curiosity)
Purpose: Turn the children into detectives and get them listening for clues.
Teacher says

“Hello, detectives! Put on your detective hat. Take your magnifying glass!”

“Lumi has a mystery bag. What is inside? Listen to the clue: /sss/... it is yellow... it is in the sky!”

“Yes! The sun! /s/ /s/ sun!”

“Today we are First Sound Detectives!”

  • مرحباً أيها المحققون! البسوا قبعة المحقق وخذوا العدسة المكبّرة!
  • لومي معه كيس غامض. ماذا فيه؟ اسمعوا الدليل: /sss/... أصفر... في السماء!
  • نعم! الشمس: sun!
  • اليوم نحن محققو الصوت الأول!
Students do
  • Make a magnifying glass with their fingers and look through it.
  • Guess 3 mystery objects from a first sound + one clue (sun, fish, clock).
  • Say the word and its first sound together.
Tips:
  • Pull the picture slowly out of the bag (or reveal it slowly on the slide) to build suspense.
  • Quickly review the open-hand (name) / fist (sound) signs from Bonus Class 1.

2. Linguistics Corner: Catch the First Sound

5–13 min
ركن اللغويات: التقط الصوت الأول
Method: Explicit phonological awareness: stretch-and-stop with a 'sound train' + whole-group response
Purpose: Learn that a word is a train of sounds and practice isolating the first one.
Teacher says

“A word is a train. Listen: sssss-un. The engine is /s/!”

“Watch my hand. I stretch the word... and stop at the engine: mmmm... /m/! Moon!”

“Your turn. Stretch it with me: fffff-ish. Stop! What is the engine? /f/!”

“Detectives listen. They do not shout the letter name. Fist up: sound!”

  • الكلمة مثل القطار، والصوت الأول هو القاطرة في المقدمة.
  • نمدّ الكلمة ثم نتوقف عند القاطرة: mmmm... /m/!
  • المحقق يستمع ويقول الصوت، لا اسم الحرف. ارفع قبضتك: صوت!
Students do
  • Stretch each word with the teacher, sliding a hand along the arm (shoulder to wrist), and stop at the first sound.
  • Say the first sound with the fist sign.
  • Point to the engine of a 3-car train on the slide while saying the first sound.
Exercise · say-the-sound — Look at the picture. Stretch the word. Say only the first sound.
انظر إلى الصورة، مدّ الكلمة، وقل الصوت الأول فقط.
  1. (picture) sun → /s/
  2. (picture) moon → /m/
  3. (picture) zebra → /z/
  4. (picture) red → /r/
  5. (picture) yellow → /j/ (the y sound in yes)
  6. (picture) water → /w/
  7. (picture) van → /v/ (buzz, not /f/)
  8. (picture) green → /g/
Tips:
  • Start with sounds you can stretch (/s/, /m/, /f/, /z/, /n/, /l/, /r/); they are much easier to isolate than /d/ or /g/.
  • If a child says 'sev' for seven, stretch again and stop earlier: 'ssss... stop! Just the engine.'

3. I Spy with Lumi and the Trickster Cases

13–22 min
لعبة أرى بعيني مع لومي وقضايا المخادعين
Method: Classic I-spy game on a picture scene + one real-object hunt + guided discovery of spelling 'tricks' (gamified, multisensory)
Purpose: Match a first sound to known words in a fast game, and discover that the sound, not the look of the letter, is what counts.
Teacher says

“I spy with my little eye something that starts with /s/... It is in the sky!”

“Point to it! Yes, the sun!”

“Now look in your room. Find something that starts with /b/! Run! 10 seconds!”

“Show me! A ball! /b/ ball! Something blue! /b/ blue!”

“Detective case! Cat and kite. Same first sound? Listen: /k/at, /k/ite. Yes! But look: C and K. Two letters, one sound!”

“Big case! One... /w/-un. It starts like water! But it is written with O. Tricky letter!”

“Xylophone: /z/... like zebra! X is playing a trick!”

  • أرى بعيني الصغيرة شيئاً يبدأ بالصوت /s/... إنه في السماء!
  • الآن ابحث في غرفتك عن شيء يبدأ بالصوت /b/! أسرع، معك 10 ثوانٍ!
  • قضية جديدة: cat و kite تبدآن بالصوت نفسه /k/، لكن بحرفين مختلفين!
  • قضية كبيرة: one تبدأ بالصوت /w/ مثل water، لكنها تُكتب بحرف O!
  • و xylophone تبدأ بالصوت /z/ مثل zebra. الحرف X يخدعنا!
  • المحقق الذكي يستمع إلى الصوت، ولا يكتفي بالنظر إلى الحرف.
Students do
  • Point on the slide (or use the Teams annotation pen) to the thing that starts with the sound.
  • Run and bring one object from home that starts with /b/, then say its name and first sound.
  • Listen to the trickster words and say their first sound.
  • Give a detective thumbs up when they catch a trick.
Exercise · point-and-say — Listen to the first sound. Find the thing in the picture that starts with it. Point and say the word. Trickster cases: say the first sound you hear.
استمع للصوت الأول، وابحث في الصورة عن الشيء الذي يبدأ به، ثم أشر إليه وقل الكلمة. في القضايا المخادعة: قل الصوت الأول الذي تسمعه.
  1. I spy /t/ ... it is green and tall → tree
  2. I spy /z/ ... it has stripes → zebra
  3. I spy /k/ ... it tells the time → clock
  4. I spy /f/ ... it swims → fish
  5. I spy /v/ ... it has wheels → van
  6. Trickster case: cat / kite → both /k/ (letters C and K)
  7. Trickster case: one → /w/, like water (letter O)
  8. Trickster case: xylophone → /z/, like zebra (letter X)
Tips:
  • Keep the object hunt to one round of 10-15 seconds and count down out loud so children come back to the camera.
  • Accept objects from home with Arabic names if the child does not know the English word: say the English word for them, and check whether the first sound still matches. If not, it's a great discovery!
  • The 'trickster' discovery is the heart of the class: children learn early that English spelling does not always match the sound, and that listening is the detective's main tool.
  • Do not explain why one is spelled with O; for this age it is enough to notice and enjoy the trick.

4. Movement Break: Jump on the Sound

22–26 min
استراحة حركة: اقفز على الصوت
Method: TPR listening game (whole body response)
Purpose: Move the body while still listening for one first sound.
Teacher says

“Stand up, detectives! Our sound is /s/.”

“If the word starts with /s/, jump! If not, stand still like a statue.”

“Sun! (jump) Ten! (statue) Six! (jump) Seven! (jump) Blue! (statue)”

  • قفوا أيها المحققون! صوتنا الآن /s/.
  • إذا بدأت الكلمة بـ /s/ اقفز، وإذا لم تبدأ به قف مثل التمثال.
Students do
  • Jump for words that start with /s/ and freeze for others.
  • Sit down quietly at the end: 'sssit'.
Exercise · listen-and-move — Jump if the word starts with /s/. Be a statue if it does not.
اقفز إذا بدأت الكلمة بالصوت /s/، وقف كالتمثال إذا لم تبدأ به.
  1. sun → jump
  2. ten → statue
  3. six → jump
  4. zebra → statue (/z/ buzzes)
  5. seven → jump
  6. blue → statue
  7. three → statue (/θ/, not /s/)
  8. sit → jump
Tips:
  • zebra and three are deliberate traps: /z/ is the buzzing partner of /s/, and three can come out as 'sree' (see the Arabic Bridge). Celebrate children who freeze.
  • Ask children to step back so the camera sees their whole body.

5. Arabic Bridge: Same Sound, Two Alphabets

26–33 min
جسر العربية: صوت واحد وأبجديتان
Method: Contrastive analysis: matching English first sounds to Arabic letters
Purpose: Use Arabic as a strength: most first sounds already exist in Arabic, and only a few are new.
Teacher says

“Sun: /s/. In Arabic: سمكة, /s/! The same sound. Friends!”

“Moon: /m/. موز, /m/! Friends!”

“Three: put your tongue out a little: /θ/. Like ث in ثلاثة! Tree: /t/, like ت.”

“Van: /v/. Is there an Arabic letter? No! New sound: teeth on lip like ف... and buzz!”

  • sun تبدأ بالصوت /s/، وسمكة تبدأ بالسين: الصوت نفسه!
  • three تبدأ مثل الثاء في ثلاثة: أخرِج طرف لسانك قليلاً. أما tree فتبدأ مثل التاء.
  • van تبدأ بصوت ليس في العربية: الأسنان على الشفة مثل الفاء، لكن مع اهتزاز في الحلق.
Students do
  • Say the English word, then the Arabic word with the same first sound.
  • Point to the Arabic letter on the slide (or hold up an Arabic letter card) that matches.
  • Cross arms when there is no Arabic letter (van, pen).
  • Say three and tree with a mirror: tongue out for three, tongue behind the teeth for tree.
Exercise · match — Say the English word. Point to the Arabic letter with the same first sound. Cross your arms if there is none.
قل الكلمة الإنجليزية، وأشر إلى الحرف العربي الذي له الصوت الأول نفسه. اعقد ذراعيك إذا لم يوجد.
  1. sun → س
  2. moon → م
  3. red → ر
  4. yellow → ي
  5. water → و
  6. three → ث
  7. tree → ت
  8. van → no Arabic letter (cross arms)
Tips:
  • Say 'friends' or 'close', not 'exactly the same': English r, for example, is softer than Arabic ر (the tongue does not tap or trill).
  • For Egyptian children, three vs tree is a real challenge because Egyptian colloquial Arabic often turns ث into ت or س. Be patient and use the mirror; it is not a 'wrong Arabic', just a different sound habit.
  • If children ask about green: ج in Egyptian Arabic and ق in many Gulf dialects already sound like /g/.
  • The vowel-start point (apple vs أرنب with its hamza) is in the Arabic Bridge table; use it only if a child asks.

6. Silly Sound Chants: Six Silly Suns

33–40 min
أناشيد الأصوات المضحكة: ست شموس مضحكة
Method: Alliteration chants with actions + creative production (children invent their own)
Purpose: Hear and say many words with the same first sound, and create a personal alliterative phrase.
Teacher says

“Listen to Lumi's chant: Big blue ball! Big blue ball! What sound do you hear again and again? /b/!”

“Say it and bounce: Big - blue - ball!”

“More chants: Six silly suns! (wiggle) Five funny fish! (swim) Ten tall trees! (stretch up)”

“Cartoon names love this game: Mickey Mouse, Donald Duck, Peppa Pig!”

“Now make your own with your name: Sara's sun! Ziad's zebra! Noor's nose! Lumi's lion!”

  • اسمعوا نشيد لومي: Big blue ball! ما الصوت الذي يتكرر؟ /b/!
  • نقولها ونحن نقفز مثل الكرة!
  • حتى أسماء الشخصيات الكرتونية تستخدم هذه اللعبة: Mickey Mouse و Donald Duck و Peppa Pig!
  • الآن اصنع جملة مضحكة باسمك: سارة مع sun، وزياد مع zebra، ونور مع nose!
Students do
  • Chant each line 2 times with its action, faster the second time.
  • Say which sound repeats in each chant.
  • Make a name chant: their name + a word with the same first sound.
Exercise · complete-the-chant — Finish the silly chant with a word that starts with the same sound.
أكمل النشيد المضحك بكلمة تبدأ بالصوت نفسه.
  1. Big blue ... → ball (or another /b/ word)
  2. Six silly ... → suns (or another /s/ word)
  3. Five funny ... → fish (or another /f/ word)
  4. Ten tall ... → trees (or another /t/ word)
  5. My name + a word: Ziad's ... → zebra (any /z/ word)
Tips:
  • Silly is good: nonsense combinations make the sound stand out and children remember them.
  • Accept any word with the right first sound, even an Arabic word; then offer an English one.
  • 'tall', 'funny' and 'silly' are support words shown with a picture or gesture; children do not need to learn them.
  • Only if there is time left: try the Arabic tongue twister from the Arabic Bridge table (خيط حرير على حيط خليل) and ask which sounds repeat.

7. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

40–45 min
بطاقة الخروج، هل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check (same or different + first sound) + curiosity close
Purpose: Check each child's first-sound listening and send home a fact to share.
Teacher says

“Last case, detectives! I say two words. Same first sound: thumbs up. Different: thumbs down.”

“Then tell me the first sound of one picture.”

“Did you know? Cat and kite start with the same sound but different letters. English letters like to play tricks. Detectives listen!”

“Mission at home: play I spy with your family in English!”

  • القضية الأخيرة: أقول كلمتين. إذا بدأتا بالصوت نفسه ارفع إبهامك، وإذا اختلفتا اخفضه.
  • هل تعلم؟ cat و kite تبدآن بالصوت نفسه لكن بحرفين مختلفين. الحروف الإنجليزية تحب الخداع، والمحقق يستمع جيداً!
  • مهمتك في البيت: العب 'أرى بعيني' مع عائلتك بالإنجليزية!
Students do
  • Show thumbs up or down for 4 word pairs.
  • Say the first sound of one picture alone.
  • Say goodbye with alliteration: 'Bye-bye, Lumi Lion!'
Tips:
  • Ask each child at least one item alone.
  • Record who confuses /v/-/f/ and /θ/-/t/ for short follow-up drills in Lessons 9-11 warm-ups.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Listen to two words. Thumbs up if they start with the same sound, thumbs down if not. Last item: say the first sound.
استمع إلى كلمتين. ارفع إبهامك إذا بدأتا بالصوت نفسه، واخفضه إذا لم تبدآ به. في السؤال الأخير قل الصوت الأول.
  1. sun - six → same (thumbs up)
  2. ten - red → different (thumbs down)
  3. cat - kite → same (thumbs up)
  4. van - five → different (thumbs down)
  5. (picture) moon: first sound? → /m/

Homework

Detective mission: play I spy at home in English with one family member. Find 3 things that start with the same sound and draw them together in one big house.

مهمة المحقق: العب 'أرى بعيني' بالإنجليزية مع أحد أفراد أسرتك. ابحث عن 3 أشياء تبدأ بالصوت نفسه وارسمها معاً داخل بيت كبير.

  • Play I spy with a family member: 'I spy something that starts with /b/.'
  • Draw a house and put 3 pictures inside that start with the same sound.
  • Say your silly name chant (Sara's sun!) to your family.

Support

  • Use only long sounds that can be stretched (/s/, /m/, /f/, /l/, /z/).
  • Give two picture choices: 'Sun or moon: which starts with /m/?'
  • Let the child answer by pointing instead of saying the sound.

Challenge

  • Find a number, a thing and a name that share a first sound (six, sun, Sara / ten, tree, Tamer).
  • Make a 3-word silly chant with no help (Seven silly snakes).
  • Listen for the last sound: sun ends with /n/, red ends with /d/.

Success criteria

  • Says the first sound (not the letter name) for at least 6 of 8 pictures.
  • Finds the picture for at least 4 of 5 I-spy clues and gives the first sound of at least 2 of 3 trickster cases.
  • Points to the matching Arabic letter (or crosses arms) for at least 6 of 8 words.
  • Makes one name chant with a matching first sound.

Watch for

  • /v/ said as /f/ (van -> fan)
  • /θ/ said as /t/ or /s/ (three -> tree / sree)
  • Letter names given instead of sounds
  • The first syllable given instead of the first sound (sev for seven)

Teacher notes

  • This class trains the ear. Children do not need to read or write letters to succeed.
  • Most of the pictures are known words from Lessons 3-8; the support words in chants (silly, tall, funny) are only shown with a picture or a gesture.
  • The trickster discovery (cat/kite, one, xylophone) is meant to plant a seed: in English, listen first, then look.
  • Bonus Class 3 moves from the start of words to the end of words (rhyme) and to beats (syllables).
  • Timing: this class is 45 minutes (5 + 8 + 9 + 4 + 7 + 7 + 5), which suits 4-6 year olds online. Keep every stage short and moving, and add a 30-second stand-and-stretch between seated stages when attention drops. If the group is slow, trim in this order: skip the object hunt in I Spy (use the picture scene only), do 3 chants instead of 5, then match 6 words instead of 8 in the Arabic Bridge (keep three, tree and van). Never cut the trickster cases, the movement break or the exit ticket.
