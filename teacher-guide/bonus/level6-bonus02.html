The Language of Rules

لغة القواعد والأنظمة
Big idea: English measures rules on a scale: must and have to mean 'necessary', mustn't means 'forbidden', and don't have to means 'free choice'; and adverbs tell us how we follow the rules, built with a small set of spelling patterns.
الإنجليزية تقيس القواعد على مقياس: must و have to تعنيان 'ضروري'، و mustn't تعني 'ممنوع'، و don't have to تعني 'أنت حر'؛ والظروف تخبرنا كيف نتبع القواعد، وتُبنى بأنماط إملائية قليلة.

Linguistics Corner — The strange club of modal verbs · النادي الغريب للأفعال الشكلية

Modal verbs (can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must) break the normal rules: no -s (she must, not she musts), no 'to' after them (must go), no -ing or -ed forms, and questions without do (Must I go?).الأفعال الشكلية (can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must) تكسر القواعد العادية: لا -s (she must)، ولا to بعدها (must go)، ولا صيغة -ing أو -ed، والسؤال بلا do (Must I go?).
have to is not a true modal (some grammars call it a 'semi-modal'): it behaves like a normal verb (she has to, did you have to?, I had to, I will have to). That is why its past 'had to' is also the past of must: must has no past form of its own.have to ليست فعلاً شكلياً حقيقياً (تسميها بعض كتب القواعد 'شبه شكلية')، بل تتصرف كفعل عادي (she has to, did you have to?, I had to). ولهذا نستخدم had to ماضياً لـ must أيضاً، لأن must ليس لها ماضٍ.
History secret: must was once a past tense! In Old English the verb 'motan' meant 'may, be allowed to', and its past form 'moste' became today's must. Could, would, should and might were also past forms once. That is why the club looks so strange today.سرّ تاريخي: must كانت في الأصل فعلاً ماضياً! في الإنجليزية القديمة كان الفعل motan يعني 'يجوز له / يُسمح له'، وصيغته الماضية moste صارت must اليوم. وكذلك could و would و should و might كانت صيغاً ماضية، ولهذا يبدو هذا النادي غريباً اليوم.
must has two meanings. Rule: 'You must wear a seatbelt.' Logic: 'You must be tired after the road trip' (= I'm almost sure). Linguists call the first deontic modality (rules) and the second epistemic modality (what we think is true). Arabic لا بد has the same two jobs: لا بد أن تذهب / لا بد أنك متعب.لـ must معنيان. قاعدة: You must wear a seatbelt. واستنتاج: You must be tired after the road trip (أي: أنا شبه متأكد). يسمّي اللغويون الأول 'الإلزام' والثاني 'الاستنتاج المعرفي'. وفي العربية 'لا بد' لها الوظيفتان نفسهما: لا بد أن تذهب / لا بد أنك متعب.
The adverb ending -ly comes from Old English '-lice', related to the word 'like' and to an old Germanic word meaning 'body, shape'. So 'quickly' first meant something like 'in a quick shape' or 'quick-like'.اللاحقة -ly أصلها في الإنجليزية القديمة -lice، وهي قريبة من كلمة like ومن كلمة قديمة تعني 'جسم، هيئة'. فكلمة quickly كانت تعني تقريباً 'على هيئة سريعة'.

Arabic Bridge

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
must / have toيجب أن / عليك أن / لا بد أن / لازمSame meaning: necessary, no choice. English has two forms: must (often the speaker's own rule or a written rule) and have to (a rule from outside: school, law, parents).
المعنى نفسه: ضروري ولا خيار. للإنجليزية صيغتان: must (غالباً قاعدة من المتكلم نفسه أو قاعدة مكتوبة) و have to (قاعدة من الخارج: المدرسة أو القانون أو الأهل).
mustn'tيجب ألّا / ممنوع / لا يجوز / (في الاستعمال الشائع: لا يجب أن)Prohibition: it is forbidden. In everyday Arabic, لا يجب أن تفعل very often means 'you mustn't'. Some careful writers of MSA prefer يجب ألّا, because word by word لا يجب can mean 'it is not necessary'; this usage is debated. Either way, it causes the trap below.
المنع: الأمر ممنوع. في الاستعمال اليومي كثيراً ما تعني 'لا يجب أن تفعل' المنع. ويفضّل بعض المدققين في الفصحى 'يجب ألّا'، لأن 'لا يجب' بحرفيتها قد تعني 'ليس واجباً'، والمسألة محل خلاف. وفي الحالتين هي سبب الفخ التالي.
don't have toليس عليك أن / لستَ مضطراً / لا داعي / مش لازم (مصري) / مو لازم (خليجي)THE TRAP: 'don't have to' does NOT mean ممنوع. It means 'not necessary, you can choose'. 'You don't have to come' = لستَ مضطراً إلى أن تأتي. 'You mustn't come' = ممنوع أن تأتي. Word-by-word translation is the danger: لا يجب (usually 'forbidden') looks like 'don't have to', and مش لازم / مو لازم ('not necessary') looks like 'not must' = mustn't. Both mistakes change the meaning completely.
الفخ: don't have to لا تعني 'ممنوع'، بل تعني 'غير ضروري، أنت حر'. You don't have to come = لستَ مضطراً إلى أن تأتي. You mustn't come = ممنوع أن تأتي. الخطر في الترجمة الحرفية: 'لا يجب' (وغالباً معناها المنع) تشبه don't have to، و'مش لازم / مو لازم' (أي غير ضروري) تشبه حرفياً 'not must' أي mustn't. وكلا الخطأين يقلب المعنى تماماً.
speak clearly / walk quicklyيتكلم بوضوح / يمشي بسرعة / دخل مسرعاًEnglish usually adds -ly to the adjective. Arabic often uses بـ + a noun (بوضوح، بهدوء، بعناية) or a حال (مسرعاً). So learners sometimes forget -ly and use the adjective: 'drive careful'.
الإنجليزية تضيف غالباً -ly إلى الصفة. أما العربية فتستخدم كثيراً بـ + مصدر (بوضوح، بهدوء، بعناية) أو الحال (مسرعاً)، لذلك ينسى المتعلم -ly أحياناً ويقول drive careful.
He speaks English well.يتكلم الإنجليزية جيداً / بشكل جيدجيد is an adjective, but جيداً here does the job of an English adverb. In English the adjective is good and the adverb is well: two different words, not good + ly.
جيد صفة، لكن 'جيداً' تؤدي هنا معنى الظرف الإنجليزي (adverb). في الإنجليزية الصفة good والظرف well: كلمتان مختلفتان، وليست good + ly.

1. Warm-up: Strange Rules of the World

0–5 min
إحماء: قواعد غريبة حول العالم
Method: True/false quiz with real-world facts (curiosity hook)
Purpose: Wake up the rule language from Lesson 5 with surprising real rules, and show that rules are part of 'English in the world'.
Teacher says

“Rules are everywhere, and some are very strange! True or false? Show your green tick for true, red X for false.”

“One: In Singapore, shops mustn't sell chewing gum. Two: In Australia, adult citizens have to vote in national elections. Three: In Japan, students often have to clean their own classrooms.”

“Four: In Venice, tourists mustn't feed the pigeons in the main square. Five: In the UK, you have to drive on the right. Six: In Finland, students have to pay for their school lunch.”

“Which rule is the strangest for you? Which rule would you like in your country?”

  • القواعد في كل مكان، وبعضها غريب جداً! صحيح أم خطأ؟
  • أي قاعدة هي الأغرب برأيك؟ وأي قاعدة تتمنى وجودها في بلدك؟
Students do
  • Vote true/false with the cards for 6 rules.
  • Guess the correct version of the false ones.
  • Say which rule they like: 'I think students should... because...' or 'I'd like a rule that...'.
Exercise · true-false — Is the rule true or false? If false, correct it.
هل القاعدة صحيحة أم خاطئة؟ إن كانت خاطئة فصححها.
  1. In Singapore, shops mustn't sell chewing gum. → True (sale has been banned since 1992; since 2004 only therapeutic gum, such as nicotine gum, can be sold by pharmacists)
  2. In Australia, adult citizens have to vote in national elections. → True (voting has been compulsory for adult citizens in federal elections since 1924; people who don't vote can get a small fine)
  3. In Japan, students often have to clean their own classrooms. → True
  4. In Venice, tourists mustn't feed the pigeons in St Mark's Square. → True (it is banned, and people who do it can be fined)
  5. In the UK, you have to drive on the right. → False: you have to drive on the left.
  6. In Finland, students have to pay for their school lunch. → False: in Finland, school lunch has been free in basic education for decades; students don't have to pay.
Tips:
  • Move fast: the facts are a hook, not a test.
  • Item 6 is a perfect seed for the next stage: 'don't have to pay' = it's free, not forbidden.

2. Linguistics Corner: The Rule Scale and the Modal Club

5–15 min
ركن اللغويات: مقياس القواعد ونادي الأفعال الشكلية
Method: Guided discovery with a visual scale + concept-check questions
Purpose: Place must, have to, mustn't and don't have to on one scale, discover the modal club rules, and learn the history of must.
Teacher says

“Look at the Rule Scale. On the left: FORBIDDEN, red. In the middle: YOUR CHOICE, yellow. On the right: NECESSARY, green.”

“'Students have to wear a uniform.' Is there a choice? No. Green. 'Students mustn't use phones in the exam.' Is it allowed? No. Red. 'Students don't have to join the charity club.' Is it forbidden? No! Is it necessary? No! It's optional. Yellow.”

“must and have to are both green. The small difference: must is often my own rule or a written rule, 'I must call my grandma, I miss her', 'Visitors must sign in'. have to is a rule from outside: 'I have to wear a uniform. It's the school rule.'”

“Now the club. must is a modal, like will from our last Bonus Class: no -s, no 'to'. have to is a normal verb: she has to, I had to. Because must has no past, we say 'Yesterday I had to study'.”

“History secret: must was once a past tense! Its old meaning was 'was allowed to'. Languages change jobs, just like will changed from 'want'.”

  • على اليسار ممنوع (أحمر)، وفي الوسط اختياري (أصفر)، وعلى اليمين ضروري (أخضر).
  • must غالباً قاعدة المتكلم أو قاعدة مكتوبة، و have to قاعدة من الخارج.
  • must ليس لها ماضٍ، فنقول had to.
Students do
  • Answer concept-check questions: Is it necessary? Is it allowed? Is there a choice?
  • Hold up red, yellow or green for each sentence in the exercise.
  • Find the modal club rules in 4 example sentences and write them as 3 short rules.
Exercise · rule-scale — Show red (forbidden), yellow (your choice) or green (necessary) for each sentence.
ارفع الأحمر (ممنوع) أو الأصفر (اختياري) أو الأخضر (ضروري) لكل جملة.
  1. You mustn't run near the swimming pool. → red - forbidden
  2. You don't have to bring food. Brunch is free. → yellow - your choice
  3. Players have to wear a helmet. → green - necessary
  4. We don't have to volunteer. It's optional. → yellow - your choice
  5. You must show your ticket at the door. → green - necessary
  6. You mustn't share your password. → red - forbidden
  7. Hamad doesn't have to tidy up today. His brother is doing it. → yellow - your choice
  8. Permission is required: you have to ask the teacher first. → green - necessary
Tips:
  • In everyday speech, have to is much more common than must, especially in American English; must sounds strong or formal. Tell students this so they do not overuse must.
  • Keep the scale on screen for the whole class: it is the anchor for the Arabic Bridge.

3. Arabic Bridge: The 'لا يجب' Trap

15–23 min
جسر العربية: فخ 'لا يجب'
Method: Contrastive analysis + translation in both directions
Purpose: Show exactly why Arabic speakers confuse mustn't and don't have to, and fix it with clear Arabic equivalents.
Teacher says

“Imagine: Sara invites Noor to a brunch. Noor is busy. Sara thinks: 'مش لازم تيجي، عادي' (it's not necessary). لازم = must, مش = not, so she says: 'You mustn't come!' How does Noor feel?”

“Yes, Noor thinks Sara doesn't want her! Sara wanted: 'You don't have to come.'”

“Here is the secret. don't have to = ليس عليك / لستَ مضطراً / مش لازم / مو لازم. mustn't = ممنوع / يجب ألّا / لا يجوز.”

“So Arabic words can't guide you word by word: 'مش لازم' means 'not necessary', but 'لا يجب' is usually used for 'forbidden'. Ask yourself one question: is it forbidden, or is it just not necessary?”

  • don't have to = ليس عليك / لستَ مضطراً / مش لازم.
  • mustn't = ممنوع / يجب ألّا / لا يجوز.
  • اسأل نفسك: هل الأمر ممنوع، أم فقط غير ضروري؟
Students do
  • Act the Sara-Noor mini-scene in two versions (wrong and right) and react.
  • Translate Arabic sentences into English, choosing mustn't or don't have to.
  • Write one 'trap' sentence of their own for a partner to translate.
Exercise · translate — Translate into English. Decide first: forbidden or not necessary?
ترجم إلى الإنجليزية. قرّر أولاً: ممنوع أم غير ضروري؟
  1. ممنوع استخدام الهاتف في الامتحان. → You mustn't use your phone in the exam.
  2. لستَ مضطراً إلى أن تدفع، فالدخول مجاني. → You don't have to pay. Entry is free.
  3. (مصري) مش لازم تصحى بدري بكرة، إجازة. → You don't have to get up early tomorrow. It's a holiday.
  4. يجب ألّا تخبر أحداً بكلمة السر. → You mustn't tell anyone the password.
  5. ليس علينا أن نرتدي الزي المدرسي يوم الخميس. → We don't have to wear our uniform on Thursday.
  6. عليها أن تنهي المشروع قبل الأحد. → She has to finish the project before Sunday.
  7. اضطُررتُ إلى المذاكرة طوال عطلة نهاية الأسبوع. → I had to study all weekend.
  8. (خليجي) مو لازم تجيب هدية. → You don't have to bring a present.
Tips:
  • Use the students' own dialect forms (Egyptian مش لازم, Gulf مو لازم / مب لازم): their meaning maps neatly onto don't have to, but warn that word by word they look like 'not must'.
  • Do not tell students that 'لا يجب' is wrong Arabic; say that in everyday use it usually means 'forbidden', that some MSA purists prefer يجب ألّا, and that it cannot be translated word by word.

4. Rule Writers: Signs, Apps and Clubs

23–32 min
كتّاب القواعد: لافتات وتطبيقات ونوادٍ
Method: Task-based rewriting of authentic signs + guided choice
Purpose: Turn real-world signs and situations into full rule sentences with the right modal.
Teacher says

“Signs speak in short words. Your job is to say them in full English. 'NO PHONES' becomes 'You mustn't use your phone here.'”

“'HELMETS REQUIRED' becomes...? Yes: 'You have to wear a helmet.' 'TICKETS OPTIONAL' becomes...? 'You don't have to buy a ticket.'”

“Now complete the rules for the crew's charity football match.”

  • اللافتات قصيرة، ومهمتك أن تقولها بجملة إنجليزية كاملة.
  • الآن أكملوا قواعد مباراة كرة القدم الخيرية.
Students do
  • Rewrite 5 sign pictures as full rules (spoken, then in the chat).
  • Complete the charity match rules with must, mustn't, have to, has to or don't/doesn't have to.
  • Explain each choice with the scale color.
Exercise · gap-fill — Complete the charity match rules with must, mustn't, have to / has to or don't / doesn't have to.
أكمل قواعد مباراة كرة القدم الخيرية بالكلمة المناسبة.
  1. All players ___ wear shin pads. It's mandatory. → have to / must
  2. Fans ___ throw bottles onto the pitch. → mustn't
  3. You ___ pay to watch. Entry is free, but donations are welcome. → don't have to
  4. The goalkeeper ___ wear a different color shirt. → has to / must
  5. Volunteers ___ stay after the match. It's optional. → don't have to
  6. Players ___ touch the ball with their hands, except the goalkeeper. → mustn't
  7. Omar is the captain, so he ___ shake hands with the referee. It's the rule. → has to / must
  8. Ziad is only a fan, so he ___ wear boots. → doesn't have to
Tips:
  • Accept must or have to in the green items; ask students which one sounds like a written rule (must).
  • Watch for 'has to' with he/she: it is the most common slip in this stage.

5. Adverb Lab: How Do We Follow the Rules?

32–42 min
مختبر الظروف: كيف نتبع القواعد؟
Method: Inductive spelling-pattern discovery + transformation drill
Purpose: Discover the adverb spelling families and the irregular adverbs, including the hard/hardly trap.
Teacher says

“Rules tell us what to do; adverbs tell us how. 'You must drive carefully.' 'You have to speak quietly.'”

“Look at these pairs and find the spelling rules: quick - quickly, easy - easily, gentle - gently, basic - basically, careful - carefully.”

“Rule 1: most adjectives + ly. Rule 2: consonant + y: change y to i, + ly: happy - happily. Rule 3: consonant + -le: drop the e, add y: gentle - gently, simple - simply. Rule 4: -ic: + ally: basic - basically, dramatic - dramatically. The famous exception: public - publicly.”

“And the rebels: good becomes well. fast stays fast, hard stays hard. Careful: hardly is a different word. 'I hardly studied' means I almost didn't study!”

  • القواعد تقول ماذا نفعل، والظروف تقول كيف نفعل.
  • اكتشفوا قواعد الإملاء من الأزواج.
  • good تصبح well، و fast و hard تبقيان كما هما. و hardly تعني 'بالكاد / تقريباً لا'.
Students do
  • Sort 10 adjectives into the five spelling families.
  • Transform adjectives into adverbs and write them with the correct spelling.
  • Say a rule with each adverb: 'You must cross the road carefully.'
Exercise · transform — Write the adverb. Watch the spelling family.
اكتب الظرف وانتبه لنوع القاعدة الإملائية.
  1. careful → carefully (+ly; keep both l's)
  2. easy → easily (y -> i + ly)
  3. gentle → gently (-le -> -ly)
  4. patient → patiently (+ly)
  5. basic → basically (-ic -> -ically)
  6. happy → happily (y -> i + ly)
  7. accurate → accurately (keep the e + ly)
  8. terrible → terribly (-le -> -ly)
  9. public → publicly (the exception!)
  10. good → well (irregular)
  11. fast → fast (no change)
  12. hard (I studied ___ for the exam.) → hard (hardly = almost not)
Tips:
  • Students often drop the second l in carefully: careful + ly = carefully, two l's.
  • Mention true - truly, whole - wholly and full - fully only to strong students as extra exceptions.

6. Team Challenge: The Mars Base Rulebook

42–53 min
تحدي الفريق: كتاب قواعد قاعدة المريخ
Method: Creative task-based writing + peer review court (gamification)
Purpose: Produce a real set of rules with modals and adverbs, then check another team's rules for grammar and logic.
Teacher says

“Lumi has a mission for you: the first teen team is moving to a base on Mars, and they need a rulebook!”

“Each team writes 8 rules: 3 'must/have to', 3 'mustn't', 2 'don't have to'. Every rule needs one adverb: 'You must close the door carefully.' 'You don't have to exercise loudly... but you can!'”

“You have 5 minutes. Then the Rule Court: the other team checks your rules. One point for every correct rule, one bonus point for the funniest logical rule.”

“Judges, ask questions: 'Do we have to...?' 'Why mustn't we...?'”

  • مهمة من لومي: أول فريق من المراهقين سينتقل إلى قاعدة على المريخ، ويحتاجون كتاب قواعد!
  • كل فريق يكتب 8 قواعد، وفي كل قاعدة ظرف.
  • ثم محكمة القواعد: الفريق الآخر يراجع قواعدكم.
Students do
  • In teams of two, write 8 rules on the shared slide (timer 5 minutes).
  • Swap and judge the other team's rules: correct modal? correct adverb spelling? logical?
  • Ask and answer follow-up questions: 'Do we have to wear a space suit at night?' 'No, you don't have to.'
  • Fix one rule from the other team that has a mistake.
Exercise · judge-the-rule — Rule Court practice: is the rule correct? If not, fix it.
تدريب محكمة القواعد: هل القاعدة صحيحة؟ إن لم تكن فصححها.
  1. You must to check your oxygen every morning. → Wrong: You must check your oxygen every morning.
  2. Everyone have to eat dinner together. → Wrong: Everyone has to eat dinner together.
  3. You mustn't open the airlock quick. → Wrong: You mustn't open the airlock quickly.
  4. You don't have to sleep in the gym, but you can. → Correct (optional)
  5. The captain musts speak clearly on the radio. → Wrong: The captain must speak clearly on the radio.
  6. You have to walk gentlely in low gravity. → Wrong: You have to walk gently in low gravity.
  7. Last week we must repair the solar panels. → Wrong: Last week we had to repair the solar panels.
  8. You don't have to talk loudly in the lab. (meaning: it's forbidden) → Wrong meaning: You mustn't talk loudly in the lab.
Tips:
  • Model one rule of each kind before the timer starts.
  • With 2-3 students, play as one team against 'Lumi' (the teacher), who writes rules with deliberate mistakes.
  • Save the best rulebook for the class wall or the group chat.

7. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

53–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check the mustn't / don't have to difference and adverb spelling for every student.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five quick items. Three minutes.”

“Did you know? In English, 'must' has two meanings. 'You must wear a helmet' is a rule. But 'You must be tired after that road trip!' means 'I'm almost sure you are tired.' Arabic لا بد works the same way: لا بد أن تذهب / لا بد أنك متعب.”

“Your mission: find two signs in your city, mall or school with rules, and say them in full English.”

  • هل تعلم؟ must لها معنيان: قاعدة، أو استنتاج قوي. ولا بد في العربية تعمل بالطريقة نفسها.
  • مهمتك: صوّر لافتتين فيهما قواعد وقلهما بجملة إنجليزية كاملة.
Students do
  • Complete the 5 exit-ticket items.
  • Make one 'must = I'm sure' sentence about a classmate: 'Noor must be hungry!'
  • Say goodbye with a rule: 'You don't have to cry. See you next class!'
Tips:
  • Anyone who confuses mustn't and don't have to in the ticket needs the scale again in the Lesson 10 warm-up (Rules at Home).
  • Keep the 'must = I'm sure' meaning as enrichment only; do not test it.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Complete or correct.
أكمل أو صحّح.
  1. The concert is free. You ___ buy a ticket. → don't have to
  2. It's dangerous. You ___ touch that wire. → mustn't
  3. Write the adverb: easy → easily
  4. Correct it: She speaks Arabic and English very good. → She speaks Arabic and English very well.
  5. Correct it: Yesterday I must help my dad. → Yesterday I had to help my dad.

Homework

My Ideal School Rulebook: write 10 rules for your dream school: 4 have to/must, 3 mustn't, 3 don't have to. Use a different adverb in at least 5 rules and check the spelling family of each adverb.

كتاب قواعد مدرستي المثالية: اكتب 10 قواعد لمدرسة أحلامك: 4 بـ have to/must، و3 بـ mustn't، و3 بـ don't have to. استخدم ظرفاً مختلفاً في 5 قواعد على الأقل وتحقق من قاعدة إملائه.

  • Write the 10-rule rulebook.
  • Photograph or copy two real signs and rewrite them as full English rules.
  • Write 3 sentences with 'don't have to' about your weekend (I don't have to wake up early on Friday).

Support

  • Work with only two options first: mustn't (red) and don't have to (yellow), the trap pair.
  • Give the Arabic key on a card: ممنوع = mustn't, مش لازم (Egyptian) / مو لازم (Gulf) = don't have to.
  • For the Adverb Lab, give the -ly family first, then add -ily and -le only.

Challenge

  • Explain the difference: 'I must go' (I decide) vs 'I have to go' (someone else decides).
  • Write two sentences with 'must' meaning 'I'm sure' (epistemic): 'He must be at home; his car is outside.'
  • Find three adverbs that look like adjectives (fast, hard, late) and one pair where -ly changes the meaning (late / lately, hard / hardly).

Success criteria

  • Places the four forms correctly on the rule scale (at least 7 of 8).
  • Translates the Arabic trap sentences with the right meaning (at least 6 of 8).
  • Spells at least 10 of 12 adverbs correctly, including well, fast and hard.
  • Writes at least 6 correct rules in the Mars Base Rulebook with a modal and an adverb.

Watch for

  • mustn't used for 'not necessary'
  • 'must to' + verb
  • he/she have to (not has to)
  • adjective instead of adverb (drive careful, speak good)
  • hard vs hardly confusion
  • spelling: carefuly, happyly, basicly, gentlely

Teacher notes

  • must is new as a form (Lessons 5 and 10 teach have to / don't have to); present it as a member of the same rule family, not as a separate topic.
  • The must / have to difference is a tendency, not a strict rule, and it is weaker in American English, where have to dominates in speech. Do not mark must/have to swaps as errors.
  • Be honest about Arabic: in everyday usage لا يجب is widely used for prohibition; in careful MSA its use is debated (some prefer يجب ألّا). Do not judge either side; the problem for English is only word-by-word translation.
  • Lesson 10 (Rules at Home) and Lesson 11 (The Way We Talk) review this class's content directly: reuse the rule scale and the adverb families in their warm-ups.
  • Bonus Class 3 looks at where English words come from; the history of must and -ly is a preview.
