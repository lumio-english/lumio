Rhyme Time and Clap the Beats

وقت القافية وصفّق للمقاطع
Big idea: Words that rhyme sound the same at the end (cat, hat), and every word has beats called syllables that we can clap (rab-bit, el-e-phant).
الكلمات المتقافية تتشابه في آخرها (cat و hat)، ولكل كلمة نبضات تسمى المقاطع نستطيع أن نصفّق لها (rab-bit و el-e-phant).

Linguistics Corner — Words have tails and words have beats · للكلمات ذيول وللكلمات نبضات

Two words rhyme when they sound the same from the vowel to the end: c-AT, h-AT; d-OG, fr-OG. Only the start is different.تتقافى الكلمتان عندما تتشابهان من الصوت المتحرك حتى النهاية: c-AT و h-AT، و d-OG و fr-OG. البداية فقط هي المختلفة.
Rhyme is about sound, not letters: two and blue rhyme, even though their endings are spelled differently.القافية تتعلق بالصوت لا بالحروف: two و blue متقافيتان مع أن آخرهما يُكتب بطريقة مختلفة.
A syllable is a beat of the voice. Almost every beat has a vowel sound in its middle, and your jaw usually drops once for each beat: put your hand under your chin and say ba-by.المقطع نبضة من نبضات الصوت. في قلب كل نبضة تقريباً صوت متحرك، وعادةً ينزل الفك مرة مع كل نبضة: ضع يدك تحت ذقنك وقل ba-by.
The same animal can have a different number of beats in different languages: elephant has 3 beats in English, but فيل has only 1 in Arabic!الحيوان نفسه قد يختلف عدد مقاطع اسمه من لغة إلى أخرى: elephant فيها 3 مقاطع في الإنجليزية، أما فيل ففيها مقطع واحد فقط!
Songs and nursery rhymes in every language use rhyme and beat, because they help our brains remember. That is why you can remember a song better than a list.الأغاني وأناشيد الأطفال في كل اللغات تستخدم القافية والإيقاع لأنهما يساعدان الدماغ على الحفظ، ولهذا نحفظ الأغنية أسهل من قائمة كلمات.

Arabic Bridge

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
cat / hatقطة / بطةArabic words rhyme in the same way: قطة and بطة sound the same at the end. Children already have the 'rhyme ear' from Arabic songs.
الكلمات العربية تتقافى بالطريقة نفسها: قطة وبطة تتشابهان في آخرهما. الأطفال يملكون 'أذن القافية' من الأناشيد العربية.
rhyme in English songsالقافية في الشعر العربيArabic poetry is famous for rhyme: in a traditional Arabic poem (qasida), every line can end with the same rhyme, from the first line to the last.
الشعر العربي مشهور بالقافية: في القصيدة العربية التقليدية قد ينتهي كل بيت بالقافية نفسها من أول بيت إلى آخره.
rab-bit (2 beats)أر-نب (مقطعان)We can clap Arabic words too: أرنب = ar-nab (2), كتاب = ki-taab (2), مدرسة = mad-ra-sa (3). Syllables exist in every language.
نستطيع أن نصفّق للكلمات العربية أيضاً: أرنب = أر-نب (2)، كتاب = كِ-تاب (2)، مدرسة = مَد-رَ-سة (3). المقاطع موجودة في كل اللغات.
cat (1), duck (1), fish (1), elephant (3)قطة (2)، بطة (2)، سمكة (3)، فيل (1)The same animal can have a different number of beats in each language, so we must clap the English word, not the Arabic one.
الحيوان نفسه قد يكون له عدد مختلف من المقاطع في كل لغة، لذلك نصفّق للكلمة الإنجليزية لا للعربية.
black, brown (1 beat)لا تبدأ الكلمة العربية بساكنينArabic words never begin with two consonants together, so Arabic speakers often add a little vowel: 'bi-lack', 'bi-rown'. That turns 1 beat into 2. In English, bl and br stick together in one beat.
لا تبدأ الكلمة العربية بصوتين ساكنين متتاليين، لذلك يضيف المتحدث بالعربية حركة صغيرة: 'بِ-لاك' و'بِ-راون'، فتصبح النبضة نبضتين. في الإنجليزية يلتصق bl و br في نبضة واحدة.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
'cat and cake rhyme'cat and hat rhyme; cat and cake start the sameYoung children often mix rhyme (same end) with alliteration (same start), especially just after Bonus Class 2. Point to the end of the 'word train'.
bi-lack, bi-rown (2 claps)black, brown (1 clap)Arabic words cannot start with two consonants, so a helping vowel slips in. Say the cluster slowly and smoothly: bl-ack.
clapping every sound: c-a-t = 3 clapscat = 1 clapThe child claps sounds instead of beats. Use the chin test: the jaw drops only once in cat.
clapping the Arabic beats for an English word (fish = 3, like سمكة)fish = 1The child translates in their head. Say the English word aloud first, then clap.
burble (for purple)purple/p/ becomes /b/ because Arabic has no /p/ (from Bonus Class 1). Puff test!

1. Warm-up: Lumi's Rhyme Rap

0–5 min
إحماء: راب القافية مع لومي
Method: Action chant with a steady beat (music and movement)
Purpose: Feel rhyme and rhythm in the body before talking about them.
Teacher says

“Hello, Sound Explorers! Today is Rhyme Time!”

“Lumi's rap. Clap and say: A cat in a hat! (touch your head) A dog on a log! (sit down) A fish on a dish! (swim) A duck in a truck! (drive)”

“Again, faster!”

“Did you hear it? Cat... hat! They sound the same at the end!”

  • مرحباً يا مستكشفي الأصوات! اليوم وقت القافية!
  • صفّقوا وقولوا معي راب لومي مع الحركات!
  • مرة أخرى بسرعة أكبر!
  • هل سمعتم؟ cat و hat تتشابهان في آخرهما!
Students do
  • Clap a steady beat and say the rap with actions.
  • Put on a hat from home for 'A cat in a hat!'
  • Repeat faster, then whisper it.
Tips:
  • Keep the beat steady with a clap or a drum; the rhythm is part of the learning.
  • hat, log, dish and truck are support words shown with pictures; they are not new vocabulary to learn.

2. Linguistics Corner: What Is a Rhyme?

5–13 min
ركن اللغويات: ما هي القافية؟
Method: Guided discovery with a 'word tail' visual + thumbs response
Purpose: Discover that rhyming words share the same ending sound and only the start changes.
Teacher says

“Look: a word is a little animal. It has a head and a tail.”

“C-at. H-at. The heads are different: /k/, /h/. The tails are the same: -at! That is a rhyme!”

“D-og, fr-og. Same tail: -og. Rhyme!”

“Cat and cake? Same head, different tail. Not a rhyme!”

“Thumbs up for rhyme. Thumbs down for no rhyme. Ready?”

  • الكلمة مثل حيوان صغير: لها رأس وذيل.
  • رأس cat هو /k/ ورأس hat هو /h/، لكن الذيل واحد: -at. هذه قافية!
  • cat و cake لهما الرأس نفسه لكن الذيل مختلف، إذن ليستا قافية.
  • ارفع إبهامك إذا كانتا متقافيتين، واخفضه إذا لم تكونا.
Students do
  • Watch the slide: the heads change colour, the tails stay the same.
  • Say the 'tail' together: -at, -og, -ish.
  • Thumbs up or down for each pair.
Exercise · listen-and-show — Listen to two words. Do they rhyme? Thumbs up or thumbs down.
استمع إلى كلمتين. هل هما متقافيتان؟ ارفع إبهامك أو اخفضه.
  1. cat - hat → rhyme (up)
  2. dog - frog → rhyme (up)
  3. cat - cake → no rhyme (down) - same start only
  4. fish - dish → rhyme (up)
  5. duck - dog → no rhyme (down)
  6. nose - toes → rhyme (up)
  7. red - bed → rhyme (up)
  8. bird - mom → no rhyme (down)
  9. two - blue → rhyme (up) - different letters, same sound
Tips:
  • Put cat - cake early: it targets the most common confusion (rhyme vs same first sound).
  • Say each pair twice, slowly and with a little stress on the ending the second time.

3. Rhyme Match: Find My Partner

13–21 min
مطابقة القافية: ابحث عن شريكي
Method: Picture matching on the slide + sentence completion (cloze by rhyme)
Purpose: Produce rhyming words, not just recognise them.
Teacher says

“Every picture wants a rhyming friend. Kite... look, who rhymes? White! Kite, white!”

“Point to the partner. Say both words.”

“Now finish my sentence. A cat in a... hat!”

“My nose and my... toes! Touch them!”

  • كل صورة تبحث عن صديق على القافية نفسها: kite و white!
  • أشر إلى الشريك وقل الكلمتين.
  • الآن أكمل جملتي بكلمة على القافية نفسها: A cat in a... hat!
Students do
  • Point to (or draw a line on the slide between) the rhyming pictures.
  • Say both words together.
  • Finish each rhyming sentence aloud and do the action.
Exercise · complete-the-rhyme — Finish the line with a word that rhymes. Look at the pictures for help.
أكمل الجملة بكلمة على القافية نفسها. استعن بالصور.
  1. A cat in a ... → hat
  2. A fish on a ... → dish
  3. A duck in a ... → truck
  4. My nose and my ... → toes
  5. My kite is ... → white
  6. A dog on a ... → log
  7. One, two, my hat is ... → blue
  8. My teddy is red, he is in my ... → bed
Tips:
  • Accept any real rhyme (a cat in a ... bat?) and celebrate it; accept made-up rhymes too and say 'That rhymes! It's a silly word.' Nonsense rhymes show the child has the idea.
  • Point to the picture only after a short wait, so children try to find the rhyme by ear first.

4. Movement Break: Rhyme Jump

21–26 min
استراحة حركة: قفزة القافية
Method: TPR whole-body listening game
Purpose: Release energy while judging rhymes quickly.
Teacher says

“Stand up! If the words rhyme, jump! If not, sit down on the floor... and stand up fast!”

“Head - bed! (jump) Hands - feet! (sit) Moon - spoon! (jump)”

“Last one: shake your body, shake your head... and quietly sit down.”

  • قفوا! إذا تقافت الكلمتان اقفزوا، وإذا لم تتقافيا اجلسوا ثم قفوا بسرعة!
  • وفي النهاية هزّوا أجسامكم ورؤوسكم واجلسوا بهدوء.
Students do
  • Jump for rhyme, crouch for no rhyme.
  • One child calls a pair for the group.
  • Shake out and sit down calmly.
Exercise · listen-and-move — Jump if the words rhyme. Sit down if they do not.
اقفز إذا كانت الكلمتان متقافيتين، واجلس إذا لم تكونا.
  1. head - bed → jump
  2. hands - feet → sit
  3. moon - spoon → jump
  4. pink - black → sit
  5. three - tree → jump
  6. mouth - mom → sit
Tips:
  • Children need room: check they can jump safely in camera view.
  • three - tree is a good revision of Bonus Class 2: they rhyme but start with different sounds (/th/ and /t/).

5. Arabic Bridge: Arabic Rhymes and Arabic Beats

26–33 min
جسر العربية: قوافٍ عربية ونبضات عربية
Method: Contrastive analysis: rhyme and syllables in both languages
Purpose: Show that rhyme and syllables exist in Arabic too, and discover that the same thing can have different beats in each language.
Teacher says

“Arabic words rhyme too! قطة... بطة! Same tail!”

“Can you find a rhyme? باب... كتاب! نار... دار!”

“Arabic poems are famous for rhyme. Every line can end with the same sound!”

“Now clap Arabic: أر-نب. Two claps! Now English: rab-bit. Two claps! The same!”

“Clap فيل: one clap. Now el-e-phant: three claps! Big difference!”

  • الكلمات العربية تتقافى أيضاً: قطة وبطة لهما الذيل نفسه!
  • من يجد قافية؟ باب وكتاب، نار ودار!
  • الشعر العربي مشهور بالقافية: قد ينتهي كل بيت في القصيدة بالصوت نفسه.
  • لنصفّق للكلمات العربية: أرنب نبضتان، و rabbit نبضتان أيضاً!
  • فيل نبضة واحدة، أما elephant فثلاث نبضات! الحيوان نفسه وعدد مختلف من النبضات.
Students do
  • Say Arabic rhyming pairs and find one more (e.g. قمر / شجر).
  • Clap Arabic and English names of the same animal and compare.
  • Say 'same' or 'different' for the number of claps.
Tips:
  • Count the Arabic beats in the everyday (pausal) form the children say: قطة = qiṭ-ṭa (2), سمكة = sa-ma-ka (3).
  • Keep it playful and quick: the point is 'every language has rhymes and beats', and 'clap the English word in English'.

6. Clap the Beats: Syllable Gym

33–43 min
صفّق للنبضات: رياضة المقاطع
Method: Multisensory syllable segmentation: clap, chin drop, robot walk, tapping
Purpose: Hear, feel and count the syllables in known words.
Teacher says

“Every word has beats. Listen: rab-bit. Clap, clap! Two beats.”

“Hand under your chin. Say ba-by. Your chin goes down two times! Two beats!”

“Robot walk! One step for each beat: el-e-phant: step, step, step!”

“Cat. Clap... one! Short and fast. Duck: one!”

“Careful! Black: one clap, not bi-lack. Bl-ack, together!”

  • لكل كلمة نبضات. rab-bit: صفّقتان!
  • ضع يدك تحت ذقنك وقل ba-by: ينزل الذقن مرتين، إذن نبضتان.
  • مشية الروبوت: خطوة لكل نبضة!
  • انتبه: black نبضة واحدة، لا نقول 'بِلاك'. نلصق b و l معاً.
Students do
  • Clap the beats of each word, then say the number with fingers.
  • Do the chin test with a hand under the chin.
  • Robot-walk the beats in front of the camera.
  • Say black and brown as one smooth beat.
Exercise · clap-and-count — Say the word. Clap the beats. Show the number with your fingers.
قل الكلمة، صفّق لنبضاتها، وأرِنا العدد بأصابعك.
  1. cat → 1
  2. rab-bit → 2
  3. ba-by → 2
  4. el-e-phant → 3
  5. duck → 1
  6. grand-ma → 2
  7. um-brel-la → 3
  8. pur-ple → 2
  9. black → 1 (not bi-lack)
  10. sis-ter → 2
  11. xy-lo-phone → 3
  12. hands → 1
Tips:
  • Write the words split with hyphens only for the teacher; children work by ear.
  • If a child claps c-a-t as 3, go back to the chin test: 'Did your chin go down 3 times?'
  • elephant is from Lesson 13 and is used here only as a picture word: it is the best 3-beat word for this age.

7. Beat Trains: Sort the Words and Clap Our Names

43–53 min
قطارات النبضات: صنّف الكلمات وصفّق لأسمائنا
Method: Categorisation game (1-, 2- and 3-carriage trains) + personalised production
Purpose: Apply syllable counting independently and to personal names, and make a beat chant.
Teacher says

“Three trains! Train 1 has one carriage. Train 2 has two. Train 3 has three.”

“Bird: clap... one! Bird goes on train 1. Yel-low: two! Train 2.”

“Now our names! Lu-mi: two! Sa-ra: two! Noor: one! Ha-mad: two!”

“What about you? Clap your name. Which train?”

“Let's make a name chant: Noor - Sa-ra - Lu-mi - el-e-phant! One, two, two, three!”

  • عندنا ثلاثة قطارات: قطار بعربة واحدة، وقطار بعربتين، وقطار بثلاث عربات.
  • صفّق للكلمة ثم ضعها في القطار الصحيح.
  • الآن أسماؤنا! لومي نبضتان، نور نبضة واحدة.
  • صفّق لاسمك: في أي قطار تركب؟
Students do
  • Clap each picture word and say which train it rides.
  • Clap their own name and their friends' names.
  • Draw a train with one carriage for each beat in their name and draw themselves inside.
  • Perform a group beat chant with names and animals.
Exercise · sort — Clap the word. Put it on train 1, 2 or 3.
صفّق للكلمة وضعها في القطار 1 أو 2 أو 3.
  1. bird → train 1
  2. yellow → train 2 (yel-low)
  3. brother → train 2 (bro-ther)
  4. umbrella → train 3 (um-brel-la)
  5. mouth → train 1
  6. zebra → train 2 (ze-bra)
  7. Lumi → train 2 (Lu-mi)
  8. Noor → train 1
  9. Lumio → train 3 (Lu-mi-o)
  10. feet → train 1
Tips:
  • Clap children's names the way the family says them (Mu-ham-mad = 3, Fa-ti-ma = 3, Ah-mad = 2).
  • Children's own names are the strongest motivation: give every child a turn to be clapped by the group.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

53–60 min
بطاقة الخروج، هل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check (rhyme + syllables) + curiosity close
Purpose: Check each child on both skills and send home a fact and a game.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket! Rhyme or no rhyme? Then clap the beats.”

“Did you know? Elephant has three beats in English, but فيل has only one in Arabic. Every language has its own music!”

“Mission at home: clap the names of everyone in your family!”

“Goodbye! Clap it: Good-bye! Two beats!”

  • بطاقة الخروج: هل تتقافى الكلمتان؟ ثم صفّق للنبضات.
  • هل تعلم؟ elephant ثلاث نبضات في الإنجليزية، وفيل نبضة واحدة في العربية. لكل لغة موسيقاها!
  • مهمتك في البيت: صفّق لأسماء كل أفراد عائلتك!
Students do
  • Answer 5 quick items, at least one alone.
  • Clap 'Good-bye' and wave.
Tips:
  • Record which children still confuse rhyme with same first sound.
  • Reuse the beat clap with new words in Lessons 13-15 (mon-key, gi-raffe, ba-na-na, chick-en).

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Items 1-3: do they rhyme? Thumbs up or down. Items 4-5: clap the beats and show the number.
الأسئلة 1-3: هل الكلمتان متقافيتان؟ ارفع إبهامك أو اخفضه. الأسئلة 4-5: صفّق للنبضات وأرِنا العدد.
  1. dog - frog → rhyme (up)
  2. fish - five → no rhyme (down) - same start only
  3. kite - white → rhyme (up)
  4. rabbit → 2
  5. elephant → 3

Homework

Beat and rhyme mission: clap the names of everyone in your family and tell them how many beats each name has. Find one rhyme for 'cat' with your family.

مهمة النبضات والقافية: صفّق لأسماء أفراد عائلتك وأخبرهم بعدد نبضات كل اسم. وابحثوا معاً عن كلمة على قافية 'cat'.

  • Clap every family member's name and draw a beat train for one of them.
  • Find a rhyme for cat (hat, bat, mat...) and draw it.
  • Say Lumi's rhyme rap to your family: A cat in a hat!

Support

  • Use only 1-beat and 2-beat words at first; add 3 beats later.
  • Give two choices for a rhyme: 'Cat rhymes with hat or dog?'
  • Let the child clap with the teacher, then alone.

Challenge

  • Make a rhyme chain: cat, hat, bat, mat, sat...
  • Find a 4-beat word (wa-ter-mel-on, a-vo-ca-do) or clap a whole sentence.
  • Invent a two-line rhyme: 'I see a duck. It is in a truck!'

Success criteria

  • Judges at least 7 of 9 rhyme pairs correctly.
  • Completes at least 5 of 8 rhyming lines.
  • Claps the right number of beats for at least 9 of 12 words.
  • Claps their own name correctly.

Watch for

  • Rhyme confused with same first sound (cat - cake)
  • Extra vowel in clusters: bi-lack, bi-rown
  • Clapping sounds instead of beats (c-a-t = 3)
  • /p/ said as /b/ in purple and pink

Teacher notes

  • Rhyme and syllables are both about hearing parts of words. They come before reading and are strong predictors of early reading success.
  • The class only uses known words plus a few picture words (hat, log, dish, truck, frog, toes, bed, spoon) needed to make rhymes.
  • Use the 'head and tail' picture of a word again in Level 1, where onset and rime become word families (c-at, h-at, m-at).
  • Bonus Class 4 moves from the ear to the eye: big and small letters and the direction of writing.
