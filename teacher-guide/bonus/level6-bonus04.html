The Art of Persuasion

فن الإقناع
Big idea: We persuade people with three tools Aristotle named over 2,300 years ago (ethos, pathos, logos), and in English we organize our ideas with signpost words and soften strong claims with hedges, so we can disagree and still sound polite.
نحن نقنع الناس بثلاث أدوات سمّاها أرسطو قبل أكثر من 2300 سنة (المصداقية والعاطفة والمنطق)، وفي الإنجليزية ننظّم أفكارنا بكلمات إرشادية ونخفّف الادعاءات القوية بعبارات تحوّط، فنستطيع أن نختلف ونبقى مهذبين.

Linguistics Corner — Aristotle, al-Jahiz and the science of persuasion · أرسطو والجاحظ وعلم الإقناع

In the 4th century BC, the Greek philosopher Aristotle wrote 'Rhetoric', a book about persuasion. He said a speaker persuades in three ways: ethos (the speaker's character and trust), pathos (the audience's emotions) and logos (logic and evidence). Advertisers, lawyers and YouTubers still use the same three tools today.في القرن الرابع قبل الميلاد كتب الفيلسوف اليوناني أرسطو كتاب 'الخطابة' عن الإقناع، وقال إن المتكلم يقنع بثلاث طرق: الإيتوس (شخصية المتكلم والثقة به)، والباثوس (مشاعر الجمهور)، واللوغوس (المنطق والدليل). وما زال المعلنون والمحامون وصنّاع المحتوى يستخدمون الأدوات الثلاث نفسها.
Arabic has its own great tradition: البلاغة, with three sciences: علم المعاني (how sentence choices fit the situation), علم البيان (images: simile, metaphor) and علم البديع (beauty of sound and form, like سجع). In the 9th century, al-Jahiz wrote 'al-Bayan wa al-Tabyin' about eloquence and public speaking.للعربية تراث عظيم خاص بها: البلاغة، بعلومها الثلاثة: علم المعاني (مطابقة الكلام لمقتضى الحال)، وعلم البيان (التشبيه والاستعارة والكناية)، وعلم البديع (تحسين الكلام لفظاً ومعنى كالسجع والطباق). وفي القرن التاسع الميلادي كتب الجاحظ 'البيان والتبيين' عن الفصاحة والخطابة.
The two traditions met. Aristotle's 'Rhetoric' was translated into Arabic and studied by al-Farabi and Ibn Rushd (Averroes). Ibn Rushd's commentary was later translated into Latin and helped Europe rediscover Aristotle.التقى التراثان: تُرجم كتاب 'الخطابة' لأرسطو إلى العربية ودرسه الفارابي وابن رشد. ثم تُرجم شرح ابن رشد إلى اللاتينية وساعد أوروبا على إعادة اكتشاف أرسطو.
Discourse markers (firstly, however, for example, in conclusion) add no new facts; they are road signs for the listener. Hedges (I think, it seems, probably, perhaps) show how sure the speaker is. In English, strong claims with no hedge ('Phones are 100% bad!') often sound rude or childish, while a careful claim with evidence sounds clever.أدوات الربط (firstly, however, for example, in conclusion) لا تضيف معلومات جديدة؛ إنها لافتات طريق للمستمع. وعبارات التحوّط (I think, it seems, probably, perhaps) تبيّن مدى يقين المتكلم. في الإنجليزية، الادعاء القوي بلا تحوّط ('الهواتف سيئة 100%!') يبدو غالباً فظاً أو طفولياً، أما الادعاء الحذر المدعوم بدليل فيبدو ذكياً.
Speakers everywhere love the 'rule of three': 'Veni, vidi, vici' (I came, I saw, I conquered). Arabic rhetoric loves balanced, rhythmic parallel phrases too. Three reasons, three examples, three words: the ear remembers them.الخطباء في كل مكان يحبون 'قاعدة الثلاثة': 'جئت، رأيت، انتصرت'. والبلاغة العربية تحب أيضاً العبارات المتوازنة الموزونة. ثلاثة أسباب، ثلاثة أمثلة، ثلاث كلمات: الأذن تتذكرها.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
I am agree with you. / I'm not agree.I agree with you. / I don't agree.Arabic موافق is an adjective; English agree is a verb.
Although phones are useful, but they are a distraction.Although phones are useful, they are a distraction. / Phones are useful, but they are a distraction.Transfer of بالرغم من ... إلا أن: English uses only one linking word.
We discussed about homework.We discussed homework. / We talked about homework.Transfer of ناقش حول: discuss takes a direct object.
You are wrong!I see your point, but... / That's a fair point, however...Direct contradiction sounds rude in English debates. Acknowledge first, then disagree.
On the other side, phones help us study.On the other hand, phones help us study.The fixed phrase is 'on the other hand' (من ناحية أخرى), not 'side'.
At the end, I think phones should be allowed.In conclusion / Finally, I think phones should be allowed.'At the end' means at the end of a place or time (at the end of the film). To close a speech, use In conclusion or Finally.

1. Warm-up: Which Pitch Wins?

0–5 min
إحماء: أي عرض يفوز؟
Method: Persuasion hook: compare three versions of the same message and vote
Purpose: Let students feel the three kinds of persuasion before they get the names.
Teacher says

“Lumi wants you to download a new study app called FocusTimer. Listen to three pitches and vote for the one that convinces you most.”

“Pitch A: 'I'm a teacher with ten years of experience, and I use this app with all my students.' Pitch B: 'Imagine the night before an exam: you're tired, stressed and scrolling... FocusTimer gives you back your calm.' Pitch C: 'In our class test, students who used it studied 40 minutes longer with fewer distractions.'”

“Which one won? Why? Hold that thought: a Greek philosopher had names for all three, 2,300 years ago.”

  • استمعوا لثلاثة عروض لتطبيق دراسة، وصوّتوا للعرض الأكثر إقناعاً.
  • لماذا فاز؟ فيلسوف يوناني أعطى لكل واحد منها اسماً قبل 2300 سنة.
Students do
  • Listen to the three pitches and vote with the Teams poll or reactions.
  • Give one reason: 'Pitch C convinced me because it has evidence.'
  • Guess what makes each pitch different (the person / the feeling / the numbers).
Tips:
  • The FocusTimer app and its numbers are fictional; say so at the end. It is a good lead into 'check the evidence'.
  • Usually the vote is split: perfect, because different people respond to different tools.

2. Linguistics Corner: The Persuasion Triangle and al-Balagha

5–15 min
ركن اللغويات: مثلث الإقناع وعلم البلاغة
Method: Direct explanation + classification of authentic-style arguments
Purpose: Learn ethos, pathos and logos, connect them to the Arabic البلاغة tradition, and identify them in arguments.
Teacher says

“Aristotle said there are three ways to persuade. Ethos: trust me, because of who I am (Pitch A). Pathos: feel this (Pitch B). Logos: look at the facts and the logic (Pitch C).”

“The word rhetoric is Greek, like technology from our last Bonus Class: a rhetor was a public speaker.”

“Arabic has a huge tradition of its own: البلاغة. Al-Jahiz wrote about eloquence in the 9th century, and Arab philosophers like al-Farabi and Ibn Rushd studied Aristotle's Rhetoric. Ibn Rushd's commentary even helped Europe rediscover Aristotle!”

“The best speakers mix all three. Now be detectives: which tool is each argument using?”

  • الإيتوس: صدّقني بسبب من أنا. الباثوس: اشعر بهذا. اللوغوس: انظر إلى الحقائق والمنطق.
  • للعربية تراث عظيم في البلاغة، والفارابي وابن رشد درسا كتاب أرسطو في الخطابة.
  • أفضل الخطباء يمزجون الأدوات الثلاث.
Students do
  • Label the three corners of the triangle with the pitches from the warm-up.
  • Classify 8 arguments as ethos, pathos or logos (some have two).
  • Share one Arabic speech, advert or poem line they remember that uses pathos.
Exercise · classify — Is the speaker using ethos (trust), pathos (feelings) or logos (logic/evidence)?
هل يستخدم المتكلم المصداقية أم العاطفة أم المنطق والدليل؟
  1. As the team captain for three years, I know how important practice is. → ethos
  2. Think about the children who have no books. How would you feel? → pathos
  3. A survey of 500 students showed that 70% sleep less than 8 hours. → logos (statistic)
  4. Doctors recommend at least one hour of exercise a day for teenagers. → ethos (expert authority) + logos
  5. If we start at 10, we will sleep more, so we will focus better. → logos (cause and effect)
  6. Imagine your little brother alone and scared in a new school. → pathos
  7. I have volunteered at this charity every summer, so I've seen the problem myself. → ethos (experience)
  8. This laptop is cheaper, more durable and more efficient than the other one. → logos (comparison with evidence)
Tips:
  • Accept two labels when the student can justify them: real arguments often mix tools.
  • Keep the triangle slide visible for the debate: the rubric uses it.

3. Arabic Bridge: Strong vs Soft: Emphasis and Hedging

15–23 min
جسر العربية: القوة والليونة: التوكيد والتحوّط
Method: Contrastive rhetoric + rewriting for tone and accuracy
Purpose: Show how English uses hedges where Arabic often uses emphasis, and fix the typical transfer errors in argument language.
Teacher says

“In Arabic we often make an argument stronger with emphasis: إنّ, لقد, بالتأكيد, لا شك. That is beautiful Arabic style.”

“In English, if you say 'Everybody knows this is 100% true!', many listeners stop trusting you. English speakers often prefer: 'It seems that...', 'Research suggests...', 'I think this is probably...'. A soft claim plus strong evidence wins.”

“Two more traps from Arabic. بالرغم من ... إلا أن: English uses only one word: 'Although it's expensive, it's useful.' No 'but'. And موافق: in English we say 'I agree', never 'I am agree'.”

“Your job: make these sentences softer, more polite and correct.”

  • التوكيد في العربية أسلوب جميل، أما في الإنجليزية فالادعاء الحذر مع دليل قوي هو الذي يقنع.
  • بالرغم من ... إلا أن = although وحدها، بلا but.
  • قل I agree لا I am agree.
Students do
  • Compare two versions of the same claim (strong vs hedged) and say which sounds more convincing in English.
  • Rewrite 8 sentences: soften them or correct the Arabic transfer error.
  • Read their best rewrite aloud with a calm, confident tone.
Exercise · rewrite — Make each sentence softer (use a hedge) or correct the mistake.
خفّف كل جملة (باستخدام عبارة تحوّط) أو صحّح الخطأ.
  1. Phones are definitely the worst thing for students. → Phones can probably be a big distraction for many students. / It seems that phones are a big distraction for students.
  2. Everybody knows homework is useless. → I think homework is not always useful. / Some research suggests that too much homework is not useful.
  3. I am agree with Sara. → I agree with Sara.
  4. Although the trip is expensive, but it is worth it. → Although the trip is expensive, it is worth it.
  5. We discussed about the school rules. → We discussed the school rules.
  6. You are wrong, Ziad. → I see your point, Ziad, but I don't agree. / That's a fair point, but...
  7. Online classes are 100% better than real classes. → In my opinion, online classes are often more convenient than classroom lessons.
  8. I'm not agree, it will never work. → I don't agree. I don't think it will work.
Tips:
  • Present this as a difference in style, not 'Arabic is too strong'. Strong Arabic speeches use emphasis well; we are learning the English style for English audiences.
  • Note the link to Bonus Class 1: 'I don't think it will work' is the natural English negative with think.

4. The Speaker's Toolkit: Signposts and Polite Disagreement

23–32 min
عدة المتحدث: أدوات الربط والاختلاف المهذب
Method: Categorized phrase bank + gap-fill + quick-fire oral practice
Purpose: Build a bank of discourse markers and polite disagreement phrases and use them accurately.
Teacher says

“Good speakers give the listener road signs. Look at our toolkit: Order: firstly, secondly, finally. Adding: moreover, also, what's more. Contrast: however, on the other hand. Example: for example, for instance. Concluding: in conclusion, to sum up.”

“And for disagreeing politely: 'That's a fair point, but...', 'I agree partly, however...', 'I see what you mean, but have you thought about...?', 'I'm not sure I agree, because...'.”

“Quick-fire round: I say a statement, you disagree politely with a different phrase each time. No repeats!”

  • المتحدث الجيد يعطي المستمع لافتات طريق: للترتيب، للإضافة، للمقابلة، للمثال، للخاتمة.
  • وللاختلاف بأدب: That's a fair point, but...
  • جولة سريعة: اختلفوا بأدب بعبارة مختلفة كل مرة!
Students do
  • Sort 12 markers into the toolkit categories on the slide.
  • Complete Hamad's short speech with the right markers.
  • Quick-fire: disagree politely with 6 teacher statements without repeating a phrase.
Exercise · gap-fill — Complete Hamad's speech with: Firstly / Moreover / For example / However / On the other hand / In conclusion / That's a fair point, but / It seems that.
أكمل خطبة حمد بالأدوات المناسبة.
  1. ___, I'd like to explain why our school should start at 10 a.m. → Firstly
  2. ___ many teenagers don't get enough sleep. → It seems that
  3. ___, my cousin falls asleep in his first class every day. → For example
  4. ___, students who sleep more are more productive. → Moreover
  5. ___, some parents start work early and need to drop their children off. → On the other hand
  6. (answering Noor) ___ the school could open a supervised study room at 8. → That's a fair point, but
  7. ___, a later start may cause problems with buses. → However
  8. ___, I believe a later start is better for our health and our grades. → In conclusion
Tips:
  • Some gaps allow two answers (However / On the other hand); accept any marker with the right function.
  • Warn against overusing 'moreover': in speech, 'also' and 'what's more' sound more natural.

5. Build a 45-Second Speech with PREP

32–42 min
ابنِ خطبة من 45 ثانية بطريقة PREP
Method: Structured planning frame (PREP) + rehearsal + peer feedback with a mini-rubric
Purpose: Plan and deliver a short persuasive speech that uses a marker, a hedge and at least two of the three appeals.
Teacher says

“A simple frame for a strong speech: PREP. Point: what you think. Reason: why. Example: evidence or a story. Point again: repeat your opinion in new words.”

“First, put this model speech in order. Then write your own on one of these topics: 'Students should have a daily screen-time limit', 'Every student should learn to cook', 'Schools should replace exams with projects'.”

“You have 4 minutes to prepare. Use notes, not full sentences. Then 45 seconds each. Speak clearly, confidently, and make eye contact with the camera!”

“Listeners: give your partner one star and one wish using the mini-rubric: Did they use logos? pathos or ethos? a marker? a hedge?”

  • إطار بسيط لخطبة قوية: الفكرة، السبب، المثال، ثم الفكرة مجدداً.
  • لديكم 4 دقائق للتحضير، ثم 45 ثانية لكل واحد. تكلموا بوضوح وثقة وانظروا إلى الكاميرا!
  • المستمعون: نجمة وأمنية لكل زميل.
Students do
  • Order the 6 sentences of the model speech and label them P, R, E, P.
  • Write PREP notes for their own topic (4 minutes, timer on screen).
  • Deliver the speech in 45 seconds.
  • Give and receive one star and one wish.
Exercise · order-and-label — Put Sara's speech in order and label each part: Point, Reason, Example, Point again (two sentences are extra support).
رتّب خطبة سارة وسمِّ كل جزء: الفكرة، السبب، المثال، الفكرة مجدداً.
  1. In my opinion, every student should learn to cook. → 1 - Point
  2. Firstly, cooking makes us more independent. → 2 - Reason
  3. Moreover, it seems that home-cooked food is usually healthier and cheaper. → 3 - Reason (logos)
  4. For example, last summer I learned to make three dishes, and now I help my mum every weekend. → 4 - Example (ethos: personal experience)
  5. Imagine your first week at university with no idea how to make breakfast! → 5 - Example (pathos)
  6. In conclusion, cooking is a life skill, so I believe schools should teach it. → 6 - Point again
Tips:
  • Weaker students can use the model speech as a frame and change only the topic words.
  • Time strictly: the 45-second limit trains speakers to choose their best arguments.
  • Record nothing without permission; feedback is spoken and kind.

6. The Lumio Mini-Debate

42–55 min
مناظرة لوميو المصغّرة
Method: Structured debate with roles, a strict timer and a peer-feedback rubric
Purpose: Use all the tools in a real, timed exchange: appeals, markers, hedges, rebuttal and polite disagreement.
Teacher says

“The motion: 'Phones should be allowed in class.' Team Proposition says yes; Team Opposition says no. You don't have to agree with your side: good debaters can argue both!”

“Roles: Speaker 1 on each team gives the opening (1 minute). Speaker 2 gives the rebuttal (1 minute): answer the other team's points. Then each team gives a 30-second closing. I'm the chair and the timekeeper.”

“Remember the rules: you mustn't interrupt; you have to use at least one marker and one polite disagreement phrase; you can use your Lesson 13 words: focus, distraction, productive.”

“Preparation: 3 minutes. Go!”

“Time's up. Judges, use the rubric: one point for each appeal used, one for markers, one for polite disagreement, one for a hedge, one for clear delivery.”

  • الموضوع: 'يجب السماح بالهواتف في الصف'. لست مضطراً أن توافق على رأي فريقك: المناظر الجيد يدافع عن الجهتين!
  • الأدوار: افتتاح دقيقة، ثم رد دقيقة، ثم ختام 30 ثانية لكل فريق.
  • ممنوع المقاطعة، ويجب استخدام أداة ربط وعبارة اختلاف مهذبة.
Students do
  • Take a role card: Proposition Speaker 1 / Speaker 2, Opposition Speaker 1 / Speaker 2.
  • Prepare arguments with the team for 3 minutes (ethos, pathos, logos; one statistic or example).
  • Debate: openings (2 x 1 min), 1 minute to prepare rebuttals, rebuttals (2 x 1 min), closings (2 x 30 s).
  • While the other team speaks, take notes of one point to answer.
  • Score the other team with the rubric and give one star and one wish.
Exercise · debate-rubric — Judge the other team. Give 1 point for each criterion you hear.
قيّم الفريق الآخر: نقطة لكل معيار تسمعه.
  1. Logos: a reason with evidence, a statistic or an example → 1 point (e.g. 'A study shows...', 'For example...')
  2. Pathos: an appeal to feelings → 1 point (e.g. 'Imagine...')
  3. Ethos: trust or experience → 1 point (e.g. 'As students, we know...')
  4. At least two discourse markers → 1 point (Firstly, However, In conclusion...)
  5. Polite disagreement in the rebuttal → 1 point (That's a fair point, but... / I agree partly, however...)
  6. A hedge that makes a claim careful → 1 point (It seems that... / probably / I think)
  7. Clear delivery: eye contact, confident voice, on time → 1 point
Tips:
  • With 2 students: one per side, each gives opening, rebuttal and closing. With 3: the third student is the judge and timekeeper. With 1: the teacher argues the other side.
  • Assign sides randomly so nobody argues only what they already believe.
  • As chair, stop any personal comments at once: we attack ideas, not people.
  • Give the result with reasons from the rubric, and praise the best polite-disagreement moment.

7. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

55–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + reflection + curiosity close
Purpose: Check the key terms and phrases and end the four Bonus Classes with a reflection on English in the world.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five quick items. Two minutes.”

“Did you know? Ibn Rushd (Averroes) from Córdoba was so famous for his commentaries on Aristotle that medieval European scholars simply called him 'The Commentator'.”

“This was our last Bonus Class of Level 6. You discovered that English has no future ending, why rules can be a trap, where words travel from, and how to persuade. That's English in the world!”

  • هل تعلم؟ كان ابن رشد القرطبي مشهوراً جداً بشروحه لأرسطو حتى إن علماء أوروبا في العصور الوسطى سمّوه 'الشارح'.
  • هذه آخر حصة إضافية في المستوى السادس: أحسنتم!
Students do
  • Complete the 5 exit-ticket items.
  • Say one thing they learned in the four Bonus Classes, using a hedge: 'I think the most interesting thing was...'.
Tips:
  • Note students who still say 'I am agree' or 'although... but' for the Lesson 17-20 review.
  • Use the debate scores to pair students for the Big Review in Lesson 20.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer or correct.
أجب أو صحّح.
  1. 'Experts say...' uses which appeal? → ethos (authority / trust)
  2. '70% of students...' uses which appeal? → logos (evidence)
  3. Correct it: I am agree with you. → I agree with you.
  4. Correct it: Although it's cheap, but it's not durable. → Although it's cheap, it's not durable.
  5. Make it polite: You're wrong. → I see your point, but... / That's a fair point, but I don't agree.

Homework

Persuasive Video Message: record (or write) a 1-minute speech for or against 'Teenagers should have a part-time job'. Use the PREP frame, at least three discourse markers, one hedge, and at least two of ethos, pathos and logos. Mark each tool in your script.

رسالة مصوّرة مقنعة: سجّل (أو اكتب) خطبة من دقيقة واحدة مع أو ضد 'يجب أن يعمل المراهقون عملاً جزئياً'. استخدم إطار PREP وثلاث أدوات ربط على الأقل وعبارة تحوّط واحدة، واثنتين على الأقل من: المصداقية والعاطفة والمنطق. حدّد كل أداة في نصك.

  • Write the 1-minute PREP script and label ethos / pathos / logos.
  • Find a real advert (online or on TV) and decide which appeal it uses most.
  • Write 3 polite disagreement replies to the statement 'Homework should be banned.'

Support

  • Give a sentence-starter card for each debate role (opening, rebuttal, closing).
  • Limit the toolkit to 5 markers: Firstly, Also, However, For example, In conclusion.
  • Allow 30 seconds of notes in Arabic during preparation, but the speech is in English.

Challenge

  • Use a 'rule of three' in your speech: three reasons or three parallel phrases.
  • In the rebuttal, quote the other team ('Omar said that... However...') before answering.
  • Analyse a short famous speech (e.g. a few lines from Malala Yousafzai's 2013 UN speech): find one example each of ethos, pathos and logos.

Success criteria

  • Classifies at least 6 of 8 arguments correctly as ethos, pathos or logos.
  • Rewrites at least 6 of 8 sentences with a hedge or the correct structure (I agree, although without but, discuss without about).
  • Delivers a 45-second PREP speech with at least one marker and one hedge.
  • Scores at least 4 of 7 on the debate rubric and disagrees politely at least once.

Watch for

  • I am agree / I'm not agree
  • although ... but
  • discuss about
  • on the other side, at the end (for in conclusion)
  • overgeneralizations without evidence (everybody, always, 100%)
  • interrupting or personal comments in the debate

Teacher notes

  • Present Arabic and English rhetorical styles as different, not better or worse. The idea that Arabic writing prefers emphasis and parallelism comes from 'contrastive rhetoric' research, which later scholars criticized as too simple; use it as a tendency, not a rule.
  • Lesson 15 (Class Debate) already introduced argue, convince, evidence, counterargument and fair point; this class gives the theory and structure behind them.
  • Keep debate motions school-friendly and avoid religious or political topics.
  • Statistics in the exercises are examples for practice, not real data; remind students that real logos needs a real source.
  • This is the last Bonus Class of Level 6; Lessons 17-20 follow, with the Big Review in Lesson 20.
