Sounds Arabic Doesn't Have: P, V and the Buzz

أصوات ليست في العربية: P و V والطنين
Big idea: Many English sounds come in twins: same mouth, but one buzzes in the throat and one only whispers. Arabic has b and f, but not their twins p and v, so we train them on purpose.
كثير من أصوات الإنجليزية تأتي توائم: الفم نفسه، لكن أحدهما يطنّ في الحلق والآخر يهمس فقط. في العربية الباء والفاء، لكن ليس فيها توأماهما p و v، لذلك ندرّبهما عن قصد.

Objectives

I can feel if a sound buzzes (voiced) or whispers (voiceless) with my hand on my throat.أستطيع أن أشعر بيدي على حلقي إن كان الصوت يطنّ (مجهوراً) أو يهمس (مهموساً).
I can say p with a puff of air and b with a buzz: pin / bin, pig / big.أستطيع أن أنطق p بنفخة هواء و b بطنين: pin / bin و pig / big.
I can say v with my top teeth on my lip and a buzz: fan / van.أستطيع أن أنطق v بوضع أسناني العليا على شفتي مع طنين: fan / van.

Linguistics Corner — The buzz box in your throat · صندوق الطنين في حلقك

In your throat there is a voice box with two tiny curtains called the vocal folds. When they shake together very fast (hundreds of times every second!) you hear a buzz. That buzz is your voice.في حلقك صندوق صوت فيه ستارتان صغيرتان اسمهما الحبلان الصوتيان. عندما تهتزّان بسرعة كبيرة جداً (مئات المرات في الثانية!) تسمع طنيناً، وهذا الطنين هو صوتك.
Some sounds use the buzz (voiced: b, d, g, v, z, m, n and all vowels). Some sounds are only air (voiceless: p, t, k, f, s). Put your hand on your throat to feel the difference.بعض الأصوات تستعمل الطنين (المجهورة: b و d و g و v و z و m و n وكل الحركات)، وبعضها هواء فقط (المهموسة: p و t و k و f و s). ضع يدك على حلقك لتشعر بالفرق.
Sound twins: p and b are made in exactly the same place (both lips). The only difference is the buzz. The same is true for t/d, k/g, f/v and s/z.توائم الأصوات: p و b تُصنعان في المكان نفسه تماماً (الشفتان معاً)، والفرق الوحيد هو الطنين. وكذلك t/d و k/g و f/v و s/z.
At the start of a word, English p, t and k come with a little puff of air. A tissue in front of your mouth flutters for pin but stays almost still for bin. After s the puff almost disappears: spin has a much smaller puff than pin.في أول الكلمة تخرج مع p و t و k في الإنجليزية نفخة هواء صغيرة. المنديل أمام فمك يرفرف مع pin ويكاد لا يتحرك مع bin. وبعد s تكاد النفخة تختفي: نفخة spin أصغر بكثير من نفخة pin.
English r is a soft sound: the tongue goes up and back but never touches the top of the mouth. Arabic ر taps or rolls (the tongue hits the roof of the mouth). In British English the r at the end of car is not said at all; in American English it is.صوت r الإنجليزي ناعم: يرتفع اللسان ويرجع للخلف لكنه لا يلمس سقف الفم أبداً. أما الراء العربية فاللسان يضرب فيها سقف الفم مرة أو يتكرر. وفي الإنجليزية البريطانية لا تُنطق r في آخر كلمة car، أما في الأمريكية فتُنطق.

Key terms

voiceless (whisper) soundصوت مهموس (يهمس)A sound with no buzz, only air: p, t, k, f, s.

Arabic Bridge

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
b (big) and p (pig)بArabic ب is the buzzing twin. Arabic has no whispering twin, so p often turns into b (pizza becomes 'bizza'). Persian and Urdu added a new letter for it: پ (ب with three dots).
الباء العربية هي التوأم الذي يطنّ، ولا يوجد في العربية التوأم الهامس، لذلك يتحوّل p غالباً إلى ب (pizza تصبح 'بيتزا'). الفارسية والأردية أضافتا له حرفاً جديداً: پ (باء بثلاث نقاط).
f (fan) and v (van)فArabic ف is the same as English f (whisper). Arabic has no buzzing twin v, so v often turns into f (seven becomes 'sefen'). Some Arabic writers use ڤ (ف with three dots) for v, as in ڤيتامين.
الفاء العربية مثل f الإنجليزية تماماً (همس). ولا يوجد في العربية التوأم الذي يطنّ v، لذلك تتحوّل v غالباً إلى ف (seven تصبح 'سِفِن'). بعض الكتّاب العرب يستعملون ڤ (فاء بثلاث نقاط) لصوت v كما في ڤيتامين.
g (got, dog)ج المصرية / ق في لهجات الخليجGood news: g is easy for us. Egyptians say ج as g (جمل = gamal) and in the Gulf ق is often g (قال = gaal). Use that sound.
خبر جميل: صوت g سهل علينا. المصريون ينطقون الجيم g (جمل = gamal)، وفي الخليج تُنطق القاف غالباً g (قال = gaal). استعمل هذا الصوت نفسه.
r (red, run)رArabic ر taps or rolls on the roof of the mouth. English r does not touch anything: tongue back, lips a little round, no rolling.
الراء العربية تضرب سقف الفم أو تتدحرج. أما r الإنجليزية فلا تلمس شيئاً: اللسان إلى الخلف والشفتان مستديرتان قليلاً، بلا تدحرج.
o (hot) and u (hut)ضمة؟ ليس تماماًBoth letters are often read as damma, but neither is damma. o in hot: mouth open and lips round. u in hut: a short relaxed 'uh', lips NOT round. Damma is nearer to the u in put.
كثيراً ما نقرأ الحرفين ضمةً، لكن لا أحد منهما ضمة. o في hot: الفم مفتوح والشفتان مستديرتان. u في hut: صوت قصير مرتاح 'أَه' بلا استدارة للشفتين. الضمة أقرب إلى u في كلمة put.

1. Warm-up: Vowel Friends Wake-up

0–4 min
إحماء: إيقاظ أصدقاء الحركات
Method: Retrieval practice with gestures (TPR)
Purpose: Recall the Bonus Class 1 vowel friends and the elevator, and get mouths moving before the new sounds.
Teacher says

“Hello, Sound Scientists! Who remembers our vowel friends? Show me Lazy A! (yawn) Quick E! (quick point) Smiley I! (tiny pinch)”

“Ride the Vowel Elevator: a... e... i! Down again: i... e... a!”

“Quick! Which friend is in cat? In ten? In big?”

“Today we meet some sounds that Arabic doesn't have. Are you ready to be brave with your mouth?”

  • مَن يتذكّر أصدقاء الحركات؟ أروني A الكسولة و E السريعة و I المبتسمة!
  • اليوم نتعرّف على أصوات ليست في العربية. هل أنتم مستعدّون؟
Students do
  • Do each vowel friend's gesture and sound.
  • Ride the elevator with a hand on the chin.
  • Name the friend in cat, ten, big, red, pin.
Tips:
  • Keep it to 3-4 minutes; it is a switch-on, not a test.
  • Have the tissues ready on the desk now so stage 4 does not stop for searching.

2. Linguistics Corner: Buzz or Whisper?

4–13 min
ركن اللغويات: طنين أم همس؟
Method: Guided discovery with touch (hand on throat) and the sound-twins pattern
Purpose: Discover the voice box and that English consonants come in twins that differ only in the buzz.
Teacher says

“Put your hand on your throat, here. Say zzz like a bee. Do you feel it shaking? That is your voice box buzzing!”

“Now say sss like a snake. Any buzz? No! Only air. sss is a whisper sound.”

“Inside your voice box there are two tiny curtains. For buzz sounds they shake very fast. For whisper sounds they stay open and the air just goes out.”

“Now look: zzz and sss. Same mouth, same teeth, same tongue. Only the buzz is different. They are twins!”

“Let's find more twins. ffff... vvvv. Hand on throat! Which one buzzes?”

“Our biggest twins today: p and b. Lips together for both. b buzzes, p whispers and puffs.”

  • ضع يدك على حلقك وقل zzz مثل النحلة. هل تشعر بالاهتزاز؟ هذا صندوق صوتك يطنّ!
  • zzz و sss توأمان: الفم نفسه، والفرق فقط في الطنين.
Students do
  • Hand on throat: say zzz / sss, vvv / fff, bbb (b-b-b) / ppp (p-p-p) and feel buzz or no buzz.
  • Show a thumbs-up for buzz and a flat hand for whisper.
  • Say each twin pair while watching the mirror: the mouth does not move differently.
  • Sort sounds on the slide into the Buzz box or the Whisper box.
Exercise · sort — Hand on your throat. Say the sound. Buzz or whisper? Show thumbs-up (buzz) or flat hand (whisper).
ضع يدك على حلقك وانطق الصوت. طنين أم همس؟ ارفع إبهامك (طنين) أو افتح يدك (همس).
  1. z → buzz (voiced)
  2. s → whisper (voiceless)
  3. v → buzz (voiced)
  4. f → whisper (voiceless)
  5. b → buzz (voiced)
  6. p → whisper (voiceless)
  7. d → buzz (voiced)
  8. t → whisper (voiceless)
  9. g → buzz (voiced)
  10. k → whisper (voiceless)
Tips:
  • Long sounds (zzz, vvv, fff, sss) are the easiest to feel. Start with them, then try b and p by repeating them: b-b-b, p-p-p.
  • Say consonants pure: 'b', not 'buh'. 'buh' adds a vowel and every vowel buzzes, so p would seem to buzz too.
  • If a child cannot feel the buzz, ask them to put both hands over their ears and say zzz: the buzz is loud inside the head.

3. Arabic Bridge: The Missing Twins

13–19 min
جسر العربية: التوأمان المفقودان
Method: Contrastive analysis with a twins chart (English vs Arabic)
Purpose: Show exactly why p and v are hard (Arabic has only one twin of each pair) and that other twins are already easy.
Teacher says

“Look at our twins chart. t and d: Arabic has ت and د. Easy! s and z: Arabic has س and ز. Easy!”

“k and g: Arabic has ك. And g? In Egypt ج says g: gamal. In the Gulf, قال is often gaal. So g is easy for you too!”

“b and p: Arabic has ب... but where is p? Missing! f and v: Arabic has ف... but where is v? Missing!”

“These are the missing twins. Your mouth has never needed them in Arabic, so we will train them today.”

“Fun fact: Persian and Urdu borrowed Arabic letters and added three dots to make پ for p. Some Arabic writers use ڤ for v, like in ڤيتامين!”

  • في العربية ت ود، س وز، ك و g في بعض اللهجات. لكن أين توأم الباء؟ وأين توأم الفاء؟ مفقودان!
  • الفارسية والأردية أضافتا ثلاث نقاط فصنعتا پ لصوت p.
Students do
  • Read the Arabic letter for each twin on the chart: ت/د, س/ز, ك/g, ب/?, ف/?.
  • Point to the two empty boxes (the missing twins p and v).
  • Say pizza the Arabic way and the English way; spot the difference.
Tips:
  • Be positive: Arabic has many sounds English does not have (ع، ح، خ، ض، ق). Every language picks its own sounds.
  • Some children already say p and v in loanwords (pizza, video) from cartoons or school. Praise them and use them as models.

4. Puffy P and Bouncy B: The Tissue Test

19–28 min
P النفّاخة و B القافزة: اختبار المنديل
Method: Multisensory articulation (tissue, hand in front of mouth, throat) + character mnemonics + minimal pairs
Purpose: Produce a clear aspirated p and a buzzing b, then hear the difference in minimal pairs.
Teacher says

“Meet Puffy P! He blows out birthday candles: p! p! p! Hold your tissue in front of your lips. Say pin. Did the tissue jump?”

“Meet Bouncy B! She has a buzz in her throat and no big puff: b! b! Hold the tissue. Say bin. The tissue stays almost still.”

“Put your other hand on your throat. pin: no buzz, big puff. bin: buzz, small puff.”

“Now I say a word. Show me p or b! pig... big... pat... bat...”

“Let's say pizza with Puffy P: p-p-pizza! Your tissue should dance!”

  • P النفّاخة تطفئ شموع عيد الميلاد: p! امسك المنديل أمام شفتيك وقل pin. هل تحرّك المنديل؟
  • B القافزة فيها طنين في الحلق ولا تنفخ كثيراً.
Students do
  • Hold a tissue (or paper strip) 3 cm from the lips and say p-p-p, then b-b-b.
  • Say the pairs pin/bin, pig/big, pat/bat, pet/bet with tissue and throat check.
  • Listening: show the p or b card for each word the teacher says.
  • Say pizza, pink, apple, peas with a strong puff.
Exercise · listen-and-show — Listen to the word. Show the p card or the b card.
استمع للكلمة وارفع بطاقة p أو بطاقة b.
  1. pin → p
  2. big → b
  3. pig → p
  4. bat → b
  5. pet → p
  6. bin → b
  7. pat → p
  8. bet → b
  9. cap (end sound) → p
  10. cab (end sound) → b
Tips:
  • The puff is strongest at the start of a word (pin, pig). At the end of a word (cap) the lips close and the puff is small, so check end sounds by ear, not with the tissue.
  • If a child says b for p, ask them to whisper the word first (a whisper has no buzz), then say it again a little louder keeping the puff.
  • Watch for over-correction: if big becomes pig, go back to the throat: 'Where is Bouncy B's buzz?'

5. Very Vibrating V and Fluffy F

28–35 min
V المهتزّة جداً و F الناعمة
Method: Mirror articulation + throat touch + chant
Purpose: Produce v (top teeth on bottom lip plus buzz) and keep it different from f in known words like seven, eleven, twelve and five.
Teacher says

“Look in the mirror. Put your top teeth gently on your bottom lip. Blow: fff. That is Fluffy F, like ف in Arabic. Easy!”

“Keep your teeth there. Now switch on the buzz: vvv. Your lip tickles! That is Very Vibrating V.”

“Hand on throat: fff (no buzz), vvv (buzz). Twins again!”

“Numbers have v inside! sev-en, e-lev-en, twel-ve, fi-ve. Five starts with f and ends with v: f... v. Feel it!”

“Chant with me: 'Five vans, very fast! Seven vans, very fast!'”

  • ضع أسنانك العليا بلطف على شفتك السفلى وانفخ: fff مثل الفاء. ثم شغّل الطنين: vvv. شفتك تدغدغك!
  • في الأرقام صوت v: seven و eleven و twelve و five.
Students do
  • Mirror check: teeth on lip for both f and v (not both lips like b).
  • Hold vvv for 3 seconds and feel the lip tickle.
  • Say fan/van, five, seven, eleven, twelve, very with the mirror.
  • Say the chant and clap on every v.
Exercise · listen-and-show — Listen. Do you hear Fluffy F or Very Vibrating V? Show the f card or the v card.
استمع: هل تسمع F الناعمة أم V المهتزّة؟ ارفع بطاقة f أو v.
  1. fan → f
  2. van → v
  3. fish → f
  4. very → v
  5. seven (middle sound) → v
  6. fine → f
  7. vet → v
  8. five (first sound) → f
Tips:
  • Some children replace v with b or w. Use the mirror: v needs teeth on the lip; b and w use both lips.
  • The letters f and v come formally in later phonics lessons (f in Lesson 9); here they are practised as sounds, so the words are simple and mostly known (numbers, fish, fine).

6. Round O, Grumpy U and Puppy R

35–45 min
O المستديرة و U المتذمّرة و R الجرو
Method: Character mnemonics + Vowel Elevator + minimal pairs + gesture contrast for r
Purpose: Separate short o (hot) from short u (hut) and replace the rolled Arabic ر with a soft English r.
Teacher says

“Two new vowel friends! Meet Round O. He is surprised and makes a round mouth: o! hot, dog, top. Make an O with your fingers around your mouth.”

“Meet Grumpy U. He is a bit grumpy and says a short, lazy 'uh': u! up, cup, sun, run. Lips relaxed, not round. Arms crossed!”

“Mirror check: hot... round lips. hut... flat, relaxed lips. hot, hut! dog, dug!”

“Careful: in Arabic we read both like damma, but damma pushes the lips forward. Grumpy U never pushes his lips.”

“Now r. Arabic ر is a Motorbike R: rrrr, the tongue taps and rolls. English r is a Puppy R: a soft little growl, the tongue pulls back and touches nothing. rrr... red, run, rat.”

“Tip: start with your lips a little round, like a tiny o, then say red.”

  • O المستديرة متفاجئة: فمها مستدير: hot و dog. U المتذمّرة تقول 'أَه' قصيرة والشفتان مرتاحتان: up و cup و sun.
  • الراء العربية مثل الدرّاجة النارية: تتدحرج. أما r الإنجليزية فمثل زمجرة جرو صغير: اللسان لا يلمس شيئاً.
Students do
  • Copy Round O (finger circle) and Grumpy U (crossed arms) with each sound.
  • Mirror pairs: hot/hut, cot/cut, dog/dug, not/nut, lock/luck.
  • Listening: show the o card or the u card.
  • Say Motorbike R (Arabic) then Puppy R (English): red, run, rat, rug, rock. Check: does your tongue touch? It should not.
Exercise · listen-and-show — Listen to the word. Show Round O (finger circle) or Grumpy U (crossed arms).
استمع للكلمة. اصنع دائرة بأصابعك لـ O المستديرة أو اعقد ذراعيك لـ U المتذمّرة.
  1. hot → o
  2. hut → u
  3. cup → u
  4. dog → o
  5. sun → u
  6. top → o
  7. duck → u
  8. sock → o
  9. nut → u
  10. not → o
Tips:
  • Keep the o simple: British o in hot has rounded lips; American o is more open, like 'ah'. Either is fine as long as it is clearly different from Grumpy U.
  • On the Vowel Elevator, Round O lives on the ground floor next to Lazy A (jaw open) but with round lips; Grumpy U lives in the middle, relaxed.
  • For r, never ask children to 'roll' or 'not roll' in words they cannot feel. Let them feel the tongue: tap ر, then pull the tongue back for r.

7. Sound Twins Challenge: Be the Teacher

45–54 min
تحدّي توائم الأصوات: كن أنت المعلّم
Method: Peer teaching + game (points) + tongue twisters
Purpose: Use all the new sounds in speaking and listening, with children testing each other.
Teacher says

“Game time! I give one child a secret word card in the private chat. You say the word. The others show the card: p or b, f or v, o or u.”

“If the group shows the right card, the speaker AND the group get a point. Clear sounds win!”

“Now our tongue twisters. Slowly, then fast!”

“'Puffy P puts pink peas in a pot.' / 'Bouncy B has a big blue bag.' / 'Seven vans, very fast!' / 'Grumpy U runs up in the sun.'”

  • سأرسل لك كلمة سرّية في الدردشة الخاصة. قلها، والباقون يرفعون البطاقة الصحيحة.
  • إذا رفع الجميع البطاقة الصحيحة يحصل المتكلّم والمجموعة على نقطة!
Students do
  • Take turns saying a secret minimal-pair word; the others show the card.
  • Give feedback to a friend: 'More puff!' or 'More buzz!'
  • Say each tongue twister slowly, then fast, with the tissue for the p twister.
  • Choose your favourite twister and say it alone.
Exercise · which-word — Listen to your friend. Which word did you hear? Type it in the chat or show the card.
استمع لصديقك. أي كلمة سمعت؟ اكتبها في الدردشة أو ارفع البطاقة.
  1. pig or big? (secret word: pig) → pig
  2. fan or van? (secret word: van) → van
  3. hot or hut? (secret word: hut) → hut
  4. pat or bat? (secret word: bat) → bat
  5. cot or cut? (secret word: cot) → cot
  6. pin or bin? (secret word: pin) → pin
  7. dog or dug? (secret word: dug) → dug
  8. pet or bet? (secret word: pet) → pet
Tips:
  • If the group shows the wrong card, it is usually the speaker's sound, not the listeners' ears: coach the speaker kindly and try again.
  • Teams private chat works well for secret words; with young children a parent can help read it.
  • Make sure every child is both speaker and listener at least twice.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

54–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check each child can hear the new contrasts alone and finish with a fact to share at home.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket! I say five words. Write the sound you hear: p or b, f or v, o or u.”

“Did you know? Every language chooses its own sounds. English has p and v but no ع or ح. Arabic has ع and ح but no p or v. Now your mouth can do both. You are becoming a sound scientist!”

“Your mission at home: do the tissue test with your family. Can they say pizza with a puff?”

  • هل تعلم؟ كل لغة تختار أصواتها. في الإنجليزية p و v وليس فيها ع ولا ح، وفي العربية ع وح وليس فيها p ولا v. والآن فمك يستطيع الاثنين!
  • مهمتك في البيت: جرّب اختبار المنديل مع عائلتك.
Students do
  • Write the letter for 5 words.
  • Say goodbye with Puffy P: 'Bye-bye, p-p-pizza!'
Tips:
  • Note who scored under 4/5 for extra minimal-pair practice in Lessons 9-11 warm-ups (Lesson 9 teaches b and f, a perfect moment to revisit p and v).

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Listen. Write the letter you hear: p or b (first sound), f or v (first sound), o or u (middle sound).
استمع واكتب الحرف الذي تسمعه: p أو b (الصوت الأول)، f أو v (الصوت الأول)، o أو u (الصوت الأوسط).
  1. pig (p or b?) → p
  2. van (f or v?) → v
  3. hut (o or u?) → u
  4. big (p or b?) → b
  5. hot (o or u?) → o

Homework

Tissue Test at Home: show your family Puffy P and Bouncy B with a tissue and the buzz with a hand on the throat. Then say five words with p (pizza, pink, pen, pig, apple) and three numbers with v (five, seven, eleven).

اختبار المنديل في البيت: أرِ عائلتك P النفّاخة و B القافزة بالمنديل، والطنين باليد على الحلق. ثم قل خمس كلمات فيها p (pizza و pink و pen و pig و apple) وثلاثة أرقام فيها v (five و seven و eleven).

  • Do the tissue test with pin / bin and pig / big for a family member.
  • Say the four tongue twisters to someone at home.
  • Draw Round O and Grumpy U and write two words under each (hot, dog / sun, cup).

Support

  • Work on one contrast only (p/b) and leave v and o/u for the next warm-ups.
  • Let the child show the gesture or card instead of writing.
  • Use only word-initial sounds (pin/bin), where the puff is easiest to see.

Challenge

  • Find the twins in the words: tap/dab, cap/cab, fan/van. Which sounds change?
  • Try the spin test: does the tissue move more for pin or for spin? (pin: the puff disappears after s.)
  • Make a new tongue twister with v (very, van, vet, seven).

Success criteria

  • Correctly sorts at least 8 of 10 sounds as buzz or whisper.
  • Shows the right card for at least 8 of 10 p/b listening items.
  • Says pin and bin so that the tissue clearly moves for pin only.
  • Says hot and hut with two clearly different vowels, and red with an unrolled r.

Watch for

  • p said as b (pizza -> bizza)
  • b said as p after practice (over-correction)
  • v said as f (seven -> sefen)
  • rolled Arabic r in red, run
  • u read as long oo (sun -> soon)
  • o and u merged (hot = hut)

Teacher notes

  • This class is about the sound system, not new vocabulary: the words are known lesson words (pizza, peas, pink, apple, red, fish, numbers) or simple CVC words.
  • The letters b and f are formally taught in Lesson 9 and v in Level 2. Here they are practised as sounds; children already know ب and ف, so the sounds themselves are familiar.
  • Do not over-correct accent. The goal is a clear contrast (pin is not bin), not a native accent.
  • Reuse 'More puff!', 'More buzz!' and the Round O / Grumpy U gestures as quick corrections in later lessons.
  • Bonus Class 3 builds on this: blending sounds into words and segmenting words into sounds.
