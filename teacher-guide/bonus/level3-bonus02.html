Word Order: Who Does What

ترتيب الكلمات: مَن يفعل ماذا
Big idea: In English the position of a word tells you its job: the doer comes first, then the verb, then the thing (S-V-O), and adjectives come before the noun and never change for plural or gender.
في الإنجليزية موقع الكلمة يخبرك بوظيفتها: الفاعل أولاً، ثم الفعل، ثم المفعول به (S-V-O)، والصفة تأتي قبل الاسم ولا تتغير مع الجمع أو التأنيث.

Linguistics Corner — The world's word orders · ترتيب الكلمات في لغات العالم

Linguists describe languages by the basic order of Subject, Verb and Object. There are six possible orders: SOV, SVO, VSO, VOS, OVS and OSV.يصف اللغويون اللغات بحسب الترتيب الأساسي للفاعل والفعل والمفعول به. هناك ستة ترتيبات ممكنة: SOV و SVO و VSO و VOS و OVS و OSV.
In a big survey of about 1,400 languages (the World Atlas of Language Structures), SOV is the most common order (Japanese, Korean, Turkish, Hindi, Persian), with SVO close behind (English, Chinese, Spanish, French). VSO, like formal Arabic, Welsh and Irish, is much rarer. Some languages have no single main order, and OSV is the rarest of all.في دراسة كبيرة شملت نحو 1400 لغة (أطلس تراكيب اللغات العالمي WALS)، الترتيب الأكثر انتشاراً هو SOV (اليابانية والكورية والتركية والهندية والفارسية)، ويليه بفارق قليل SVO (الإنجليزية والصينية والإسبانية والفرنسية). أما VSO، مثل العربية الفصحى في الجملة الفعلية والويلزية والأيرلندية، فأندر بكثير. وبعض اللغات ليس لها ترتيب أساسي واحد، و OSV هو الأندر على الإطلاق.
Languages show 'who did what' in two main ways: by word endings (case) or by position. Classical Arabic uses endings (usually ـُ for the doer, ـَ for the object), so the order can move. English lost most of its endings gradually, between about 900 and 600 years ago, so position does the job.تُظهر اللغات 'مَن فعل ماذا' بطريقتين أساسيتين: بعلامات الإعراب أو بموقع الكلمة. العربية الفصحى تستعمل الإعراب (الضمة للفاعل والفتحة للمفعول به في الغالب)، لذلك يمكن أن يتغير الترتيب. أما الإنجليزية فقد فقدت معظم علامات الإعراب تدريجياً بين نحو 900 و600 سنة مضت، فصار الموقع هو الذي يحدد الوظيفة.
Old English (about 1,000 years ago) had case endings like Arabic and a freer order. Modern English kept only a little of this: the possessive 's (Omar's phone) and pronoun forms like he / him, she / her.الإنجليزية القديمة (قبل نحو 1000 سنة) كان فيها إعراب مثل العربية وترتيب أكثر حرية. لم يبقَ في الإنجليزية الحديثة إلا القليل: علامة الملكية 's (Omar's phone) وأشكال الضمائر مثل he / him و she / her.

Key terms

TermبالعربيةMeaning

verb (V)الفعلThe action or state. In an English statement it usually comes right after the subject.
object (O)المفعول بهThe person or thing that receives the action: Sara texts Noor.
word orderترتيب الكلماتThe order in which a language puts the parts of a sentence.
agreementالمطابقةWhen one word changes to match another (in gender, number or 'the'). Arabic adjectives agree with the noun; English adjectives do not.
possessive 'sعلامة الملكية 'sAdded to the owner, who comes first: Omar's backpack (حقيبة عمر).

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
I have a jacket black.I have a black jacket.Arabic order: noun + adjective (سترة سوداء). English puts the adjective first.
She has two blacks hoodies.She has two black hoodies.Arabic adjectives change to match the noun (قلمان أسودان); English adjectives never take -s.
the phone of SaraSara's phoneWord-for-word copy of هاتف سارة. English puts the owner first with 's.

1. Warm-up: Who Texts Who?

0–5 min
إحماء: مَن يراسل مَن؟
Method: Meaning puzzle with two pictures (noticing through contrast)
Purpose: Show that the same three words in a different order mean something different in English.
Teacher says

“Look at picture A and picture B. Same people: Sara and Noor.”

“Sentence 1: 'Sara texts Noor.' Sentence 2: 'Noor texts Sara.' Same three words! Which sentence goes with which picture?”

“Who has the phone in sentence 1? ... Sara! So the first person is the doer.”

“One more: 'Ziad follows Hamad.' / 'Hamad follows Ziad.' Who is in front?”

“Interesting: in English, just moving a word changes the whole story. Why? That's today's mystery.”

  • الكلمات الثلاث نفسها، لكن المعنى تغيّر! لماذا؟ هذا لغز اليوم.
  • مَن يمسك الهاتف؟ الشخص الأول في الجملة هو الذي يقوم بالفعل.
Students do
  • Match each sentence to picture A or B (type A or B).
  • Explain who does the action in each sentence.
  • Make their own pair with two crew names: '___ helps ___.'
Tips:
  • Keep it fast and visual; the 'aha' is that position = meaning.
  • Use crew names so students do not have to think about vocabulary.

2. Discovery: Find the Rules of English Order

5–17 min
اكتشاف: ابحث عن قواعد الترتيب في الإنجليزية
Method: Guided discovery (find-the-rule) with color-coded sentence strips
Purpose: Students discover S-V-O and adjective-before-noun from Lesson 5-8 sentences and state the rules themselves.
Teacher says

“Here are eight sentences from our last lessons, colored like in Bonus Class 1.”

“'Omar wears a black hoodie.' 'My cousins love new sneakers.' 'Noor charges her phone.' 'The principal rings the bell.'”

“Rule hunt 1: where is the doer - the subject? ... First, in every sentence here! Where is the verb? ... Second! What comes after the verb? ... The thing - the object. S - V - O.”

“Rule hunt 2: look at the green words: black, new. Before or after the noun? ... Before!”

“Rule hunt 3: 'a black hoodie' / 'two black hoodies'. Does black change? ... No! English adjectives are lazy: they never change for plural.”

“Rule hunt 4: 'Sara's phone', 'my charger'. Where is the owner? ... First!”

“Now write the four rules in your own words. Short and clear.”

  • أين الفاعل؟ في البداية في كل هذه الجمل. والفعل؟ بعده مباشرة. ثم المفعول به: S - V - O.
  • الصفة قبل الاسم، ولا تتغير مع الجمع أبداً.
  • صاحب الشيء يأتي أولاً: Sara's phone.
Students do
  • Label S, V, O in four sentences using annotation or by typing.
  • Answer the four rule-hunt questions.
  • Write the four rules in their own words and compare with a partner (or the teacher).
  • Do the labeling exercise.
Exercise · label-svo — Label the sentence: S (subject), V (verb), O (object). Type them in order.
حدّد في الجملة: S (الفاعل)، V (الفعل)، O (المفعول به). اكتبها بالترتيب.
  1. Omar wears a black hoodie. → S = Omar, V = wears, O = a black hoodie
  2. My cousins love new sneakers. → S = My cousins, V = love, O = new sneakers
  3. Noor charges her phone. → S = Noor, V = charges, O = her phone
  4. The principal rings the bell. → S = The principal, V = rings, O = the bell
  5. Our teammates need a new captain. → S = Our teammates, V = need, O = a new captain
  6. Ziad forgets his password. → S = Ziad, V = forgets, O = his password
  7. My siblings watch funny videos. → S = My siblings, V = watch, O = funny videos
  8. Sara loses her earbuds every week. → S = Sara, V = loses, O = her earbuds
Tips:
  • Teach that the subject or object can be a group of words (my cousins, a black hoodie): the whole noun group is one slot.
  • 'every week' is extra time information after S-V-O; label it 'extra' if students ask.
  • Let students write the rules before you show yours. Their wording sticks better.

3. Linguistics Corner: The World's Word Orders

17–25 min
ركن اللغويات: ترتيب الكلمات في لغات العالم
Method: Mini-lecture with a map and a 'guess the language' quiz
Purpose: Broaden general knowledge: word order types across languages, and why English depends on position.
Teacher says

“Linguists sort languages by the order of S, V and O. There are six possible orders. English is SVO.”

“Guess: which order is the most common in the world? ... SOV! Like Japanese and Turkish: 'I football play.'”

“SVO, like English, Chinese and Spanish, is a close second. VSO, like formal Arabic and Welsh, is much rarer.”

“So why is English so strict? Old English had endings, like Arabic إعراب. When the endings disappeared, position had to show who did what.”

“Arabic still has endings in formal Arabic: الطالبُ with damma is the doer, wherever it is. English has no damma, so it needs the first seat.”

  • الترتيب الأكثر انتشاراً في العالم هو فاعل - مفعول - فعل، مثل اليابانية والتركية.
  • الإنجليزية القديمة كان فيها إعراب مثل العربية، ولما اختفى الإعراب صار موقع الكلمة هو الذي يحدد وظيفتها.
Students do
  • Guess the most common order before the answer.
  • Match languages to word order types.
  • Say an English sentence in 'Japanese order' and 'formal Arabic order' for fun.
Exercise · match-the-order — Which word order is it? Write SVO, SOV or VSO.
ما نوع الترتيب؟ اكتب SVO أو SOV أو VSO.
  1. English: Omar plays football. → SVO
  2. Japanese style: Omar football plays. → SOV
  3. Formal Arabic: يلعبُ عمرُ الكرةَ. → VSO
  4. Chinese style: Omar plays football. → SVO
  5. Turkish style: Sara her phone charges. → SOV
  6. Welsh style: Texts Sara Noor. → VSO
  7. Spoken Arabic (Egyptian): عمر بيلعب كورة. → SVO
Tips:
  • The ranking comes from the World Atlas of Language Structures (WALS, chapter 81). Do not give exact percentages; say 'most common', 'close behind' and 'much rarer', because many languages have no single dominant order.
  • The 'Japanese style' sentences are English words in another order, just to feel the pattern; say this clearly.

4. Arabic Bridge: Free Arabic, Strict English

25–35 min
جسر العربية: عربية مرنة وإنجليزية صارمة
Method: Contrastive analysis with mirror sentences (Arabic and English side by side)
Purpose: Make the three big Arabic-English differences visible: sentence order, adjective position and agreement, and possession order.
Teacher says

“Difference 1: Arabic can say يلعبُ عمرُ الكرةَ or عمرُ يلعبُ الكرةَ. English only says: Omar plays football.”

“Difference 2: سترة سوداء - noun, then adjective. English flips it: a black jacket. Think of a mirror!”

“Difference 3: Arabic adjectives change: قلم أحمر، سيارة حمراء، السيارة الحمراء. They agree in masculine/feminine, singular/plural and ال. English: red, red, red. Always the same.”

“Difference 4: هاتف سارة - phone, then owner. English: Sara's phone - owner, then phone. Mirror again!”

“Same: على المكتب - on the desk. Prepositions come first in both languages. Easy points!”

“In Arabic, especially when we speak, we can say أخي... هو يلعب. English has one subject seat only: My brother plays. No extra he.”

  • الفرق الأول: العربية تقبل أكثر من ترتيب، والإنجليزية ترتيباً واحداً.
  • الفرق الثاني: الصفة في الإنجليزية قبل الاسم، كأنها صورة في المرآة.
  • الفرق الثالث: الصفة العربية تطابق الاسم، أما الإنجليزية فلا تتغير.
  • الفرق الرابع: في الإنجليزية المالك أولاً: Sara's phone.
  • الشيء المتشابه: حرف الجر قبل الاسم في اللغتين.
Students do
  • Translate Arabic phrases into English, flipping the order.
  • Hold up two cards (noun, adjective) and physically swap them for English order.
  • Spot the difference: say what changes in Arabic and what stays the same in English.
Exercise · mirror-translation — Say it in English. Watch the order and do not change the adjective.
قلها بالإنجليزية. انتبه للترتيب ولا تغيّر الصفة.
  1. سترة سوداء → a black jacket
  2. سترات سوداء → black jackets (not blacks)
  3. هاتف سارة → Sara's phone
  4. حقيبة عمر الجديدة → Omar's new backpack (or Omar's new bag)
  5. أصدقائي الجدد → my new friends (not news)
  6. شاحني القديم → my old charger
  7. هذه الأحذية الرياضية الجديدة → these new sneakers
  8. خزانة نور (locker) بجانب الباب. → Noor's locker is next to the door.
Tips:
  • Be honest: Arabic word order is not 'free' in every way; it is flexible because of إعراب and context. In everyday dialect, the doer usually comes first (SVO).
  • Point out that non-human plurals in Arabic usually take a feminine singular adjective (سيارات حمراء). Students know this without thinking; it shows how much work Arabic adjectives do.
  • Item 5: Arabic الجدد is plural, but English new never changes: my new friends.

5. Sentence Scramble: Rebuild the Message

35–45 min
الجمل المبعثرة: أعد بناء الرسالة
Method: Unjumbling with color-coded word cards (structured production)
Purpose: Practice S-V-O, adjective position, possessives and this/these by building sentences from scrambled words.
Teacher says

“Oh no! Lumi's group chat got scrambled. The words are all there, but the order is crazy.”

“Rebuild each message. Start with the blue subject, then the red verb, then the rest.”

“Hamad, you drive for message 1: tell me which card goes first and I'll drag it.”

“Check your sentence with the four rules: S-V-O? Adjective before noun? Adjective with no -s? Owner first?”

  • ابدأ بالفاعل الأزرق، ثم الفعل الأحمر، ثم الباقي.
  • تحقق من القواعد الأربع قبل أن تجيب.
Students do
  • Take turns directing the teacher where to drag each card (or use the shared whiteboard).
  • Type the full sentence in the chat.
  • Check another student's sentence against the four rules.
Exercise · unjumble — Put the words in the right order to make a statement. The word with a capital letter goes first.
رتّب الكلمات لتكوّن جملة خبرية. الكلمة التي تبدأ بحرف كبير تأتي أولاً.
  1. hoodie / wears / a / gray / Ziad → Ziad wears a gray hoodie.
  2. sneakers / are / new / These / my → These are my new sneakers.
  3. charger / Sara's / is / white → Sara's charger is white.
  4. cousin / My / big / a / has / backpack → My cousin has a big backpack.
  5. locker / next to / is / the gym / His → His locker is next to the gym.
  6. cool / Noor / neighbors / has → Noor has cool neighbors.
  7. The cafeteria / between / is / the library / and / the gym → The cafeteria is between the library and the gym. (or: between the gym and the library)
  8. earbuds / Those / our / are / captain's → Those are our captain's earbuds. (Also correct: Those earbuds are our captain's.)
  9. charger / black / borrows / my / Omar → Omar borrows my black charger.
  10. sunglasses / cool / wears / Hamad → Hamad wears cool sunglasses.
Tips:
  • Online drag-and-drop: if students cannot control the slide, they dictate the order ('first Ziad, then wears...'). Dictating is also good speaking practice.
  • Item 8 is the hardest: two possessives in one noun group (our captain's). Give a hint if needed: 'Whose earbuds?'

6. Error Auction

45–54 min
مزاد الأخطاء
Method: Error auction (gamified accuracy judgement in teams)
Purpose: Test whether students can judge correct and incorrect word order quickly, then fix the wrong ones.
Teacher says

“Welcome to the Lumio Grammar Auction! Each team has 100 coins.”

“I show a sentence. If you think it's CORRECT, bid coins to buy it - 10, 20 or 30.”

“At the end: if you bought a correct sentence, you get double your bid back. If you bought a broken one, you lose your bid!”

“Then, for every broken sentence, the first team to fix it wins 10 bonus coins.”

“Bidding starts... now! Team Noor, how much?”

  • لكل فريق 100 عملة. اشترِ الجمل التي تظنها صحيحة.
  • إذا اشتريت جملة صحيحة تسترد ضعف ما دفعت، وإذا اشتريت جملة خاطئة تخسر ما دفعت.
Students do
  • Discuss quickly with their teammate and bid (type the bid in the chat).
  • After the reveal, fix the broken sentences for bonus coins.
  • Explain the rule behind each fix.
Exercise · error-auction — Correct or broken? Bid on correct sentences; fix the broken ones.
صحيحة أم خاطئة؟ زايد على الجمل الصحيحة وأصلح الخاطئة.
  1. I have a jacket red. → Broken -> I have a red jacket.
  2. Omar's sneakers are new. → Correct
  3. She has two news backpacks. → Broken -> She has two new backpacks.
  4. The phone of Hamad is on the shelf. → Broken -> Hamad's phone is on the shelf.
  5. My sister she loves selfies. → Broken -> My sister loves selfies.
  6. The library is above the cafeteria. → Correct
  7. Charges Noor her phone every night. → Broken -> Noor charges her phone every night.
  8. This headphones are new. → Broken -> These headphones are new.
  9. Our teammates are cool. → Correct
  10. Ziad has cools sunglasses. → Broken -> Ziad has cool sunglasses.
Tips:
  • Keep the coin bank visible on the slide and update it after each sentence; the tension is the fun.
  • Students who bid on a broken sentence should explain the error after the reveal: mistakes become teaching moments, not punishments.
  • With only 2 students, each student is a team.

7. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Mission

54–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والمهمة
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check S-V-O, adjective order and possessives individually; finish with a fact to share at home.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five items, alone, private chat.”

“Did you know? Yoda in Star Wars often puts the object first: 'Much to learn you still have.' That's OSV order, and it's one reason he sounds strange: OSV is the rarest basic order in the world's languages!”

“Your mission: teach someone at home the mirror rule: سترة سوداء = a black jacket, هاتف سارة = Sara's phone.”

  • هل تعلم؟ شخصية يودا في حرب النجوم تقدّم المفعول به كثيراً: 'Much to learn you still have'، وهذا ترتيب مفعول - فاعل - فعل (OSV)، ومن أسباب غرابة كلامه أنه أندر ترتيب أساسي في لغات العالم!
  • مهمتك: علّم أحداً في البيت قاعدة المرآة.
Students do
  • Answer 5 items in a private chat.
  • Say a Yoda sentence and then 'translate' it into normal English order.
Tips:
  • Students who miss the adjective items need a quick 'mirror' warm-up in Lesson 9.
  • Yoda's order is not perfectly consistent, and lines like 'Powerful you have become' put a describing word first, not an object. 'Often' and the 'Much to learn' example keep the fact honest.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Write the correct English.
اكتب الجملة الإنجليزية الصحيحة.
  1. Put in order: football / plays / Hamad → Hamad plays football.
  2. Fix it: I want a cap white. → I want a white cap.
  3. Fix it: They have greens caps. → They have green caps.
  4. Say it in English: شاحن عمر → Omar's charger
  5. Fix it: My best friend he lives next to me. → My best friend lives next to me.

Homework

Mirror Mission: describe 6 things in your bag or room using adjective + noun and possessives (my black backpack, my brother's old charger). Then write 3 S-V-O sentences about your crew and label S, V and O.

مهمة المرآة: صِف 6 أشياء في حقيبتك أو غرفتك باستعمال صفة + اسم والملكية (my black backpack, my brother's old charger). ثم اكتب 3 جمل عن أصدقائك بترتيب فاعل - فعل - مفعول وحدّد S و V و O.

  • Write 6 noun groups with adjectives and possessives.
  • Write 3 S-V-O sentences and label them.
  • Bonus: write one Yoda sentence and its normal English version.

Support

  • Give the subject card already in place in the scramble activity.
  • Work only with one adjective per noun.
  • Use the S-V-O color strip on screen as a visual frame during every task.

Challenge

  • Two adjectives before a noun: English has a usual order (opinion before color): a cool black hoodie, not a black cool hoodie. Find the rule from 4 examples.
  • Explain how formal Arabic can say يراسلُ زيادٌ عمرَ or يراسلُ عمرَ زيادٌ and both mean 'Ziad texts Omar' (the endings show the doer), while English cannot move the words.
  • Find 2 other languages spoken by people you know and find out their word order.

Success criteria

  • Labels S, V and O correctly in at least 6 of 8 sentences.
  • Unjumbles at least 8 of 10 sentences correctly.
  • Uses adjective + noun with no plural adjective in speaking and writing.
  • Uses 's for possession instead of 'the ... of ...'.

Watch for

  • Noun + adjective order: a hoodie black
  • Plural adjectives: blacks, news, cools
  • the phone of Sara instead of Sara's phone
  • Double subject: My brother he...
  • Verb-first statements

Teacher notes

  • The central idea is 'position = job' in English. Return to the warm-up pictures (Sara texts Noor) whenever a student moves a word.
  • Do not present Arabic as 'messy' or 'free for no reason': its flexibility comes from case endings and context, which is a smart system. English is strict because it lost its endings.
  • 'The ... of ...' is not always wrong in English (the end of the movie, the top of the page); for people's things, 's is the normal form. Teach the people rule only at this level.
  • Bonus Class 3 builds on this: questions are the main place where English changes S-V-O, by moving the helper verb to the front.
