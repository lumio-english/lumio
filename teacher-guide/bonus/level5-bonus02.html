How Stories Are Built: The Story Mountain

كيف تُبنى الحكايات: جبل القصة
Big idea: Most stories, in English or in Arabic, climb a similar mountain: a setting, a problem that grows, a climax, and an ending. Linking words are the ropes that hold the parts together.
معظم القصص، بالإنجليزية أو بالعربية، تصعد جبلاً متشابهاً: مكان وزمان، ثم مشكلة تكبر، ثم ذروة، ثم نهاية. وكلمات الربط هي الحبال التي تشدّ الأجزاء معاً.

Linguistics Corner — Stories have a skeleton · للقصص هيكل عظمي

About 2,300 years ago the Greek thinker Aristotle wrote that a good plot has a beginning, a middle and an end. In 1863 the German writer Gustav Freytag described the shape of a play as a pyramid with five parts (in sad plays his last part was the 'catastrophe'). Schools today use a simpler version called the story mountain.قبل نحو 2300 سنة كتب الفيلسوف اليوناني أرسطو أن الحبكة الجيدة لها بداية ووسط ونهاية. وفي عام 1863 وصف الكاتب الألماني غوستاف فرايتاغ بناء المسرحية بهرم من خمسة أجزاء (وكان الجزء الأخير عنده في المسرحيات الحزينة «الكارثة»)، وتستخدم المدارس اليوم صورة أبسط منه تسميها «جبل القصة».
The five parts: setting (who, where, when) - rising action (a problem grows) - climax (the top!) - falling action (fixing it) - resolution (the ending and the feeling).الأجزاء الخمسة: التمهيد (من، أين، متى) - تصاعد الأحداث (المشكلة تكبر) - الذروة (القمة!) - انحدار الأحداث (حل المشكلة) - الخاتمة (النهاية والشعور).
It is not only for books. In the 1960s the linguist William Labov recorded ordinary people telling true stories about their lives. Their stories had similar parts with other names: an 'orientation' (who, where, when), a 'complicating action' (what happened) and a 'resolution' (how it ended). Many also had a short 'abstract' at the start ('Did I tell you about...?'), a 'coda' at the end that brings us back to now, and an 'evaluation', the part that says why the story matters ('It was the scariest moment of my life!').ليس للكتب فقط. في الستينيات سجّل اللغوي ويليام لابوف أشخاصاً عاديين يحكون قصصاً حقيقية من حياتهم، فوجد في قصصهم أجزاء متشابهة بأسماء أخرى: «التوجيه» (من، أين، متى)، و«الحدث المعقِّد» (ماذا حدث)، و«الحل» (كيف انتهى). وفي كثير منها أيضاً «ملخّص» قصير في البداية، و«خاتمة» تعيدنا إلى الحاضر، و«التقييم»: الجزء الذي يقول لماذا القصة مهمة.
Linking words do a special job that linguists call cohesion: they glue sentences together and tell the listener what comes next. 'Suddenly' warns: a surprise is coming. 'So' shows a result. 'In the end' says: the story is closing.تقوم كلمات الربط بعمل يسميه اللغويون «التماسك»: تلصق الجمل ببعضها وتخبر السامع بما سيأتي. Suddenly تنبّه إلى مفاجأة، و so تُظهر نتيجة، و in the end تقول إن القصة تُختم.
Every culture has story formulas: 'Once upon a time' in English, 'كان يا ما كان، في قديم الزمان' in Arabic. They are signals: they tell the listener 'relax, a story is starting'.لكل ثقافة صيغ ثابتة للحكاية: Once upon a time بالإنجليزية، و«كان يا ما كان، في قديم الزمان» بالعربية. إنها إشارات تقول للسامع: «استرخِ، الحكاية بدأت».
Some of the most famous cliffhangers in world literature are in an Arabic classic: in 'One Thousand and One Nights', Shahrazad stops every night at the exciting part, so the king wants to hear the rest the next night.من أشهر النهايات المعلّقة في الأدب العالمي ما نجده في الكتاب العربي «ألف ليلة وليلة»: تتوقف شهرزاد كل ليلة عند الجزء المثير، فيريد الملك أن يسمع البقية في الليلة التالية.

Arabic Bridge

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
Once upon a time... / A long time ago...كان يا ما كان، في قديم الزمان، وسالف العصر والأوانBoth languages have a fixed, magical opener for folk tales. English is short; the Arabic one is longer and has rhyme and rhythm. For a true personal story, English starts with a time phrase: 'Last summer...', 'Two days ago...'.
في اللغتين بداية ثابتة سحرية للحكايات الشعبية. الإنجليزية قصيرة، والعربية أطول وفيها سجع وإيقاع. وللقصة الشخصية الحقيقية تبدأ الإنجليزية بعبارة زمن مثل Last summer.
...and they lived happily ever after.وعاشوا في تبات ونبات، وخلّفوا صبيان وبنات (خاتمة شعبية باللهجة المصرية)Fixed endings exist in both (this Arabic one is Egyptian dialect). In modern stories English usually ends with a feeling or a lesson: 'In the end, we were tired but happy.'
توجد خواتيم ثابتة في اللغتين. وفي القصص الحديثة تنتهي الإنجليزية غالباً بشعور أو درس: In the end, we were tired but happy.
then / after that / suddenly / so / becauseو / فـ / ثم / فجأةً / لذلك / لأنّArabic grammar books already describe the difference: فـ means 'then, right away' and ثم means 'then, after some time'. English shows this with different words: then, immediately, after that, later. Arabic writing often joins many sentences with و; in English a long chain of 'and... and then...' sounds weak in writing. Use periods and different linking words.
النحو العربي يعرف الفرق أصلاً: الفاء للترتيب والتعقيب، وثم للترتيب والتراخي. والإنجليزية تُظهر ذلك بكلمات مختلفة: then و immediately و after that و later. الكتابة العربية تربط جملاً كثيرة بالواو، أما في الإنجليزية فسلسلة and then الطويلة تبدو ضعيفة في الكتابة. استخدم النقطة وكلمات ربط متنوعة.
two days ago / last weekقبل يومين / الأسبوع الماضيArabic puts قبل BEFORE the time; English puts ago AFTER it: two days ago (not 'before two days'). And 'last week' needs no preposition (not 'in last week').
العربية تضع «قبل» قبل المدة، والإنجليزية تضع ago بعدها: two days ago وليس before two days. و last week لا تحتاج إلى حرف جر (وليس in last week).
Because there was traffic, we were late. / There was traffic, so we were late.لأنه كان هناك زحام، تأخرنا. / كان هناك زحام، لذلك تأخرنا.Use ONE linker: because OR so, not both. Arabic can say «لأنني كنت متعباً فنمت» with فـ in the second half, but English cannot say 'Because I was tired, so I slept'.
استخدم أداة ربط واحدة: because أو so وليس الاثنتين. العربية تقول «لأنني كنت متعباً فنمت» بالفاء في النصف الثاني، لكن الإنجليزية لا تقبل Because I was tired, so I slept.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
Before two days I went to the mall.Two days ago I went to the mall.Direct translation of قبل يومين. In English, ago comes after the time.
In last week we visited my uncle.Last week we visited my uncle.Translating في الأسبوع الماضي word by word. last / next / this + time take no preposition.
There was many people in the queue.There were many people in the queue.In Arabic كان هناك often stays the same; in English it must agree: was + one, were + many.
Because I was tired, so I went to bed.Because I was tired, I went to bed. / I was tired, so I went to bed.Using two linkers, influenced by Arabic لأنّ... فـ. English takes one.
We went to the mall and then we ate and then we saw a movie and then we went home.First, we went to the mall. After that, we ate. Then we saw a movie. In the end, we went home.Long و-style chains. English prefers shorter sentences with varied linking words.
We were late because the traffic.We were late because of the traffic. / ...because there was a lot of traffic.because + a sentence (subject + verb); because of + a noun.

1. Warm-up: Which Language Is This Opener?

0–5 min
إحماء: بداية حكاية بأي لغة؟
Method: Prediction hook + cultural comparison
Purpose: Show that story formulas are universal and switch on 'story mode'.
Teacher says

“Story Scientists, listen: 'كان يا ما كان، في قديم الزمان...' What happens next? A story! How do you know? Those words are a signal.”

“English has a signal too: 'Once upon a time...' What kind of story starts like that? A fairy tale!”

“And a true story from your life? You do not say 'once upon a time'. You say: 'Last Friday...', 'Two days ago...'.”

“Quick round: start a true story with a time phrase in 10 seconds. Go!”

  • «كان يا ما كان» إشارة تقول: الحكاية بدأت. وفي الإنجليزية Once upon a time للحكايات الخيالية.
  • أما القصة الحقيقية من حياتك فتبدأ بعبارة زمن: Last Friday أو Two days ago.
Students do
  • Guess what follows each opener.
  • Each student says one true story opener with a time phrase: 'Last summer, there was...'
Tips:
  • If a student says 'Before two days', note it and come back to it in the Arabic Bridge stage.
  • Keep the pace fast; this is a hook.

2. Linguistics Corner: Climb the Story Mountain

5–15 min
ركن اللغويات: تسلّق جبل القصة
Method: Graphic organizer + guided discovery (genre analysis)
Purpose: Learn the five parts of narrative structure and find them in a model story.
Teacher says

“Draw a mountain on your paper with five flags. Flag 1 at the bottom left: setting. Flag 2 going up: rising action. Flag 3 at the top: climax. Flag 4 going down: falling action. Flag 5 at the bottom right: resolution.”

“A German writer, Gustav Freytag, described a shape like this for plays in 1863. Aristotle said it more simply, about 2,300 years ago: beginning, middle, end.”

“Now listen to Hamad's true story, 'The Lost Ticket'. Which part is each sentence? Put your finger on the flag.”

“And the linguist William Labov found something else: real people add why the story matters. 'It was the most stressful day of my life!' That makes listeners care.”

  • ارسم جبلاً عليه خمس رايات: التمهيد، تصاعد الأحداث، الذروة، انحدار الأحداث، الخاتمة.
  • استمع إلى قصة حمد واجعل إصبعك على الراية المناسبة لكل جملة.
Students do
  • Draw the five-flag mountain.
  • Listen to and read 'The Lost Ticket' and label each sentence with a flag number.
  • Underline the linking words in the story (Last Friday, First, so, When, Suddenly, In the end).
Exercise · label — Read each sentence from 'The Lost Ticket'. Which part of the mountain is it? 1 setting, 2 rising action, 3 climax, 4 falling action, 5 resolution.
اقرأ كل جملة من قصة «التذكرة المفقودة». في أي جزء من الجبل هي؟ 1 التمهيد، 2 تصاعد الأحداث، 3 الذروة، 4 انحدار الأحداث، 5 الخاتمة.
  1. Last Friday, there was a big football match in the city. Hamad and Omar had two tickets and they were very excited. → 1 setting
  2. First, they took the bus, but there was a lot of traffic, so they were late. → 2 rising action
  3. When they arrived at the stadium, there was a long queue. → 2 rising action
  4. Suddenly, Hamad checked his pocket. His ticket wasn't there! → 3 climax
  5. They ran back to the bus stop. The bus driver found the ticket on a seat and gave it back. → 4 falling action
  6. In the end, they watched the match and their team won. It was unforgettable! → 5 resolution
  7. Now Hamad always checks his pocket twice! → 5 resolution (Labov would call this the coda: it brings us back to now)
Tips:
  • Rising action can have several sentences; that is normal and a good discussion point.
  • Ask: 'Where is the feeling?' (very excited, unforgettable). Labov's evaluation is often hidden in adjectives from Lesson 4.

3. Arabic Bridge: Openers, Endings and Ropes

15–22 min
جسر العربية: البدايات والخواتيم والحبال
Method: Contrastive analysis + matching
Purpose: Use Arabic story knowledge as a tool and fix the translation traps (before two days, in last week, because... so).
Teacher says

“Arabic grammar is clever here. What is the difference between 'دخل فجلس' and 'دخل ثم جلس'? With فـ he sat down immediately. With ثم there was some time. English uses words for this: then, immediately, after that, later.”

“Trap 1: قبل يومين. Many students say 'before two days'. English flips it: two days ago. The time first, then ago.”

“Trap 2: في الأسبوع الماضي. English: just 'last week'. No 'in'!”

“Trap 3: لأنني كنت متعباً فنمت. In English: ONE rope only. 'Because I was tired, I slept.' or 'I was tired, so I slept.'”

“Let's match Arabic and English story ropes.”

  • الفاء للتعقيب (فوراً)، وثم للتراخي (بعد مدة). والإنجليزية تعبّر عن ذلك بكلمات مثل immediately و after that و later.
  • قبل يومين = two days ago، والأسبوع الماضي = last week بلا in، واستخدم أداة ربط واحدة: because أو so.
Students do
  • Match the Arabic expressions with their English partners.
  • Say each English phrase in a sentence about their week.
  • Spot the trap: say which items would be wrong if translated word by word.
Exercise · match — Match the Arabic with the best English. Watch out for word-by-word traps!
صِل العبارة العربية بأفضل مقابل إنجليزي. احذر فخاخ الترجمة الحرفية!
  1. كان يا ما كان، في قديم الزمان → Once upon a time / A long time ago
  2. قبل يومين → two days ago (not 'before two days')
  3. في الأسبوع الماضي → last week (not 'in last week')
  4. وفجأةً → Suddenly
  5. وفي النهاية → In the end / Finally
  6. لأنه كان هناك زحام → because there was a lot of traffic
  7. لذلك تأخرنا → so we were late
  8. وعاشوا في سعادة إلى الأبد → and they lived happily ever after
Tips:
  • The فـ / ثم point impresses teens who study Arabic grammar at school; keep it to one example.
  • Do not say Arabic 'has no punctuation'; say Arabic style accepts longer chains with و.

4. The Rope Workshop: Linking Words in Action

22–32 min
ورشة الحبال: كلمات الربط في العمل
Method: Guided practice: cloze with choices + reason talk
Purpose: Choose the right linking word for time, surprise, reason and result.
Teacher says

“Here is Noor's story with missing ropes. Each rope has a job: time order, surprise, reason, result or ending.”

“Before you choose, ask: what is the job here?”

“Then read the whole story aloud with your rope. Does it flow?”

  • لكل كلمة ربط وظيفة: ترتيب زمني، مفاجأة، سبب، نتيجة أو خاتمة. اسأل نفسك أولاً: ما الوظيفة هنا؟
Students do
  • Complete the gaps (type in the chat or write on paper).
  • Name the job of each linking word.
  • Read the completed story aloud, one sentence each.
Exercise · gap-fill — Complete Noor's story with: ago, there were, first, then, suddenly, because, so, in the end.
أكمل قصة نور بالكلمات: ago, there were, first, then, suddenly, because, so, in the end.
  1. Two weeks ___, Noor went to a school science fair. → ago
  2. ___ a lot of students and parents in the hall. → There were
  3. ___, she set up her volcano project on the table. → First
  4. ___ she explained it to the judges. → Then
  5. ___, the volcano exploded too early! → Suddenly
  6. It happened ___ she put in too much vinegar. → because
  7. The table was wet, ___ she cleaned it quickly. → so
  8. ___, the judges laughed and gave her a special prize. → In the end
Tips:
  • Accept 'Finally' for the last gap and 'Next' for gap 4 and discuss small differences.
  • Point out the comma after First, Suddenly, In the end at the start of a sentence.

5. Story Surgeons: Put It Back in Order

32–40 min
جرّاحو القصص: أعِد الترتيب
Method: Text reconstruction (jigsaw sentences) with cohesion clues
Purpose: Use linking words and the mountain shape as clues to order a story.
Teacher says

“Oh no! Ziad's story fell off the mountain. The sentences are mixed up.”

“Your clues: time words, 'suddenly', 'so', 'in the end', and the mountain. Which sentence is the setting? Which is the climax?”

“Teams of two: you have 3 minutes. Then explain one clue that helped you.”

  • قصة زياد سقطت من الجبل وتبعثرت جملها! استخدم كلمات الربط وشكل الجبل لترتيبها.
Students do
  • Order the 8 sentences A-H (drag on the whiteboard or write the letters).
  • Explain one clue: 'C is first because it has the setting: Last summer, there was...'
  • Read the story in the right order.
Exercise · ordering — Put the sentences of Ziad's story in the right order (1-8).
رتّب جمل قصة زياد بالترتيب الصحيح (1-8).
  1. A. Suddenly, there was a loud noise and the lights went out. → 4
  2. B. At the end of the visit, we went to the gift shop and I bought a dinosaur key ring. → 7
  3. C. Last summer, there was a new dinosaur museum in our city, and my class visited it. → 1
  4. D. In the end, it was my favorite school trip ever. → 8
  5. E. First, we watched a short movie about dinosaurs. → 2
  6. F. A minute later, the lights came on again. It was only a storm, so everyone laughed. → 6
  7. G. Then we walked into the big hall and saw a huge T. rex. → 3
  8. H. Everyone screamed because it was very dark. → 5
Tips:
  • Correct order: C, E, G, A, H, F, B, D. 'At the end of the visit' puts B after the lights story; accept another order only if the student gives a logical reason.
  • This activity works well on the Teams Whiteboard with sticky notes.

6. Team Creation: The Chain Story Mountain

40–52 min
إبداع جماعي: جبل القصة المتسلسل
Method: Collaborative storytelling (chain story) with story dice prompts
Purpose: Produce a whole story together, each student building one part of the mountain with the right linking words.
Teacher says

“Now you are the writers. I roll the Story Dice: we get a place, a problem and a twist.”

“Student 1 builds the setting: 'Last ___, there was/were...'. Student 2 builds the rising action: 'First... then... so...'. Student 3 is the climax: 'Suddenly...!'. Student 4 fixes it and ends it: 'Because... In the end... It was...'”

“Each part: two or three sentences. Use past verbs and at least two linking words.”

“Then we swap roles and roll again!”

  • أرمي نرد القصة فنحصل على مكان ومشكلة ومفاجأة. كل طالب يبني جزءاً من الجبل بجملتين أو ثلاث وبكلمتي ربط على الأقل.
  • ثم نتبادل الأدوار ونرمي من جديد!
Students do
  • Roll (or choose) a story card set and plan their part for 1 minute.
  • Tell the story in a chain, one part each; the teacher types it on the whiteboard.
  • Swap roles and make a second story with new cards.
  • Vote for the best climax sentence.
Exercise · story-dice — Use the dice cards. Build one part of the story with 2-3 sentences and at least two linking words.
استخدم بطاقات النرد. ابنِ جزءاً من القصة بجملتين أو ثلاث وكلمتي ربط على الأقل.
  1. Place: a crowded mall / Problem: a lost phone / Twist: a strange message → Model: Last weekend, there were hundreds of people at the mall... Suddenly, my phone wasn't in my bag!
  2. Place: a desert camp / Problem: a sandstorm / Twist: a surprise visitor → Model: Last winter, my family went to a desert camp... Suddenly, there was a sandstorm...
  3. Place: the school sports day / Problem: a broken shoe / Twist: a prize → Model: Two weeks ago, there was a sports day at school... In the end, I won a prize!
  4. Place: a birthday dinner / Problem: a long delay / Twist: the cake fell → Model: Yesterday, it was my grandma's birthday... the food was late because there was a problem in the kitchen...
  5. Place: a football match / Problem: heavy traffic / Twist: a famous player → Model: Last Friday, there was a big match... there was a lot of traffic, so we were late...
  6. Place: a library / Problem: a noisy group / Twist: a hidden message in a book → Model: A few days ago, the library was quiet... Suddenly, I found an old note inside a book!
Tips:
  • Answers are models: any logical story with correct parts and linking words is right.
  • Give the climax student a dramatic voice tip: pause before 'Suddenly...'.
  • With fewer than 4 students, one student does both falling action and resolution.

7. Exit Ticket, Shahrazad's Cliffhanger and Goodbye

52–60 min
بطاقة الخروج ونهاية شهرزاد المعلّقة والوداع
Method: Formative check + cultural curiosity close
Purpose: Check the key ideas individually and end with a cliffhanger technique students can use.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five questions. Answer in the chat.”

“Did you know? One of the most famous storytellers in world literature is Shahrazad, in the Arabic classic 'One Thousand and One Nights'. Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer the five questions.
أجب عن الأسئلة الخمسة.
  1. Put in order: climax, setting, resolution, rising action, falling action → setting, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution
  2. 'Suddenly, the lights went out!' Which part of the mountain is it usually? → the climax
  3. English for كان يا ما كان → Once upon a time
  4. Correct it: Before three days I visited my cousin. → Three days ago I visited my cousin.
  5. Correct it: Because it was raining, so we stayed home. → Because it was raining, we stayed home. / It was raining, so we stayed home.

Homework

The Shahrazad Challenge: write a true or invented story (100-120 words) that climbs the mountain but STOPS at the climax. Use there was/were, a time phrase, at least four linking words and one 'because' or 'so'. Draw the mountain and write which sentence goes with each flag.

تحدي شهرزاد: اكتب قصة حقيقية أو خيالية (100-120 كلمة) تصعد الجبل لكنها تتوقف عند الذروة. استخدم there was/were وعبارة زمن وأربع كلمات ربط على الأقل و because أو so. ارسم الجبل واكتب أي جملة تقع عند كل راية.

  • Write the cliffhanger story (100-120 words).
  • Highlight every linking word in one color.
  • Write two possible endings on a separate paper; your classmates will guess next time.

Support

  • Use a three-part mountain first (beginning, problem, ending), then split it into five.
  • Give a sentence frame for each flag: Last ___, there was... / First... then... / Suddenly... / ... so ... / In the end, it was...
  • In the chain story, let the student choose the part they feel safest with (often the setting).

Challenge

  • Add Labov's 'evaluation': one sentence that says why the story matters, in the middle of the story, not only at the end.
  • Retell 'The Lost Ticket' from the bus driver's point of view.
  • Use 'meanwhile' to show two places at the same time (Meanwhile, at the stadium...).

Success criteria

  • Labels at least 6 of 7 sentences of 'The Lost Ticket' with the right mountain part.
  • Orders Ziad's story correctly or with a logical, explained variation.
  • Uses at least two different linking words correctly in their chain-story part.
  • Uses 'ago' and 'last' without 'before' / 'in'.

Watch for

  • 'before two days' / 'in last week'
  • 'There was many...'
  • because + so together
  • 'because the traffic' (missing 'of' or a verb)
  • long chains of 'and then'

Teacher notes

  • This class teaches structure, not new vocabulary: the stories mostly use words from Lessons 1-8. Quickly gloss the few new nouns (science fair, volcano, vinegar, judges, gift shop, key ring).
  • The mountain is a thinking tool, not a law: many great stories bend it. Say so to strong students.
  • Keep the Arabic examples respectful and light: folk tale formulas and Shahrazad are shared heritage the students can be proud of.
  • Bonus Class 3 adds the 'background' layer to the setting: was/were + -ing (It was raining...).
