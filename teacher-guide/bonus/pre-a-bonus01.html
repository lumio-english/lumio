Linguistics Corner — Your mouth is a music box · فمك صندوق موسيقى

Speech starts with air from your lungs. In your throat there is a voice box. For some sounds it shakes (buzzes), like a guitar string: put your hand on your throat and say mmm or zzz.الكلام يبدأ بهواء يخرج من الرئتين. في الحلق صندوق الصوت (الحنجرة)، وهو يهتز مع بعض الأصوات مثل وتر الجيتار: ضع يدك على حلقك وقل mmm أو zzz.
Then the lips, teeth and tongue shape the air. /m/, /b/ and /p/ are lip sounds. /t/, /d/, /n/ and /l/ are tongue-tip sounds. /k/ and /g/ are made with the back of the tongue. /f/ is made with the top teeth on the bottom lip.ثم تشكّل الشفتان والأسنان واللسان الهواء. /m/ و/b/ و/p/ أصوات شفوية. /t/ و/d/ و/n/ و/l/ يصنعها طرف اللسان. /k/ و/g/ يصنعهما مؤخر اللسان. و/f/ نصنعه بوضع الأسنان العليا على الشفة السفلى.
/m/ and /n/ are nose sounds: the air comes out of your nose. Pinch your nose and try to hum mmm: the sound stops!/m/ و/n/ صوتان أنفيان: يخرج الهواء من الأنف. أمسك أنفك وحاول أن تقول mmm طويلاً: سيتوقف الصوت!
Every letter has a name and a sound. Most English letter names have the sound inside them (B 'bee', M 'em', K 'kay'), but some are tricky: H is called 'aitch', yet it says /h/ as in hat.لكل حرف اسم وصوت. معظم أسماء الحروف الإنجليزية فيها صوت الحرف (B 'بي'، M 'إم'، K 'كاي')، لكن بعضها مخادع: H اسمه 'إيتش' وصوته /h/ كما في hat.
Animals make noises to call each other, but only people put sounds together to make thousands of words. Birds like Lumi do not even use a voice box like ours: they sing with a special organ deep in the chest called a syrinx.الحيوانات تُصدر أصواتاً لتنادي بعضها، لكن الإنسان وحده يجمع الأصوات ليصنع آلاف الكلمات. والطيور مثل لومي لا تستخدم حنجرة مثلنا، بل تغرّد بعضو خاص في عمق الصدر اسمه المِصفار (syrinx).
Babies can hear the sounds of every language in the world. By about their first birthday, their ears tune in to the languages they hear at home. Learning English young keeps those ears open.الرضّع يستطيعون سماع أصوات كل لغات العالم، وقرب عيد ميلادهم الأول تتخصص آذانهم في اللغات التي يسمعونها في البيت. تعلّم الإنجليزية في الصغر يُبقي الأذن مفتوحة.

Arabic Bridge

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
B: name 'bee', sound /b/ب: اسمها 'باء'، صوتها /b/Exactly the same idea in both languages: we call the letter by its name, but in a word it says only its sound. Ball starts with /b/, not 'bee'. باب starts with /b/, not 'باء'.
الفكرة نفسها في اللغتين: ننادي الحرف باسمه، لكنه في الكلمة يقول صوته فقط. ball تبدأ بالصوت /b/ لا بـ'بي'، وباب تبدأ بالصوت /b/ لا بـ'باء'.
m, n, l, t, d, f, kم، ن، ل، ت، د، ف، كArabic already has these sounds, made in the same place in the mouth. Moon starts like موز, nose like نور, lion like ليمون, fish like فيل.
هذه الأصوات موجودة في العربية وتُصنع في المكان نفسه من الفم: moon تبدأ مثل موز، و nose مثل نور، و lion مثل ليمون، و fish مثل فيل.
/m/ and /n/ hum in the noseالغُنّة في م و نChildren who learn Quran recitation already know the ghunna: the humming nose sound of م and ن. English /m/ and /n/ hum in the nose in the same way.
الأطفال الذين يتعلمون التلاوة يعرفون الغُنّة: صوت الأنف في الميم والنون. و/m/ و/n/ في الإنجليزية يخرجان من الأنف بالطريقة نفسها.
P in penلا يوجد حرف p في العربيةArabic has no /p/, so pen can come out as 'ben'. /p/ is a lip sound like ب, but with no buzz and a little puff of air you can feel on your hand.
لا يوجد صوت /p/ في العربية، لذلك قد تتحول pen إلى 'ben'. الـ/p/ صوت شفوي مثل الباء، لكن بلا اهتزاز في الحلق ومع نفخة هواء صغيرة تشعر بها على يدك.
H in hat, Hamadهـ وليست حEnglish /h/ is soft, like ه in هدية. English has no ح and no ع, so the names Hamad (حمد) and Omar (عمر) sound a little different when English speakers say them.
صوت /h/ الإنجليزي خفيف مثل الهاء في هدية. لا يوجد في الإنجليزية حاء ولا عين، لذلك يُنطق اسما حمد وعمر بطريقة مختلفة قليلاً على لسان المتحدث بالإنجليزية.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
ben (for pen)penArabic has no /p/ sound, so the child uses the nearest Arabic sound, ب. Feel the puff of air on the hand for /p/.
'bee-all' (letter name used inside the word ball)ball, starting with the sound /b/The child mixes the letter's name with its sound. Use the two hand signs: open hand = name, fist = sound.
E called 'ee' and I called 'ee' (E and I mixed up)E is 'ee', I is 'eye'The English names of E and I are easy to swap, and Arabic has no letter names that match them. Use pictures: E-egg, I-ice cream.
G called 'jay' and J called 'jee'G is 'jee', J is 'jay'The two names sound alike and are swapped by many young learners (native speakers too). Anchor them: G-goat, J-juice.
'buh', 'tuh', 'muh' (big extra vowel after every sound)/b/, /t/, /mmm/ said short and cleanChildren (and teachers) add a vowel to make the sound easier to hear. A small extra vowel is normal at this age, but keep /m/, /s/, /f/ long and clean and /b/, /t/ short.

1. Warm-up: Hello Song and the Sound Box

0–5 min
إحماء: أغنية مرحباً وصندوق الأصوات
Method: Song with gestures + listening hook (attention and curiosity)
Purpose: Get bodies and ears ready, and make children curious about sounds before any letters appear.
Teacher says

“Hello, Sound Explorers! Hello, Sara! Hello, Ziad! Wave hello!”

“Let's sing: Hello, hello, how are you? (wave)”

“Look! Lumi has a sound box. Shhh... close your eyes. Listen.”

“(Knock on the table.) What is it? A knock! (Ring the bell / tap the cup.) What is it?”

“Open your eyes. Today we explore sounds. Our mouth makes music!”

  • مرحباً يا مستكشفي الأصوات! لوّحوا بأيديكم!
  • لومي معه صندوق أصوات. أغمضوا عيونكم واسمعوا جيداً.
  • ما هذا الصوت؟ طَرق! وهذا؟ جرس!
  • اليوم سنستكشف الأصوات. فمنا يصنع الموسيقى!
Students do
  • Sing and wave the hello song.
  • Close eyes and guess 3 noises (knock, bell or cup, clap).
  • Clap back the teacher's rhythm: clap-clap, clap-clap-clap.
Tips:
  • Keep it fast and joyful; accept Arabic guesses and say the English word back.
  • If a child cannot keep eyes closed, ask them to cover their eyes with both hands: it becomes a game.

2. Linguistics Corner: Noise or Word?

5–13 min
ركن اللغويات: ضجيج أم كلمة؟
Method: Guided discovery + whole-group response with gestures
Purpose: Discover that speech is special: a noise has no meaning, but speech sounds join together to make words.
Teacher says

“Listen. (Clap.) Is it a word? No! It is a noise.”

“Listen. 'Hello!' Is it a word? Yes! Hello is a word. My mouth made it.”

“A dog says woof woof. A dog cannot say 'hello'. Only people make words!”

“Noise: hands on your ears (cover ears). Word: point to your mouth. Ready?”

“Lumi is a bird. Birds sing tweet tweet. They do not have lips like us!”

  • التصفيق ضجيج، ليس كلمة. أما 'hello' فكلمة صنعها فمي.
  • الكلب يقول هاو هاو، لكنه لا يستطيع أن يقول 'hello'. الإنسان وحده يصنع الكلمات!
  • إذا سمعت ضجيجاً غطِّ أذنيك. إذا سمعت كلمة أشر إلى فمك.
  • لومي طائر، والطيور تغرّد، لكن ليس لها شفتان مثلنا!
Students do
  • Listen to each sound and show the gesture: cover ears for a noise, point to the mouth for a word.
  • Say each word back after the teacher.
  • Make one noise of their own (stamp, clap, knock) and say one word they know (hello, cat, moon).
Exercise · listen-and-show — Listen. Is it a noise or a word? Cover your ears for a noise. Point to your mouth for a word.
استمع. هل هو ضجيج أم كلمة؟ غطِّ أذنيك للضجيج، وأشر إلى فمك للكلمة.
  1. (a clap) → noise
  2. hello → word
  3. (a knock on the table) → noise
  4. cat → word
  5. (a cough) → noise
  6. moon → word
  7. (a whistle) → noise
  8. goodbye → word
Tips:
  • Hide your mouth behind a card for the noises so children cannot guess by watching you.
  • Keep the language at one level: 'noise' and 'word'. Do not go into animal communication; the simple point is enough.

3. Meet the Mouth Helpers: Lips, Teeth, Tongue

13–23 min
تعرّف على مساعدي الفم: الشفتان والأسنان واللسان
Method: Multisensory phonics: mirror, hand on throat, pinch the nose, tissue in front of the mouth
Purpose: Feel and see where sounds are made, so later letters have a body memory.
Teacher says

“Take your mirror. Look at your lips. Say mmm... like yummy food! Lips close: mmm.”

“Now pinch your nose and say mmm. Oh! It stops! The air goes out of your nose.”

“Say /b/ /b/ /b/ like a ball bouncing. Lips! Now /p/ /p/ /p/ for pen. Hold your hand here. Feel the puff of air!”

“Bite your bottom lip softly: /fff/ like a fish blowing bubbles. Teeth and lip!”

“Now the tongue. /l/ /l/ /l/ for lion: your tongue goes up behind your teeth. /t/ /t/ for tap, tap!”

“Hand on your throat. Say zzz like a bee. Buzz! Now sss like a snake. No buzz!”

  • خذ المرآة وانظر إلى شفتيك. قل mmm مثل الأكل اللذيذ. الشفتان تُغلقان!
  • أمسك أنفك وقل mmm... توقف الصوت! لأن الهواء يخرج من الأنف.
  • ضع يدك أمام فمك وقل /p/: هل تشعر بالنفخة؟ ومع /b/ لا توجد نفخة كبيرة.
  • عضّ شفتك السفلى بلطف وقل /fff/ مثل سمكة تنفخ فقاعات.
  • ارفع لسانك خلف أسنانك وقل /l/ مثل lion.
  • ضع يدك على حلقك: zzz يهتز مثل النحلة، و sss لا يهتز مثل الثعبان.
Students do
  • Watch their lips in the mirror for /m/, /b/, /p/.
  • Pinch the nose during mmm and feel it stop.
  • Hold a tissue or paper in front of the mouth: it moves for /p/, not much for /b/.
  • Hand on throat: feel buzz for zzz and mmm, no buzz for sss and /fff/.
  • Point to the right mouth helper on the slide (lips, teeth, tongue) for each sound.
Exercise · point-and-say — Say the sound. Look in the mirror. Point to the helper: lips, teeth or tongue.
انطق الصوت وانظر في المرآة، ثم أشر إلى المساعد: الشفتان أم الأسنان أم اللسان.
  1. /m/ (moon) → lips (and the nose hums)
  2. /b/ (ball) → lips
  3. /p/ (pen) → lips (with a puff of air)
  4. /f/ (fish) → teeth on the bottom lip
  5. /l/ (lion) → tongue
  6. /t/ (ten) → tongue
  7. /n/ (nose) → tongue (and the nose hums)
  8. /k/ (kite) → the back of the tongue
Tips:
  • Say sounds clean: /mmm/ not 'muh'. Stretch the long ones (/m/, /f/, /s/, /l/) and keep the short ones (/b/, /p/, /t/, /k/) tiny.
  • For /k/, children can feel it at the back: 'Say /k/ like a little cough at the back of the mouth.'
  • The tissue test needs a light tissue held very close; a camera on the tissue makes it fun for the whole group.

4. Movement Break: Lumi's Sound Dance

23–28 min
استراحة حركة: رقصة أصوات لومي
Method: TPR: one sound, one movement
Purpose: Release energy while repeating the sounds with the body.
Teacher says

“Stand up! Lumi wants to dance!”

“/b/ /b/ /b/: bounce like a ball! /fff/: swim like a fish!”

“/mmm/: rub your tummy! /zzz/: fly like a bee! /sss/: wiggle like a snake!”

“Freeze! ... Now sit down slowly: /sssss/.”

  • قفوا! لومي يريد أن يرقص!
  • /b/ نقفز مثل الكرة، /fff/ نسبح مثل السمكة، /mmm/ نفرك بطوننا، /zzz/ نطير مثل النحلة.
  • تجمّد! والآن اجلس ببطء مع sssss.
Students do
  • Do the movement and make the sound together.
  • Freeze when the teacher says 'Freeze!'
  • One child calls a sound; the others do its movement.
Tips:
  • Children should be able to move without leaving the camera view: show the space first.
  • End with a slow, quiet sound to calm the group before the next stage.

5. Arabic Bridge: Name and Sound in Two Languages

28–36 min
جسر العربية: الاسم والصوت في لغتين
Method: Contrastive analysis with known Arabic letters + two hand signs
Purpose: Show that the letter-name / letter-sound idea is not new: children already know it from Arabic.
Teacher says

“In Arabic this letter is ب. Its name is باء. But in باب we hear only /b/.”

“In English this is B. Its name is 'bee'. In ball we hear only /b/. The same!”

“Two hands. Open hand: the name. Fist: the sound. B... (open) 'bee'! (fist) /b/!”

“M: name 'em', sound /mmm/, moon. In Arabic: ميم, /mmm/, موز. Friends!”

“P is new. Arabic has no P. Lips, no buzz, and a puff: /p/, pen.”

  • هذا حرف الباء. اسمه 'باء'، لكن في كلمة باب نسمع صوته فقط: /b/.
  • وفي الإنجليزية: B اسمه 'بي'، وفي ball نسمع صوته فقط: /b/. الفكرة نفسها!
  • اليد المفتوحة تعني اسم الحرف، والقبضة تعني صوت الحرف.
  • حرف P جديد علينا، لا يوجد في العربية. نصنعه بالشفتين مع نفخة هواء: pen.
Students do
  • Show open hand and say the name, then fist and say the sound, for B, M, N, L, F, D, K, P.
  • Match each English letter to its Arabic friend on the slide (B-ب, M-م, N-ن, L-ل, F-ف, D-د, K-ك).
  • Find the letter with no Arabic friend: P.
Exercise · match — Say the English letter's sound. Point to the Arabic letter with the same sound. If there is none, cross your arms.
انطق صوت الحرف الإنجليزي، وأشر إلى الحرف العربي الذي له الصوت نفسه. إذا لم يوجد، اعقد ذراعيك.
  1. B (ball) → ب
  2. M (moon) → م
  3. N (nose) → ن
  4. L (lion) → ل
  5. F (fish) → ف
  6. D (dog) → د
  7. K (kite) → ك
  8. P (pen) → no Arabic letter (cross arms)
Tips:
  • Say 'close to' or 'friends', not 'exactly the same': English /t/ and /d/, for example, are made a little further back than Arabic ت and د.
  • Praise children who notice that Arabic letter names also begin with their sound (باء, ميم, نون): they are thinking like linguists.

6. Game: Name or Sound? Lumi Gets Mixed Up

36–46 min
لعبة: الاسم أم الصوت؟ لومي يخلط بينهما
Method: Puppet error-correction game (children correct the character) + retrieval of A-R pictures
Purpose: Practise switching between letter names and letter sounds, and connect each sound to a known picture word.
Teacher says

“Lumi is silly today! Lumi says: 'Bee-all!' Is that right? No! It's ball: /b/ /b/ ball!”

“Show me: is Lumi saying the name or the sound? Open hand or fist?”

“Look at the picture. What is it? A kite! What is the first sound? /k/!”

“Tricky letter! H is called 'aitch', but in hat it says /h/. Like a little breath on a cold window: /h/ /h/.”

  • لومي يخلط اليوم! يقول 'بي-آل'، هل هذا صحيح؟ لا! نقول ball بالصوت /b/.
  • هل قال لومي اسم الحرف أم صوته؟ اليد المفتوحة أم القبضة؟
  • انظر إلى الصورة: ما هي؟ وما أول صوت فيها؟
  • حرف مخادع: H اسمه 'إيتش' لكن صوته /h/ مثل نفَس دافئ على زجاج بارد.
Students do
  • Show open hand (name) or fist (sound) for what Lumi says.
  • Correct Lumi by saying the word the right way.
  • Look at a picture from Lessons 3-4 and say its first sound.
  • Take a turn being Lumi and say a letter name or sound for the group.
Exercise · say-the-sound — Look at the picture. Say the word, then say its first sound only.
انظر إلى الصورة. قل الكلمة، ثم قل أول صوت فيها فقط.
  1. (picture) ball → /b/
  2. (picture) moon → /m/
  3. (picture) fish → /f/
  4. (picture) dog → /d/
  5. (picture) lion → /l/
  6. (picture) nose → /n/
  7. (picture) hat → /h/ (the letter's name is 'aitch')
  8. (picture) pen → /p/ (with a puff, not /b/)
  9. (picture) rabbit → /r/
  10. (picture) kite → /k/
Tips:
  • Children love correcting a puppet; let Lumi be wrong often and be very surprised.
  • Avoid cat, goat and queen as first-sound items today: C ('see'), G ('jee') and Q ('kyoo') have names that do not match their sound in these words. Keep them for the detectives class.
  • If a child gives the name instead of the sound, just show the fist sign and model the sound.

7. Production: My Name Sound

46–54 min
إنتاج: صوت اسمي
Method: Personalised speaking + drawing (multisensory)
Purpose: Use the new idea for the thing every child cares about most: their own name.
Teacher says

“My name is Teacher Mona. My first sound is /m/! Mmm-ona.”

“Lumi: /l/ /l/ Lumi! Sara: /sss/ Sara! Ziad: /zzz/ Ziad! Noor: /nnn/ Noor!”

“What is your name? What is your first sound? Show me your mouth helper!”

“Draw a big picture of you. Write or copy your first letter next to it.”

  • اسمي يبدأ بصوت /m/!
  • لومي يبدأ بـ/l/، وسارة بـ/s/، وزياد بـ/z/، ونور بـ/n/.
  • ما اسمك؟ وما أول صوت فيه؟ أرنا هل تستخدم الشفتين أم اللسان أم الأسنان.
  • ارسم نفسك واكتب أول حرف من اسمك بجانب الرسمة.
Students do
  • Say: 'My name is ...' and then the first sound of their name.
  • Show the mouth helper for that sound in the mirror.
  • Say the first sound of a friend's name.
  • Draw themselves and copy the first letter of their name (teacher shows it on the slide).
Exercise · say-the-sound — Say the name. Say the first sound. Is it lips, teeth or tongue?
قل الاسم، ثم قل أول صوت فيه. هل نصنعه بالشفتين أم الأسنان أم اللسان؟
  1. Lumi → /l/ - tongue
  2. Sara → /s/ - teeth (tongue close behind them), no buzz
  3. Ziad → /z/ - teeth, with buzz
  4. Noor → /n/ - tongue (nose hums)
  5. Mona → /m/ - lips (nose hums)
  6. Badr → /b/ - lips
  7. Hamad → /h/ - a soft breath (English has no ح)
  8. Fatima → /f/ - teeth on the bottom lip
Tips:
  • Use each child's real name. For names that start with ع, ح, خ, غ or ق, celebrate it: 'English does not have this sound - your name has a special Arabic sound!' and give the letter used in the English spelling.
  • Use the teacher's own first name in the model; 'Mona' is only an example.
  • Names that start with a vowel (Omar, Ahmed, Eman) are fine: say the first sound together without testing it.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

54–60 min
بطاقة الخروج، هل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check with gestures + curiosity close
Purpose: Check quickly who can give a letter sound and who can tell name from sound; send home one fact.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket! I show a picture. You say the first sound and show your mouth helper.”

“Did you know? Babies can hear the sounds of every language in the world. Your ears are still super ears!”

“Mission at home: hum mmm and pinch your nose. Show your family the magic!”

“Goodbye, Sound Explorers! Goodbye, Lumi!”

  • بطاقة الخروج: أُريكم صورة، وأنتم تقولون أول صوت فيها.
  • هل تعلم؟ الرضّع يستطيعون سماع أصوات كل لغات العالم، وآذانكم ما زالت آذاناً خارقة!
  • مهمتك في البيت: قل mmm وأمسك أنفك، وأرِ عائلتك السحر!
  • مع السلامة يا مستكشفي الأصوات!
Students do
  • Answer 5 quick picture items one by one (each child gets at least one alone).
  • Wave goodbye and say 'Goodbye, Lumi!'
Tips:
  • Ask each child at least one item alone so you can really hear them.
  • Note who still gives letter names instead of sounds: replay the open-hand / fist game as a 2-minute warm-up in Lessons 5-7.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Look at the picture. Say the first sound. Then show: is it a lips sound, a tongue sound or a teeth sound?
انظر إلى الصورة وقل أول صوت فيها، ثم أرِنا: هل هو صوت شفتين أم لسان أم أسنان؟
  1. (picture) moon → /m/ - lips
  2. (picture) lion → /l/ - tongue
  3. (picture) fish → /f/ - teeth on lip
  4. (picture) ball → /b/ - lips
  5. B: name or sound? 'bee' → name (open hand)

Homework

Sound Explorer at home: show your family the nose trick (pinch your nose and hum mmm). Find 3 things at home and say their first sound.

مستكشف الأصوات في البيت: أرِ عائلتك خدعة الأنف (أمسك أنفك وقل mmm). ابحث عن 3 أشياء في البيت وقل أول صوت في اسمها بالإنجليزية.

  • Pinch your nose and hum mmm for your family. Then say moon.
  • Find a ball, a pen and a book (or any 3 things) and say the first sound of each.
  • Draw your face with big lips, teeth and tongue. Colour the lips red.

Support

  • Use only four sounds (/m/, /b/, /s/, /l/) with their pictures and gestures.
  • Let the child answer with the gesture or by pointing to the mouth picture instead of saying the sound.
  • Give the Arabic letter first (م), then the English sound.

Challenge

  • Find the tricky letters whose names do not match their sounds in A-R (C, G, H, Q) and explain with a picture.
  • Sort the mouth sounds into buzz and no buzz with a hand on the throat (/b/ /p/, /d/ /t/, /z/ /s/).
  • Say the first sound of each person's name in their family.

Success criteria

  • Shows the right gesture for at least 6 of 8 noise-or-word items.
  • Gives the first sound (not the letter name) for at least 7 of 10 pictures.
  • Points to the right mouth helper (lips, teeth, tongue) for /m/, /f/ and /l/.
  • Says the first sound of their own name.

Watch for

  • Letter names used as sounds ('bee' for /b/)
  • /p/ said as /b/ (pen -> ben)
  • A big vowel added to every sound ('muh', 'luh')
  • E/I and G/J letter names swapped

Teacher notes

  • This class is about noticing sounds, not about reading. No child needs to read anything today.
  • Keep your English sentences very short and use Arabic to explain the 'why' quickly, then go straight back to English play.
  • The open-hand (name) / fist (sound) signs are the group's shared code for the whole level: reuse them in every alphabet warm-up.
  • Arabic speakers already understand letter names vs sounds from Arabic (باء / /b/). Say so: it builds confidence.
  • Bonus Class 2 builds on this: First Sound Detectives, with S-Z, numbers and colours.
