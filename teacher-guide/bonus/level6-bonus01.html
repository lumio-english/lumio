Does English Have a Future Tense?

هل في الإنجليزية زمن مستقبل؟
Big idea: English verbs have no future ending; English talks about the future with tools (will, be going to, the present continuous, the present simple), and we choose the tool by meaning: a prediction, a plan, an arrangement or a timetable.
الفعل الإنجليزي ليس له نهاية خاصة بالمستقبل؛ الإنجليزية تتحدث عن المستقبل بأدوات (will و be going to والمضارع المستمر والمضارع البسيط)، ونختار الأداة حسب المعنى: توقّع أو خطة أو موعد مرتّب أو جدول مواعيد.

Linguistics Corner — The missing future ending · نهاية المستقبل المفقودة

English verbs change their own form for only two tenses: present (walk, walks) and past (walked). There is no future ending: no English verb looks like 'walk-future'.الفعل الإنجليزي يغيّر شكله لزمنين فقط: المضارع (walk, walks) والماضي (walked). لا توجد نهاية للمستقبل: لا يوجد فعل إنجليزي بصيغة خاصة بالمستقبل.
Many languages do have one. Spanish 'hablaré' and French 'parlerai' both mean 'I will speak', and the ending does the job. Fun fact: that French ending comes from the Latin verb 'have' (parler + ai = 'I have to speak').لغات كثيرة لها هذه النهاية. hablaré في الإسبانية و parlerai في الفرنسية تعنيان 'سأتكلم'، والنهاية تؤدي المهمة. معلومة طريفة: النهاية الفرنسية أصلها فعل 'يملك' في اللاتينية (أي: عليّ أن أتكلم).
So English uses tools instead: the modal will, the phrase be going to, the present continuous (I'm meeting Sara at 5) and the present simple for timetables (The bus leaves at 7). That is why many linguists say 'English has no future tense, but many ways to talk about the future'. Others call will + verb 'the future tense'. Both sides have a point: it depends on whether 'tense' means a verb ending or a meaning.لذلك تستخدم الإنجليزية أدوات بدلاً منها: الفعل الشكلي will، والعبارة be going to، والمضارع المستمر (I'm meeting Sara at 5)، والمضارع البسيط للجداول (The bus leaves at 7). ولهذا يقول كثير من اللغويين: 'لا يوجد زمن مستقبل في الإنجليزية، بل طرق كثيرة للحديث عن المستقبل'. وآخرون يسمّون will + الفعل 'زمن المستقبل'. لكل رأي وجهة: الأمر يتوقف على معنى كلمة 'زمن': هل هو نهاية في الفعل أم معنى؟
Future words are born from normal words. In Old English 'willan' meant 'to want' (we still say 'free will' and 'willing'). 'Going to' first meant real walking: 'I am going to London to see the king'. Slowly it became a grammar word, and in fast speech it became 'gonna'. Linguists call this grammaticalization.كلمات المستقبل تولد من كلمات عادية. في الإنجليزية القديمة كان willan يعني 'يريد' (ما زلنا نقول free will أي الإرادة الحرة). و going to كانت تعني الذهاب الحقيقي: 'أنا ذاهب إلى لندن لأرى الملك'، ثم أصبحت كلمة نحوية، وفي الكلام السريع صارت gonna. يسمّي اللغويون هذا 'التحوّل النحوي'.
Arabic did the same thing. Some Arab grammarians say the prefix سـ is a short form of سوف. In Levantine Arabic, رح (رح روح) comes from رايح, 'going', and many linguists trace Egyptian هـ (هروح) to the same word: exactly the English 'going to' story! Many Gulf dialects use بـ (بروح، بسير).العربية فعلت الشيء نفسه. بعض النحاة العرب يرون أن السين اختصار لـ سوف. وفي اللهجة الشامية 'رح' (رح روح) أصلها 'رايح' أي ذاهب، ويرجع كثير من اللغويين الهاء المصرية (هروح) إلى الكلمة نفسها: إنها قصة going to الإنجليزية بالضبط! وكثير من اللهجات الخليجية تستخدم الباء (بروح، بسير).

Key terms

TermبالعربيةMeaning
tenseزمن (صيغة الفعل)A change in the form of the verb itself to show time: walk / walked.
inflectionتصريف (تغيير في شكل الكلمة)An ending or change inside a word that adds grammar: -s, -ed, -ing.
modal verbفعل مساعد شكلي (فعل وجهي)A helper verb such as will, can, must, should. It never takes -s and is followed by the base verb.

intentionنيّةSomething you have already decided to do.

الإنجليزية المحكية تختصر المستقبل (I'll, gonna) تماماً كما تختصر اللهجات العربية سوف ورايح. gonna مقبولة في الكلام والدردشة لا في الكتابة الرسمية.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
I will to go to the science fair.I will go to the science fair.Mixing will with 'going to'. will is a modal: it is followed directly by the base verb, like سوف أذهب.
I going to visit my cousins.I'm going to visit my cousins.Arabic has no 'be' verb in the present (أنا ذاهب), so am/is/are disappears. going to always needs am/is/are.
Tomorrow I go to my uncle.Tomorrow I'm going to my uncle's. / I'm visiting my uncle tomorrow.Arabic أذهب غداً uses the plain present. In English the present simple is only for timetables; for personal plans use the present continuous or going to.
He will goes / He wills go.He will go.Modals never take -s and the verb after them stays in the base form.
Look at those black clouds! It will rain.Look at those black clouds! It's going to rain.When we can see the evidence now, English prefers going to. will is for opinions and beliefs (I think it will rain next week).
(The phone rings.) I'm going to answer it!I'll get it! / I'll answer it!A decision made at the moment of speaking uses will ('ll). going to is for decisions made before.
I think it won't rain.I don't think it will rain.Not wrong grammatically, but English usually puts the negative on think. Arabic أعتقد أنها لن تمطر pushes learners to the first form.
I'm gonna the mall.I'm going to the mall. / I'm gonna go to the mall.gonna only replaces 'going to' + verb. When 'to' is a preposition before a place, we never say gonna.

1. Warm-up: Lumi the Fortune Teller

0–5 min
إحماء: لومي قارئة المستقبل
Method: Curiosity hook with personalized predictions
Purpose: Get students talking about the future at once and plant the question: which word in these sentences carries the future?
Teacher says

“Welcome to Lumi's Crystal Ball! I can see your future... Omar, you will become a famous engineer. Sara, you're going to apply for a scholarship. Noor, you're meeting a new friend on Friday!”

“Now you are the fortune teller. Make one prediction about the person on your right. Start with: 'I see... you will...'”

“Quick question: in all these sentences, which word tells us it is the future? Is it the verb itself? Keep that question in your head.”

  • مرحباً بكم عند كرة لومي السحرية! أرى مستقبلكم...
  • الآن أنت قارئ المستقبل: قل توقعاً واحداً عن زميلك.
  • سؤال: ما الكلمة التي تخبرنا أن الجملة عن المستقبل؟ هل هو الفعل نفسه؟
Students do
  • Listen to the teacher's predictions and react (Really? No way!).
  • Each student makes one prediction for a classmate: 'I see... you will...'.
  • Write in the chat the one word that shows the future in their sentence.
Tips:
  • Keep predictions positive and funny; teens enjoy exaggeration (You will have one million followers).
  • Collect the words from the chat (will, going to, 'm meeting) and leave them on screen for the next stage.

2. Linguistics Corner: The Tense Detective

5–15 min
ركن اللغويات: محقق الأزمنة
Method: Guided discovery + cross-language comparison + mini vote (debate seed)
Purpose: Discover that English verbs only inflect for present and past, compare with Spanish, French and Arabic, and meet the 'no future tense' debate.
Teacher says

“Look at the verb walk. Walk, walks, walked, walking. The verb itself changes for present and for past. Where is the future form?”

“Exactly: there is no 'walk-future'. To talk about the future we add a helper: will walk, am going to walk, am walking tomorrow.”

“Now look at Spanish: hablo = I speak, hablaré = I will speak. The ending -é carries the future. In Arabic: أمشي, سأمشي. Arabic adds the prefix سـ to the verb.”

“So here is the big debate. Some linguists say: English has no future tense, only two tenses, present and past. Others say: will + verb is the future tense. What do you think? Vote now: thumbs up for 'no future tense', thumbs down for 'yes, it has one'.”

“One more secret: will once meant 'want' in Old English, and 'going to' once meant real walking. Words change their jobs over time. Linguists call it grammaticalization.”

  • الفعل الإنجليزي يتغير شكله للمضارع وللماضي فقط. أين صيغة المستقبل؟ لا توجد!
  • في الإسبانية النهاية تحمل المستقبل، وفي العربية نضيف السين إلى الفعل.
  • صوّتوا: هل في الإنجليزية زمن مستقبل أم لا؟ لكل رأي حجته.
Students do
  • Complete the verb-form table on the slide (walk, walks, walked, walking, was walking, will walk).
  • Circle the part that shows time in each language example.
  • Vote and give one reason in one sentence: 'I think English has / doesn't have a future tense because...'.
Exercise · sort — What shows the time: a change in the verb itself (ending/prefix) or a separate helper word? Sort each example.
ما الذي يدل على الزمن: تغيير في الفعل نفسه (نهاية أو سابقة) أم كلمة مساعدة منفصلة؟ صنّف كل مثال.
  1. walked → change in the verb (-ed = past)
  2. walks → change in the verb (-s = present)
  3. will walk → separate helper word (will)
  4. is going to walk → separate helper words (is going to)
  5. is walking tomorrow → helper (is) + time word (tomorrow)
  6. hablaré (Spanish) → change in the verb (ending -é = future)
  7. سأمشي → change in the verb (prefix سـ = future)
  8. سوف أمشي → separate helper word (سوف)
Tips:
  • Present the debate honestly: both views are respectable. The useful conclusion is that English future = tools chosen by meaning.
  • Note the nice twist: Arabic has both systems - a prefix (سـ) and a separate word (سوف).

3. Arabic Bridge: One Arabic Future, Many English Futures

15–23 min
جسر العربية: مستقبل عربي واحد ومستقبلات إنجليزية كثيرة
Method: Contrastive analysis + meaning-based translation
Purpose: Show that سأفعل can become will, going to or the present continuous in English, so students translate meaning, not words.
Teacher says

“In Arabic, سأساعدك, سأسافر, سأقابل all use the same سـ. English asks a question first: is it a prediction, a promise, a plan or an arrangement?”

“Remember: سوف + verb, will + verb. No 'to' in the middle. But with going to, 'to' must stay.”

“And the negative: لن أتأخر = I won't be late. Both languages have a special word for 'not in the future'.”

“Your Arabic dialect is a secret weapon: رح comes from رايح, 'going'. English going to also came from 'going'. Same idea, two languages!”

  • العربية تستخدم السين لكل المعاني، أما الإنجليزية فتسأل أولاً: هل هو توقع أم وعد أم خطة أم موعد؟
  • سوف + فعل، will + فعل، بلا to. أما going to فلا بد من to.
  • لن أتأخر = I won't be late.
Students do
  • Read each Arabic sentence and its situation, then decide the meaning (prediction, promise, decision now, plan, evidence, arrangement, timetable).
  • Translate into English using the best tool; hold up the W, G or C card first.
  • Compare answers in pairs and explain one choice.
Exercise · translate-by-meaning — Read the Arabic sentence and the situation. Write the best English sentence.
اقرأ الجملة العربية والموقف، ثم اكتب أفضل جملة بالإنجليزية.
  1. سأساعدك! (Your friend drops all her books right now.) → I'll help you! (will: decision at the moment / offer)
  2. سأتقدم للمنحة السنة القادمة. (You decided last month.) → I'm going to apply for the scholarship next year. (going to: intention)
  3. سأقابل المدرب يوم الأحد الساعة الرابعة. (It is in your calendar.) → I'm meeting the coach on Sunday at 4. (present continuous: arrangement)
  4. ستمطر! انظر إلى الغيوم السوداء. → It's going to rain! Look at the black clouds. (going to: evidence now)
  5. أعتقد أن فريقنا سيفوز. → I think our team will win. (will: opinion / prediction)
  6. لن أتأخر، أعدك. → I won't be late, I promise. (will: promise)
  7. تغادر الطائرة الساعة السابعة صباحاً. (Airport timetable.) → The plane leaves at 7 a.m. (present simple: timetable)
  8. سننتقل إلى مدينة جديدة. (Your parents already decided.) → We're going to move to a new city. (going to: plan)
Tips:
  • Accept 'We're moving to a new city' for item 8 if the move is already arranged: both are correct. Discuss the small difference.
  • Students often want one 'correct' answer; teach them that English often allows two forms with a slightly different message.

4. The Future Toolbox: Meaning Map with the Crew

23–33 min
صندوق أدوات المستقبل: خريطة المعاني مع الفريق
Method: Guided discovery from mini-dialogues + rule-writing by students
Purpose: Students write the rules for will, going to and the present continuous themselves from crew examples, then apply them.
Teacher says

“Here are six mini-scenes from the crew. Read them and find the tool each person uses.”

“Scene 1: Omar's phone rings. 'I'll get it!' Scene 2: Sara: 'I'm going to apply for an internship this summer.' Scene 3: Noor: 'I'm meeting the coach at 4 tomorrow. It's in my calendar.' Scene 4: Ziad looks at the sky: 'It's going to rain.' Scene 5: Hamad: 'Our team will definitely win the final!' Scene 6: Lumi: 'The science fair starts at 9.'”

“Now you are the grammar writers. Complete the rules: We use will for... We use going to for... We use the present continuous for... We use the present simple for...”

“Great rules! Now test them.”

  • اقرؤوا المشاهد الستة واكتشفوا الأداة التي يستخدمها كل شخص.
  • الآن أنتم من يكتب القاعدة: نستخدم will لـ... ونستخدم going to لـ...
Students do
  • Match each scene to a tool and a meaning.
  • In pairs, write the four rules in their own words on the shared slide.
  • Compare with the teacher's Future Toolbox slide and correct their rules.
  • Do the choose-the-tool exercise and justify each answer with a meaning word.
Exercise · choose-and-justify — Choose the best form and say why (prediction, decision now, promise, plan, evidence, arrangement, timetable).
اختر الصيغة الأفضل وقل السبب.
  1. A: I'm thirsty. B: Wait, I ___ (get) you some water. → 'll get (decision now / offer)
  2. Sara has bought her ticket. She ___ (travel) to Cairo on Thursday. → is traveling (arrangement)
  3. I believe robots ___ (do) most jobs in 2050. → will do (prediction / belief)
  4. Ziad has decided: he ___ (join) the robotics team next term. → is going to join (intention / plan)
  5. Look! Hamad is running too fast with that tray. He ___ (drop) it! → is going to drop (evidence now)
  6. The train to Dubai ___ (leave) at 6:45 tomorrow morning. → leaves (timetable)
  7. Don't worry, Mum. I ___ (not / forget) your birthday this year. → won't forget (promise)
  8. We ___ (host) a party on Saturday. The guest list is ready. → are hosting (arrangement)
  9. The forecast says it ___ (be) sunny all weekend. → will be (prediction from a source)
  10. Noor ___ (meet) her cousins at the airport at 8. Her dad booked it. → is meeting (arrangement)
Tips:
  • The going to / present continuous line is fuzzy for native speakers too: if a plan has a time, a place and other people, the present continuous sounds most natural.
  • Ask 'Why?' after every answer; the reason is the real learning.
  • Keep the four-icon toolbox slide visible for the rest of the class.

5. Sound of the Future: 'll, won't, gonna + Error Clinic

33–41 min
صوت المستقبل: 'll و won't و gonna + عيادة الأخطاء
Method: Listening discrimination + pronunciation drill + error correction
Purpose: Hear and say the weak forms of the future, then fix the typical errors of Arabic speakers.
Teacher says

“In real English, will is almost always short: I'll, you'll, she'll, we'll. Listen: I'll call you. I'll call you. Can you hear the 'll?”

“Be careful: won't and want sound close. won't rhymes with 'don't'. 'I won't go' = لن أذهب. 'I want to go' = أريد أن أذهب. Big difference!”

“Gonna is how people say 'going to' + verb in fast speech: I'm gonna study. But never 'I'm gonna the mall'.”

“Now the Error Clinic: you are the doctors. Each sentence is sick. Find the problem and cure it.”

  • will تُختصر غالباً في الكلام: I'll, she'll.
  • انتبهوا: won't تعني 'لن'، و want تعني 'يريد'.
  • gonna تحل محل going to + فعل فقط.
Students do
  • Listen to 6 sentences and write if they hear 'll or nothing (I'll call / I call).
  • Listen and decide: won't or want?
  • Say the sentences with contractions in a chain around the group.
  • Correct the 8 sick sentences and explain the cure.
Exercise · error-correction — Each sentence has one mistake. Find it and correct it.
في كل جملة خطأ واحد. اكتشفه وصححه.
  1. I will to visit my grandmother on Friday. → I will visit my grandmother on Friday.
  2. She going to start a new hobby. → She's going to start a new hobby.
  3. Tomorrow I go to the dentist at 10. (personal appointment) → Tomorrow I'm going to the dentist at 10.
  4. He will plays in the final. → He will play in the final.
  5. Look at that car! It will crash! → Look at that car! It's going to crash!
  6. I'm gonna the science fair. → I'm going to the science fair. / I'm gonna go to the science fair.
  7. Are you come to my party on Saturday? → Are you coming to my party on Saturday?
  8. I think it won't be difficult. → I don't think it will be difficult. (more natural)
Tips:
  • Item 8 is about naturalness, not a hard error: tell students both are understood.
  • If a student pronounces won't like want, try lip-rounding: won't has an 'o' shape, like 'own' with a w.

6. Crystal Ball Studio and the Crew Calendar

41–53 min
استوديو الكرة السحرية وتقويم الفريق
Method: Gamified team challenge (certainty scale) + information-gap role-play
Purpose: Use will with certainty adverbs for predictions and the present continuous for arrangements in free, personal speech.
Teacher says

“Round 1: Crystal Ball Studio. Two teams. Make predictions about the world in 2040: phones, schools, cars, football, food. Use the certainty scale: will definitely, will probably, probably won't, definitely won't, it's unlikely that...”

“Careful with word order: 'will probably' but 'probably won't'. The adverb goes after will but before won't.”

“The other team answers: I agree / I don't think so. Best prediction wins a point.”

“Round 2: Crew Calendar. You are Noor, Ziad, Sara or Hamad. Your calendar is on your slide. Ask each other: What are you doing on Friday evening? Are you free on Sunday? Find a time when all of you can play a board game.”

  • الجولة الأولى: توقّعوا شكل العالم سنة 2040 باستخدام درجات اليقين.
  • انتبهوا للترتيب: will probably لكن probably won't.
  • الجولة الثانية: أنتم شخصيات الفريق، اسألوا عن مواعيد بعضكم واتفقوا على وقت مشترك.
Students do
  • Team task: write 5 predictions for 2040 on the shared slide using different certainty levels.
  • Read predictions aloud; the other team reacts and votes.
  • Put the adverb in the right place in the sentence-builder exercise.
  • Role-play: ask and answer about the week with the present continuous until they find a free slot, then agree with will: 'OK, I'll bring the game!'
Exercise · sentence-builder — Put the words in the right order to make a prediction.
رتّب الكلمات لتكوّن جملة توقّع صحيحة.
  1. will / cars / fly / probably / in 2040 → Cars will probably fly in 2040.
  2. won't / definitely / paper books / disappear → Paper books definitely won't disappear.
  3. unlikely / it's / that / will / rain / it / tomorrow → It's unlikely that it will rain tomorrow.
  4. certain / I'm / win / we / will → I'm certain we will win.
  5. probably / schools / won't / have / homework → Schools probably won't have homework.
  6. believe / I / will / a cure / scientists / find → I believe scientists will find a cure.
Tips:
  • Give each student a slightly different calendar so they really need to ask (information gap).
  • Reward reasons: 'Cars will probably fly because...' earns a bonus point.
  • Correct only the target forms during the game; note other errors for the end.

7. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

53–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + debate recap + curiosity close
Purpose: Check that every student can choose the right future form and explain the big idea in one sentence.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket! Five sentences. Write only the missing words. You have three minutes.”

“Last word on our debate: Does English have a future tense? Say your answer in one sentence and one reason.”

“Did you know? Some languages, such as Chinese (Mandarin), have no tense endings at all, not even for the past. They use time words like 'yesterday' and 'tomorrow'. And people still talk about the future perfectly well!”

“Your mission: tonight, tell someone at home about one plan, one arrangement and one prediction in English.”

  • هل تعلم؟ بعض اللغات مثل الصينية ليس فيها نهايات للأزمنة أصلاً، حتى للماضي، وتستخدم كلمات مثل 'أمس' و'غداً'.
  • مهمتك: أخبر أحداً في البيت عن خطة وموعد وتوقّع بالإنجليزية.
Students do
  • Complete the 5 exit-ticket items.
  • Say their final debate answer with a reason.
  • Say goodbye with a promise: 'I'll do my homework!'
Tips:
  • Students under 4/5 need a toolbox reminder in the warm-ups of Lessons 5-8 (Lesson 8 reviews going to).
  • Accept any well-argued answer to the debate; the reason matters more than the side.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Complete each sentence with the best future form.
أكمل كل جملة بأفضل صيغة للمستقبل.
  1. (The doorbell rings.) Don't move, I ___ (open) it. → 'll open
  2. I ___ (meet) Omar at the mall at 5. We booked a table. → 'm meeting
  3. She has decided. She ___ (join) the football team. → is going to join
  4. I think it ___ (be) a great year. → will be
  5. Correct the mistake: I will to call you tonight. → I will call you tonight.

Homework

Future Diary: write a short diary page about next week (8-10 sentences) with at least two arrangements (present continuous), two plans (going to), two predictions (will + probably/definitely) and one promise (won't).

مذكرات المستقبل: اكتب صفحة قصيرة عن الأسبوع القادم (8-10 جمل) فيها موعدان مرتبان (المضارع المستمر)، وخطتان (going to)، وتوقعان (will + probably/definitely)، ووعد واحد (won't).

  • Write the Future Diary and underline each future tool in a different color.
  • Find one future sentence in a song, a video or a game in English and decide which tool it uses.
  • Ask a family member how to say 'I will go' in their dialect (سأذهب، هروح، بروح، رح روح...) and write it down.

Support

  • Work with two tools first (will for predictions/decisions now, going to for plans), then add the present continuous.
  • Give a sentence frame for every task: 'I'm going to ___ because I decided...'.
  • Let students hold up the W/G/C card before writing the full sentence.

Challenge

  • Add the present simple for timetables and explain why 'The match starts at 8' is fine but 'I go to the dentist tomorrow' sounds odd.
  • Prepare a 1-minute argument for or against 'English has a future tense' with two reasons.
  • Compare 'I'll see you tomorrow' and 'I'm seeing you tomorrow': what is the difference in feeling?

Success criteria

  • Explains in English or Arabic that English verbs only change form for present and past, and names at least three future tools.
  • Chooses the correct form in at least 8 of 10 choose-and-justify items and gives a meaning reason.
  • Produces at least two correct predictions with will + certainty adverb in the right position.
  • Uses the present continuous for at least two arrangements in the calendar role-play.

Watch for

  • 'will to' + verb
  • missing be in going to (I going to)
  • present simple for personal plans (Tomorrow I go to...)
  • won't pronounced like want
  • 'probably won't' vs 'won't probably' word order

Teacher notes

  • The 'no future tense' claim is a real scholarly position (based on inflection), but some grammars call will + verb a future tense. Present it as an open debate; the practical message is to choose tools by meaning.
  • Lesson 4 was about the past continuous; use it briefly in the verb-form table to show that English changes the verb form only for present and past, and builds everything else with helpers.
  • Do not punish going to vs present continuous mix-ups for arrangements: both are often acceptable. Focus on the clear errors (will to, missing be, present simple for plans).
  • Bonus Class 2 continues with modal verbs (must, have to): students already know that modals take no -s and no 'to'.
