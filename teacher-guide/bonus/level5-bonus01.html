The Three Sounds of -ed and the Old Verbs

أصوات -ed الثلاثة والأفعال القديمة
Big idea: The past ending -ed is one spelling with three sounds, /t/, /d/ and /ɪd/, and the last sound of the verb chooses which one. Many irregular verbs are old 'fossils' that show the past by changing the vowel inside the word, a little like Arabic does.
نهاية الماضي -ed تُكتب بطريقة واحدة لكن لها ثلاثة أصوات: /t/ و/d/ و/ɪd/، والصوت الأخير في الفعل هو الذي يختار. أما كثير من الأفعال الشاذة فهي «حفريات» قديمة تُظهر الماضي بتغيير الحركة داخل الكلمة، بطريقة تشبه قليلاً ما تفعله العربية.

Linguistics Corner — One spelling, three sounds, and a museum of old verbs · كتابة واحدة وثلاثة أصوات، ومتحف للأفعال القديمة

-ed always looks the same, but it sounds /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/. Your mouth chooses the easiest one: after a voiceless sound (p, k, f, s, sh, ch, th) it stays voiceless /t/ (walked, watched, stopped); after a voiced sound (vowels, b, g, v, z, m, n, l, r) it buzzes /d/ (played, cleaned, lived). Linguists call this assimilation: a sound copies its neighbor.-ed تبدو دائماً بالشكل نفسه، لكنها تُنطق /t/ أو /d/ أو /ɪd/. الفم يختار الأسهل: بعد صوت مهموس (p, k, f, s, sh, ch, th) تبقى مهموسة /t/، وبعد صوت مجهور (الحركات و b, g, v, z, m, n, l, r) تصبح /d/. يسمي اللغويون هذا «المماثلة»: الصوت يقلّد جاره.
After t or d we need a full extra syllable /ɪd/ (want-ed, need-ed, visit-ed, rest-ed). Why? 'wantt' or 'needd' would sound almost exactly like want and need, and nobody would hear the past.بعد t أو d نحتاج مقطعاً إضافياً كاملاً /ɪd/ (want-ed و need-ed). لماذا؟ لأن wantt أو needd ستُسمع تقريباً مثل want و need ولن يسمع أحد الماضي.
It is the last SOUND, not the last letter: baked ends with the letter e but the sound /k/, so it is /beɪkt/. danced ends in /s/, so /dænst/. fixed ends in /ks/, so /fɪkst/.المهم هو آخر صوت وليس آخر حرف: baked تنتهي بحرف e لكن بصوت /k/، لذلك تُنطق /beɪkt/.
Why is the e written if we do not say it? About 600 to 700 years ago (in Chaucer's time), English speakers really did say 'walk-ed' with two syllables. Over the next 200 years or so the extra vowel slowly disappeared, but the spelling stayed. You can still hear the old sound in a few special words: beloved, a learned man, a blessed day.لماذا نكتب e إن كنا لا ننطقها؟ قبل نحو 600 إلى 700 سنة (في زمن الشاعر تشوسر) كان متحدثو الإنجليزية ينطقون walk-ed بمقطعين فعلاً. ثم اختفى هذا الصوت تدريجياً خلال نحو قرنين، وبقيت الكتابة. وما زال الصوت القديم يُسمع في كلمات قليلة مثل beloved.
Irregular verbs are fossils. Old English, like its cousins German and Dutch, had many 'strong verbs' (German and Dutch still have lots of them) that showed the past by changing the vowel inside: sing - sang - sung, drink - drank - drunk, ride - rode - ridden. Linguists call this ablaut.الأفعال الشاذة حفريات. كانت في الإنجليزية القديمة، كما في قريبتيها الألمانية والهولندية، «أفعال قوية» كثيرة (وما زالت كثيرة فيهما) تُظهر الماضي بتغيير الحركة في الداخل: sing - sang - sung. يسمي اللغويون هذا ablaut.
The -ed pattern slowly won. Some verbs changed teams: help was once a strong verb (its old past was something like 'healp'). Almost every NEW verb is regular: texted, googled, emailed. Nobody invents a vowel-change past like 'I taxt' for text!نمط -ed انتصر ببطء. بعض الأفعال غيّرت فريقها: help كان فعلاً قوياً قديماً (كان ماضيه قريباً من healp). وكل فعل جديد تقريباً يكون منتظماً: texted و googled و emailed.
Why did some fossils survive? Because they are used all the time (be, have, go, get, see, take). We hear them so often that our brains keep their old forms (that is what many linguists think). go - went is a borrowed past: went was the past of another old verb, wend (to go on your way).لماذا بقيت بعض الحفريات؟ لأنها تُستخدم طوال الوقت (be, have, go, get, see). نسمعها كثيراً فيحفظ الدماغ أشكالها القديمة. أما went فهي ماضٍ «مستعار» من فعل قديم آخر هو wend (يمضي في طريقه).
Small English-speaking children say 'I goed' and 'she eated' around age 3 or 4. It is not a silly mistake: it shows their brain has found the -ed rule. Then they learn the exceptions, just like you.الأطفال الصغار من أهل الإنجليزية يقولون I goed و she eated في نحو الثالثة أو الرابعة من العمر. ليس خطأ سخيفاً: إنه دليل على أن دماغهم اكتشف قاعدة -ed. ثم يتعلمون الاستثناءات مثلك تماماً.

Key terms

TermبالعربيةMeaning
voiced soundصوت مجهورA sound that makes your throat buzz, for example b, d, g, v, z, m, n, l, r, th in 'this', and all vowels.
voiceless soundصوت مهموسA sound made with air only, no buzz: p, t, k, f, s, sh, ch, th in 'think'.
syllableمقطعA beat in a word. walked = 1 syllable; wanted = 2 syllables (want-ed).
regular verbفعل منتظمA verb that makes the past with -ed: play - played.
irregular verbفعل شاذA verb with its own past form: sing - sang, buy - bought, go - went. Many are old 'strong' verbs that change the vowel inside.
ablautتبدّل الحركة الداخليةLinguists' name for changing the vowel inside a word to change its meaning or time: ride - rode.

1. Warm-up: Omar's Weekend Diary

0–5 min
إحماء: مذكرات عمر في نهاية الأسبوع
Method: Listening hook + noticing (input flood)
Purpose: Activate the past verbs from Lessons 1-4 and plant a puzzle: why does -ed sound different in different words?
Teacher says

“Welcome, Story Scientists! This term we study how stories work. Most stories are told in the past, so today we open the engine of the past tense.”

“Listen to Omar's diary. Every time you hear a past verb, raise your hand: 'On Friday I walked to the mall with Ziad. We watched a movie and I bought popcorn. Then we played football. It was amazing. In the evening I helped my dad. He cooked dinner and we visited my grandma. I wanted to sleep early, but I needed to finish my homework.'”

“How many past verbs did you catch? Now listen again to three of them: walked... played... wanted. Is -ed the same in all three? Keep that question in your head.”

  • أهلاً يا علماء الحكاية! معظم القصص تُحكى بالماضي، فاليوم نفتح محرّك زمن الماضي.
  • ارفع يدك كلما سمعت فعلاً ماضياً.
  • هل تبدو -ed متشابهة في الكلمات الثلاث؟ احتفظ بالسؤال في ذهنك.
Students do
  • Raise a hand (or use the Teams hand) at each past verb.
  • Write the number of past verbs they heard (10).
  • Say walked, played, wanted after the teacher and guess: same or different?
Tips:
  • Read naturally, not slowly; the noticing comes later.
  • Answer: 10 (walked, watched, bought, played, was, helped, cooked, visited, wanted, needed). 'finish' is a base verb after 'to': a good extra question for strong students.

2. Linguistics Corner: Buzz or No Buzz?

5–16 min
ركن اللغويات: طنين أم لا طنين؟
Method: Guided discovery with hand-on-throat (multisensory) + syllable clapping
Purpose: Let students discover the three -ed sounds and the rule behind them themselves.
Teacher says

“Put your hand on your throat. Say zzzz. Feel the buzz? That is a voiced sound. Now ssss. No buzz, just air: a voiceless sound.”

“Try these: b-p, d-t, v-f, g-k. One buzzes, one does not. Arabic has this too: ز buzzes, س does not.”

“Now clap the beats: walked. One clap. played. One clap. wanted. Two claps: want-ed!”

“Detective question: what is the LAST sound before -ed? walk ends in k: no buzz. So -ed is /t/: walkt. play ends in a vowel: buzz! So -ed is /d/: playd. want ends in t... 'wantt' sounds like 'want', so English adds a whole beat: want-ed.”

“So here is the rule. You tell me: no buzz gives...? /t/. Buzz gives...? /d/. t or d gives...? /ɪd/!”

“Careful! It is the last SOUND, not the last letter. baked: last letter e, last sound k. So it is /t/.”

  • ضع يدك على حنجرتك. zzz فيها طنين: صوت مجهور. sss بلا طنين: صوت مهموس، مثل ز وس في العربية.
  • بعد الصوت المهموس ← /t/، بعد المجهور ← /d/، بعد t أو d ← مقطع إضافي /ɪd/.
  • انتبه: المهم آخر صوت، لا آخر حرف.
Students do
  • Hand on throat: feel b-p, d-t, v-f, z-s.
  • Clap the syllables of walked, played, wanted, cleaned, visited, cooked.
  • Complete the rule on the slide in their own words (English or Arabic).
  • Hold up the T, D or ID card for each verb in the exercise.
Exercise · listen-and-show — Say the verb. Feel the last sound. Show the card: T, D or ID.
انطق الفعل وتحسّس آخر صوت فيه، ثم ارفع البطاقة: T أو D أو ID.
  1. walked → T (k = no buzz)
  2. played → D (vowel = buzz)
  3. visited → ID (ends in t)
  4. cleaned → D (n = buzz)
  5. helped → T (p = no buzz)
  6. rested → ID (ends in t)
  7. watched → T (ch = no buzz)
  8. baked → T (last sound k, not the letter e)
  9. cooked → T (k = no buzz)
Tips:
  • Say the -ed verbs naturally; do not over-pronounce the ending or you will create the 'walk-ed' problem yourself.
  • If a student says 'played sounds like /t/', ask them to whisper it and then say it: whispering kills the buzz.
  • Accept the rule in Arabic first; ask a strong student to say it in English.

3. Team Challenge: The Sound Sorting Race

16–24 min
تحدي الفرق: سباق فرز الأصوات
Method: Gamified sorting race (retrieval practice, two teams)
Purpose: Apply the rule quickly to new verbs from Lesson 3 and to spelling traps.
Teacher says

“Two teams: Team Taxi and Team Drum. I show a verb; the first team to say the -ed form correctly AND name its box wins a point.”

“You must SAY it, not just choose. If you say 'finish-ed', no point!”

“Bonus point if you can explain the trap: 'stopped' has two p's but one sound.”

  • فريقان. أُظهر فعلاً، وأول فريق ينطق صيغة -ed صحيحة ويذكر صندوقها يربح نقطة.
  • يجب أن تنطقها، لا أن تختار فقط!
Students do
  • Read the base verb, say the past form aloud and name the box (/t/, /d/, /ɪd/).
  • Type the past form in the chat to check the spelling (stop - stopped, reply - replied).
  • Teams take turns being the judge for one round.
Exercise · sort — Say the past form and put it in the right box: /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/.
انطق صيغة الماضي وضعها في الصندوق الصحيح: /t/ أو /d/ أو /ɪd/.
  1. finish → finished /t/
  2. call → called /d/
  3. attend → attended /ɪd/
  4. miss → missed /t/
  5. answer → answered /d/
  6. check → checked /t/
  7. deliver → delivered /d/
  8. need → needed /ɪd/
  9. reply → replied /d/ (y changes to i)
  10. stop → stopped /t/ (double p, one sound)
  11. live → lived /d/
  12. dance → danced /t/ (last sound /s/)
Tips:
  • The race keeps energy high; keep each item under 15 seconds.
  • Note who says 'finish-ed' or 'check-ed': they need the clap test again.
  • Spelling rules (double consonant, y to i) are a side note today; the target is the sound.

4. Arabic Bridge: Why We Say 'Walk-ed' and What لم Teaches Us

24–31 min
جسر العربية: لماذا نقول walk-ed وماذا تعلّمنا «لم»
Method: Contrastive analysis (Arabic as a tool)
Purpose: Understand the source of the extra-syllable error and use Arabic لم + المضارع to remember did + base verb.
Teacher says

“In Arabic, we read almost every letter we write. ل ع ب ت: لعبتُ. So our brain wants to read walk-e-d. But English spelling is a museum: the e was pronounced about 600 years ago and is silent now.”

“Look at Arabic: لعبتُ، لعبتَ، لعبتِ، لعبوا. Many endings! English: I played, you played, they played. One ending for everybody. Lucky you!”

“Now a secret. How do we say 'I didn't go' in Arabic? لم أذهب. Is أذهب past or present form? Present form! The past lives in لم. English is the same: the past lives in did, and the verb goes back to base: I didn't go. Did you see?”

“So 'I didn't went' is like saying لم ذهبتُ. It sounds strange, right?”

  • في العربية نقرأ كل حرف تقريباً، لذلك نميل إلى walk-ed. لكن e في -ed صامتة في الإنجليزية إلا بعد t و d.
  • قولنا I didn't went يشبه قولنا «لم ذهبتُ»: غريب، صحيح؟ الماضي يسكن في did، والفعل يعود لأصله.
Students do
  • Compare the Arabic and English person endings on the slide and count them (Arabic many, English one).
  • Translate quickly: لم أشاهد التلفاز / هل أنهيت واجبك؟ / لم يتصل بي.
  • Explain the لم = didn't trick to a partner in one sentence.
Tips:
  • Expected answers: I didn't watch TV. / Did you finish your homework? / He didn't call me.
  • Keep the claim modest: لم + المضارع is a memory trick, not proof that the grammars are the same.
  • Students love finding that English is easier than Arabic somewhere; use it for confidence.

5. The Fossil Museum: Families of Old Verbs

31–41 min
متحف الحفريات: عائلات الأفعال القديمة
Method: Pattern discovery + categorising (chunking for memory) + word history
Purpose: Make irregular verbs less random by grouping them into families and linking them to the Arabic idea of internal vowel change.
Teacher says

“Welcome to the Fossil Museum! These verbs are older than the -ed rule. Listen: sing, sang, sung. drink, drank, drunk. ride, rode, ridden. What changes? Only the vowel inside!”

“Does Arabic do this? كَتَبَ (he wrote), كُتِبَ (it was written). كِتاب، كُتُب. Same letters, new vowels, new meaning. English strong verbs work a bit like this, but only a small group of verbs does it.”

“Hall 1: vowel change: see-saw, eat-ate, take-took, get-got, find-found, meet-met. Hall 2: the -ought family, vowel change plus t: buy-bought, think-thought, bring-brought. Hall 3: totally different: go-went. Went was stolen from an old verb, wend!”

“Fun fact: help was once a fossil too, but it joined the -ed team. And every new verb is regular: texted, googled. Would anyone make a new past like 'I taxt my friend yesterday'? No! We say texted.”

“Your job: be the museum guide. Put each verb in its hall.”

  • هذه الأفعال أقدم من قاعدة -ed. لاحظ: تتغير الحركة في الداخل فقط.
  • العربية تفعل شيئاً مشابهاً: كَتَبَ / كُتِبَ، كِتاب / كُتُب. الفكرة متشابهة لكن الإنجليزية تفعلها مع مجموعة صغيرة من الأفعال فقط.
  • كل فعل جديد يكون منتظماً: texted و googled.
Students do
  • Say the verb families aloud as chants: sing-sang-sung, drink-drank-drunk.
  • Write the past form and the 'hall' for each verb.
  • Find one more verb for each hall from Lesson 2 (e.g. took, got, ate).
Exercise · categorise — Write the past form. Then choose the hall: A = vowel change, B = -ought/-aught family, C = totally different.
اكتب صيغة الماضي ثم اختر القاعة: A = تغيير الحركة، B = عائلة -ought، C = مختلف تماماً.
  1. sing → sang - A
  2. see → saw - A
  3. buy → bought - B
  4. go → went - C
  5. take → took - A
  6. find → found - A
  7. think → thought - B
  8. meet → met - A
  9. eat → ate - A
  10. get → got - A
Tips:
  • Mention the third forms (sung, drunk, ridden) only as 'you will need them later'; do not teach the present perfect.
  • Be careful with the Arabic comparison: similar idea, not the same system.
  • Hall B is a teaching label; strictly, the -ought verbs are old 'weak' verbs with a vowel change, which is why they end in t. meet - met is historically the same kind (like keep - kept), but students hear only a vowel change, so Hall A is fine for today.

6. Error Clinic: Fix the Past

41–48 min
عيادة الأخطاء: أصلِح الماضي
Method: Error correction with explanation (metalinguistic awareness), pairs then group
Purpose: Recognize and repair the typical mistakes of Arabic speakers in the past tense.
Teacher says

“Dr Sara's clinic is full of sick sentences! Each one has ONE problem. Find it, fix it and tell us the reason.”

“Use our new words: fossil, did + base, was for describing.”

“Read the fixed sentence aloud: watch your -ed sounds!”

  • عيادة الدكتورة سارة مليئة بالجمل المريضة! في كل جملة خطأ واحد: جده وأصلحه واذكر السبب.
Students do
  • Fix the sentences in pairs (breakout room or private chat), then share.
  • Give a reason for each fix in English or Arabic.
  • Read the corrected sentences aloud with correct -ed sounds.
Exercise · error-correction — Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it and say why.
في كل جملة خطأ واحد. صحّحه واذكر السبب.
  1. Yesterday I go to the mall with Noor. → Yesterday I went to the mall with Noor. (the verb must show the past)
  2. Hamad goed to the match last Friday. → Hamad went to the match last Friday. (fossil: go - went)
  3. Did you saw the new movie? → Did you see the new movie? (did + base verb)
  4. I didn't went to school on Sunday. → I didn't go to school on Sunday. (didn't + base, like لم أذهب)
  5. She buyed a new phone. → She bought a new phone. (-ought family)
  6. We was very excited. → We were very excited. (we/you/they + were)
  7. I was eat pizza at the mall. → I ate pizza at the mall. (action = past verb alone, no was; 'I was eating pizza' is also correct English, but that form comes in Lesson 10)
  8. Did Ziad finished his homework? → Did Ziad finish his homework? (did + base)
Tips:
  • Ask 'Why?' every time; the reason is the learning.
  • If a group finishes early, they write one sick sentence for the other group.

7. Production: The 30-Second Weekend Podcast

48–55 min
إنتاج: بودكاست نهاية الأسبوع في 30 ثانية
Method: Task-based speaking + self-recording (podcast style) + peer listening
Purpose: Use all three -ed sounds and at least three fossils in a short personal story, and listen to yourself.
Teacher says

“You are now podcast hosts. Your show: 'My Weekend in 30 Seconds'.”

“Rules: use one verb from each sound box (/t/, /d/, /ɪd/) and three fossils. Plus one was/were sentence.”

“Plan for 2 minutes. Then record yourself on your phone or speak live. Listeners: catch the verbs and give a 'clean -ed' star.”

  • أنتم مقدّمو بودكاست: «عطلتي في 30 ثانية». استخدم فعلاً من كل صندوق صوتي وثلاثة أفعال شاذة وجملة فيها was/were.
Students do
  • Write 5-6 short notes (not full sentences) for their weekend.
  • Record or present a 30-second podcast (e.g. 'Last weekend I visited my cousins. We played video games and watched a match. Then we went to a restaurant and I ate a huge burger. It was amazing!').
  • Listeners write the verbs they hear and give one star for clear -ed sounds and one tip.
Tips:
  • Recording on a phone lets shy students re-try; they can share audio in the Teams chat.
  • Give feedback on -ed sounds and fossils only; ignore other small errors today.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

55–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check the rule and the fossils individually, and send students home with a fact to share.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five quick questions. Write your answers in the chat on my signal, all together.”

“Did you know? In big word counts of English, the most common verbs are nearly all irregular: be, have, do, say, go, get, make, know, think, take. One reason fossils survive: we use them every day!”

“Next time we use these verbs to build real stories. See you, Story Scientists!”

  • هل تعلم؟ في الإحصاءات الكبيرة لكلمات الإنجليزية تكاد كل الأفعال الأكثر استخداماً تكون شاذة؛ ومن أسباب بقاء الحفريات أننا نستخدمها كل يوم!
Students do
  • Answer the 5 exit-ticket items.
  • Say goodbye with one past sentence: 'Today I learned...'.
Tips:
  • Students with fewer than 4/5 should repeat the throat test in Lesson 5-6 warm-ups.
  • Collect common podcast errors for the homework feedback.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer quickly. Write the sound or the correct form.
أجب بسرعة. اكتب الصوت أو الصيغة الصحيحة.
  1. What sound is -ed in 'watched'? /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/ → /t/
  2. What sound is -ed in 'cleaned'? → /d/
  3. How many syllables in 'needed'? → 2 (need-ed, /ɪd/)
  4. Past of 'buy' → bought
  5. Correct it: I didn't saw him. → I didn't see him.

Homework

Sound Hunter: record a 45-second voice note about your best day last week using 6 regular verbs (2 for each -ed sound) and 4 fossils. Then make a mini 'Fossil Museum' poster with three halls and three verbs in each.

صائد الأصوات: سجّل رسالة صوتية مدتها 45 ثانية عن أفضل يوم في الأسبوع الماضي، مستخدماً 6 أفعال منتظمة (2 لكل صوت من أصوات -ed) و4 أفعال شاذة. ثم اصنع ملصقاً صغيراً لـ«متحف الحفريات» فيه ثلاث قاعات وثلاثة أفعال في كل قاعة.

  • Record the 45-second voice note and listen back: no 'walk-ed'!
  • Make the Fossil Museum poster (vowel change / -ought / totally different).
  • Find 3 new English verbs from technology (texted, downloaded, scrolled...) and write their -ed sound.

Support

  • Work with only /t/ and /ɪd/ first (the most important contrast for Arabic speakers), then add /d/.
  • Give the T/D/ID card answers with a hint: 'Is the last sound k, p, s, sh, ch?'.
  • Podcast frame on screen: Last weekend I ___ed... Then I ___... It was ___.

Challenge

  • Explain why 'texted' is /ɪd/ (ends in t) and 'scrolled' is /d/.
  • Find the hidden pattern: sing-sang-sung, ring-rang-rung, drink-drank-drunk, swim-swam-swum (i-a-u).
  • Research: 'learned' is two syllables in 'a learned man' but one in 'I learned'. Why might that be?

Success criteria

  • Sorts at least 9 of 12 verbs correctly into /t/, /d/, /ɪd/ and says them without an extra syllable.
  • Explains the voiced/voiceless rule in English or Arabic.
  • Gives the correct past form of at least 8 of 10 fossil verbs and names a family for each.
  • Uses did/didn't + base verb correctly in speech.

Watch for

  • Extra syllable in walked, watched, cooked, finished
  • 'I goed', 'buyed', 'eated'
  • 'Did you saw / didn't went'
  • 'I was go' for simple actions
  • Dropping -ed completely before a consonant: 'I walk to school yesterday'

Teacher notes

  • The big pronunciation priority for Arabic speakers is the /t/ group: walked, watched, helped, cooked must be ONE syllable. The /d/ vs /t/ difference matters less for being understood.
  • Do not teach phonetic symbols as a test item; they are labels for the boxes. Students only need to hear and say the three sounds.
  • Keep the Arabic comparisons honest: 'similar idea', never 'the same'.
  • Bonus Class 2 uses these verbs to build stories with the story mountain; reuse the T/D/ID cards in later warm-ups.
