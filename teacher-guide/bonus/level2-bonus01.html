Two Letters, One Sound: Digraphs

حرفان وصوت واحد: الحروف المزدوجة
Big idea: English has about 44 sounds but only 26 letters, so it often uses two letters to write one sound: sh, ch, th, ck, ph and wh. A spelling detective counts sounds, not letters.
في الإنجليزية نحو 44 صوتاً لكن 26 حرفاً فقط، لذلك تستخدم أحياناً حرفين لكتابة صوت واحد: sh و ch و th و ck و ph و wh. محقق التهجئة يعدّ الأصوات لا الحروف.

Linguistics Corner — Not enough letters! · الحروف لا تكفي!

English is written with the Latin (Roman) alphabet, which today has 26 letters. But English has about 44 sounds. So English pairs letters up to write the missing sounds.تُكتب الإنجليزية بالأبجدية اللاتينية، وفيها اليوم 26 حرفاً، لكن في الإنجليزية نحو 44 صوتاً، لذلك تجمع حرفين معاً لتكتب الأصوات الناقصة.
Long ago English had its own special letters for th: þ (thorn) and ð (eth). Writers were already starting to use th, and when printing came to England about 550 years ago, the printers' letter sets came from Europe and had no thorn. So th won.قديماً كان للإنجليزية حرفان خاصان لصوت th هما þ و ð. وكان الكتّاب قد بدأوا يكتبون th، وعندما وصلت الطباعة إلى إنجلترا قبل نحو 550 سنة جاءت حروف المطابع من أوروبا بلا هذين الحرفين، فغلبت كتابة th.
By then, handwritten þ looked a lot like y, so printers sometimes used y for it: 'ye' for 'the'. That is why shop signs that want to look old say 'Ye Olde Shop'. The word was always read 'the', not 'ye'!وكان þ المكتوب باليد قد صار يشبه y، فاستخدم الطابعون أحياناً y مكانه: ye بدل the. ولهذا نرى لافتات محلات تريد أن تبدو قديمة مكتوباً عليها Ye Olde Shop. لكن الكلمة كانت تُقرأ the دائماً، لا ye!
ph comes from Greek words: phone, photo, elephant, alphabet. Greek had one letter for this sound: φ (phi). The word alphabet itself is alpha + beta, the first two Greek letters, just like Arabic أبجدية comes from أ ب ج د.ph تأتي من كلمات يونانية مثل phone و photo و elephant و alphabet، وكان لليونانية حرف واحد لهذا الصوت هو φ. وكلمة alphabet نفسها من alpha + beta أول حرفين يونانيين، تماماً كما جاءت كلمة «أبجدية» من أ ب ج د.
wh used to be written hw (Old English hwæt = what), and people really said h + w. Today most English speakers say just w: what, when, white. Some speakers in Scotland, Ireland and parts of the US still breathe the h.كانت wh تُكتب قديماً hw ويُنطق فيها h ثم w. أما اليوم فمعظم المتحدثين ينطقونها w فقط: what و when و white، وما زال بعض المتحدثين في اسكتلندا وأيرلندا وأجزاء من أمريكا ينطقون h قبلها.

1. Warm-up: The Mystery Sound

0–5 min
إحماء: الصوت الغامض
Method: Detective role-play hook + sound guessing
Purpose: Set up the detective theme and recall sh and ch from Lesson 3.
Teacher says

“Hello, Spelling Detectives! Here is your badge. Today we solve a big mystery.”

“Listen: shhh! (Finger on lips.) What does it mean? Be quiet! Which letters write shhh?”

“Listen: ch-ch-ch, like a train! Which letters write it?”

“Mystery: shhh is ONE sound. But we write TWO letters. Why? Let's investigate!”

  • أهلاً يا محققي التهجئة! اليوم نحلّ لغزاً كبيراً.
  • shhh صوت واحد، لكننا نكتبه بحرفين. لماذا؟ هيا نحقق!
Students do
  • Make the 'quiet' sound and the 'train' sound with gestures (finger on lips, train arm).
  • Type or show the letters for each sound: sh, ch.
  • Say one word they remember from Lesson 3 with sh or ch.
Tips:
  • Keep it quick and dramatic: a whisper voice for the mystery works well.
  • Accept ship, fish, chip, chair from Lesson 3 as warm-up words.

2. Discovery: Count the Sounds, Not the Letters

5–15 min
اكتشاف: عُدّ الأصوات لا الحروف
Method: Guided discovery with finger counting (phoneme segmentation) and sound boxes
Purpose: Discover that a digraph is two letters for one sound by comparing letter count and sound count.
Teacher says

“Look: s-h-i-p. How many letters? Four. Now say it slowly and count with your fingers: sh... i... p. How many sounds? Three!”

“Four letters, three sounds. Two letters are holding hands to make ONE sound. We call them a digraph. Di means two, graph means writing.”

“Detective rule: first circle the letters that hold hands, then read.”

“Now you: fish. Letters? Sounds?”

  • حرفان يمسكان بيد بعضهما ليصنعا صوتاً واحداً: هذا هو الحرف المزدوج.
  • قاعدة المحقق: ضع دائرة حول الحرفين المتماسكين أولاً، ثم اقرأ.
Students do
  • Count letters (write the number) and sounds (show fingers) for each word.
  • Draw sound boxes: one box per sound, so the digraph shares one box.
  • Circle the digraph in each word with a colored pen.
Exercise · count-letters-and-sounds — Write how many letters. Show how many sounds. Circle the digraph.
اكتب عدد الحروف، وأرِني بأصابعك عدد الأصوات، وضع دائرة حول الحرف المزدوج.
  1. ship → 4 letters, 3 sounds (sh-i-p)
  2. fish → 4 letters, 3 sounds (f-i-sh)
  3. chip → 4 letters, 3 sounds (ch-i-p)
  4. shop → 4 letters, 3 sounds (sh-o-p)
  5. much → 4 letters, 3 sounds (m-u-ch)
  6. dish → 4 letters, 3 sounds (d-i-sh)
  7. chin → 4 letters, 3 sounds (ch-i-n)
  8. lunch → 5 letters, 4 sounds (l-u-n-ch)
Tips:
  • Children often count 4 sounds for ship at first. Say it very slowly with them and ask: did you hear an s and then an h? No - one sound.
  • Sound boxes can be drawn on paper and held up to the camera.

3. Linguistics Corner: Why English Needs Digraphs

15–23 min
ركن اللغويات: لماذا تحتاج الإنجليزية الحروف المزدوجة؟
Method: Storytelling with images (history of writing) + quick quiz
Purpose: Understand where digraphs come from and widen general knowledge about alphabets.
Teacher says

“Arabic has 28 letters. English has only 26 letters, but about 44 sounds! Not enough letters. So English makes teams.”

“Long ago, English had a special letter for th. Look: þ. Its name was thorn. Then printing machines came from Europe, and they had no thorn! So printers wrote t + h.”

“Look at this sign: Ye Olde Shop. People think it says 'ye'. No! The y is really a thorn. It says 'The Old Shop'!”

“ph comes from Greek: phone, photo, elephant. And the word alphabet? Alpha + beta, the first two Greek letters. Like أبجدية: أ ب ج د!”

  • الحروف الإنجليزية لا تكفي لكل الأصوات، فتصنع فِرَقاً من حرفين.
  • كلمة alphabet جاءت من أول حرفين في اليونانية، كما جاءت كلمة أبجدية من أ ب ج د.
Students do
  • Answer the quick quiz with thumbs up (true) or thumbs down (false).
  • Try to write the old letter þ on paper.
  • Say alphabet and أبجدية and spot the same idea.
Exercise · true-or-false — True or false? Show thumbs up or thumbs down.
صح أم خطأ؟ ارفع إبهامك لأعلى أو لأسفل.
  1. English has more sounds than letters. → true (about 44 sounds, 26 letters)
  2. sh is two sounds. → false (one sound, two letters)
  3. Long ago English had a letter called thorn for th. → true
  4. 'Ye Olde Shop' really means 'The Old Shop'. → true
  5. ph in phone comes from Greek. → true
  6. Arabic needs two letters to write ش. → false (one letter)
Tips:
  • Keep the history to pictures and short sentences; the children only need to retell one fact.
  • The 'Ye Olde' story is a favorite to retell at home - push it as the fact of the day.

4. Arabic Bridge: One Arabic Letter, Two English Letters

23–31 min
جسر العربية: حرف عربي واحد وحرفان إنجليزيان
Method: Contrastive analysis: matching English digraphs to Arabic letters
Purpose: Use Arabic letters as anchors for the digraph sounds and spot the sounds that need care.
Teacher says

“Detectives, match the English team to the Arabic letter. sh = ش. Easy!”

“ch: Arabic has no single letter. It is t + sh glued fast: tsh! Some of you say چ at home, like چم. Same sound!”

“th is the clever one. Arabic has TWO letters: ث and ذ. English uses th for both!”

“ph = ف. ck = ك. wh = و. You already know these sounds - only the spelling is new.”

  • sh مثل الشين تماماً. و ch مثل «تش» ملتصقة.
  • th لها صوتان: مثل الثاء في think ومثل الذال في this.
  • ph تُنطق فاء، و ck كاف، و wh واو.
Students do
  • Draw a line on the slide from each digraph to its Arabic letter (or say the pair aloud).
  • Write the Arabic letter next to each digraph on paper.
  • Say a word for each: ship / شمس, chip / چاي (Gulf), think / ثلاثة, this / هذا.
Exercise · match — Match the English digraph to the Arabic sound.
صِل الحرف المزدوج الإنجليزي بالصوت العربي المناسب.
  1. sh (ship) → ش
  2. ch (chip) → تش / چ
  3. th (think) → ث
  4. th (this) → ذ
  5. ph (phone) → ف
  6. ck (duck) → ك
  7. wh (what) → و
Tips:
  • If a child says 'chai' with چ, celebrate it: their dialect already has the English ch.
  • For Egyptian children, link th to ث and ذ as pronounced in Standard Arabic and in reciting the Holy Quran, not to everyday تلاتة or دهب.

5. The Th Twins: Think and This

31–41 min
توأم th: think و this
Method: Multisensory articulation: mirror, tissue test and hand on throat
Purpose: Pronounce th correctly (tongue between teeth) and hear the voiceless and voiced th apart.
Teacher says

“Mirror time! Put the tip of your tongue between your teeth. Just a little bit out. Blow: thhh. That is th in think, three, thank you.”

“Hold the tissue close to your mouth: thhh - the tissue moves a little. No buzz in the throat. Like ث.”

“Now hand on throat: the, this, that, mother, brother. Buzz! Like ذ.”

“If your tongue stays inside, you say sink, not think! Tongue out, detectives!”

  • ضع طرف لسانك بين أسنانك. هذا هو th.
  • think بلا اهتزاز في الحلق مثل الثاء، و this مع اهتزاز مثل الذال.
Students do
  • Watch their tongue in the mirror for think, three, this, that.
  • Hand on throat: decide buzz or no buzz for each word.
  • Show a card: ث (no buzz) or ذ (buzz).
  • Say the chant: 'Thank you, thank you, three times thank you! This is my mother, that is my brother.'
Exercise · listen-and-sort — Listen. Is th like ث (no buzz) or like ذ (buzz)? Show your card.
استمع: هل th مثل الثاء (بلا اهتزاز) أم مثل الذال (مع اهتزاز)؟ ارفع البطاقة.
  1. think → ث (voiceless)
  2. this → ذ (voiced)
  3. three → ث (voiceless)
  4. that → ذ (voiced)
  5. thank you → ث (voiceless)
  6. mother → ذ (voiced)
  7. bath → ث (voiceless)
  8. brother → ذ (voiced)
Tips:
  • Tell children it is polite and correct to show the tongue for th; some are shy about it.
  • Common pattern worth noticing: little words like the, this, that, they use the buzzing ذ sound.
  • th is taught fully in Lesson 5; today is a preview, so praise the tongue position more than perfect accuracy.

6. Detective Case Files: Digraph Hunt and Sort

41–51 min
ملفات القضايا: صيد الحروف المزدوجة وتصنيفها
Method: Sound sorting game (word sort) + the ck detective rule (guided discovery)
Purpose: Recognize all six digraphs in words and discover when we spell k as ck.
Teacher says

“New case! Each word hides a digraph. Find it and put the word in the right case file: sh, ch, th, ck, ph or wh.”

“Look at the ck words: duck, back, sock, neck. What comes just before ck? A short vowel: u, a, o, e! Detective rule: at the end of a short word, right after a short vowel, the k sound is spelled ck.”

“And the wh words: what, when, white. Many question words start with wh: what, when, where, why!”

  • كل كلمة تُخفي حرفاً مزدوجاً. جده وضع الكلمة في الملف الصحيح.
  • قاعدة: في آخر الكلمة القصيرة، إذا جاء صوت k مباشرة بعد صوت متحرك قصير نكتبه ck.
Students do
  • Read each word, circle the digraph and say the case file (sh, ch, th, ck, ph, wh).
  • Race in pairs: who sorts the words first in the chat?
  • Find the pattern in the ck words and say the rule in English or Arabic.
Exercise · sort — Find the digraph. Put the word in the right case file.
جد الحرف المزدوج وضع الكلمة في الملف الصحيح.
  1. shop → sh
  2. lunch → ch
  3. thin → th
  4. duck → ck
  5. phone → ph
  6. when → wh
  7. dish → sh
  8. back → ck
  9. photo → ph
  10. white → wh
  11. chin → ch
  12. that → th
Tips:
  • phone, photo and white contain long vowels the children have not studied; tell them to focus only on the digraph.
  • Turn the fastest finisher into the 'chief detective' who checks the others' answers.
  • If a child finds who or whose: these wh words say h, not w. English spelling has surprises.

7. Missing Letters: Crack the Code

51–56 min
الحروف الناقصة: فُكّ الشفرة
Method: Spelling from sound (dictation with a gap) + peer checking
Purpose: Move from reading digraphs to spelling them.
Teacher says

“I say a word. You write the missing digraph. Listen: ship. _ _ i p. Which two letters?”

“Check your partner's answer: is it correct? Detectives always check!”

  • أقول الكلمة وأنت تكتب الحرف المزدوج الناقص.
  • تحقق من إجابة زميلك!
Students do
  • Write the full word on paper and hold it up to the camera.
  • Check a partner's answer and say 'Case closed!' when correct.
Exercise · fill-the-gap — Listen and write the missing two letters.
استمع واكتب الحرفين الناقصين.
  1. _ _ip (a big boat) → sh (ship)
  2. _ _ip (potato snack) → ch (chip)
  3. fi_ _ → sh (fish)
  4. du_ _ → ck (duck)
  5. _ _ree (the number 3) → th (three)
  6. _ _at? (a question word) → wh (what)
Tips:
  • Say each word twice, clearly.
  • Pair the ship/chip items on purpose: same ending, different digraph.

8. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

56–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check who can hear and spell digraphs alone, and send home one fact.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: I say five words. Write the digraph you hear.”

“Did you know? 'Ye Olde Shop' means 'The Old Shop'. The y was really an old letter for th!”

“Mission: find three things at home with sh, ch or th and teach your family the word 'digraph'.”

  • هل تعلم؟ عبارة Ye Olde Shop تعني The Old Shop، لأن y كانت حرفاً قديماً لصوت th!
  • مهمتك: جد ثلاثة أشياء في البيت فيها sh أو ch أو th.
Students do
  • Write the digraph for 5 words.
  • Say goodbye with a digraph word: 'Bye-bye, ship!'
Tips:
  • Note who scored under 4/5 and give them sh/ch/th word-reading in the warm-ups of Lessons 5-7.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Listen. Write the digraph you hear: sh, ch, th, ck, ph or wh.
استمع واكتب الحرف المزدوج الذي تسمعه: sh أو ch أو th أو ck أو ph أو wh.
  1. chip → ch
  2. fish → sh
  3. three → th
  4. sock → ck
  5. phone → ph

Homework

Digraph Detective Notebook: find and write 6 words with digraphs (books, boxes, food packets at home). Circle the digraph in each word. Then tell your family the 'Ye Olde Shop' story.

دفتر المحقق: ابحث عن 6 كلمات فيها حروف مزدوجة (في الكتب والعلب وأغلفة الطعام في البيت) واكتبها وضع دائرة حول الحرف المزدوج. ثم احكِ لعائلتك قصة Ye Olde Shop.

  • Write 6 digraph words and circle the digraph.
  • Draw sound boxes for ship, chip and fish.
  • Say 'Thank you, this is my brother' in the mirror with your tongue out for th.

Support

  • Work with sh and ch only (taught in Lesson 3) and add th at the end.
  • Give the Arabic letter as a hint card next to each digraph.
  • Let the child count sounds with counters or fingers before writing.

Challenge

  • Find words with two digraphs: chick (ch + ck), shock (sh + ck), check (ch + ck), thick (th + ck).
  • Find a trigraph (three letters, one sound): tch in watch and match.
  • Spot more ph words: elephant, dolphin, alphabet.

Success criteria

  • Explains 'two letters, one sound' in English or Arabic with an example.
  • Counts letters and sounds correctly for at least 6 of 8 words.
  • Sorts at least 9 of 12 words into the right digraph file.
  • Puts the tongue between the teeth for th and shows voiceless vs voiced on at least 6 of 8 items.

Watch for

  • reading s-h as two sounds
  • th said as s, t, z or d
  • ch said as sh (chip -> ship)
  • k spelled k instead of ck after a short vowel (duk)

Teacher notes

  • Only sh and ch have been taught at this point. th, ck, ph and wh are a discovery preview: the goal is recognizing the 'two letters, one sound' idea, not mastery.
  • th returns properly in Lesson 5 (with ng); refer back to the ث / ذ cards then.
  • phone, photo and white include long-vowel spellings taught later; read them for the children if needed and keep the focus on the digraph.
  • Keep the detective badge and the 'case file' slides: all four Level 2 Bonus Classes use the detective theme.
  • Bonus Class 2 moves from two letters for one sound to two sounds side by side: consonant clusters.
