The -s Mystery and the Time Triangle

لغز حرف -s ومثلث الوقت
Big idea: English verbs once had an ending for almost every person, like Arabic verbs do; today only he/she/it keeps one (-s), and it has three sounds. For time, think of a triangle: at a point, on a day, in a longer period.
كانت الأفعال الإنجليزية قديماً تتغيّر مع كل ضمير تقريباً كما تتغيّر الأفعال العربية؛ أما اليوم فلم يبقَ إلا حرف -s مع he/she/it، وله ثلاثة أصوات. وللوقت تخيّل مثلثاً: at لنقطة، و on ليوم، و in لفترة أطول.

Linguistics Corner — The case of the disappearing endings · قضية اللواحق المختفية

About 600 years ago English verbs changed for many persons, a bit like Arabic: 'I come, thou comest, he cometh'. Thou was the old word for 'you' (one person).قبل نحو 600 سنة كانت الأفعال الإنجليزية تتغيّر مع ضمائر كثيرة، تقريباً كالعربية: I come و thou comest و he cometh. وكانت thou الكلمة القديمة لـ«أنتَ» (لشخص واحد).
In the south and middle of England the ending for he/she was -eth: 'he cometh, she maketh, he hath, he doth' (as a main verb also 'he doeth'). In the north of England people already said -s instead: 'he comes'.في جنوب إنجلترا ووسطها كانت لاحقة he/she هي -eth: he cometh و she maketh و he hath و he doth (ويُكتب أيضاً doeth حين يكون فعلاً رئيسياً). أما في شمال إنجلترا فكان الناس يقولون -s بدلاً منها منذ زمن بعيد: he comes.
The northern -s won. By Early Modern English (Shakespeare's time, about 1600) -s was replacing -eth in everyday speech, and by about 1700 -eth was found mainly in old books, prayers and poems.انتصرت -s الشمالية. ففي الإنجليزية الحديثة المبكرة (زمن شكسبير، نحو عام 1600) كانت -s تحلّ محل -eth في الكلام اليومي، وبحلول نحو عام 1700 صارت -eth تظهر غالباً في الكتب القديمة والصلوات والشعر.
All the other person endings (-est for thou, plural endings) disappeared. So today -s is a fossil: the last survivor of a big old family of endings. That is why only he/she/it gets it.واختفت كل لواحق الضمائر الأخرى (-est مع thou ولواحق الجمع). لذلك فإن -s اليوم «أحفورة»: آخر الناجين من عائلة لواحق كبيرة قديمة، ولهذا لا يأخذها إلا he/she/it.
-s has three sounds, decided by the last sound of the verb: /ɪz/ after hissing sounds (s, z, sh, ch, j, x: watches, relaxes), /s/ after other voiceless sounds (p, t, k, f: naps, works), /z/ after voiced sounds and vowels (plays, studies, hangs).لـ -s ثلاثة أصوات يحدّدها آخر صوت في الفعل: /ɪz/ بعد الأصوات الصفيرية (watches و relaxes)، و /s/ بعد الأصوات المهموسة الأخرى (naps و works)، و /z/ بعد الأصوات المجهورة وأصوات العلة (plays و studies و hangs).
Prepositions of time come from prepositions of place: at a point (at the door / at 7), on a surface (on the table / on Monday), in a container (in the box / in June). Your brain uses space to think about time!حروف الجر الزمنية جاءت من حروف جر المكان: at لنقطة (at the door / at 7)، و on لسطح (on the table / on Monday)، و in لشيء يحتوي (in the box / in June). عقلك يستخدم المكان ليفكّر في الزمن!

العربية أيضاً تستطيع أن تقولها دون «في» (ظرف زمان منصوب: الأسبوعَ القادمَ)، فالفكرة مألوفة لك. لكن الإنجليزية صارمة: لا تقل in next week ولا on every day.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
He go to school at seven.He goes to school at seven.The -s is tiny and carries no new meaning (he already shows the person), so the brain drops it. Arabic speakers do mark person, but on every verb, so one lonely ending is hard to notice.
Does she plays tennis?Does she play tennis?Double marking. The -s can only appear once: it moves onto does, and the main verb goes back to its base form.
She don't like homework.She doesn't like homework.Same rule in negatives: does + not = doesn't for he/she/it.
He studys / she watchs / he dosHe studies / she watches / he doesSpelling rules: consonant + y becomes -ies; after s, z, sh, ch and x add -es (buzzes, watches). Do and go also add -es: does, goes.
I wakes up at six.I wake up at six.Over-use of the new rule. The -s is only for he/she/it, never for I, you, we or they.
My appointment is in Monday.My appointment is on Monday.Arabic في is translated as in. Days and dates take on.
I have a workout in 5 o'clock.I have a workout at 5 o'clock.Clock times are points, so they take at.
We have a break at the morning.We have a break in the morning.Parts of the day are periods (in the morning / afternoon / evening), but at night is a fixed exception.
The holiday starts in next week.The holiday starts next week.No preposition before next, last, this, every, today, tomorrow.

1. Warm-up: The Case File

0–5 min
إحماء: ملف القضية
Method: Mystery hook + noticing (input flood)
Purpose: Create curiosity: students notice that one tiny letter appears in some sentences and not in others, and form a question we will answer during class.
Teacher says

“Welcome, detectives! Today we have a case. Read Sara's routine on the slide.”

“I wake up at six. My brother wakes up at seven. We have breakfast at seven thirty. Sara studies after school. Her friends hang out on Fridays.”

“Something is hiding in these sentences: one letter that comes and goes. What is it? When does it appear?”

“Write your theory in the chat. Don't worry if it's wrong: detectives start with theories.”

  • أهلاً أيها المحققون! عندنا اليوم قضية: حرف صغير يظهر ويختفي. متى يظهر؟
  • اكتبوا نظريتكم في المحادثة، ولا بأس إن كانت خاطئة؛ المحقق يبدأ بنظرية.
Students do
  • Read the five sentences and underline every verb.
  • Type a theory in the chat: 'The -s comes when...'.
  • Make the 7 cards for today: /s/, /z/, /ɪz/, AT, ON, IN, X.
Tips:
  • Do not confirm the rule yet; just collect theories and promise to test them.
  • If a student already knows 'he/she/it + s', ask the harder question: 'Yes, but WHY only he/she/it?' That is the real mystery.

2. Discovery: Who Gets the -s?

5–15 min
اكتشاف: من يأخذ حرف -s؟
Method: Guided discovery (test the theory with examples, then state the rule)
Purpose: Students build the full present simple -s rule themselves, including questions, negatives and spelling.
Teacher says

“Let's test your theories. Look at the table: I play, you play, he plays, she plays, it plays, we play, they play. How many forms of the verb? Just two!”

“So the -s is for he, she, it, and any one person or thing: Omar, my alarm, the bus.”

“Now look: Does Ziad play football? Where is the -s now? Yes, it jumped onto does! The -s can only live in one place.”

“Same in negatives: Hamad doesn't nap. Not 'doesn't naps'.”

“Spelling check: study becomes studies, watch becomes watches, go becomes goes. Why do you think we add an e? Try to say 'watchs'. Hard, right?”

  • كم شكلاً للفعل؟ اثنان فقط! -s لـ he و she و it ولأي شخص أو شيء واحد.
  • في السؤال والنفي تقفز -s إلى does، ويعود الفعل إلى أصله: Does he play?
  • حرف -s لا يسكن إلا في مكان واحد في الجملة.
Students do
  • Complete the rule on the slide: 'In the present simple, add -s to the verb after ______.'
  • Do the 'Jumping -s' exercise: choose the correct form.
  • Explain one answer to the group in their own words (English or Arabic).
Exercise · choose-the-form — Choose the correct form. Remember: the -s lives in only one place.
اختر الشكل الصحيح. تذكّر: حرف -s يسكن في مكان واحد فقط.
  1. Noor (practice / practices) the piano every day. → practices
  2. My friends (hang / hangs) out on Fridays. → hang
  3. (Do / Does) your brother have a workout today? → Does
  4. Does Omar (relax / relaxes) after school? → relax
  5. Ziad (don't / doesn't) check his planner. → doesn't
  6. My alarm (go / goes) off at six. → goes
  7. Sara (study / studys / studies) before her deadline. → studies
  8. We (wake / wakes) up early on school days. → wake
  9. Hamad (watchs / watches) videos at bedtime. → watches
  10. Lumi never (do / does / dos) homework. Lumi is a chick! → does
Tips:
  • Highlight the -s in a bright color on the slide every time it 'jumps' to does.
  • Point out that 'my alarm' and 'the bus' are also he/she/it: students often forget nouns, not just pronouns.

3. Linguistics Corner: The Old English Time Machine

15–23 min
ركن اللغويات: آلة الزمن إلى الإنجليزية القديمة
Method: Storytelling + authentic old examples + decoding challenge
Purpose: Answer the real mystery: -s is the last survivor of an old system of person endings, which makes the rule logical and memorable.
Teacher says

“Now the WHY. Let's get into the time machine and go back 600 years.”

“Back then English verbs changed for many persons, a bit like Arabic. They said: I come, thou comest, he cometh. Thou meant 'you', for one person.”

“Listen to these: he cometh, she maketh, he doth, he hath. In the south of England, the ending for he and she was -eth!”

“But in the north of England, people said 'he comes'. Slowly the northern -s spread everywhere. In Shakespeare's time, about 1600, both were used. Then -eth disappeared from normal speech.”

“And the other endings? -est for thou, the plural endings... all gone. Only one ending survived: the -s for he, she and it. It's a fossil, like a dinosaur bone inside modern English!”

“That's why the rule looks strange. It isn't random; it's history.”

  • قبل 600 سنة كانت الأفعال الإنجليزية تتغيّر مع ضمائر كثيرة مثل العربية: he cometh و thou comest.
  • في الشمال قالوا he comes، وانتشرت -s حتى حلّت محل -eth.
  • اختفت كل اللواحق الأخرى، وبقيت -s وحدها: أحفورة داخل الإنجليزية الحديثة!
Students do
  • Listen to the story and fill the timeline on the slide: Old English (an ending for almost every person) → Middle English (-eth in the south, -s in the north) → Early Modern English (-s wins) → today.
  • Decode the old sentences into modern English (exercise).
  • Bonus: say the old version of a modern sentence in a 'Shakespeare voice' (e.g. 'She studieth every night').
Exercise · decode — Time machine! Turn the old English into modern English.
آلة الزمن! حوّل الإنجليزية القديمة إلى إنجليزية حديثة.
  1. He cometh home at night. → He comes home at night.
  2. She maketh a schedule. → She makes a schedule.
  3. He hath a nap after lunch. → He has a nap after lunch.
  4. What doth he do on Fridays? → What does he do on Fridays?
  5. Thou playest well. → You play well.
  6. Thou art late! → You are late!
  7. She knoweth the answer. → She knows the answer.
Tips:
  • Keep the dates round and simple; the aim is the story, not memorizing years.
  • Link to Arabic with pride: 'Arabic kept its rich system of person endings; English lost almost all of them.'
  • If asked: 'has' and 'does' look irregular because they are old, very frequent verbs (hath, doth).

4. Sound Lab: The Three Voices of -s

23–32 min
مختبر الأصوات: أصوات -s الثلاثة
Method: Multisensory pronunciation (hand on throat) + sorting with response cards
Purpose: Hear and say /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/ correctly and understand the rule that decides each one.
Teacher says

“The -s is written the same, but it has three voices. Listen: works /s/, plays /z/, watches /ɪz/.”

“Hand on your throat. Say sss: no buzz. Say zzz: buzz! Arabic has both: س and ز.”

“Rule 1: after a hissing sound (s, z, sh, ch, j, x), we need an extra syllable: watch-es, relax-es, brush-es. One syllable becomes two!”

“Rule 2: after a quiet sound like p, t, k, f, the -s is quiet too: naps, works, wakes.”

“Rule 3: after a buzzing sound or a vowel, the -s buzzes: plays, studies, hangs, goes.”

“Careful: does sounds like 'duz', not 'doos'. And says sounds like 'sez'!”

  • ضع يدك على حنجرتك: س بلا اهتزاز، وز مع اهتزاز.
  • بعد الأصوات الصفيرية نضيف مقطعاً كاملاً: watch-es.
  • بعد الأصوات المهموسة تكون -s مهموسة /s/، وبعد المجهورة تكون مجهورة /z/.
Students do
  • Hold up the /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/ card for each verb the teacher says.
  • Count syllables with fingers: watch (1) → watches (2).
  • Read Ziad's routine aloud with clear -s endings: 'Ziad wakes up, brushes his teeth, packs his bag and plays football.'
Exercise · sort — Say the verb with -s. Which sound do you hear: /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/? Show the card.
انطق الفعل مع -s. أي صوت تسمع: /s/ أم /z/ أم /ɪz/؟ ارفع البطاقة.
  1. works → /s/
  2. plays → /z/
  3. watches → /ɪz/
  4. naps → /s/
  5. relaxes → /ɪz/
  6. studies → /z/
  7. wakes → /s/
  8. practices → /ɪz/
  9. hangs → /z/
  10. brushes → /ɪz/
  11. gets → /s/
  12. goes → /z/
Tips:
  • Practices ends in the sound /s/ (spelled -ce), so it takes /ɪz/: look at the sound, not the letter.
  • Teens often say all -s as /s/. The difference /s/ vs /z/ rarely breaks meaning, but /ɪz/ matters: 'watch' and 'watches' must have a different number of syllables.
  • Mention plurals use the same three sounds (cats, dogs, buses) and so does possessive 's (Ziad's, Sara's, Lumi's).

5. Arabic Bridge + The Time Triangle

32–42 min
جسر العربية + مثلث الوقت
Method: Contrastive analysis + visual metaphor (space for time) + team quiz
Purpose: Replace the 'في = in' habit with a clear picture: at = point, on = day, in = longer period, plus the no-preposition words.
Teacher says

“In Arabic we can say في الساعة السابعة، في يوم الاثنين، في يونيو. One word: في. English uses three!”

“Look at the triangle. At the sharp tip: AT, for a point: at 7, at 6:30, at bedtime, at night, at noon.”

“In the middle: ON, for one day: on Monday, on my birthday, on June 5, on the weekend in American English.”

“At the wide base: IN, for longer periods: in June, in the summer, in 2025, in the morning, in the second semester.”

“Remember the place picture: at the door, on the table, in the box. Time copies space!”

“And some words need nothing: next week, last year, this morning, every day, tomorrow. Arabic can do this too: الأسبوعَ القادمَ, no في!”

“Small surprise: in the morning but on Monday morning, because now it's a day.”

  • العربية تكتفي بـ«في»، أما الإنجليزية فتستخدم ثلاث كلمات.
  • at للنقطة، و on لليوم، و in للفترة الأطول.
  • next و last و this و every و tomorrow لا تحتاج حرف جر، كما نقول في العربية: الأسبوعَ القادمَ.
  • في بريطانيا يقولون at the weekend، وفي أمريكا on the weekend، وكلاهما صحيح.
Students do
  • Draw the Time Triangle on paper and write 3 examples on each level.
  • Team quiz (Team Omar vs Team Noor): the teacher shows a time expression; the first team to show the right card (AT, ON, IN or an X for 'nothing') wins a point.
  • Correct the 'Arabic-style' sentences on the slide.
Exercise · card-race — Show AT, ON, IN or X (no preposition). Fastest correct team wins the point.
ارفع AT أو ON أو IN أو X (بدون حرف جر). الفريق الأسرع والصحيح يربح النقطة.
  1. ___ 7 o'clock → at
  2. ___ Monday → on
  3. ___ June → in
  4. ___ the morning → in
  5. ___ night → at
  6. ___ my birthday → on
  7. ___ next week → X (no preposition)
  8. ___ 2026 → in
  9. ___ bedtime → at
  10. ___ Friday afternoon → on
  11. ___ the second semester → in
  12. ___ every weekend → X (no preposition)
  13. ___ March 15 → on
  14. ___ noon → at
Tips:
  • 'The weekend' is not in the race because it has two right answers: on the weekend (American, as in our lessons) and at the weekend (British). Teach both and accept both in speaking and writing.
  • 'At night' is an exception to 'parts of the day = in'. Call it 'the night owl exception'.
  • Use the team score to keep energy high, but always ask the winning team to explain the rule.

6. Game: The Error Auction

42–50 min
لعبة: مزاد الأخطاء
Method: Error auction (gamified error correction with risk and reasoning)
Purpose: Make students judge grammar carefully: they bet coins only on sentences they think are correct, then fix the wrong ones.
Teacher says

“Each team has 100 Lumio coins. I am selling sentences. Some are perfect, some have a mistake.”

“Buy only the correct ones! If you buy a wrong sentence, you lose your money. Minimum bid: 10 coins.”

“After each sale, the other team can win 10 coins by finding and fixing the mistake.”

“Who will be the richest team? Let's go: sentence number one!”

  • لكل فريق 100 عملة. اشتروا الجمل الصحيحة فقط!
  • إن اشتريتم جملة خاطئة تخسرون المبلغ، ويربح الفريق الآخر 10 عملات إن صحّحها.
Students do
  • Discuss in pairs (breakout chat or private notes) and bid.
  • Fix every wrong sentence and say the rule.
  • Count the coins at the end and crown the Grammar Billionaires.
Exercise · error-auction — Correct or wrong? Bid on correct sentences only. Fix the wrong ones.
صحيحة أم خاطئة؟ زايدوا على الجمل الصحيحة فقط وصحّحوا الخاطئة.
  1. Noor has a dentist appointment on Tuesday. → Correct
  2. Omar go to the gym on Saturdays. → Wrong: Omar goes to the gym on Saturdays.
  3. Does Sara wakes up early? → Wrong: Does Sara wake up early?
  4. Our semester starts in September. → Correct
  5. My deadline is in Thursday. → Wrong: My deadline is on Thursday.
  6. Hamad rarely relaxes before exams. → Correct
  7. We have a break at 10:30. → Correct
  8. The countdown starts in next Friday. → Wrong: The countdown starts next Friday.
  9. She don't use a planner. → Wrong: She doesn't use a planner.
  10. Ziad studys at the evening. → Wrong: Ziad studies in the evening.
Tips:
  • Put the most tempting errors (Does Sara wakes...) in the middle, when teams are confident.
  • Online: each team types its bid in the chat at the same time after 'Bids now!' so nobody copies.
  • Sentence 10 has two errors: give the fixing team 20 coins if they find both.

7. Production: The Crew Calendar Interview

50–56 min
إنتاج: مقابلة تقويم الفريق
Method: Task-based speaking (information gap) + reporting in the third person
Purpose: Use do/does questions, -s endings and at/on/in in real communication, then report about a classmate (forcing the he/she -s).
Teacher says

“Interview a partner about their week. Ask at least four questions: What time do you wake up on school days? When do you have your workout? What do you do on Fridays? When is your next holiday?”

“Then report to the group about your partner: 'Ali wakes up at six. He plays football on Tuesdays. His holiday starts in December.'”

“Listeners: you are -s detectives. Raise your hand if you hear a missing -s or a wrong preposition!”

  • اسأل زميلك أربعة أسئلة على الأقل عن أسبوعه، ثم أخبر المجموعة عنه.
  • المستمعون محققو -s: ارفعوا أيديكم إذا سمعتم -s ناقصة أو حرف جر خاطئ.
Students do
  • Ask and answer 4+ questions in pairs.
  • Report 3 sentences about the partner using he/she + -s and at/on/in.
  • Catch and fix errors in classmates' reports politely.
Tips:
  • With an odd number of students, the teacher becomes a partner and plays 'Lumi', who has a very strange schedule.
  • Focus feedback on the two targets only (-s and in/on/at) so it stays manageable.

8. Exit Ticket and Did You Know?

56–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check each student alone on the two big ideas and send them home with a fact to share.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five quick items. Write your answers privately in the chat to me.”

“Did you know? In some old prayers and poems you can still read 'he cometh' and 'thou art'. And in a few English dialects today people still say 'I goes' or 'he go'! Standard English chose one rule: -s for he/she/it.”

“Mission: explain to someone at home why only he/she/it gets -s. You are now a language historian!”

  • هل تعلم؟ ما زال بعض الناس في لهجات إنجليزية محلية يقولون I goes أو he go، لكن الإنجليزية المعيارية اختارت قاعدة واحدة.
  • مهمتك: اشرح لأحد في البيت لماذا يأخذ he/she/it وحدها حرف -s.
Students do
  • Answer 5 exit-ticket items in a private chat.
  • Say one 'Did you know?' fact they will tell at home.
Tips:
  • Note who still drops the -s in speech, not just in writing; use 1-minute -s warm-ups in Lessons 5-8 for them.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Complete each sentence. Then answer the last question.
أكمل كل جملة، ثم أجب عن السؤال الأخير.
  1. My sister ___ (practice) the guitar every evening. → practices
  2. ___ Hamad ___ (like) early alarms? → Does Hamad like
  3. We have a test ___ Wednesday ___ 9 o'clock. → on / at
  4. Which sound does -s make in 'watches': /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/? → /ɪz/
  5. In one sentence: why does only he/she/it get -s today? → Older English had endings for many persons (he cometh, thou comest); the others disappeared and only the -s for he/she/it survived.

Homework

Family Routine Report: write 8 sentences about the routine of a family member (or a famous person). Use he or she, all three -s sounds (/s/, /z/, /ɪz/), and at, on and in at least twice each. Then record yourself reading it (voice message) and check that every -s is clear.

تقرير روتين العائلة: اكتب 8 جمل عن روتين أحد أفراد أسرتك (أو شخص مشهور) باستخدام he أو she، وفيها أصوات -s الثلاثة، واستخدم at و on و in مرتين على الأقل لكل منها. ثم سجّل صوتك وأنت تقرؤها وتأكّد أن كل -s واضحة.

  • Write 8 sentences in the third person about one person's routine.
  • Underline every -s and write /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/ above it.
  • Circle every at, on and in and check it with the Time Triangle.
  • Record a voice message reading your report.

Support

  • Give a verb bank with the -s forms already written (wakes, plays, watches) and a Time Triangle card to keep on screen.
  • In the Error Auction, tell the student which word to look at before they decide.
  • Allow Arabic for explaining the history story; the English target is only the rule.

Challenge

  • Find the 'irregular' -s verbs and explain them: have → has, do → does /dʌz/, say → says /sez/, go → goes.
  • Research: why does 'news' end in -s but take a singular verb ('The news is good')?
  • Write a 4-line routine in 'Shakespeare English' with -eth and thou, then translate it back.

Success criteria

  • Uses -s with he/she/it in at least 8 of 10 written items and in spontaneous speech during the interview.
  • Does not add -s after does/doesn't.
  • Sorts at least 9 of 12 verbs into /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/ and says /ɪz/ as an extra syllable.
  • Chooses at/on/in correctly for at least 11 of 14 time expressions.
  • Can tell the -eth / -s story in a few sentences (English or Arabic).

Watch for

  • Dropping -s in speech (he go, she like)
  • Double marking in questions (Does he plays?)
  • Over-generalizing -s to I/you/we/they (I wakes)
  • in + day (in Monday) and in + clock time (in 5 o'clock)
  • Adding a preposition before next/last/every (in next week)

Teacher notes

  • This class explains rules students met in Lessons 1-4; it adds no new topic vocabulary. All examples use Lesson 1-4 words.
  • Keep the history light and story-like; the point is that grammar has reasons, which helps teens remember and respect the rule.
  • The three sounds of -s were introduced for plurals in Level 2. If students remember it, celebrate: 'Same three sounds for verbs, plurals and possessives!'
  • Our lesson materials use American spelling and usage (practice, on weekends); accept British forms (practise, at the weekend) too.
  • Bonus Class 2 continues the theme of 'English vs Arabic grammar maps' with countable and uncountable nouns and the pronoun 'you'.
