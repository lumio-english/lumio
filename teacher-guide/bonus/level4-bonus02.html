How English Counts the World

كيف تَعُدّ الإنجليزية العالم
Big idea: Every language draws its own map of the world: English decides that some things are counted (a coin, two coins) and some are measured (money, information), even when Arabic counts them; and English uses one 'you' where Arabic has five.
كل لغة ترسم خريطتها الخاصة للعالم: الإنجليزية تقرّر أن بعض الأشياء تُعَدّ (a coin, two coins) وبعضها يُقاس (money, information) حتى لو كانت العربية تعدّها؛ وتستخدم الإنجليزية you واحدة حيث تستخدم العربية خمسة ضمائر.

Linguistics Corner — Languages cut the world into different pieces · اللغات تقطّع العالم قطعاً مختلفة

Countable or uncountable is a decision of the grammar, not of nature. Coins and money are the same thing in your wallet, but English counts coins and measures money.كون الاسم معدوداً أو غير معدود قرار نحوي وليس من الطبيعة. العملات المعدنية والمال شيء واحد في محفظتك، لكن الإنجليزية تعدّ coins وتقيس money.
Languages disagree: English says information (no plural), Arabic says معلومات and French says informations, both plural. English hair is uncountable, but French cheveux is plural.اللغات تختلف: الإنجليزية تقول information (بلا جمع)، والعربية تقول معلومات، والفرنسية informations، وكلاهما جمع. و hair في الإنجليزية غير معدود، لكنه جمع في الفرنسية.
English often uses a general 'mass' word for a group of different things: furniture (chairs, tables, beds), luggage (bags, suitcases), homework (exercises, tasks). Arabic أثاث is also a collective word, but Arabic is happy to say أثاثات or واجبات.كثيراً ما تستخدم الإنجليزية كلمة «كتلة» عامة لمجموعة أشياء مختلفة: furniture للكراسي والطاولات والأسرّة، و luggage للحقائب، و homework للتمارين. وكلمة «أثاث» في العربية اسم جمع أيضاً، لكن العربية لا تمانع أن تقول أثاثات أو واجبات.
Some English nouns change meaning when they cross the line: chicken (meat) / a chicken (the animal); paper (material) / a paper (a newspaper); a coffee in a café means a cup of coffee.بعض الأسماء الإنجليزية يتغيّر معناها حين تعبر الحدّ: chicken (لحم) و a chicken (الحيوان)، و paper (مادة الورق) و a paper (صحيفة)، و a coffee في المقهى تعني فنجان قهوة.
English once had thou (one person) and ye/you (more than one). Like French tu/vous, you also became the polite form, and over time it pushed thou out. Now one word covers everyone.كان في الإنجليزية قديماً thou لشخص واحد و ye/you لأكثر من شخص. وكما في الفرنسية tu/vous صارت you أيضاً صيغة الاحترام، ومع الوقت أزاحت thou تماماً. والآن كلمة واحدة تكفي للجميع.
Speakers still want a plural 'you', so they invent one: 'you guys' (US), 'y'all' (southern US), 'youse' (Ireland, Australia). Language keeps fixing its own gaps!ما زال المتحدثون يحتاجون you للجمع فيخترعونها: you guys في أمريكا، و y'all في جنوب أمريكا، و youse في أيرلندا وأستراليا. اللغة تسدّ ثغراتها بنفسها!

Key terms

TermبالعربيةMeaning
countable nounاسم معدودA noun you can count: a coin, two coins. It has a plural and can take a / an.
uncountable (mass) nounاسم غير معدودA noun English treats as a mass: money, rice, information. No plural, no a / an.
unit (partitive)وحدة قياس / لفظ التجزئةA countable word that lets us count a mass: a piece of, a slice of, a bottle of.
collective nounاسم جمعA word for a group of things seen as one: furniture (chairs, tables, beds...).
dualالمثنّىA grammar form for exactly two. Arabic has it (أنتما، هما، كتابان); modern English does not.
second personضمير المخاطَبThe person or people you talk to: you (Arabic أنتَ، أنتِ، أنتما، أنتم، أنتنّ).

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
information / advice / homework / news (no plural)معلومات / نصائح / واجبات / أخبار (جمع)These are plural in Arabic, so learners add -s: informations, advices, homeworks, newses. In English they stay singular: much information, some advice.
هذه الكلمات جمع في العربية، فيضيف المتعلم -s: informations و advices. أما في الإنجليزية فتبقى مفردة: much information و some advice.
a piece of advice / a piece of information / a piece of furnitureنصيحة / معلومة / قطعة أثاثArabic makes a singular directly (نصيحة، معلومة). English needs a unit word to count a mass: a piece of.
العربية تصوغ المفرد مباشرة (نصيحة، معلومة)، أما الإنجليزية فتحتاج كلمة وحدة لتعدّ الكتلة: a piece of.
news is / money is (singular verb)الأخبار سيئة / الفلوس كثيرةNews ends in -s but it is uncountable and singular: The news is good. Money is singular too: Money is important (not 'Moneys are').
news تنتهي بـ -s لكنها غير معدودة ومفردة: The news is good. و money مفردة أيضاً: Money is important.
you (one word for 1, 2 or 100 people, boys or girls)أنتَ، أنتِ، أنتما، أنتم، أنتنّArabic shows gender and number (singular, dual, plural) in 'you'; English shows neither. Context tells you who is meant. The verb never changes either: you play, for everyone.
العربية تُظهر الجنس والعدد (مفرد ومثنى وجمع) في ضمير المخاطب، والإنجليزية لا تُظهر أياً منهما، والسياق يوضّح المقصود. والفعل لا يتغيّر أيضاً: you play للجميع.
he / she / it / theyهو، هي، هما، هم، هنّEnglish keeps gender only in he/she (and his/her, him/her). They covers two people and more, boys or girls.
الإنجليزية تحتفظ بالتذكير والتأنيث في he/she فقط (و his/her و him/her). أما they فتشمل اثنين فأكثر، ذكوراً أو إناثاً.
his / her follows the owner, like Arabicكتابُه / كتابُهاSame logic in both languages: his bag = the bag of a boy, her bag = the bag of a girl. The bag itself has no gender in English.
المنطق واحد في اللغتين: his bag حقيبة ولد، her bag حقيبة بنت. والحقيبة نفسها لا جنس لها في الإنجليزية.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
I need some informations.I need some information.Arabic معلومات is plural, so learners add -s. In English information is uncountable.
She gave me an advice. / two advicesShe gave me some advice. / a piece of advice / two pieces of adviceArabic has نصيحة / نصائح. English advice has no singular with a and no plural.
I have many homeworks.I have a lot of homework. / I have many homework tasks.Arabic واجبات is plural. English homework is uncountable; count the tasks or exercises instead.
We bought new furnitures.We bought new furniture.Furniture is a collective mass word; to count, say pieces of furniture or name the items (two chairs).
The news are bad.The news is bad.News looks plural but is uncountable and takes a singular verb.
How many money do you have?How much money do you have?Money is uncountable: how much. Coins and dollars are countable: how many coins.
My luggages are heavy.My luggage is heavy. / My bags are heavy.Luggage is uncountable in English, even though it means several bags.
This is mine pen. / Is this pen your?This is my pen. / Is this pen yours?Arabic uses the same ending for both (قلمي، القلم لي). English has two forms: my/your before a noun, mine/yours alone.

1. Warm-up: Hamad's Shopping Bag

0–5 min
إحماء: كيس تسوّق حمد
Method: Noticing challenge (can you put a number in front of it?)
Purpose: Activate Lesson 7-8 words and show a puzzle: some things take numbers and some don't, even when they look similar.
Teacher says

“Hamad went shopping with his allowance. Here's his bag! Look at the 12 things.”

“Challenge: put a number in front of each one. Three eggs? Fine. Three rice? Hmm. Three moneys? Strange!”

“Which ones sound strange with a number? Write them in the chat. 60 seconds!”

  • ضعوا رقماً قبل كل كلمة. أي الكلمات تبدو غريبة مع رقم؟
  • عندكم 60 ثانية!
Students do
  • Try 'two ___' with each item and type the strange ones in the chat.
  • Make the C and U cards.
Tips:
  • Expect a debate on money vs coins: perfect, that is today's mystery.
  • Do not explain yet; let the confusion build curiosity.

2. Discovery: Count It or Measure It?

5–15 min
اكتشاف: نَعُدّه أم نقيسه؟
Method: Guided discovery + sorting with response cards
Purpose: Students find the grammar signals of countable and uncountable nouns: a/an, plural -s, many vs much, few vs little.
Teacher says

“Look at these two lists. List 1: a coin, two coins, many coins. List 2: money, some money, much money. What can list 1 do that list 2 can't?”

“Yes! Countable nouns take a or an, a plural -s and many. Uncountable nouns take none of these. They use much, some, a lot of.”

“Uncountable things are often liquids (water, juice), tiny grains (rice, sugar), materials (paper, cash) or ideas (advice, information).”

“Now show me C or U for each word. Ready? Three, two, one, show!”

  • الاسم المعدود يأخذ a/an وجمع -s و many. وغير المعدود لا يأخذها، بل يأخذ much و some و a lot of.
  • غالباً ما يكون غير المعدود سائلاً، أو حبوباً صغيرة، أو مادة، أو فكرة.
Students do
  • Complete the rule table on the slide (a/an? plural? many or much?).
  • Show C or U for each word.
  • Give a reason for 2 tricky ones (e.g. why groceries and leftovers are always plural).
Exercise · card-sort — Show C (countable) or U (uncountable).
ارفع C (معدود) أو U (غير معدود).
  1. coin → C
  2. money → U
  3. cash → U
  4. receipt → C
  5. rice → U
  6. snack → C
  7. juice → U
  8. ingredient → C
  9. homework → U
  10. recipe → C
  11. change (money you get back) → U
  12. earbuds → C (usually plural: a pair of earbuds)
Tips:
  • Groceries and leftovers are plural-only words (like scissors): 'The groceries are in the fridge.' Mention it as a bonus fact.
  • 'Change' = coins you get back is uncountable; 'a change' = something different is countable. Great example of a meaning switch.

3. Linguistics Corner: Every Language Draws Its Own Map

15–23 min
ركن اللغويات: كل لغة ترسم خريطتها
Method: Cross-language comparison + unit-word matching
Purpose: Understand that countability is a grammar choice that varies across languages, and learn the 'unit trick' to count any mass.
Teacher says

“Here's a big idea from linguistics: languages cut the world into different pieces.”

“In your wallet you have coins. English counts them. But put them together and call them money, and English stops counting. Same metal, different grammar!”

“Arabic says معلومات, plural. French says informations, plural. English says information, never plural. Who's right? Everybody! Each language has its own map.”

“So how does English count a mass? With a unit: a bottle of water, a slice of bread, a piece of advice. The unit is countable: two bottles of water.”

“Funny ones: chicken is meat, a chicken is an animal! Paper is the material, a paper is a newspaper.”

  • اللغات تقطّع العالم قطعاً مختلفة، ولا توجد لغة «صحيحة» وأخرى «خاطئة».
  • لعدّ شيء غير معدود نستخدم وحدة: a bottle of water و a piece of advice.
Students do
  • Match each mass noun to its unit.
  • Say a full phrase with a number: 'two slices of bread'.
  • Discuss: 'Name one thing Arabic counts and English doesn't.'
Exercise · match — Match the uncountable noun to a unit. Then say it with the number 2.
صِل الاسم غير المعدود بوحدته، ثم قلها مع الرقم 2.
  1. water → a bottle of water → two bottles of water
  2. bread → a slice / loaf of bread → two slices of bread
  3. advice → a piece of advice → two pieces of advice
  4. information → a piece of information → two pieces of information
  5. furniture → a piece of furniture → two pieces of furniture
  6. rice → a bag / bowl of rice → two bags of rice
  7. juice → a glass / carton of juice → two glasses of juice
  8. news → a piece of news → two pieces of news
  9. luggage → a piece of luggage → two pieces of luggage (or: two bags)
Tips:
  • Accept any sensible unit (a bowl of rice, a cup of juice).
  • Keep the French example short; the point is that Arabic is not 'wrong', just different.

4. Arabic Bridge: The 'Informations' Trap

23–31 min
جسر العربية: فخ «Informations»
Method: Contrastive analysis + error correction race
Purpose: Target the exact words that are plural in Arabic but uncountable in English, and fix the errors they cause.
Teacher says

“Here are the trap words. In Arabic they are plural: معلومات، نصائح، واجبات، أثاث، أخبار، أمتعة، فلوس. In English: information, advice, homework, furniture, news, luggage, money. No -s, no a, singular verb!”

“Your brain translates from Arabic and adds an -s. That's normal: it's called transfer. Now we train the brain to stop.”

“Two tricks: 1) use some, a lot of, much. 2) count with a piece of, or count the real things: two homework tasks, three bags.”

“Race time! Fix the sentences. First correct answer in the chat wins.”

  • هذه الكلمات جمع في العربية، لكنها غير معدودة في الإنجليزية: لا -s ولا a، والفعل مفرد.
  • يسمّي اللغويون هذا «النقل من اللغة الأم»، وهو طبيعي. لنتدرّب على تجنّبه.
Students do
  • Read the Arabic/English pairs aloud.
  • Race: correct each sentence in the chat.
  • Write one true sentence with one trap word about their own life.
Exercise · error-correction-race — Each sentence has one mistake. Fix it fast!
في كل جملة خطأ واحد. صحّحه بسرعة!
  1. Noor has a lot of homeworks tonight. → Noor has a lot of homework tonight.
  2. Can you give me an advice about my budget? → Can you give me some advice / a piece of advice about my budget?
  3. The price tag has some useful informations. → The price tag has some useful information.
  4. The news are on TV at eight. → The news is on TV at eight.
  5. How many money is in your wallet? → How much money is in your wallet?
  6. Our new furnitures are from a sale. → Our new furniture is from a sale.
  7. Ziad's luggages are very heavy. → Ziad's luggage is very heavy.
  8. I have two advices for you. → I have two pieces of advice for you.
  9. Sara saves her moneys in a moneybox. → Sara saves her money in a moneybox.
Tips:
  • Say 'transfer is normal' with warmth: teens are relieved that the mistake has a reason.
  • Note for the teacher: 'moneys / monies' exists in legal English, but students should never use it.

5. The 'You' Puzzle: One Word for Five

31–40 min
لغز «you»: كلمة واحدة بدل خمس
Method: Contrastive pronoun map + history story + translation challenge
Purpose: Show how Arabic pronouns mark gender and number (including dual) while English 'you' marks neither, and practise translating with context words (both, all, guys).
Teacher says

“Arabic, how many words for 'you'? Let's count: أنتَ، أنتِ، أنتما، أنتم، أنتنّ. Five! Boy or girl, one, two or many.”

“English? One: you. You, Omar. You, Sara. You two. You, the whole class. Always you!”

“Did English always have one? No! 600 years ago it had thou for one person and ye or you for more. You also became the polite word, like a sign of respect, and thou slowly disappeared.”

“Then people missed a plural, so they invented new ones: you guys, y'all. Language fixes its own holes!”

“So how do we show 'two' in English? We add a word: you both, you two, both of you. And 'all of you' for a group.”

“Third person: هو، هي → he, she. But هما، هم، هنّ → all become they. English keeps gender only in he, she, his, her, him.”

  • في العربية خمسة ضمائر للمخاطب، وفي الإنجليزية ضمير واحد: you.
  • كانت thou قديماً للمفرد و ye/you للجمع، ثم صارت you للجميع.
  • لإظهار العدد نضيف كلمة: you both أو all of you. وهما وهم وهنّ كلها تصبح they.
Students do
  • Translate the Arabic sentences into English, adding both/all when it helps.
  • Answer: 'Which language gives more information in its pronouns? Which is simpler to learn?'
  • Write one sentence using an object pronoun (them, her) and one possessive (hers, theirs) from Lessons 5-6.
Exercise · translate — Translate into English. Keep the meaning of number (one, two, many) when needed.
ترجم إلى الإنجليزية، وحافظ على معنى العدد (واحد، اثنان، جمع) عند الحاجة.
  1. أنتِ تحبين الوجبات الخفيفة الحارة. → You like spicy snacks.
  2. أنتما صديقاي. → You are my friends. / You two are my friends. / You are both my friends.
  3. هل أنتم جاهزون؟ → Are you ready? / Are you all ready?
  4. هما يرسلان رسالة. → They send a message. / They are sending a message.
  5. هي تتصل به كل يوم. → She calls him every day.
  6. هذه السماعات لها. → These earbuds are hers.
  7. أنتنّ تجمعن المال للرحلة. → You (all) save money for the trip.
  8. هذه المحفظة لكَ أم لها؟ → Is this wallet yours or hers?
Tips:
  • Avoid saying English is 'poorer': it shows the same information with other words (both, all) or context.
  • If students ask about 'they' for one unknown person ('Someone left their jacket'): yes, English uses it, and has done for centuries.

6. Team Quiz Show: How Much or How Many?

40–49 min
مسابقة الفرق: How much أم How many؟
Method: Gamified retrieval practice (quiz show with point values)
Purpose: Practise the full system fast: a/an vs some, some vs any, much vs many, units, singular verb with uncountable nouns.
Teacher says

“Welcome to 'Count Me In!', the quiz show. Team Noor against Team Ziad!”

“Easy questions are 1 point, hard ones are 3 points. Choose your level!”

“Discuss for 15 seconds, then one team captain answers. If you're wrong, the other team can steal the points.”

  • أهلاً بكم في مسابقة Count Me In! الأسئلة السهلة بنقطة، والصعبة بثلاث نقاط.
  • إن أخطأ فريق يستطيع الفريق الآخر سرقة النقاط.
Students do
  • Choose a question level, discuss quickly, and answer.
  • Explain each answer with the rule (C or U?).
  • Steal points by correcting the other team.
Exercise · quiz-show — Complete the sentence. Say why (countable or uncountable?).
أكمل الجملة واذكر السبب (معدود أم غير معدود؟).
  1. How ___ snacks are in the pantry? (1 pt) → many
  2. How ___ cash do you have? (1 pt) → much
  3. Is there ___ milk in the fridge? (1 pt) → any
  4. I'd like ___ apple and ___ juice. (2 pts) → an / some
  5. We don't have ___ ingredients for the recipe. (2 pts) → any
  6. There ___ (is / are) a lot of information on the receipt. (2 pts) → is
  7. How ___ homework do you get on weekdays? (2 pts) → much
  8. Can I give you ___ advice? It's about your savings. (3 pts) → some / a piece of
  9. Ask about price: 'How much ___ these sneakers?' (3 pts) → are (How much are these sneakers?)
  10. Make it countable: 'two ___ bread' (3 pts) → slices of / loaves of
  11. Correct it: 'The leftovers is in the fridge.' (3 pts) → The leftovers are in the fridge. (leftovers is plural-only)
Tips:
  • Online: captains type answers in the chat after a 15-second countdown, so the other team can't copy.
  • Item 9 is a classic: 'How much' asks about price with any noun, because we're really asking about money.

7. Production: The Crew Picnic Budget

49–56 min
إنتاج: ميزانية نزهة الفريق
Method: Task-based role-play (planning with a budget and a price list)
Purpose: Use countable/uncountable nouns, some/any, how much/many and pronouns in a realistic planning conversation.
Teacher says

“The crew is planning a picnic. Your budget is 50 dollars. Look at the price list: juice 2 dollars a bottle, bread 3 dollars a loaf, snacks 1 dollar each, rice 4 dollars a bag, fruit 5 dollars a box...”

“In pairs, plan your shopping list. Ask: How many bottles of juice do we need? How much rice? Do we have any leftovers in the fridge?”

“Then present: 'We need four bottles of water and some bread. We don't need any rice. It costs 46 dollars, so we have 4 dollars left.'”

  • عندكم 50 دولاراً لنزهة الفريق. خطّطوا قائمة التسوق بالأسئلة: How many و How much و any.
  • ثم قدّموا خطتكم للمجموعة.
Students do
  • Plan a shopping list in pairs using at least 6 items, 2 units and 4 questions.
  • Present the plan in 4-5 sentences.
  • Listeners check: is the budget correct? Is every noun right (C or U)?
Tips:
  • Write the useful frames on screen: How many ___ do we need? / How much ___ is there? / We need some ___. / We don't need any ___.
  • If time is short, let one pair present and the other give feedback.

8. Exit Ticket and Did You Know?

56–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check each student alone on the trap words and the 'you' contrast.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five items, privately in the chat to me.”

“Did you know? Fish is usually the same in the plural (one fish, ten fish), and 'fishes' is used mainly for different kinds of fish. And in Arabic, سمك is a collective word and سمكة is one fish. Languages love playing with counting!”

“Mission: teach someone at home the 'informations' trap.”

  • هل تعلم؟ في العربية «سمك» اسم جمع و«سمكة» للواحدة، والإنجليزية أيضاً تلعب بالعدّ بطرق عجيبة.
  • مهمتك: علّم أحداً في البيت فخ «informations».
Students do
  • Answer 5 items privately.
  • Share one 'map' difference between English and Arabic.
Tips:
  • Note who still writes informations/advices; recycle in Lessons 9-12 (Lesson 12 uses advice a lot!).

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer or correct.
أجب أو صحّح.
  1. Correct: 'I need some informations about the sale.' → I need some information about the sale.
  2. How ___ (much / many) receipts are in your wallet? → many
  3. Make 'advice' countable: 'Here are two ___ advice.' → pieces of
  4. Translate: أنتما جاهزان. → You are (both) ready. / You two are ready.
  5. Choose: The news (is / are) great! → is

Homework

Kitchen and Wallet Detective: list 10 things in your kitchen or your bag, write C or U next to each, and write 6 sentences using some, any, how much, how many and a piece of / a bottle of. Then write 3 sentences to your Arabic-speaking friends that use the trap words correctly (homework, advice, information).

محقق المطبخ والمحفظة: اكتب 10 أشياء في مطبخك أو حقيبتك، وضع C أو U بجانب كل منها، واكتب 6 جمل باستخدام some و any و how much و how many و a piece of / a bottle of. ثم اكتب 3 جمل تستخدم فيها كلمات الفخ بشكل صحيح (homework و advice و information).

  • List and label 10 nouns (C or U).
  • Write 6 sentences with some / any / how much / how many / units.
  • Write 3 correct sentences with homework, advice and information.
  • Bonus: ask a family member how many words for 'you' they know in other languages.

Support

  • Keep the C/U lists on screen during all games.
  • Give only the 4 most common trap words first (information, advice, homework, money).
  • In translation, give the English pronoun options (you / they / she / him / hers) as a word bank.

Challenge

  • Find 3 words that can be both C and U with different meanings (chicken, paper, coffee, time: 'I have no time' / 'three times').
  • Explain why we say 'a few coins' but 'a little money'.
  • Investigate 'they' for one person: find an example in a book or a website.

Success criteria

  • Labels at least 10 of 12 nouns correctly as C or U.
  • Uses the 7 trap words without -s and without a in writing and speech.
  • Uses at least two unit phrases correctly (a piece of, a bottle of).
  • Translates Arabic second-person pronouns into English and adds both / all when needed.
  • Explains in a sentence that countability is a grammar choice that differs between languages.

Watch for

  • informations, advices, homeworks, furnitures, luggages
  • an advice / a homework / a news
  • How many money
  • The news are...
  • Adding a plural marker to you ('yous') or confusing he/she in fast speech

Teacher notes

  • This class explains Lessons 5-8; it uses their vocabulary (money words from Lesson 8, food from Lesson 7, belongings from Lesson 6).
  • Advice, information, furniture, news and luggage are named in the outline because they are the classic trap words for Arabic speakers; advice returns in Lesson 12, so this class pre-teaches the trap.
  • Present Arabic as a valuable tool: 'You already know the idea of a unit: سمكة from سمك, نصيحة as one advice.'
  • Bonus Class 3 moves from 'grammar maps' to 'word building': how English and Arabic make new words.
