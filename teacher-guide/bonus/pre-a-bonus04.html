Big Letters, Small Letters, Left to Right

حروف كبيرة وحروف صغيرة، من اليسار إلى اليمين
Big idea: Every English letter has two shapes, a big (capital) one and a small one, and English is written from left to right. Arabic is written from right to left, has no capital letters, and its letters change shape depending on where they sit in the word.
لكل حرف إنجليزي شكلان: كبير وصغير، والإنجليزية تُكتب من اليسار إلى اليمين. أما العربية فتُكتب من اليمين إلى اليسار، وليس فيها حروف كبيرة، وتتغير أشكال حروفها حسب موضعها في الكلمة.

Linguistics Corner — Two shapes, one letter · شكلان لحرف واحد

English has 26 letters, but each one has a big shape and a small shape, so we must learn 52 shapes! Arabic has 28 letters, and most of them have four shapes!في الإنجليزية 26 حرفاً، لكن لكل حرف شكلاً كبيراً وشكلاً صغيراً، فنتعلم 52 شكلاً! أما العربية ففيها 28 حرفاً، ولمعظمها أربعة أشكال!
A big letter and its small letter have the same name and the same sound: B and b both say /b/. Some pairs look alike (C c, O o, S s, W w, Z z); some look very different (A a, B b, G g, Q q, R r).الحرف الكبير والصغير لهما الاسم نفسه والصوت نفسه: B و b كلاهما /b/. بعض الأزواج متشابهة (C c, O o, S s, W w, Z z)، وبعضها مختلف جداً (A a, B b, G g, Q q, R r).
Long ago, the Romans carved big letters in stone. Small letters grew slowly over hundreds of years from the quicker handwriting people used with pens.قديماً كان الرومان ينحتون الحروف الكبيرة على الحجر، ثم نشأت الحروف الصغيرة ببطء عبر مئات السنين من الخط السريع الذي كان الناس يكتبون به بالأقلام.
Printers kept metal letters in wooden boxes called cases: the big letters in the upper case and the small letters in the lower case. That is why big letters are also called uppercase and small letters lowercase.كان عمّال المطابع يحفظون الحروف المعدنية في صناديق خشبية: الكبيرة في الصندوق العلوي والصغيرة في الصندوق السفلي، ولهذا تسمى الحروف الكبيرة في الإنجليزية uppercase والصغيرة lowercase.
English is written from left to right, Arabic from right to left. So an Arabic book opens on the side where an English book ends!الإنجليزية تُكتب من اليسار إلى اليمين، والعربية من اليمين إلى اليسار، ولهذا يُفتح الكتاب العربي من الجهة التي ينتهي عندها الكتاب الإنجليزي!
The numbers we use in English (0-9) are called 'Arabic numerals', because Europe learned them from mathematicians who wrote in Arabic (such as al-Khwarizmi), and they had learned them from India. In Arabic the numbers ١ ٢ ٣ are often called Indian numerals.الأرقام التي نستخدمها في الإنجليزية (0-9) تسمى 'الأرقام العربية'، لأن أوروبا أخذتها عن علماء كتبوا بالعربية (مثل الخوارزمي)، وهؤلاء أخذوها بدورهم عن الهند. أما الأرقام ١ ٢ ٣ فتُسمّى في العربية غالباً الأرقام الهندية.

1. Warm-up: The Big and Small ABC Song

0–4 min
إحماء: أغنية الحروف بصوت كبير وصوت صغير
Method: Song with voice and body contrast (music + TPR)
Purpose: Wake up the alphabet and introduce the idea of 'big' and 'small' through the body.
Teacher says

“Hello, Sound Explorers! Today our letters are big and small!”

“When you see a BIG letter, stand up tall and sing loud! A small letter: crouch down and sing quietly.”

“A, B, C... (tall and loud) a, b, c... (small and quiet)”

“Well done! Big and small are... the same letters!”

  • مرحباً! اليوم حروفنا كبيرة وصغيرة!
  • إذا رأيت حرفاً كبيراً قف طويلاً وغنِّ بصوت عالٍ، وإذا رأيت حرفاً صغيراً انزل وغنِّ بهدوء.
  • أحسنتم! الحرف الكبير والصغير حرف واحد!
Students do
  • Sing the ABC song while the slide flashes big or small letters.
  • Stand tall for big letters and crouch for small letters.
  • Say 'big' or 'small' for 3 flashed letters.
Tips:
  • Flash letters in random order after the first round so children really look.
  • Keep the song well known: it is the anchor for the whole class.

2. Linguistics Corner: One Letter, Two Shapes

4–12 min
ركن اللغويات: حرف واحد بشكلين
Method: Character story (letter families) + short history story (curiosity)
Purpose: Understand that big and small letters are the same letter, and learn where they come from.
Teacher says

“Meet the A family. Mommy A is big. Baby a is small. Same name: 'ay'. Same sound: /æ/, apple!”

“B family: big B, small b. Same sound: /b/, banana!”

“Some families are twins: big O, small o. Hands together! Some are different: big A, small a. Hands apart!”

“A long, long time ago, the Romans carved big letters in stone. Small letters came later, when people wrote fast with pens.”

“Printers kept the big letters in the top box and the small letters in the bottom box.”

“Big letters are for the start of names: Lumi, Sara, Ziad.”

  • هذه عائلة A: الأم A كبيرة، والطفل a صغير. لهما الاسم نفسه والصوت نفسه!
  • بعض العائلات توائم: O و o لهما الشكل نفسه، فضمّ يديك. وبعضها مختلف: A و a، فافتح يديك.
  • قديماً كان الرومان ينحتون الحروف الكبيرة على الحجر، ثم جاءت الحروف الصغيرة عندما صاروا يكتبون بسرعة بالأقلام.
  • كان عمّال المطابع يضعون الحروف الكبيرة في الصندوق العلوي والصغيرة في الصندوق السفلي.
  • نكتب الحرف الكبير في أول الأسماء: Lumi و Sara و Ziad.
Students do
  • Say the name and sound of each letter family (A a, B b, C c, M m).
  • Show 'twins' (hands together) or 'different' (hands apart) for O o, A a, S s and G g.
  • Draw a mommy letter and a baby letter in the air.
  • Point to the big letter at the start of Lumi, Sara and Ziad on the slide.
Exercise · match — Find the baby letter for each mommy letter. Point to it and say the sound.
ابحث عن الحرف الصغير لكل حرف كبير، وأشر إليه وقل صوته.
  1. A → a - /æ/ apple
  2. B → b - /b/ banana
  3. E → e - /e/ egg
  4. R → r - /r/ rice
  5. G → g - /g/ goat
  6. Q → q - /kw/ queen
Tips:
  • Choose pairs that look different first (A a, B b, E e, R r, G g, Q q): they are the ones children need to learn.
  • The history is told as a story; no dates are needed.
  • Keep a clear, simple print font on the slide (a single-story 'a' and 'g' as children write them); mention that books sometimes use a fancy a and g.

3. Movement Break: Sky Writing

12–16 min
استراحة حركة: الكتابة في السماء
Method: Gross-motor letter formation (whole arm in the air, TPR)
Purpose: Move the whole body while feeling letter shapes and the left-to-right direction.
Teacher says

“Stand up! Your finger is a magic pencil. The sky is your paper!”

“Write a BIG O with your whole arm. Now a tiny o with one finger!”

“Big L: down... and across to the right! Small l: just down!”

“Now an arrow: point left... and swoosh to the right! English goes this way!”

  • قفوا! إصبعك قلم سحري، والسماء ورقتك!
  • اكتب O كبيراً بذراعك كلها، ثم o صغيراً بإصبع واحد.
  • أشر إلى اليسار ثم اسحب إلى اليمين: هكذا تسير الإنجليزية!
Students do
  • Sky-write big letters with the whole arm and small letters with one finger: O o, L l, C c, S s.
  • Swoosh the left-to-right arrow 3 times.
  • Shake out the arm and sit down.
Tips:
  • On camera the children see you face to face, so your left hand is on their right. Turn your back to the camera when you sky-write (or write mirror-style), so the arrow really goes to the children's right.
  • Mark the children's left hand with a sticker or a green crayon dot if they mix up left and right.

4. Arabic Bridge: Two Ways to Write

16–24 min
جسر العربية: طريقتان للكتابة
Method: Contrastive discovery: comparing a real Arabic book and an English book
Purpose: Make the differences between Arabic and English writing visible and explicit, so they stop causing confusion.
Teacher says

“Show me your Arabic book. Where does it start? Here, on the right!”

“Now the English book. It starts here, on the left! Opposite!”

“Arabic: right to left. Point! English: left to right. Point!”

“Arabic has no big letters. سارة starts with the same س. In English: big S, Sara!”

“Look at ب. Alone: ب. At the start of باب: بـ. In the middle of لعبة: ـبـ. At the end of كلب: ـب. It changes! English b stays the same: bag, rabbit.”

“Arabic letters hold hands. English letters in books stand apart.”

  • أرِني كتابك العربي. من أين يبدأ؟ من اليمين!
  • والكتاب الإنجليزي يبدأ من اليسار. عكس بعضهما!
  • العربية ليس فيها حروف كبيرة: سارة تبدأ بالسين نفسها. أما في الإنجليزية فنكتب S كبيراً.
  • حرف الباء يغيّر شكله حسب مكانه: ب، بـ، ـبـ، ـب. أما b الإنجليزي فيبقى كما هو.
  • الحروف العربية تتصل وكأنها تمسك أيدي بعضها، أما حروف الكتب الإنجليزية فمنفصلة.
Students do
  • Open an Arabic book and an English book on camera and show where each starts.
  • Point with a finger: right to left for Arabic, left to right for English.
  • Find ب in different shapes in a short Arabic word on the slide (باب, لعبة, كلب).
  • Answer 'Arabic or English?' for each fact.
Exercise · arabic-or-english — Listen. Is it Arabic or English? Point right for Arabic, point left for English.
استمع: هل هذا في العربية أم الإنجليزية؟ أشر إلى اليمين للعربية، وإلى اليسار للإنجليزية.
  1. We write from left to right. → English (point left)
  2. There are no big letters. → Arabic
  3. A letter changes shape at the start, middle or end of a word (ب بـ ـبـ ـب). → Arabic
  4. In a book, the letters stand apart. → English
  5. Most letters join the next letter. → Arabic
  6. A name starts with a big letter: Sara. → English
Tips:
  • Present the two systems as equally clever: 'Two ways, both right.' Never 'English is easier' or 'Arabic is harder'.
  • Be accurate: English handwriting can join letters too (joined-up or cursive writing), but in printed books and in this class the letters stand apart.
  • Children who do not have an English book at home can use the class slide or the cover of any product with English writing.

5. Left to Right Train and the 'bed' Trick

24–32 min
قطار اليسار إلى اليمين وحيلة 'bed'
Method: Finger tracking under print + a gesture mnemonic for b and d
Purpose: Practice reading direction with known words and stop the most common letter reversal.
Teacher says

“Green dot: start! Red dot: stop! Put your finger on the green dot.”

“b - a - g. Slide your finger to the right. Bag!”

“Which letter comes first in book? Point! b! The one on the left!”

“Now a secret trick. Make two fists, fingers facing you. Thumbs up! Left hand is b, right hand is d. Look: b...e...d. A bed! b comes first, on the left.”

“Banana starts with... b! Use the bed: left hand! Dog starts with... d! Right hand!”

  • النقطة الخضراء للبداية، والحمراء للنهاية. ضع إصبعك على النقطة الخضراء!
  • اسحب إصبعك إلى اليمين تحت الحروف: b-a-g، bag!
  • حيلة سرية: اصنع قبضتين وأصابعك نحوك، وارفع إبهاميك. اليد اليسرى تصبح b واليمنى تصبح d، وبينهما كلمة bed مثل السرير!
  • إذا نسيت هل هو b أم d، اصنع السرير بيديك!
Students do
  • Track known words with a finger from the green dot to the red dot and say them.
  • Point to the first letter of a word (on the left).
  • Make the 'bed' with both hands and use it to decide b or d.
Exercise · point-and-say — Look at the word. Put your finger on the first letter (on the left). Say its sound. Is it b or d? Use your bed!
انظر إلى الكلمة، وضع إصبعك على أول حرف (على اليسار) وقل صوته. هل هو b أم d؟ استخدم السرير!
  1. bag → b - /b/ (left hand)
  2. dog → d - /d/ (right hand)
  3. book → b - /b/
  4. dad → d - /d/ (and d again at the end!)
  5. banana → b - /b/
  6. cake → c - /k/ (not b or d: a trickster!)
Tips:
  • Ask children to look at their own hands, not at the screen: the camera self-view is mirrored, so the bed can look backwards on screen.
  • Reversals are normal until about age 7; treat them with a smile and the trick, never as a mistake to worry about.

6. My Name Card: Big First, Then Small

32–40 min
بطاقة اسمي: الحرف الكبير أولاً ثم الحروف الصغيرة
Method: Personalized writing + puppet error-correction
Purpose: Apply both rules (big first letter, left to right) to the child's own name, then correct Lumi's mistakes.
Teacher says

“Let's write our names! Green dot on the left. First letter: big! Then small, small, small.”

“S-a-r-a. Big S, then small a, r, a. Sara!”

“Lumi wrote some words. Oh no! Lumi made mistakes! Can you help Lumi?”

“Lumi wrote 'sara'. What's wrong? Yes! Sara needs a big S!”

  • لنكتب أسماءنا! نبدأ من النقطة الخضراء على اليسار، والحرف الأول كبير، وبقية الحروف صغيرة.
  • لومي كتب بعض الكلمات وأخطأ! هل تساعدون لومي؟
  • كتب لومي 'sara' بحرف صغير. ما الخطأ؟ الاسم يحتاج حرفاً كبيراً في أوله!
Students do
  • Copy their name from the slide: big first letter, then small letters, left to right.
  • Hold the name card up to the camera.
  • Find and correct Lumi's mistakes by saying what is wrong.
Exercise · fix-the-mistake — Lumi made a mistake. Say what is wrong and how to fix it.
لومي أخطأ. قل ما الخطأ وكيف نصلحه.
  1. sara → Sara - a name starts with a big letter
  2. bAg → bag - no big letter in the middle
  3. dook (for book) → book - b, not d (use the bed!)
  4. koob (book written backwards, from the right) → book - start on the left: b-o-o-k
  5. nOOr → Noor - big N first, then small o, o, r
Tips:
  • Write each child's name on the slide first so they can copy it; some 4-year-olds will only manage the first letter, and that is fine.
  • Children with names that are spelled in different ways in English (Noor / Nour) can use the family's spelling.

7. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Goodbye

40–45 min
بطاقة الخروج، هل تعلم؟ والوداع
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check letter-family matching and directionality, and close the Sound Explorers bonus series.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket! Match the families, then show me which way English goes.”

“Did you know? The numbers 1, 2, 3 are called Arabic numerals in English. People in Europe learned them long ago from scientists who wrote in Arabic!”

“You are real Sound Explorers now. You know names and sounds, first sounds, rhymes, beats, big and small letters!”

“Goodbye! Write 'Bye' in the air: big B, small y, small e!”

  • بطاقة الخروج: طابق العائلات، ثم أرِني اتجاه الإنجليزية.
  • هل تعلم؟ الأرقام 1 و2 و3 تسمى في الإنجليزية 'الأرقام العربية'، لأن أوروبا تعلّمتها قديماً من علماء كتبوا بالعربية!
  • أصبحتم مستكشفي أصوات حقيقيين: تعرفون الاسم والصوت، والصوت الأول، والقافية، والنبضات، والحروف الكبيرة والصغيرة!
  • مع السلامة! اكتبوا Bye في الهواء.
Students do
  • Answer 5 quick items, at least one alone.
  • Show their name card one more time.
  • Sky-write 'Bye' and wave.
Tips:
  • Give a 'Sound Explorer' badge or star on the slide: this is the last bonus class of Pre-A.
  • Note children who still start on the right: give them a green start dot on every worksheet in Lessons 17-20.

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Items 1-3: point to the small letter for the big letter. Item 4: show which way English goes. Item 5: which letter in Sara is big?
الأسئلة 1-3: أشر إلى الحرف الصغير المناسب للحرف الكبير. السؤال 4: أرِنا اتجاه الإنجليزية. السؤال 5: أي حرف في Sara كبير؟
  1. A -> a / e / o → a
  2. B -> d / b / p → b
  3. G -> q / y / g → g
  4. Which way does English go? → left to right (finger moves from left to right)
  5. Sara: which letter is big? → S, the first letter

Homework

Letter family hunt: find 3 big letters and their small letters on boxes, books or packets at home. Write your name for your bedroom door with a big first letter.

رحلة البحث عن عائلات الحروف: ابحث عن 3 حروف كبيرة وحروفها الصغيرة على العلب أو الكتب أو الأغلفة في البيت. واكتب اسمك لباب غرفتك بحرف أول كبير.

  • Find 3 letter families at home (on a cereal box, a book, a toy) and show them to your family.
  • Make a name card for your door: green dot, big first letter, then small letters.
  • Show your family the 'bed' trick with your hands.

Support

  • Work only with the 'twin' families first (C c, O o, S s), then add A a and B b.
  • Give the child dotted letters of their name to trace instead of copying.
  • Answer direction questions by pointing only.

Challenge

  • Match all 26 letter families in a timed game.
  • Write a family member's name with a big first letter.
  • Find the four shapes of another Arabic letter (ت تـ ـتـ ـت) and the six Arabic letters that never join the next letter (ا د ذ ر ز و).

Success criteria

  • Matches at least 5 of 6 big letters to their small letters.
  • Answers at least 5 of 6 'Arabic or English?' items correctly.
  • Tracks a word from left to right without help.
  • Writes (or copies) their name starting with a big letter on the left.

Watch for

  • b/d and p/q reversals
  • Starting a word or line on the right
  • Big letters in the middle of words (bAg)
  • Small first letter in names (sara)

Teacher notes

  • This is the first class of Pre-A that looks at print. Most of the writing is copying or tracing; nobody is expected to spell.
  • Arabic-speaking children have to learn two directions and two systems at once. Name the difference openly: knowing why helps them switch.
  • The class closes the Sound Explorers strand: in the exit stage, remind children of all four big ideas (name and sound, first sound, rhyme and beats, big and small letters).
  • Level 1 Bonus Class 4 (Tricky Words and Sentence Rules) builds on the capital-letter rule with periods and question marks.
  • Timing: this class is 45 minutes (4 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 5), which suits 4-6 year olds online. Keep every stage short and moving, and add a 30-second stand-and-stretch between seated stages when attention drops. If the group is slow, trim in this order: shorten the history story in the Linguistics Corner to one sentence about the printers' boxes, fix only 3 of Lumi's mistakes in My Name Card, then do 4 'Arabic or English?' items instead of 6. Never cut the name writing, the movement break or the exit ticket.
