Every Word Has a Job: Parts of Speech

لكل كلمة وظيفة: أقسام الكلام
Big idea: Every word in a sentence has a job (noun, verb, adjective, pronoun, preposition), and every English sentence needs a verb, even when Arabic does not: He is a teacher, not He teacher.
لكل كلمة في الجملة وظيفة (اسم، فعل، صفة، ضمير، حرف جر)، وكل جملة إنجليزية تحتاج إلى فعل حتى عندما لا تحتاجه العربية: He is a teacher وليس He teacher.

Builds on

  • Lessons 1-2: Present Simple (I/you/we/they and he/she/it + -s): We hang out. She plays basketball.

Skill focus

grammar, word classes, sentence structure, linguistics

Linguistics Corner — Word classes: the building blocks of every language · أقسام الكلام: حجارة البناء في كل لغة

Almost every language in the world has at least two big word classes: nouns (things) and verbs (actions). Linguists think this is one of the few near-universal features of human language.تكاد كل لغات العالم تملك على الأقل قسمين كبيرين من الكلمات: الأسماء (الأشياء) والأفعال (الأحداث). ويرى اللغويون أن هذا من الصفات القليلة المشتركة بين كل اللغات البشرية تقريباً.
Arabic grammarians, like Sibawayh more than 1,200 years ago, divided words into three classes: اسم (noun), فعل (verb) and حرف (particle). Greek grammarians, about 2,000 years ago, used eight. English school grammar still uses eight parts of speech.قسّم علماء النحو العرب، مثل سيبويه قبل أكثر من 1200 سنة، الكلام إلى ثلاثة أقسام: اسم وفعل وحرف. أما نحويو اليونان قبل نحو 2000 سنة فقد استعملوا ثمانية أقسام، وما زال النحو المدرسي الإنجليزي يستعمل ثمانية.
A word's class depends on its job, not only on the word. In English the same word can change job with no change in form: I text my crew (verb) / I got a text (noun). Linguists call this conversion.قسم الكلمة يعتمد على وظيفتها في الجملة وليس على شكلها فقط. في الإنجليزية قد تغيّر الكلمة وظيفتها دون أن يتغيّر شكلها: I text my crew (فعل) / I got a text (اسم). يسمّي اللغويون هذا التحويل (conversion).
Nonsense test: in 'The blorks zibbed under the frim desk', you know blorks is a noun, zibbed is a verb and frim is an adjective, even though the words are not real. Your brain knows grammar by position and endings.اختبار الكلمات المخترعة: في جملة 'The blorks zibbed under the frim desk' تعرف أن blorks اسم و zibbed فعل و frim صفة مع أنها كلمات غير حقيقية. دماغك يعرف القواعد من موقع الكلمة ونهايتها.

Key terms

TermبالعربيةMeaning
nounاسمA word for a person, place, thing or idea: crew, desk, joke, Omar.
verbفعلA word for an action or a state: chat, play, laugh, is, are, like.
adjectiveصفةA word that describes a noun: awesome, boring, weird, big.
pronounضميرA word that stands in for a noun: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.
prepositionحرف جرA small word that shows place or connection: on, in, under, by, with.

Arabic Bridge

EnglishالعربيةWhat to notice
noun, adjective, pronounاسمIn traditional Arabic grammar, nouns, adjectives and pronouns are all اسم. English gives each of them its own class.
في النحو العربي التقليدي، الاسم والصفة والضمير كلها تدخل تحت قسم الاسم، أما الإنجليزية فتجعل لكل منها قسماً خاصاً.
verbفعلSame idea in both languages: a word for an action or event, with time inside it (play / يلعب).
الفكرة نفسها في اللغتين: كلمة تدل على حدث وفيها زمن (play / يلعب).
preposition (on, in, under)حرف جر (على، في، تحت)English prepositions are close to Arabic حروف الجر. In Arabic, حرف also includes و، هل، لا; in English these are conjunctions, question words or helpers.
حروف الجر الإنجليزية قريبة من حروف الجر العربية، لكن قسم الحرف في العربية يضم أيضاً و وهل ولا، وهذه في الإنجليزية لها أقسام أخرى.
He is a teacher. / My room is big.هو معلم. / غرفتي كبيرة.An Arabic nominal sentence (الجملة الاسمية) has no verb in the present. English always needs a verb, so we add am / is / are.
الجملة الاسمية العربية ليس فيها فعل في زمن الحاضر، أما الجملة الإنجليزية فتحتاج دائماً إلى فعل، لذلك نضيف am / is / are.
He was a teacher. / He isn't a teacher.كان معلماً. / ليس معلماً.Arabic does use a 'be' word in the past (كان) and in the negative (ليس). Only the present positive sentence has no verb. English uses be everywhere.
العربية تستعمل كلمة بمعنى 'يكون' في الماضي (كان) وفي النفي (ليس). الجملة الحاضرة المثبتة فقط بلا فعل، أما الإنجليزية فتستعمل be في كل الحالات.
There is a lamp on my desk.يوجد مصباح على مكتبي. / على مكتبي مصباح.Arabic can start with the place and use no verb; English needs the frame There is / There are.
العربية قد تبدأ بالمكان دون فعل، أما الإنجليزية فتحتاج إلى There is / There are.

Common errors of Arabic speakers

Student saysCorrectWhy
He teacher.He is a teacher.Direct copy of هو معلم: Arabic present nominal sentences have no verb. English needs is (and a before a job).
My room very big.My room is very big.Same nominal sentence pattern (غرفتي كبيرة جداً). Adjectives cannot be the verb in English.
The meme funny.The meme is funny.Missing be before an adjective; the sentence has no verb.
I am play football every day.I play football every day.Students learn 'am' as a sign of 'present' and add it to every sentence. Play is already the verb; two verbs crash.
She is like awesome games.She likes awesome games.Same overuse of is. Like is the verb here; with she it needs -s.
There is books on the shelf.There are books on the shelf.The verb must match the noun after it: one thing -> is, many things -> are.

1. Warm-up: The Nonsense Sentence

0–5 min
إحماء: الجملة المجنونة
Method: Curiosity hook with an invented-word sentence (grammar intuition)
Purpose: Show students they already 'feel' word classes, even with words that do not exist.
Teacher says

“Welcome to the Grammar Lab, crew! Today you are scientists. Our lab partner is Lumi.”

“Lumi sent us a weird message: 'The blorks zibbed under the frim desk.' None of these words are real!”

“Question: what is a blork? A thing, an action or a description? How do you know?”

“And zibbed? And frim? Write your guess in the chat: thing, action or description.”

“You knew it without a dictionary. That means your brain already knows grammar. Today we name what your brain knows.”

  • لومي أرسل لنا رسالة فيها كلمات غير حقيقية. هل تعرفون أيها شيء وأيها حدث وأيها وصف؟
  • عرفتم الجواب بدون قاموس! هذا يعني أن دماغكم يعرف القواعد. اليوم سنعطي أسماء لما يعرفه دماغكم.
Students do
  • Read the nonsense sentence aloud.
  • Type in the chat: blorks = thing / zibbed = action / frim = description.
  • Say one clue that helped (the -s on blorks, the -ed on zibbed, frim sits before desk).
Tips:
  • Do not explain yet; let them argue for 30 seconds. Curiosity is the goal.
  • Accept answers in Arabic: شيء، حدث، وصف.

2. Discovery: Meet the Five Word Jobs

5–17 min
اكتشاف: تعرّف على وظائف الكلمات الخمس
Method: Guided discovery (inductive grammar) with colour-coded sentences
Purpose: Students find the five word classes themselves from real Lesson 1-4 sentences, then get the names and colours.
Teacher says

“Look at these five sentences from our lessons. Some words are blue, some red, some green, some purple, some orange.”

“1. 'We hang out after school.' 2. 'She plays basketball.' 3. 'This game is awesome.' 4. 'There is a lamp on my desk.' 5. 'He makes a funny joke.'”

“Look only at the BLUE words: school, basketball, game, lamp, desk, joke. What do they have in common? ... Yes! They are things or people. These are NOUNS.”

“Now the RED words: hang out, plays, is, makes. They tell us what happens or what is. These are VERBS.”

“GREEN: awesome, funny. They describe a noun. ADJECTIVES.”

“PURPLE: we, she, he. They stand in for a person's name. PRONOUNS.”

“ORANGE: after, on. Small words that show place or connection. PREPOSITIONS.”

“Find the rule: how many red words are in each sentence? ... At least one in every sentence! Remember this - it's the key to today.”

  • انظروا إلى الكلمات الزرقاء فقط. ما الشيء المشترك بينها؟ كلها أشياء أو أشخاص: هذه أسماء.
  • الكلمات الحمراء أفعال، والخضراء صفات، والبنفسجية ضمائر، والبرتقالية حروف جر.
  • لاحظوا: في كل جملة كلمة حمراء واحدة على الأقل. هذا هو سرّ درس اليوم.
Students do
  • Look at the colour groups and say what each group has in common (English or Arabic).
  • Hold up the right card (N, V, Adj, P, Prep) when the teacher points to a word.
  • Count the red words in each sentence and notice every sentence has at least one.
  • Sort 10 new words into the five classes.
Exercise · sort — What is the word's job? Type N, V, Adj, P or Prep in the chat.
ما وظيفة الكلمة؟ اكتب N أو V أو Adj أو P أو Prep في الدردشة.
  1. crew → N (noun)
  2. laugh (We laugh a lot.) → V (verb)
  3. weird → Adj (adjective)
  4. they → P (pronoun)
  5. under → Prep (preposition)
  6. poster → N (noun)
  7. studies (He studies after dinner.) → V (verb)
  8. boring → Adj (adjective)
  9. in (in the corner) → Prep (preposition)
  10. she → P (pronoun)
Tips:
  • Let them find the pattern before you say the term. The name comes last.
  • Keep the colours identical in every Grammar Lab class: the colours become the group's shared language.
  • If a student says 'a noun is a thing', add 'or a person, a place or an idea' (Omar, school, joke).

3. Linguistics Corner: Same Word, Different Job

17–25 min
ركن اللغويات: الكلمة نفسها بوظيفة مختلفة
Method: Mini-lecture with a puzzle (concept check through conversion)
Purpose: Understand that a word's class depends on its job in the sentence, and that word classes exist in all languages.
Teacher says

“Fact: almost every language on Earth has nouns and verbs. Linguists say it's one of the very few things all languages share.”

“Old Arabic grammarians, like Sibawayh, used three classes: اسم، فعل، حرف. Old Greek grammarians used eight. English school grammar still uses eight.”

“Now a puzzle. 'I text my crew.' Is text a noun or a verb? ... Verb! 'I got a text.' Now? ... Noun!”

“Same word, different job. Linguists call this conversion. English does it all the time: chat, joke, text, laugh.”

“So how do you know? Look at the neighbours. After a / the / my -> probably a noun. After I / we / they -> probably a verb.”

  • كل لغات العالم تقريباً فيها أسماء وأفعال.
  • في الإنجليزية الكلمة نفسها قد تكون اسماً أو فعلاً حسب مكانها. انظر إلى الكلمة التي قبلها: بعد a / the / my غالباً اسم، وبعد I / we / they غالباً فعل.
Students do
  • Decide noun or verb for the same word in two sentences.
  • Explain the clue they used (the neighbour word).
  • Challenge: make your own pair with chat or joke.
Exercise · noun-or-verb — Is the underlined word a noun or a verb here? Say the clue word that helped you.
هل الكلمة المحددة اسم أم فعل هنا؟ اذكر الكلمة التي ساعدتك.
  1. I text my crew every day. (text) → verb (after I)
  2. I got a text from Sara. (text) → noun (after a)
  3. We chat after school. (chat) → verb (after we)
  4. Our group chat is so funny. (chat) → noun (after group / our)
  5. That joke is hilarious. (joke) → noun (after that)
  6. They joke all the time. (joke) → verb (after they)
  7. Ziad has a cool laugh. (laugh) → noun (after a cool)
  8. We laugh a lot together. (laugh) → verb (after we)
Tips:
  • Keep the history facts short; the puzzle is the real learning.
  • Arabic also builds words from roots (ضحك / ضحكة), but it changes the form; English often keeps the same form. Mention only if students ask.

4. Arabic Bridge: The Missing Verb

25–33 min
جسر العربية: الفعل المفقود
Method: Contrastive analysis with side-by-side sentences
Purpose: Compare English classes with اسم/فعل/حرف and explain why English needs am / is / are where Arabic has no verb.
Teacher says

“Arabic grammar has three classes: اسم، فعل، حرف. Where do our five English classes go? Nouns, adjectives and pronouns are all اسم in Arabic grammar. Prepositions are حروف جر.”

“Now look: هو معلم. How many verbs? ... Zero! It's a nominal sentence - جملة اسمية. Perfectly correct Arabic.”

“English: He teacher. Is there a red word? No! An English sentence without a verb is broken. English says: He IS a teacher.”

“Same with غرفتي كبيرة -> My room IS big. And النكتة مضحكة -> The joke IS funny.”

“Interesting: Arabic uses كان in the past: كان معلماً. And ليس in the negative: ليس معلماً. So Arabic only drops the verb in the present positive. English never drops it.”

“Rule of the Lab: every English sentence needs a red word.”

  • جملة 'هو معلم' جملة اسمية صحيحة تماماً في العربية، ولا فعل فيها.
  • لكن الجملة الإنجليزية بلا فعل جملة مكسورة، لذلك نقول He is a teacher.
  • العربية تستعمل كان في الماضي وليس في النفي، وتحذف الفعل فقط في الحاضر المثبت. الإنجليزية لا تحذفه أبداً.
Students do
  • Translate three short Arabic nominal sentences and spot where English needs is / are.
  • Say each English sentence and tap the table on the red word (is / are).
  • Compare: كان معلماً = He was a teacher; ليس معلماً = He isn't a teacher.
Exercise · translate-and-add-the-verb — Say it in English. Remember the red word (am / is / are).
قلها بالإنجليزية، ولا تنسَ الكلمة الحمراء (am / is / are).
  1. هو معلم. → He is a teacher.
  2. غرفتي كبيرة. → My room is big.
  3. أنا متعب. → I am tired.
  4. أصدقائي رائعون. → My friends are awesome.
  5. المصباح على المكتب. → The lamp is on the desk.
  6. هذه اللعبة مملة. → This game is boring.
  7. يوجد ملصق على الحائط. → There is a poster on the wall.
Tips:
  • Say clearly that هو معلم is correct Arabic. The point is difference, not 'Arabic is missing something'.
  • Point out the article too: Arabic معلم -> English a teacher.

5. Grammar Detective: Fix the Broken Sentences

33–43 min
المحقق النحوي: أصلح الجمل المكسورة
Method: Error correction as a detective case (noticing + self-correction)
Purpose: Practise finding the verb and repairing typical Arabic-speaker sentences.
Teacher says

“Detectives! Lumi's robot wrote these sentences, but some are broken. Your job: find the problem and fix it.”

“Step 1: find the red word. Step 2: no red word? Add one. Two crashing verbs? Remove one.”

“Careful - some sentences are already correct. Don't fix what isn't broken!”

“Noor, you're detective one. Read sentence 1. Is it broken?”

  • ابحثوا عن الفعل أولاً. إذا لم يوجد فأضيفوه، وإذا وُجد فعلان متصادمان فاحذفوا واحداً.
  • انتبهوا: بعض الجمل صحيحة.
Students do
  • Take turns reading a sentence and deciding: correct or broken.
  • Type the fixed sentence in the chat.
  • Name the reason in one word: missing verb / two verbs / is or are.
Exercise · error-correction — Correct or broken? Fix the broken ones.
صحيحة أم مكسورة؟ أصلح الجمل المكسورة.
  1. Omar my best friend. → Omar is my best friend. (missing verb)
  2. The beanbag very soft. → The beanbag is very soft. (missing verb)
  3. I am chat with my crew every day. → I chat with my crew every day. (two verbs)
  4. We hang out after school. → Correct
  5. There is two lamps in my room. → There are two lamps in my room. (is/are)
  6. She is like weird memes. → She likes weird memes. (two verbs)
  7. My cousins annoying. → My cousins are annoying. (missing verb)
  8. Hamad doesn't like boring shows. → Correct
  9. The speaker on the shelf. → The speaker is on the shelf. (missing verb)
  10. There are a rug under my desk. → There is a rug under my desk. (is/are)
Tips:
  • Praise the reason, not just the answer: 'missing verb' is real grammar thinking.
  • 'I am chat' is a very common error: students think 'am' means 'present'. Say: 'chat is already your red word'.

6. Team Challenge: Sentence Builder Race

43–53 min
تحدي الفرق: سباق بناء الجمل
Method: Colour-coded sentence building + team competition (gamification)
Purpose: Produce correct sentences from word-class patterns, with a verb in every one.
Teacher says

“Two teams: Team Omar and Team Sara. I show a colour pattern. You build a true sentence about you that fits the pattern.”

“Example: purple - red - green: 'I am tired.' / 'We are awesome.'”

“One point for a correct sentence, one bonus point for the fastest team, and one bonus point for a funny sentence.”

“Golden rule: if there's no red word, no points!”

  • أعرض عليكم نمطاً من الألوان، وأنتم تبنون جملة صحيحة عن أنفسكم تطابق النمط.
  • لا نقاط لجملة بلا فعل!
Students do
  • Work in pairs (or alone in a group of 2) to build a sentence for each colour pattern.
  • Type it in the chat or say it on the mic; the other team checks the colours.
  • Use words from Lessons 1-4: crew, joke, meme, shelf, desk, poster, awesome, weird, chat, laugh, text.
Exercise · build-the-pattern — Build a sentence that matches the colour pattern. (Model answers below - any correct sentence wins.)
ابنِ جملة تطابق نمط الألوان. (إجابات نموذجية أدناه، وأي جملة صحيحة مقبولة.)
  1. pronoun + verb + noun → We play football. / I text Noor.
  2. pronoun + verb + adjective → She is funny. / They are awesome.
  3. noun + verb + preposition + noun → Lumi laughs at memes. / Books are on the shelf.
  4. pronoun + verb + adjective + noun → He has a weird nickname. / I like cool posters.
  5. noun + verb + adjective → My room is small. / The joke is lame.
  6. There + verb + noun + preposition + noun → There is a lamp on my desk. / There are posters on the wall.
  7. pronoun + don't/doesn't + verb + adjective + noun → I don't like boring shows. / He doesn't watch lame movies.
  8. Free choice: a sentence with all five colours → She chats with her crew in a cool group chat. (pronoun, verb, preposition, adjective, noun)
Tips:
  • Show patterns as coloured blocks, not words, so students must think in classes.
  • With 2 students, play student vs Lumi (the teacher plays Lumi and makes deliberate mistakes for them to catch).
  • Accept possessives (my, her) and articles (a, the) as 'helpers'; we do not score them as a colour today.

7. Exit Ticket, Did You Know? and Mission

53–60 min
بطاقة الخروج وهل تعلم؟ والمهمة
Method: Formative check + curiosity close
Purpose: Check that each student can find the verb and fix a verbless sentence; send them home with a fact to share.
Teacher says

“Exit ticket: five quick items. Work alone, type your answers in a private chat to me.”

“Did you know? Many languages - Arabic, Russian, Indonesian and others - can say 'He teacher' with no verb in the present. English is the strict one: it always wants a verb.”

“Your mission: explain to someone at home why English says 'He is a teacher' but Arabic says 'هو معلم'.”

  • هل تعلم؟ لغات كثيرة مثل العربية والروسية والإندونيسية تقول 'هو معلم' بلا فعل في الحاضر، أما الإنجليزية فصارمة: تريد فعلاً دائماً.
  • مهمتك: اشرح لأحد في البيت لماذا نقول He is a teacher بالإنجليزية.
Students do
  • Answer the 5 exit-ticket items in a private chat.
  • Say the big idea in one sentence (English or Arabic).
Tips:
  • Anyone who scores under 4/5: give them 2 'find the verb' sentences in the warm-up of Lesson 5.
  • Keep the Did You Know? fact for the start of Lesson 5 too: 'Who explained it at home?'

Exit ticket

Exercise · exit ticket — Answer quickly: name the word class or fix the sentence.
أجب بسرعة: اذكر قسم الكلمة أو أصلح الجملة.
  1. What class is 'awesome' in 'This game is awesome'? → adjective
  2. Find the verb: 'My crew hangs out at the mall.' → hangs out
  3. Fix it: 'My sister a student.' → My sister is a student.
  4. Noun or verb? 'I got a text.' (text) → noun
  5. Fix it: 'There is three posters on the wall.' → There are three posters on the wall.

Homework

Colour Detective: write 6 sentences about your room and your crew. Colour every word: nouns blue, verbs red, adjectives green, pronouns purple, prepositions orange. Check that every sentence has a red word.

المحقق الملوّن: اكتب 6 جمل عن غرفتك وأصدقائك، ولوّن كل كلمة: الأسماء بالأزرق، والأفعال بالأحمر، والصفات بالأخضر، والضمائر بالبنفسجي، وحروف الجر بالبرتقالي. تأكد أن في كل جملة كلمة حمراء.

  • Write and colour 6 sentences (3 about your room, 3 about your friends).
  • Write 2 Arabic nominal sentences (like هو معلم) and their English translations with is / are.
  • Find one word that can be both a noun and a verb (like text or chat) and write two sentences.

Support

  • Start with only three classes (noun, verb, adjective), then add pronoun and preposition.
  • Give a word bank sorted by colour for the Sentence Builder Race.
  • Let students answer with the card or colour instead of the term.

Challenge

  • Learn the other three English parts of speech: adverb (quickly), conjunction (and, but), interjection (wow!). Find one of each in a sentence.
  • Write a nonsense sentence like Lumi's and let the group label the classes.
  • Find a word that can be two classes with no change in form, like 'cool' (adjective: a cool jacket / verb: Let the tea cool.). Write a sentence for each job.

Success criteria

  • Sorts at least 8 of 10 words into the correct class.
  • Finds the verb in any Lesson 1-4 sentence.
  • Fixes at least 7 of 10 broken sentences and names the reason.
  • Explains in English or Arabic why English needs is in He is a teacher.

Watch for

  • Missing be: He teacher / My room big / The meme funny
  • Extra be with a main verb: I am play / She is like
  • there is + plural noun
  • Confusing adjectives and nouns (awesome vs game)

Teacher notes

  • This class gives the group a shared metalanguage (noun, verb, adjective...) and five colours. Use the same colours in every lesson and Bonus Class after this.
  • Do not teach adverbs, conjunctions and interjections to the whole group today; they are a challenge item only.
  • Arabic is presented as a different system, not a wrong one. Students who know some Arabic grammar (مبتدأ وخبر) will enjoy the comparison; others only need 'Arabic can skip the verb, English can't'.
  • Bonus Class 2 builds on this: once students know the word classes, they learn the fixed order in which English puts them (S-V-O and adjective before noun).
