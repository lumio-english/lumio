In May 2023, Rayyanah Barnawi looked down at the Arabian Peninsula from the International Space Station. She was the first Saudi woman in space, and she was doing science up there: experiments about cells and rain. Ziad watched the launch online and decided something. "In 2040," he said, "I will be up there too."

His class made predictions for the year 2040. Sara thinks cars will drive themselves, and the city will be greener, with farms inside tall buildings. Hamad predicts that students won't carry bags, because every book will be on one small screen. Noor believes doctors will cure diseases that we cannot cure today.

Not every prediction is happy. Omar thinks the summers will be hotter, so cities will build more shade and plant more trees. "Technology won't fix everything," he says. "People will have to change too." The class agreed that this was the most honest prediction of the day.

Ziad's prediction is the biggest. He believes a Saudi scientist will discover something important on the Moon, and that a Gulf city will invent a new way to make water from air. "Will you succeed?" Sara asks him. Ziad shrugs. "I will try," he says. "And if I fail, I will have tried from space."
