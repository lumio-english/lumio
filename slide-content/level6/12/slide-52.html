When the Riyadh Metro opened in December 2024, Sara's family had a debate. Her father loves his car. It is comfortable, it is convenient, and nobody else is in it. Her mother thinks the metro is the most reasonable option: it is cheaper, it is faster at rush hour, and you can read on it.

So Sara tested both. On Sunday she went to the library by car. It took forty-five minutes because the highway was full. Her father was patient, but the car was not moving. On Monday she took the metro: twenty-two minutes, a cool station, a seat by the window and three chapters of her book.

The car is more flexible, Sara wrote in her notebook. You can leave whenever you want, and you can carry heavy things. But the metro is more reliable, more affordable and far more efficient during the day. The stations are the most beautiful buildings in the city.

Her conclusion surprised her father. "Metro to school, car to Grandmother's house," she said. "Grandmother lives far from a station, and she sends us home with twelve boxes of food." Her father smiled. The most useful answer, he admitted, is usually 'both'.
