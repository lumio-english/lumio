On the first morning of Eid, Omar's phone did not stop. His family group chat had two hundred messages before breakfast. His aunt sent a picture of the sheep. His uncle sent a video of the kids. Everyone sent 'Eid Mubarak' with a hundred lanterns.

Omar replied to all of them. He called his grandmother. He checked his messages every ten minutes. But he did not send one message, and he did not notice. His cousin Faisal, who moved to Dammam last year, was waiting.

On the third day, Faisal wrote: "Did you forget me?" Omar's face went red. Did he forget? Yes, he did. He didn't reply because he didn't see Faisal's name in the long list. "I'm so sorry," he typed. "Did you attend the Eid prayer? Did you miss Riyadh?" Faisal did not answer.

So Omar did something better. He bought a box of chocolates, wrote a card by hand, and asked his father to drive to Dammam on Friday. When Faisal opened the door, he laughed. "You didn't text me," he said, "but you came." "A reminder," Omar said. "Next Eid, you are first on my list."
