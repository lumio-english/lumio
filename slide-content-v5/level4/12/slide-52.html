Reading Lab · Grandfather's Advice
52 / 66

Sara's grandfather is eighty years old. He does not have an Instagram account, but he has a lot of opinions about phones. "You should look up," he tells Sara. "The sky is bigger than your screen."

Sara laughs, but she listens. On the wall, there is an old photo of a pearl-diving boat. In the photo, her grandfather's father is a young pearl diver. When summer comes, the divers stay at sea for four months. "Their advice is simple," her grandfather says. "Be patient. The sea does not hurry, so you shouldn't hurry either."

His tips are simple. You should sleep eight hours. You shouldn't check your phone at the table. You should call your friends, not just text them. And you should make one mistake every day, because a day without a mistake is a day without learning.

Sara tries to improve. She puts her phone in a basket at dinner. It is hard, and she makes mistakes. But her grandfather only smiles. "Patience," he says, and he gives her a date from his pocket. "Now tell me about your day. All of it."
