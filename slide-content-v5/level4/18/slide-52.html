Reading Lab · Guests Are a Gift

In the Gulf, a guest is a gift. When someone knocks on the door, the family does not ask why. They open the door, they say "Ahlan wa sahlan", and they bring coffee. The guest sits in the best seat, and the host serves gahwa with the right hand, a little at a time.

There are rules, and everyone knows them. You offer dates before coffee. You do not fill the cup; a full cup means 'please leave'. The guest usually drinks one to three cups, then shakes the cup to say 'enough'. At the end, there is bukhoor, and the smell of incense follows the guest home.

PART 1 · teacher192 words · 12 known

Omar's class is studying customs around the world. In Japan, you take off your shoes and bow. In Morocco, the host pours mint tea from very high. In Turkey, a guest gets coffee and a small glass of water. Every culture says 'welcome' in a different way.

"What is the same everywhere?" the teacher asks. Omar thinks for a moment. "Something to drink," he says, "and the best seat." The teacher smiles. "Yes. Hospitality is a language, and it does not need a passport."
