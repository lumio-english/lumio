This year, Noor's family is hosting Eid lunch for the first time, and the house is in planning mode. Her mother is making the guest list tonight; her father is picking up the lamb on Thursday morning; her brother is leaving school early to help. "Forty-two guests," her mother says, looking at the sheet. "Forty-five," says her father. "Your cousins always bring cousins."

Noor is in charge of the sweets. On Wednesday evening she is baking maamoul with her grandmother, who does not use a recipe and does not trust anyone who does. On Thursday, she is decorating the majlis: cushions along the walls, the long mat, fresh flowers and a tray of dates by the door.

There is also a schedule on the fridge. Eid prayer at six. Breakfast at seven, but only a small one. Guests are arriving at one, starting with the grandparents. The young cousins are coming after prayer and staying until midnight, which worries the cat.

"Are you nervous?" Sara asks on the phone. Noor looks at the list, the oven and the flowers. "A little," she says. "But Grandmother is bringing her coffee pot, and when that pot is in the house, nothing goes wrong." Besides, next year her aunt is hosting, and Noor is attending as a guest. With an appetite.
