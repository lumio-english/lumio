Reading Lab · Ziad's Trip to Muscat
54 / 68

Last spring, Ziad's family packed three suitcases and drove to the airport. The flight to Muscat was short, but Ziad was so excited that he checked his boarding pass ten times. They landed at noon, and the air smelled of the sea.

The hotel was near the old port of Mutrah. On the first day, they explored the corniche. White houses with wooden windows stood along the water, and brown mountains rose behind them. Ziad's father explained that ships from India and Africa stopped here hundreds of years ago.

The next morning, they visited the souq. It was dark and cool inside, with a thousand small lamps. Ziad's mother bought frankincense, a famous resin from Oman. The seller showed them how to burn it. The smoke smelled like old books and lemons.

They stayed for five days. They climbed a fort, swam in a quiet bay and ate rice with fish. Ziad returned with a small bag of frankincense and one plan: next year, the mountains of Salalah. "Travel is a habit," his father said, "and we just started it."
