Reading Lab · Green Flags Everywhere

Last September, Hamad's school asked him to speak at the National Day assembly. He was proud, but he was also terrified. He practiced his speech forty times in front of the mirror and twice in front of his cat.

On the morning of the 23rd, the whole city was green. There were flags on the cars, flags on the balconies, and green lights on the tallest towers. Hamad's heart beat fast when he walked onto the stage. The microphone made a loud noise, and everyone laughed. He was embarrassed for one second. Then he smiled, and the fear went away.

His speech was about his grandfather, who moved to Riyadh when it was a small town with one main street. "My grandfather was surprised by every new building," Hamad said. "Today, I am surprised by nothing. But I am grateful for everything."

When he finished, the hall was silent for a moment. Then the clapping started. His teacher was thrilled, and his mother cried a little. Hamad felt relieved and calm, like after a long race. That night, the fireworks were green, and so was his heart.
