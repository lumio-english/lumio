Last Thursday, Sara's family turned the majlis into a cinema. Her father hung a white sheet on the wall, her brother connected the projector, and her mother made three bowls of popcorn. Noor came too, because everyone was there.

They chose a Saudi film about a boy and his falcon in the desert. While the opening credits were rolling, Sara's little sister was already asking questions. "Is the falcon real? Where is the boy's mother?" Everyone was laughing and shushing her at the same time.

The film was better than they expected. In the middle, there was a plot twist: the falcon was not lost, it was stolen. The whole room gasped. Sara's grandmother, who was knitting, stopped knitting. Her father, who was checking his phone, put it down.

Then, at the best scene, Sara fell asleep. She was leaning on a cushion when the boy found his falcon, and she missed the ending. The next morning, her sister explained everything, twice, with sound effects. "I recommend it," Sara said at school. "Especially the part I didn't see."
